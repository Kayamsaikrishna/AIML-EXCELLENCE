Calculus 1.6 Expansion

Divergence Theorem

Measuring the "Source" vs "Flow" across a boundary.

Gauss's Theorem

The Divergence Theorem relates the flow (flux) across a closed surface to the sum of sources/sinks inside. \[ \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F}) \, dV = \unicode{x222F}_S \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{S} \]

Intuition: If you have a room full of air, the total amount of air leaving through the windows (Flux) must equal the total expansion of air inside the room (Divergence of the volume). If the room is sealed and incompressible, both are zero.

2. AI/ML Connection: Normalizing Flows

Conservation of Probability

In Normalizing Flows (a type of generative model), we transform a simple probability distribution (like a Gaussian) into a complex one (images of faces) using a sequence of invertible functions.

The "Change of Variables" formula in probability is essentially the Divergence Theorem in disguise. As we stretch and squeeze the space, the probability density must adjust so that the total probability remains 1.0. The log-determinant of the Jacobian measures this local expansion/contraction—exactly what Divergence captures in continuous-time flows (ODE-based generative models).

3. Python: Verification on a Cube

verify_divergence.py
import sympy as sp

# Field F = < x^2, y, z >
# Volume V: Cube from 0 to 1 in x, y, z
# Surface S: 6 faces of the unit cube

x, y, z = sp.symbols('x y z')
F = sp.Matrix([x**2, y, z])

# 1. Volume Integral: Triple Integral of Div(F)
div_F = sp.diff(F[0], x) + sp.diff(F[1], y) + sp.diff(F[2], z)
# Div(F) = 2x + 1 + 1 = 2x + 2

vol_integral = sp.integrate(div_F, (z, 0, 1), (y, 0, 1), (x, 0, 1)) 
# Integral (2x+2) dx = [x^2 + 2x] from 0 to 1 = 3.
# Integrate over y (0 to 1) -> 3
# Integrate over z (0 to 1) -> 3
print(f"Volume Integral (Div): {vol_integral}")

# 2. Surface Integral: Sum of Flux on 6 faces
# Face x=1 (Normal i): F=<1,y,z> . <1,0,0> = 1. Int(1) dydz = 1.
# Face x=0 (Normal -i): F=<0,y,z> . <-1,0,0> = 0.
# Face y=1 (Normal j): F= . <0,1,0> = 1. Int(1) dxdz = 1.
# Face y=0 (Normal -j): F= . <0,-1,0> = 0.
# Face z=1 (Normal k): F= . <0,0,1> = 1. Int(1) dxdy = 1.
# Face z=0 (Normal -k): F= . <0,0,-1> = 0.

flux_sum = 1 + 0 + 1 + 0 + 1 + 0
print(f"Surface Integral (Flux): {flux_sum}")

print(f"Theorem Holds: {vol_integral == flux_sum}")
