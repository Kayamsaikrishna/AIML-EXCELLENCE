Cross-Entropy Definition

Measuring the Gap. How much extra "Surprise" occurs when you use the wrong distribution to predict a source?

The Inefficient Code

Imagine a source produces messages using distribution \(P\). You, however, believe the source uses distribution \(Q\).

Cross-entropy is the average number of bits you need to transmit the messages if you build your code based on your belief (\(Q\)) instead of the truth (\(P\)). It is the total measurement of uncertainty plus the cost of your incorrect assumption.

1. Definition

For two probability distributions \(P\) (true) and \(Q\) (predicted) over the same set of outcomes:

$$ H(P, Q) = E_P [-\log Q] = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) \log Q(x) $$

Compare this to Shannon Entropy \(H(P)\), where the log term also uses \(P\). In Cross-Entropy, the weights are true, but the log-surprise is based on the prediction.

2. The Decomposition

A fundamental identity links entropy, cross-entropy, and KL Divergence:

$$ H(P, Q) = H(P) + D_{KL}(P \| Q) $$
  • H(P): The inherent uncertainty of the data (Irreducible).
  • D_KL(P || Q): The "Extra bits" wasted because your model \(Q\) is imperfect.
The Minimum Principle: Because \(D_{KL} \ge 0\), the lowest possible value for Cross-Entropy is the Entropy of the source itself. You can never predict a source better than the source itself!

Deep Learning Classification

The Standard AI Loss

When we train a classifier (e.g., Logistic Regression or a BERT model), we are almost always minimizing Cross-Entropy.

  • One-Hot Labels: In classification, \(P(x)\) is usually `1` for the correct class and `0` for others. This simplifies Cross-Entropy to just \(-\log Q(correct\_class)\).
  • Optimization Goal: By minimizing this value, we are forcing the model's prediction \(Q\) to get closer to the distribution of the true labels \(P\).
  • Information Theory Perspective: Training a model is the process of reducing the "Wasteful Bits" (\(D_{KL}\)) until the Cross-Entropy nearly matches the data's inherent Entropy.
