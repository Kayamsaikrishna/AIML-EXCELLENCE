13. Bayes Factors

Quantifying the strength of evidence for one model over another.

1. Evidence Ratio

A Bayes Factor (BF) is the ratio of the marginal likelihoods of two competing models. It measures how much the data changed your relative belief in Model A vs. Model B.

$$ BF_{12} = \frac{P(D | M_1)}{P(D | M_2)} $$

Interpretation: $BF=10$ means the data is 10 times more likely under Model 1 than Model 2. It is an "Odds" metric.

2. The Automatic Occam's Razor

A unique property of Bayes Factors is that they **Punish Complexity** automatically. A model with 1,000 parameters has a lower marginal likelihood because its "Probability Mass" is spread very thin over many possibilities. Only if the simple model *cannot* explain the data will the complex model win. This makes Bayes Factors immune to Overfitting.

3. AI/ML Connection: Architectural Selection and Physics-Informed AI

Scientific Model Picking

In AI, Bayes Factors are used for Universal Model Selection.

When deciding between a **Simple Linear Model** and a **Complex Neural Network** for a critical task (like Predicting Earthquakes), we don't just look at validation error. A Neural Network might have a lower error but be totally overfitted. Using **Bayes Factors**, we can say: "The evidence for the Linear Model is 50x stronger given the small amount of data we have." This is also essential in Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**: we use Bayes Factors to test if including a physical constraint (like Gravity) actually improves the model's validity or if the AI is just "Faking it" with high-dimensional noise.

# BF12 < 1: Evidence for Model 2
