04. Posterior Distribution

The synthesis of prior knowledge and new evidence.

1. The Final Verdict

The Posterior Distribution $P(\theta | x)$ represents your updated belief about the parameter $\theta$ after observing data $x$. It is essentially the "Answer" to a Bayesian inference problem.

2. The Balancing Act

The posterior is a compromise between the **Prior** and the **Likelihood**.

  • If you have **Little Data**: The posterior stays close to the Prior.
  • If you have **Massive Data**: The Likelihood dominates, and even a strong prior is eventually "overwhelmed" by the evidence.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Learned Model

Prediction with Confidence

In AI, the Posterior is the Learned Model.

When you train a **Spam Filter**, the Posterior represents everything the AI knows about "Spammy" words. But unlike a simple "Yes/No" score, the posterior gives you a distribution. This is used in Probabilistic Deep Learning** to create **Ensemble effects** without multiple models. Instead of asking one model for its guess, we "Average" the predictions across the weights in the posterior distribution (Bayesian Model Averaging). This leads to predictions that are not only more accurate but also more **Robust to Noise**. If the posterior for a specific input is "Bi-modal" (two separate peaks), the AI knows it's seeing something ambiguous and should perhaps defer to a human expert.

4. Python: Posterior vs. Prior

# Scenario: We have a Prior N(0, 1) and observe data N(2, 0.5)
prior = norm.pdf(x, 0, 1)
likelihood = norm.pdf(x, 2, 0.5)

# Posterior is proportional to Prior * Likelihood
unnormalized_posterior = prior * likelihood
# We normalize it so it sums to 1
posterior = unnormalized_posterior / np.trapz(unnormalized_posterior, x)

plt.plot(x, prior, '--', label='Prior')
plt.plot(x, likelihood, ':', label='Likelihood')
plt.plot(x, posterior, '-', label='Posterior')
# Notice how the Posterior sits BETWEEN the initial guess and the evidence!
