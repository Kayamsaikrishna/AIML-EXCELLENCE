04. Algebraic & Geometric Multiplicity

When eigenvalues repeat, does the space collapse?

1. Algebraic Multiplicity (AM)

The number of times an eigenvalue appears as a root of the characteristic polynomial.

$$ P(\lambda) = (\lambda - 2)^3 \implies \lambda = 2 \text{ has AM = 3} $$

2. Geometric Multiplicity (GM)

The number of linearly independent eigenvectors associated with that eigenvalue. It is the dimension of the Null Space of \((\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I})\).

$$ \text{GM}(\lambda) = \text{nullity}(\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I}) $$

The Cardinal Rule: \( \text{GM} \leq \text{AM} \)

If \( \text{GM} < \text{AM} \), the matrix is Defective. It cannot be fully diagonalized because it lacks a full basis of eigenvectors.

3. AI Bottleneck Analogy

In a neural network layer, if the weight matrix is defective, certain degrees of freedom in the latent representation are permanently lost (information collapse), potentially hindering learning capacity.

4. Python: Detecting Multiplicity

import numpy as np

# A defective matrix
A = np.array([[2, 1], [0, 2]])

# Char Poly: (lambda - 2)^2 -> AM = 2
eigenvals, eigenvects = np.linalg.eig(A)

print(f"Eigenvalues: {eigenvals}") 
# Result: [2, 2]

# Check nullity of (A - 2I)
nullity = A.shape[0] - np.linalg.matrix_rank(A - 2*np.eye(2))
print(f"Geometric Multiplicity (GM): {nullity}")
# Result: 1. (GM < AM -> Defective)
