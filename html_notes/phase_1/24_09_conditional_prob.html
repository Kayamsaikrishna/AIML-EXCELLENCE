09. Conditional Probability Definition

Updating our beliefs based on new information.

1. What is Conditional Probability?

Conditional Probability is the probability of an event \(A\) occurring, given that another event \(B\) has already occurred. It represents the "reduced" sample space where only outcomes in $B$ are considered.

2. The Formula

The probability of $A$ given $B$ is the ratio of the intersection area to the total area of $B$:

$$ P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} $$

This is only defined when $P(B) > 0$. If $B$ is impossible, we cannot condition on it.

3. AI/ML Connection: Recommender Systems

Collaborative Filtering

In Recommendation Engines (like Amazon's "Customers who bought this also bought..."), conditional probability is the primary driver.

The system calculates:

$$ P(\text{Buy Book Y} | \text{Bought Book X}) = \frac{\text{Users who bought BOTH}}{\text{Users who bought Book X}} $$

By ranking items based on these conditional probabilities, AI can suggest the most relevant products. Every "Prediction" an AI makes is technically a conditional probability: P(Label | Features). You are asking the model: "Given that I see these pixels, what is the probability that the object is a car?" Understanding the math of conditioning is the first step toward understanding how AI makes decisions based on evidence.

4. Python: Calculating Conditional Probability

# Experiment: Survey of 100 people
# 60 like Coffee (B)
# 40 like Tea
# 30 like BOTH (A and B)

p_b = 60 / 100
p_a_and_b = 30 / 100

# Condition: Probability someone likes Tea (A) GIVEN they like Coffee (B)
p_a_given_b = p_a_and_b / p_b

print(f"P(Tea | Coffee): {p_a_given_b:.2f}") # 0.50
