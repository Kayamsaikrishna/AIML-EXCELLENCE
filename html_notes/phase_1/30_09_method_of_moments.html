09. Method of Moments (MoM)

Estimation by matching observation to theory.

1. The Core Idea

The Method of Moments is the oldest and simplest technique for estimation. It works by setting the **Sample Moments** (like the sample mean and sample variance) equal to the **Population Moments** (the theoretical formulas involving $\theta$) and solving for the parameters.

$$ E[X^k] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^k $$

2. Advantages & Disadvantages

  • **Fast:** Requires very little computation compared to iterative methods.
  • **Always Possible:** You can almost always write down these equations.
  • **Not Always Efficient:** MoM estimates are often "noisy" and aren't as precise as Maximum Likelihood estimates.

3. AI/ML Connection: Parameter Priming and Initialization

The "Warm Start" for AI

In modern AI, MoM is often used for Parameter Initialization.

When training a complex model like a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) or a Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), we often need a "good starting point" before we begin heavy optimization. We use Method of Moments to get a "quick and dirty" estimate of the clusters' means and variances. This prevents the model from getting stuck in bad local minima. In Spectral Learning (a branch of AI), MoM is used to recover the parameters of hidden states in a sequence (like a Hidden Markov Model) without using iterative algorithms like Expectation-Maximization. It provides a "Consistent" (though maybe not "Efficient") baseline for every AI research project.

4. Python: Estimating Gamma Parameters

# Population: Gamma distribution with alpha=9, beta=2
# Theoretical Mean = alpha/beta = 4.5
# Theoretical Var = alpha/beta^2 = 2.25

sample = np.random.gamma(9, 1/2, 1000)
s_mean = np.mean(sample)
s_var = np.var(sample)

# MoM Formulas: 
# beta_hat = mean / var
# alpha_hat = mean^2 / var

beta_est = s_mean / s_var
alpha_est = (s_mean**2) / s_var

print(f"Est Alpha: {alpha_est:.2f} (True: 9)")
print(f"Est Beta: {beta_est:.2f} (True: 2)")
