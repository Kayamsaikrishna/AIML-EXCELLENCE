09. Functions as Power Series

Building complex equations from simple blocks.

1. The Geometric Base

The simplest representation is the geometric series:

$$ \frac{1}{1 - x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \quad \text{for } |x| < 1 $$

We can manipulate this to find series for other functions. For example, replacing \(x\) with \(-x^2\) gives the series for \(1/(1 + x^2)\).

2. Term-by-Term Calculus

Inside the interval of convergence, you can differentiate or integrate a power series just like a standard polynomial. The new series will have the same radius of convergence.

$$ \frac{d}{dx} \sum c_n x^n = \sum n c_n x^{n-1} $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Polynomial Kernels & Feature Maps

Infinite Feature Spaces

In Kernel Methods and SVMs, we often lift data into a higher-dimensional space to make it linearly separable. A Polynomial Kernel \(K(x, y) = (1 + x \cdot y)^n\) essentially expands the data into products of its features.

By viewing these kernels as Power Series, we can theoretically work with Infinite-Dimensional features. As long as the series representation of the "Kernel" converges, the AI can perform calculations in an infinite feature space without ever having to explicitly list every feature. This is the "Kernel Trick" that allows AI to learn extremely complex patterns with relatively modest computing power. The RBF Kernel (Gausssian) is the power series of the exponential function!

4. Python: Integrating a Series

# Series for 1/(1+x) = 1 - x + x^2 - x^3 ...
# Integral is ln(1+x) = x - x^2/2 + x^3/3 - x^4/4 ...

import numpy as np

def ln_approx(x, terms=10):
    return sum(((-1)**(n+1) * x**n)/n for n in range(1, terms+1))

val = 0.5
print(f"Approx ln(1.5): {ln_approx(val):.6f}")
print(f"Actual ln(1.5): {np.log(1.5):.6f}")
