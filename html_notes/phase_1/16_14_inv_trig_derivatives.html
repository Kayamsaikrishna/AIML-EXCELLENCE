14. Derivatives of Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Differentiating the "Arcs".

1. The Primary Derivatives

Inverse trig functions have derivatives that result in algebraic (non-trig) expressions. This is one of the most surprising and useful results in calculus.

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[\arcsin x] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} $$ $$ \frac{d}{dx}[\arccos x] = \frac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} $$ $$ \frac{d}{dx}[\arctan x] = \frac{1}{1+x^2} $$

2. Importance of the Domain

Because \(\arcsin\) and \(\arccos\) are only defined for \(x \in [-1, 1]\), their derivatives approach infinity near the boundaries. \(\arctan\), however, is defined for all real numbers and has a very smooth derivative.

3. AI/ML Connection: Bounded Output Learning

Arctan and Phase Estimation

The Arctan function is often used in models that deal with Angles or Phase estimation (e.g., in radar signal processing or computer vision when calculating orientation).

The derivative \(\frac{1}{1+x^2}\) is particularly nice for AI because it never explodes (reaches infinity) and is always positive. This is similar to the Cauchy distribution in statistics, and it is used in some specialized neural network layers where we need a "soft" version of a bounded ramp.

4. Python: Symbolic Verification

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
fns = [sp.asin(x), sp.acos(x), sp.atan(x)]

for f in fns:
    print(f"Derivative of {f} is: {sp.diff(f, x)}")
