12. f-Divergences

The unified family of statistical distances.

1. The Generalized Formula

f-Divergence** is a general framework that encompasses many different ways of measuring the difference between probability distributions. For a convex function $f$, the f-divergence is:

$$ D_f(P || Q) = \int q(x) f\left(\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right) dx $$

By changing the function $f$, we get different "Distances."

2. The Famous Relatives

  • $f(t) = t \log t$: Yields KL Divergence.
  • $f(t) = |t - 1| / 2$: Yields Total Variation Distance.
  • $f(t) = (\sqrt{t} - 1)^2$: Yields Hellinger Distance.
  • $f(t) = (t - 1)^2$: Yields Pearson $\chi^2$ Divergence.

3. AI/ML Connection: f-GANs and Variational Logic

Pick Your Poison

In AI, f-Divergence allows us to customize how a model learns.

In f-GAN Architecture**, we replace the standard GAN loss with different f-divergences. For example, using **Pearson Divergence** makes the GAN more stable in certain high-dimensional spaces. In Variational Inference**, choosing Hellinger distance instead of KL can make the model more robust to outliers because it "Penalizes" differences in a more balanced way. By understanding the f-divergence family, AI engineers can choose the "Learning Metric" that best fits the noise profile of their data. It is the toolkit for **Custom Optimization Objectives**.

4. Mathematical Property

All f-divergences satisfy the Data Processing Inequality**: processing our data (e.g., adding noise or filtering) can never *increase* the f-divergence between two signals. Information, once lost, cannot be recovered by further calculation.
