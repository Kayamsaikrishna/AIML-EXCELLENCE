09. Triple Scalar Product

Measuring volume and coplanarity.

1. Definition & Geometric Meaning

The Triple Scalar Product combines a dot and a cross product. It results in a scalar that represents the signed volume of the parallelepiped formed by three vectors.

$$ \mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w}) = \begin{vmatrix} u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \\ w_1 & w_2 & w_3 \end{vmatrix} $$

Key Theorems

  • Coplanarity: If \( \mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w}) = 0 \), the three vectors lie in the same plane.
  • Cyclic Permutation: \( \mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{w} \cdot (\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{v} \cdot (\mathbf{w} \times \mathbf{u}) \).

2. Convex Hull & Spatial AI

Intersection Tests

In Autonomous Driving and Spatial Computing, we need to know if a point lies within a 3D volume. The Triple Scalar Product is used to calculate the volume of tetrahedrons, which is the building block for 3D mesh algorithms.

  • Geometric Reasoning: Determining if three vectors are linearly independent in 3D by checking if their TSP is non-zero.
  • Stability: Detecting "degenerate" cases where a 3D object has collapsed into a 2D plane.

3. Python: Volume Calculation

import numpy as np

u = np.array([1, 0, 0])
v = np.array([0, 1, 0])
w = np.array([0, 0, 1])

# Volume of a unit cube
volume = np.dot(u, np.cross(v, w))

print(f"Volume: {volume}") # Output: 1.0

# Using Determinant form
det_volume = np.linalg.det(np.array([u, v, w]))
print(f"Volume (Det): {det_volume}")
