Prob 1.4 Rare Events

The Poisson Distribution

Modeling the flow of time and space.

The Law of Rare Events

Imagine a Binomial distribution where \(n \to \infty\) (millions of trials) and \(p \to 0\) (very rare chance of success), but the average number of successes \(np = \lambda\) stays constant.

This limit is the Poisson Distribution. It models the number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space.

1. The Formula

$$ P(X=k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!} $$ Where \(\lambda > 0\) is the average rate (events per interval).
  • Wait time at a call center (calls per minute).
  • Radioactive decay (particles per second).
  • Typos on a page (errors per page).

2. Properties

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \lambda\)
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = \lambda\)

A key property of Poisson is that Mean = Variance. If your data has Variance >> Mean, use Negative Binomial (as discussed previously).

3. Poisson Process

If events follow a Poisson distribution, the time between events follows an Exponential Distribution (continuous).

4. ML Connection: Poisson Regression

GLMs Regression

Predicting Counts

Standard Linear Regression (MSE Loss) assumes the target \(y\) is Gaussian (continuous, symmetric, can be negative).

When predicting counts (e.g., number of website visitors), we use Poisson Regression (a Generalized Linear Model).
We predict the log of the rate: \(\log(\lambda) = w^T x\).
The loss function derived from Poisson likelihood is: $$ \text{Loss} = \hat{y} - y \log(\hat{y}) $$
