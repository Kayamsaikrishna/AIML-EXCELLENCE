10. Implicit Differentiation

Differentiating equations where \(y\) is not alone.

1. When to use it?

Normally we differentiate functions in explicit form like \(y = x^2\). But what if the variables are tangled up, like in a circle: \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)?

Instead of solving for \(y\) (which gives messy square roots), we differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a function of \(x\) and using the Chain Rule.

2. The Technique

Whenever you differentiate a term with \(y\), you must multiply by \(\frac{dy}{dx}\).

Example: \(\frac{d}{dx} [y^2] = 2y \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}\)

Finally, you isolate \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) algebraically.

3. AI/ML Connection: Implicit Surfaces & Manifolds

Constrained Landscapes

In Dimensionality Reduction and Manifold Learning (like T-SNE or UMAP), we hypothesize that data lives on an "implicit surface" or manifold. We often have equations that define what "valid" data looks like, but we can't express it as a simple \(y=f(x)\) function.

Implicit differentiation allows us to find the "normal vector" to these surfaces. In Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), we use implicit differentiation to ensure the network's predictions satisfy complex equations like conservation of energy or fluid dynamics.

4. Python: Symbolic Implicit Differentiation

import sympy as sp

x, y = sp.symbols('x y')
# Equation of a circle: x^2 + y^2 - 25 = 0
eq = x**2 + y**2 - 25

# Implicitly differentiate w.r.t x
dy_dx = sp.idiff(eq, y, x)

print(f"Eq: {eq} = 0")
print(f"dy/dx = {dy_dx}") 
# Output: -x/y
