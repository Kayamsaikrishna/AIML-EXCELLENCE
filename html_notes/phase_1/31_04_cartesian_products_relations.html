Cartesian Products & Relations

Building Connections. Moving from isolated collections to pairs and triplets that describe how different dimensions of data relate to one another.

The Product of Worlds

A single set is a group. A Cartesian Product is the set of all possible pairs between two groups. A Relation is any subset of that product — it picks only the "meaningful" connections between the sets.

1. The Cartesian Product (\(A \times B\))

The set of all ordered pairs \((a, b)\) where \(a \in A\) and \(b \in B\).

\( A \times B = \{(a, b) \mid a \in A \land b \in B\} \)

Coordinates

The standard 2D plane \(\mathbb{R}^2\) is the Cartesian product \(\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}\). Every point is a pair of numbers.

Size

\(|A \times B| = |A| \cdot |B|\). This multiplicative growth is why high-dimensional spaces explode in complexity (Curse of Dimensionality).

2. Binary Relations (\(R\))

A Relation \(R\) from \(A\) to \(B\) is a subset of \(A \times B\). It represents a specific rule or connection.

  • Mathematics: The relations \(<\), \(=\), or "is a factor of."
  • Sociology: "Is a friend of," "Lives next to."

Reflexive

Every element is related to itself (\(a R a\)).

Symmetric

If \(a R b\), then \(b R a\).

Transitive

If \(a R b\) and \(b R c\), then \(a R c\).

A relation that is all three is called an Equivalence Relation, which allows us to "partition" a set into groups.

Relations in Modern Systems

  • Relational Databases (SQL): A table in SQL is literally a Relation. It's a subset of the Cartesian product of its columns (\(\text{ID} \times \text{Name} \times \text{Email}\)). We "query" it using relational algebra.
  • Knowledge Graphs: These are collections of relations (triplets). \((Subject, Relation, Object)\). "Paris" (A) has the relation "is capital of" with "France" (B).
  • Recommendation Systems: The "User-Item Matrix" is a relation. The interaction \(R(u, i) = 1\) if user \(u\) bought item \(i\), and \(0\) otherwise. Matrix Factorization helps us predict missing links in this relation.
  • Word Embeddings: We use cosine similarity (a relation) to find how close two vectors are in the high-dimensional Cartesian space of the model.
