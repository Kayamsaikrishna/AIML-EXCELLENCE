Stats 1.8 Bayesian Optimization

Multi-Armed Bandits (MAB)

Learning while doing. Why waiting for an A/B test to finish is often a waste of money.

The Explore-Exploit Dilemma

In a traditional A/B test, you spend 50% of your traffic on a potentially "worse" version (Treatment A or B) for two weeks to get a p-value. This is "Regret".

Bandits solve this by actively shifting traffic toward the "Winner" as soon as it starts to pull ahead. - Explore: Try new/uncertain options to gather data. - Exploit: Use the option that currently has the best performance.

1. Three Famous Strategies

1. \(\epsilon\)-Greedy

With probability \(\epsilon\) (e.g., 0.1), pick a random arm. Otherwise, pick the best-performing one. Simple, but "Explore" is blind.

2. Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)

Pick the arm with the highest potential ("Optimism in the face of uncertainty"). It favors arms we haven't tried in a while.

3. Thompson Sampling

The Bayesian King: Maintain a Beta distribution for each arm. Sample from each distribution and pick the highest. Elegant and state-of-the-art.

3. ML Connection: Reinforcement Learning

Active Learning RecSys

The Foundation of Active Intelligence

MAB is the simplest form of Reinforcement Learning (RL).

  • Recommender Systems: Netflix uses Bandits to decide which images (box art) to show for a movie. They don't wait for two weeks; they switch the art in real-time as users click more on one version.
  • Dynamic Pricing: Airlines use Bandits to explore which price point yields the highest revenue without losing seat occupancy.
  • Bayesian Optimization: When optimizing Neural Network hyperparameters, we use UCB (Upper Confidence Bound) to decide which set of weights to "explore" next.
Pro-Tip: If you care about Statistically Significant Proof, use an A/B test. If you care about Maximizing Revenue/Clicks while testing, use a Bandit.
