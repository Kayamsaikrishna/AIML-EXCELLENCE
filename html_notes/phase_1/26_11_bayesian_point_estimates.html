Stats 1.6 MAP Inference

Bayesian Point Estimates

Converting a "cloud of probability" into a single decision or prediction.

The Choice of Best

Bayesians usually prefer the whole distribution, but sometimes you just need one number (e.g., what weight should I put in this CSV?).

Which number you pick depends on what you are afraid of (your Loss Function).

1. The Big Three

Estimate Mathematical Definition Loss Function Minimized
Posterior Mean \( E[\theta | D] \) L2 Loss (Squared Error)
Posterior Median Value where \( P(\theta < val)=0.5 \) L1 Loss (Absolute Error)
Posterior Mode (MAP) \(\text{argmax}_{\theta} P(\theta|D)\) 0-1 Loss (Accuracy)

2. Deep Dive: MAP Estimation

MAP (Maximum A Posteriori) is the most common Bayesian bridge to classical ML.

$$ \theta_{MAP} = \text{argmax}_{\theta} [ \log P(D|\theta) + \log P(\theta) ] $$

It's just Maximum Likelihood (MLE) with an extra "Prior" term added. - If the Prior is "Flat" (Uniform), MAP = MLE.

3. ML Connection: L2 Regularization (Ridge)

Supervised Regularization

Why Ridge is Bayesian

In Ridge Regression, we minimize: $$ \text{Loss} = \sum (y - \hat{y})^2 + \lambda \sum w^2 $$

Where did that \(\lambda \sum w^2\) come from? It's not just "magic magic to stop overfitting."

Mathematically, this is exactly the MAP Estimate of a model where the Prior distribution of the weights is a Normal distribution \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1/\lambda)\). - Frequentist: "I'm punishing large weights." - Bayesian: "I'm assuming (Prior) that weights are small and centered at zero."

Similarly, Lasso (L1) Regularization is the MAP estimate with a Laplace Prior.
