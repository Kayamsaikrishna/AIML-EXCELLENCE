1. Symmetric Matrices

A matrix is symmetric if it equals its transpose: \( A = A^T \).

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \color{blue}{7} & \color{red}{3} \\ \color{blue}{7} & 4 & \color{green}{5} \\ \color{red}{3} & \color{green}{5} & 9 \end{bmatrix} $$

Importance: They ALWAYS have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors. Covariance, Hessian, and Distance matrices are all symmetric.

2. Orthogonal Matrices

A matrix \( Q \) is orthogonal if its columns are orthonormal unit vectors.

$$ Q^T Q = I \implies Q^{-1} = Q^T $$

Multiplying by \( Q \) is just a Rotation (or reflection). It never stretches or shrinks the vector ($ ||Qx|| = ||x|| $). This is crucial for numerical stability in algorithms (like QR decomposition in RNNs).

3. Rotation Matrix

Creating an Orthogonal matrix that rotates space.

import numpy as np

# Rotation by 45 degrees
theta = np.radians(45)
c, s = np.cos(theta), np.sin(theta)

Q = np.array([
    [c, -s],
    [s, c]
])

print("Rotation Matrix Q:")
print(Q)

# Check Orthogonality: Q.T @ Q should be Identity
check = np.dot(Q.T, Q)
print(f"\nQ^T * Q:\n{check.round(2)}")

# Apply to vector [1, 0]
v = np.array([1, 0])
v_rot = np.dot(Q, v)
print(f"\nRotated Vector: {v_rot}")
# Should be [0.707, 0.707]
