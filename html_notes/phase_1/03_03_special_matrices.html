03. Diagonal, Triangular, and Symmetric Matrices

Structured matrices for efficient computation.

1. Definitions & Visuals

Symmetric Matrix (\( \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}^T \))

The elements are mirrored across the main diagonal. \( a_{ij} = a_{ji} \).

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \color{blue}{2} & \color{red}{3} \\ \color{blue}{2} & 5 & \color{green}{6} \\ \color{red}{3} & \color{green}{6} & 9 \end{bmatrix} $$

AI Use: The Covariance Matrix and the Hessian Matrix (used in optimization) are always symmetric.

Diagonal Matrix

All non-diagonal elements are zero. Multiplying by a diagonal matrix is a simple element-wise scaling.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} w_1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & w_2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & w_3 \end{bmatrix} $$

Triangular Matrices

  • Upper Triangular (U): Elements below the diagonal are zero.
  • Lower Triangular (L): Elements above the diagonal are zero.

AI Use: These are the output of LU Decomposition, used to solve linear systems efficiently.

2. Skew-Symmetric Matrices (\( \mathbf{A} = -\mathbf{A}^T \))

Diagonal elements must be zero. They represent infinitesimal rotations in physics engines for robotics.

3. Python: Working with Structure

import numpy as np

# Create a Symmetric Matrix
A = np.array([[1, 2], [2, 5]])
is_symmetric = np.allclose(A, A.T)

# Extract Diagonal
diag = np.diag(A)

# Create Triangular matrices
U = np.triu(A)
L = np.tril(A)

print(f"Upper Triangular:\n{U}")
