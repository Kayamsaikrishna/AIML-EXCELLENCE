10. Vector Fields & Line Integrals

Integrating along a path through a "windy" space.

1. Vector Fields

A vector field \(\vec{F}(x, y)\) assigns a vector to every point in space. Think of it as a map of wind speeds or gravitational pull.

2. Line Integrals of Vector Fields

A Line Integral calculates how much the field "helps" or "hinders" you as you move along a curve \(C\). This is the definition of Work (\(W\)):

$$ W = \int_{C} \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \int_{a}^{b} \vec{F}(\vec{r}(t)) \cdot \vec{r}'(t) dt $$

It is the integral of the dot product of the force and the direction of travel.

3. AI/ML Connection: Policy Gradients & Drift

Movement through Weight Space

An AI model training over time is a "particle" moving through a high-dimensional vector field of gradients. The Line Integral of the model's trajectory tells us the "Total Energy" or "Total Learning" the model has performed.

In Reinforcement Learning, we measure "Policy Drift". If an AI agent's behavior changes too much along a path, it might become unstable. Researchers use line integrals to calculate the "Cumulative Violation" of constraints along the agent's path. If the line integral of the "Reward Field" along the path is high, the AI's training is considered efficient and successful.

4. Python: Work along a Parabolic Path

# Field F =  (A vortex)
# Path r(t) =  from t=0 to 1
# dr/dt = <1, 2t>
# F dot dr/dt = (t^2 * 1) + (-t * 2t) = -t^2

import sympy as sp
t = sp.Symbol('t')
work = sp.integrate(-t**2, (t, 0, 1))

print(f"Total Work along path: {work}") # -1/3
