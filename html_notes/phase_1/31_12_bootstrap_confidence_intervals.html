12. Bootstrap Confidence Intervals

Turning 1,000 simulations into a solid guarantee.

1. Three Ways to Build an Interval

Once you have 1,000 bootstrap estimates ($\hat{\theta}^*_1, ..., \hat{\theta}^*_{1000}$), you can construct a CI in three main ways:

  • The Generic (Standard) Method: $\hat{\theta} \pm Z_{\alpha/2} \times SE_{boot}$. (Assumes the bootstrap distribution is Normal).
  • The Percentile Method: Take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of your 1,000 simulations. (Assumes the distribution is symmetric).
  • The BCa Method (Bias-Corrected and Accelerated): A complex correction that adjusts for "Lean" (bias) and "Spread" (skewness) in the results. This is the **Gold Standard** for publication-quality statistics.

2. Why Percentile is "Risky"

If your estimator is biased (e.g., $s^2$ systematically underestimates $\sigma^2$ in small samples), the Percentile Interval will be centered in the wrong place. The BCa method detects this "Shift" and moves the interval boundaries to compensate, ensuring a true 95% coverage rate.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Precision Bounds

Reporting like a Forensic Scientist

In AI, the way you report error bars matters for **Commercial Credibility**.

If you are pitching an AI product to a client, saying "Our model is 95% accurate" sounds like a marketing claim. Saying "Our model achieved a **95% BCa Bootstrap Confidence Interval** of $[94.2\%, 96.1\%]$ based on 5,000 trials" sounds like a mathematical fact. It proves that you have considered the risk of a "Lucky Test Set." Furthermore, in Hyperparameter Optimization, we use these intervals to prune search branches—if the upper bound of a configuration's performance is already lower than the lower bound of our best known config, we can safely kill that experiment and save compute power.

4. Python: Percentile vs. BCa

# Scipy handles the complex math of BCa
from scipy.stats import bootstrap

data = (np.random.normal(50, 5, 20),) # Data must be a tuple
res = bootstrap(data, np.mean, confidence_level=0.95, method='BCa')

print(f"BCa Confidence Interval: [{res.confidence_interval.low:.2f}, {res.confidence_interval.high:.2f}]")
print(f"Standard Error of Mean: {res.standard_error:.4f}")
