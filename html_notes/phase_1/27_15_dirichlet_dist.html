15. Dirichlet Distribution

The distribution of probability vectors (Multivariate Beta).

1. Definition

The Dirichlet Distribution, denoted $Dir(\alpha)$, is a family of continuous multivariate probability distributions parameterized by a vector $\alpha$ of positive reals. It is the multivariate generalization of the Beta distribution.

$$ f(x_1, \dots, x_k; \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_k) = \frac{1}{B(\alpha)} \prod_{i=1}^k x_i^{\alpha_i-1} $$

Where the values $x_i$ lie on a **Simplex** (meaning $x_i \ge 0$ and $\sum x_i = 1$).

2. The "Concentration" Parameter

The vector $\alpha$ controls the "density" of the distribution. If all $\alpha_i < 1$, the mass is concentrated at the corners of the simplex (sparse distributions). If all $\alpha_i> 1$, the mass is concentrated at the center (balanced distributions). If $\alpha_i = 1$, it is a uniform distribution over the simplex.

3. AI/ML Connection: Topic Modeling and Bayesian Networks

The Prior for Proportions

The Dirichlet distribution is the backbone of Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA).

In **Natural Language Processing**, we assume that every document is a mix of topics, and every topic is a mix of words. The "Mix" is a probability vector, and the Dirichlet distribution acts as the **Prior belief** for those vectors. By adjusting $\alpha$, we can force the AI to produce "Sparse" topics (where each document only has a few main topics) or "Dense" ones. It is also used in **Bayesian Neural Networks**, where instead of learning single weight values, the model learns a Dirichlet distribution over possible categories, allowing the AI to express Uncertainty about its own classification.

4. Python: Sampling a Probability Vector

import numpy as np

# Dirichlet with alpha = [2, 2, 2] (Balanced)
alpha_balanced = [2, 2, 2]
sample1 = np.random.dirichlet(alpha_balanced)
print(f"Balanced Sample: {sample1.round(3)}")

# Dirichlet with alpha = [0.1, 0.1, 0.1] (Sparse)
alpha_sparse = [0.1, 0.1, 0.1]
sample2 = np.random.dirichlet(alpha_sparse)
print(f"Sparse Sample: {sample2.round(3)}") # Likely contains zeros/near-zeros
