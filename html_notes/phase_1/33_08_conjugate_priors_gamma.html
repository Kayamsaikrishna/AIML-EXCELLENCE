08. Gamma-Poisson Conjugate Pair

Estimating rates and frequencies with Bayesian counts.

1. The Scenario

If you are monitoring "Counts" (number of clicks, number of server failures, number of stars), the data follows a **Poisson** distribution with a rate $\lambda$. The conjugate prior for $\lambda$ is the Gamma Distribution.

2. The Update Rule

The Gamma distribution has two parameters: $k$ (shape) and $\theta$ (scale), or more commonly $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

  • Prior: $Gamma(\alpha, \beta)$
  • Data: $n$ observations with a total sum of counts $S = \sum x_i$.
  • Posterior: $Gamma(\alpha + S, \beta + n)$

Again, learning is purely additive. $\alpha$ accumulates the events, and $\beta$ accumulates the "Time" or "Opportunities."

3. AI/ML Connection: CLV and Traffic Prediction

Modeling Rarity and Frequency

In AI, the Gamma-Poisson pair is the standard for Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)** models.

Not every customer is the same. Some buy once a year; others buy every week. We use a **Poisson Likelihood** to model their purchasing frequency. To capture the "Group Wisdom," we use a **Gamma Prior** representing the general population behavior. As a specific customer makes purchases, their individual $\lambda$ is updated via the Gamma-Poisson rule. This allows the AI to predict how many times a user will visit in the next 6 months. It's also vital in Cybersecurity AI**: we use it to establish a "Baseline Arrival Rate" for server requests. If the posterior suddenly shifts toward a much higher $\lambda$, the AI can flag a potential **DDoS attack** in its earliest stages.

4. Python: Updating a Rate

# Initial belief: alpha=2, beta=1 (Mean rate = 2)
a, b = 2, 1

# We observe 3 minutes where counts were [5, 4, 6]
sum_counts = 15
minutes = 3

# Posterior update
a_post = a + sum_counts
b_post = b + minutes

# New distribution: Gamma(17, 4) -> Mean rate = 4.25
# The AI has learned that the 'Rate' is higher than initially thought.
