21. Directed Information

Measuring the arrow of causality.

1. Beyond Symmetric Correlation

Mutual Information $I(X; Y)$ is symmetric—it tells you how much they share, but not who is "leading" whom. Directed Information $I(X^n \to Y^n)$** measures the information flow from sequence $X$ to sequence $Y$ over time, without including the common future.

It is the information-theoretic generalization of Granger Causality.

2. The Causal Bottleneck

Directed information tells you how much information you gain about $Y_t$ by knowing previous $X_{1 \dots t}$, *over and above* what you already knew from $Y_{1 \dots t-1}$. It isolates the unique causal influence of $X$ on $Y$.

3. AI/ML Connection: Causal discovery and RNN Dynamics

Who Drives the Signal?

In AI, Directed Information is the gold standard for Causal Discovery in Multi-agent RL** and Financial Forecasting.

When training a Multi-agent System, we use directed information to measure whether Agent A is influencing Agent B. This allows the AI to learn "Follower" and "Leader" behaviors. In Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)** and Transformers, we use directed info to analyze the "Arrow of Information." Does the model truly rely on the context, or is it just echoing the most recent token? Recent researchers use Transfer Entropy** (a form of directed info) to visualize how gradients flow through time. It is the math that allows us to build Explainable AI (XAI): we can prove that "Feature X caused Prediction Y" rather than just showing they are correlated.

4. Mathematical Note

Directed Information is particularly important in Human-in-the-loop AI. We want to maximize the directed information from the human's feedback to the AI's weight updates, ensuring the AI "Listens" rather than just fitting the noise in the rewards.
