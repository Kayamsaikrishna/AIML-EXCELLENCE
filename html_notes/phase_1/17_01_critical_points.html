01. Critical Points & Extrema

Finding the mountains and valleys of a function.

1. What is a Critical Point?

A point \(c\) in the domain of \(f\) is a critical point if:

  1. \(f'(c) = 0\) (Horizontal Tangent)
  2. OR \(f'(c)\) does not exist (Sharp corners or vertical tangents)

2. Extrema types

  • Local Maximum: The peak of a local hill.
  • Local Minimum: The bottom of a local valley.
  • Global Maximum: The absolute highest point on the interval.
  • Global Minimum: The absolute lowest point on the interval.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Search for Minima

Stationary Points

In Machine Learning, we define a Loss Function (Cost Function). The "best" model is the one with the minimum loss. Calculus tells us that the minimum must occur at a critical point.

Therefore, training a model consists solely of finding a critical point where the gradient is zero. However, critical points can also be maxima or "saddle points"—which is why we need deeper tests to confirm we found a minimum.

4. Python: Finding Critical Points

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 5
f = x**3 - 3*x**2 + 5

# Step 1: Find derivative
f_prime = sp.diff(f, x)

# Step 2: Solve f'(x) = 0
crit_points = sp.solve(f_prime, x)

print(f"Function: {f}")
print(f"Critical Points occur at: {crit_points}") 
# Output: [0, 2]
