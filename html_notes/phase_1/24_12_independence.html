12. Independence of Events

When outcomes don't care about each other.

1. Defining Independence

Two events $A$ and $B$ are Independent if the occurrence of one does not change the probability of the other. Mathematically, knowing that $B$ happened gives you zero new information about the likelihood of $A$.

2. The Multiplication Test

The definitive test for independence is whether the probability of both occurring is exactly the product of their individual probabilities:

$$ P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B) $$

Equivalently: $P(A|B) = P(A)$ and $P(B|A) = P(B)$.

3. AI/ML Connection: Disentangled Representations

Feature Independence

In Feature Selection and Dimensionality Reduction, we often look for independent features. If two features are perfectly independent, they provide unique information. If they are dependent, one is redundant.

Independent Component Analysis (ICA) is a powerful AI technique used for "Blind Source Separation" (e.g., separating two voices talking at the same time). It works by assuming the original sources are statistically independent. By maximizing the independence of the output signals, the AI can "Unmix" complex data. Independence is the mathematical goal of Disentanglement: we want our AI's internal logic to separate "Style" from "Content" or "Shape" from "Color", treating each as an independent knob it can turn.

4. Python: Dependency Check

# Dataset: 100 images
# A: Image has a Dog (20/100)
# B: Image has a Blue Background (30/100)
# Intersection: Dog AND Blue Background (6/100)

p_a = 0.2
p_b = 0.3
p_inter = 0.06

is_independent = np.isclose(p_a * p_b, p_inter)
print(f"Independent? {is_independent}") # True: (0.2 * 0.3 = 0.06)
