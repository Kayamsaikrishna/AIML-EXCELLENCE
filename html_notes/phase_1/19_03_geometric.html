Prob 1.4 The Wait

The Geometric Distribution

How many failures before the first success?

Persistence Pays Off

Instead of fixing the number of trials (Binomial), what if we fix the goal: "Keep trying until you get a Success."

This is the Geometric Distribution. It models waiting times.

1. Definition and PMF

Let \(X\) be the number of Bernoulli trials needed to get the first success. $$ P(X=k) = (1-p)^{k-1} p $$ for \(k = 1, 2, 3, \dots\)
Logic: You must fail \(k-1\) times (prob \((1-p)^{k-1}\)) and then succeed on the \(k\)-th try (prob \(p\)).

Note: Sometimes defined as the number of *failures* before success (support starts at 0). In that case, \(P(Y=k) = (1-p)^k p\). Be careful which definition is being used! We use the "trials count" definition here (starts at 1).

2. Moments

  • Expectation: \(E[X] = \frac{1}{p}\).
    If probability of success is 1/10, you expect to wait 10 trials.
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = \frac{1-p}{p^2}\).

3. The Memoryless Property

The Geometric distribution is the only discrete distribution that is memoryless.

$$ P(X > n + k \mid X > n) = P(X > k) $$

Interpretation: If you've already flipped the coin 100 times and failed every time, the probability that you will need 5 more flips is the same as if you were starting from scratch. The coin has no memory. The past failures don't make a success "due".

4. ML Connection: Reinforcement Learning

RL Grid World

Grid World Exploration

In a simple RL environment, if an agent takes a random action that leads to the goal with probability \(p\), the number of steps to reach the goal is Geometrically distributed.

This helps in estimating the time horizon needed for training episodes. If \(p\) is very small (sparse rewards), \(E[X] = 1/p\) explodes, making training difficult (the "Sparse Reward Problem").
