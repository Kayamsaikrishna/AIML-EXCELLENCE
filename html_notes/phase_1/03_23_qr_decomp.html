23. QR Decomposition

Orthogonality satisfies numerical stability.

1. The Definition

QR decomposition factors a matrix \( \mathbf{A} \) into an Orthogonal Matrix \( \mathbf{Q} \) and an Upper Triangular Matrix \( \mathbf{R} \).

$$ \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{QR} $$

Recall: For an orthogonal matrix, \( \mathbf{Q}^T\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{I} \).

2. The Gold Standard for Least Squares

Numerical Stability in Regression

To solve the Normal Equation \( \mathbf{w} = (\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{y} \), directly calculating the inverse is bad. Using QR is much more stable:

$$ \mathbf{Rw} = \mathbf{Q}^T\mathbf{y} $$

Since \( \mathbf{R} \) is triangular, we solve for \( \mathbf{w} \) using simple backward substitution.

  • Algorithm: Usually implemented via Householder Reflections rather than Gram-Schmidt for better stability.
  • Eigenvalues: Identifying the eigenvalues of a matrix iteratively (the QR algorithm) relies on this decomposition.

3. Python: QR for Regression

import numpy as np

# Random data
X = np.random.rand(10, 3)
y = np.random.rand(10, 1)

# QR Decomposition
Q, R = np.linalg.qr(X)

# Solve for weights w
# R @ w = Q.T @ y
w = np.linalg.solve(R, Q.T @ y)

print(f"Regression Weights via QR:\n{w}")
