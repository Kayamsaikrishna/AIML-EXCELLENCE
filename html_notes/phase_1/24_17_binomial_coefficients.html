17. Binomial Coefficients and Pascal's Triangle

The scaling of binomial probability.

1. Expanding the Power

The Binomial Theorem describes the algebraic expansion of powers of a binomial. The coefficients of these terms, denoted \(\binom{n}{k}\), are the same as our combination formula.

$$ (x + y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^k $$

2. Pascal's Identity

Pascal's Triangle is a visual representation where each number is the sum of the two directly above it. Mathematically:

$$ \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k} $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Binary Classification Sequences

Bernoulli Trials

When an AI model makes a binary prediction (Yes/No), it is performing a Bernoulli Trial. If we run 10 such trials, the probability of getting exactly 7 "Yes" predictions is given by the Binomial Distribution, which relies entirely on binomial coefficients.

Binomial Naive Bayes is an AI algorithm used specifically for binary data (like the presence or absence of a word in a document). It uses these coefficients to estimate the likelihood of a document belonging to a class. Furthermore, understanding how binomial coefficients grow (the central terms being the largest) explains why "Average" outcomes are so frequent in data while "Extreme" outcomes (all 0s or all 1s) are statistically rare. This is the math of Sampling Variance.

4. Python: Plotting Coefficients

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from scipy.special import comb

n = 20
k_vals = range(n + 1)
coeffs = [comb(n, k) for k in k_vals]

plt.bar(k_vals, coeffs)
plt.title(f"Binomial Coefficients for (x+y)^{n}")
plt.xlabel("k")
plt.ylabel("Value")
plt.show()
