01. Double Integrals over Rectangles

Integrating across a two-dimensional domain.

1. The Concept

While a single integral measures the area under a 1D curve, a double integral measures the volume under a 3D surface \(z = f(x, y)\) over a rectangular region \(R\) in the \(xy\)-plane.

$$ V = \iint_{R} f(x, y) dA = \lim_{n,m \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} f(x_i, y_j) \Delta A $$

Where \(\Delta A = \Delta x \Delta y\).

2. Interpretation

If \(f(x, y) = 1\), the double integral simply calculates the Area of region \(R\). If \(f\) represents density, the integral calculates the total mass. If \(f\) is a probability density, the integral calculates Joint Probability.

3. AI/ML Connection: Joint Probability Distributions

Correlation & Independence

In AI, we often deal with multiple random variables (e.g., \(X\) = weight, \(Y\) = height). The Joint Probability Density Function (PDF), \(p(x, y)\), tells us the likelihood of seeing a specific combination.

To find the probability that a person's weight is between 60-80kg AND height is between 160-180cm, we must perform a Double Integral over that rectangular range. If we cant solve this integral, we cant predict how likely a new data point is, which is the foundation of Anomaly Detection and Naive Bayes classifiers.

4. Python: Numerical Double Integration

from scipy.integrate import dblquad
import numpy as np

# Integrate f(x, y) = x*y over square [0,1]x[0,1]
# Analytic: (1/2)*(1/2) = 0.25

f = lambda y, x: x * y
area, error = dblquad(f, 0, 1, lambda x: 0, lambda x: 1)

print(f"Volume under x*y: {area:.4f}")
