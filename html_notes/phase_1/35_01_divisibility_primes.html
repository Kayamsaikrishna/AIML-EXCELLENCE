Number Theory Integer Properties

Divisibility & Primes

The Atoms of Arithmetic. Exploring the fundamental properties of integers that underpin modern cryptography, hashing, and error-correcting codes.

The Integer Grid

Number theory is the study of discrete properties. Unlike Calculus, where we deal with smooth curves, here we deal with the "cracks" between numbers. In AI, this discrete nature is what allows us to define reliable communication protocols and secure model weights.

1. Formal Divisibility

We say $a$ divides $b$ (written $a|b$) if there exists an integer $k$ such that:

$$ b = a \cdot k $$

If $a$ does not divide $b$, we have the Division Algorithm:

$$ b = a \cdot q + r \quad (0 \leq r < |a|) $$

Where $q$ is the quotient and $r$ is the remainder (the core of modular arithmetic).

2. Prime Numbers: The Building Blocks

A prime number $p > 1$ is an integer that has exactly two divisors: 1 and itself.

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every integer $n > 1$ can be uniquely represented as a product of prime powers: $n = p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} \dots p_k^{e_k}$. This is the "DNA" of a number.

Sieve of Eratosthenes

A simple algorithm to find all primes up to $n$ by iteratively marking the multiples of each prime starting from 2. Complexity: $O(n \log \log n)$.

Hashing Security

Primes in the AI Workflow

  • Hash Functions: Primes (like $2^{31}-1$) are used as table sizes to minimize collisions. Because primes have no common factors with most numbers, they ensure a more uniform distribution of data in memory.
  • Random Number Generation: Linear Congruential Generators (LCGs) often use large primes as the modulus to ensure a long period of pseudo-random values.
  • RSA Encryption: The security of HTTPS (and how you download your models securely) depends on the difficulty of factoring the product of two giant primes.
  • Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH): Choosing prime coefficients for hash functions ensures that the probability of accidental collisions is mathematically bounded.
