Stats 1.4 Inference

Population vs. Sample

Understanding the bridge between what we see (data) and what truly exists (truth).

The Big Picture

In probability, we assume we know the "rules" (the distribution) and predict the outcomes. In Statistics, we see the outcomes and try to guess the rules. This is called Inference.

Population

  • The entire group of interest (e.g., all humans, all possible credit card transactions).
  • Represented by Parameters (\(\mu\), \(\sigma^2\), \(\theta\)).
  • Usually infinite or unobservable.

Sample

  • A subset of the population (our dataset).
  • Represented by Statistics (\(\bar{X}\), \(s^2\), \(\hat{\theta}\)).
  • Observable and finite.

1. The Goal of Inference

graph LR Truth[Population - Parameters] -- Random Sampling --> Data[Sample - Statistics] Data -- Inference/ML --> Truth

The "Statisticians's Dream" is to build a bridge from the right side back to the left side with as little error as possible.

2. ML Connection: Generalization

Foundational Dataset Design

Training Set vs. The Real World

In ML, your Training Set is the sample. The "Real World" is the population.

If your training set is not representative of the population (e.g., you train a face detector only on one ethnicity), you have Sampling Bias. No amount of training will make the model generalize well because the sample doesn't contain the truth of the population.
