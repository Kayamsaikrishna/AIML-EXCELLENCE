04. Comparison Tests

Finding a simpler "Big Brother" to prove convergence.

1. Direct Comparison Test (DCT)

If you have a series \(\sum a_n\) and a known convergent series \(\sum b_n\):

  • If \(0 \leq a_n \leq b_n\) and \(\sum b_n\) converges, then \(\sum a_n\) converges.
  • If \(a_n \geq b_n \geq 0\) and \(\sum b_n\) diverges, then \(\sum a_n\) diverges.

2. Limit Comparison Test (LCT)

If looking at the "whole" formula is too hard, look at the limit of the ratio:

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = L $$

If \(L\) is a positive finite number, then \(\sum a_n\) and \(\sum b_n\) share the same fate (both converge or both diverge).

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Benchmarking

Dominating the Error

In Theoretical Machine Learning, we cannot always calculate the exact error of a Deep Neural Network. Instead, we compare it to a simpler "Benchmark" model (like a Linear Regression).

If we can prove that the mistake-rate of the Neural Network is always less than the mistake-rate of the Linear model (which we know converges), then we have mathematically proven that the Neural Network will also converge. This is used in PAC Learning (Probably Approximately Correct) to give safety guarantees to medical and financial AI systems. If a "Big Brother" model is safe, and your model is "Smaller" in error, your model is safe too.

4. Python: Testing the Limit

# Series a_n = (n + 1) / (n^3 - 4)
# Looks like b_n = 1/n^2.
import numpy as np

n = 1000000
an = (n + 1) / (n**3 - 4)
bn = 1 / (n**2)

limit_ratio = an / bn
print(f"Limit of an/bn: {limit_ratio:.6f}")
# Since ratio -> 1, and 1/n^2 converges, our series converges!
