06. Gauss-Markov Theorem

Why OLS is the king of linear models.

1. The BLUE Guarantee

The Gauss-Markov Theorem states that if the error terms have zero mean and are uncorrelated with constant variance (Homoscedastic), then the OLS estimator is the BLUE:

  • Best: It has the minimum variance (lowest uncertainty).
  • Linear: It is a linear function of the data.
  • Unbiased: On average, it hits the true parameter.
  • Estimator: It's a rule for guessing based on data.

2. The Caveat

Gauss-Markov is NOT about accuracy on new data; it is about the efficiency of estimating the *true* underlying model. If your assumptions (like homoscedasticity) fail, OLS is no longer BLUE—you might need **Weighted Least Squares** or **Regularization**.

3. AI/ML Connection: Theoretical Guarantees and Dataset Sanity

Knowing when to Trust the Model

In AI, Gauss-Markov provides the Theoretical Lower Bound for Error**.

When you evaluate an **AutoML system**, it attempts thousands of models for your data. The Gauss-Markov theorem tells us that for a linear problem, you *cannot* beat OLS in terms of estimation efficiency if the noise is Gaussian. In Financial AI**, where we need "Unbiased" returns, this theorem is used to prove that a specific trading algorithm is the most efficient possible use of the signal. If we find a model that has even lower variance than OLS, we have either **Overfitted** or **Violated Linearity**. It serves as a "Reality Check" for complex deep learning architectures: if a 50-layer ResNet can't significantly beat the OLS-BLUE baseline, your data might just be inherently linear and much simpler to solve.

4. Python: Checking Homoscedasticity

# If variance is NOT constant, OLS is NOT BLUE
residuals = y - model.predict(X)
plt.scatter(model.predict(X), residuals)
plt.axhline(0, color='red')
# If the plot looks like a 'Fan' shape, Gauss-Markov is broken!
