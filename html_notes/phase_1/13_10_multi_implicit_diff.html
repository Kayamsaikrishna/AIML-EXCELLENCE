Calculus 1.6 Level Sets

Implicit Differentiation (Multi)

Derivatives without solving for y.

The Ratio of Partials

Sometimes we cannot isolate \( z \) (e.g., \( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1 \)). To find \( \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} \), we use the Implicit Function Theorem formula: \[ \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = - \frac{F_x}{F_z} \] Where \( F(x, y, z) = 0 \) is the implicit form.

This elegant formula tells us that the slope of a surface depends on the ratio of how "steep" the function is in the x-direction vs the z-direction.

2. AI/ML Connection: Implicit Layers

Deep Equilibrium Models

Recent advances in AI (like Deep Equilibrium Models) describe neural networks not as a sequence of layers, but as finding the fixed point \( z^* \) of an implicit equation: \[ z^* = f(x, z^*) \]

To train these networks, we need to backpropagate through this *infinite* depth. We use Implicit Differentiation to compute gradients directly at the fixed point without unrolling the loop forever. This allows for constant-memory training of infinitely deep networks.

3. Python: Implicit Derivatives

implicit_slope.py
import sympy as sp

x, y, z = sp.symbols('x y z')
# Sphere equation: F = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 25 = 0
F = x**2 + y**2 + z**2 - 25

# Find dz/dx at point (3, 4, 0) -> Wait, z=0 is vertical tangent!
# Let's try point (3, 0, 4)

Fx = sp.diff(F, x) # 2x
Fz = sp.diff(F, z) # 2z

dz_dx = -Fx / Fz # -x/z

print(f"Implicit Derivative dz/dx: {dz_dx}")
print(f"Value at (3, 0, 4): {dz_dx.subs({x:3, z:4})}") # -3/4 = -0.75
