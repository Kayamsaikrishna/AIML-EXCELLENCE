Calculus 1.3 Linearity

Sum & Difference Rules

Differentiation is a linear operator. This means you can break a big problem into small pieces, solve them, and add the answers.

The Principle of Superposition

In Physics and Linear Systems, "Superposition" means the effect of A+B is the effect of A plus the effect of B. Calculus respects this principle.

1. The Linearity Property

The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x) \pm g(x)] = f'(x) \pm g'(x) \]

This allows us to differentiate complex polynomials term-by-term.

2. AI/ML Connection: Regularized Loss

Why we can "Add" Penalties

In training models, we often want to minimize error AND keep weights small (Regularization). We define our Total Loss as:

\[ L_{total} = L_{data} + \lambda L_{reg} \]

Thanks to the Sum Rule, we don't need a complex new formula for the gradient. We just calculate the gradient of the error, calculate the gradient of the regularization term, and add them up.

\[ \nabla L_{total} = \nabla L_{data} + \lambda \nabla L_{reg} \]

This modularity allows libraries like PyTorch to let you stick arbitrary loss compoments together Lego-style.

3. Python: Modular Gradients

sum_rule_demo.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')

# Component 1: Data Fitting (Polynomial)
loss_data = x**4 - 3*x**2

# Component 2: Regularization (Keep x small)
loss_reg = 0.5 * x**2

# Total Loss
loss_total = loss_data + loss_reg

# Differentiate separately
grad_data = sp.diff(loss_data, x)
grad_reg = sp.diff(loss_reg, x)

# Differentiate combined
grad_total = sp.diff(loss_total, x)

print(f"Gradient of Data Term: {grad_data}")
print(f"Gradient of Reg Term:  {grad_reg}")
print(f"Sum of Gradients:      {grad_data + grad_reg}")
print(f"Gradient of Total:     {grad_total}")

if sp.simplify((grad_data + grad_reg) - grad_total) == 0:
    print("\n✅ The Sum Rule holds: The derivative of the sum is the sum of the derivatives.")
