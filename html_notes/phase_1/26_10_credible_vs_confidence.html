Stats 1.6 Intuition Check

Credible vs. Confidence Intervals

They look the same on a plot, but they mean completely different things to your brain.

The Interpretation War

Almost everyone misinterprets a Frequentist Confidence Interval. Ironically, they misinterpret it as a Bayesian Credible Interval.

1. The Comparison

Confidence Interval (Frequentist)

Definition: "If I repeat this experiment 100 times, 95 of the resulting intervals will capture the true value."

The Truth: Once the data is collected, the interval is fixed. The probability that the truth is inside that *specific* interval is either 0 or 1 (we just don't know which).

Focus: The Procedure, not the Outcome.

Credible Interval (Bayesian)

Definition: "There is a 95% probability that the true parameter lies within this interval."

The Truth: This is what most people *want* to say. It treats the parameter as a random variable and the interval as the home of its probability mass.

Focus: The Outcome, based on the current data.

2. Two Types of Credible Intervals

Because Bayesian posteriors can be weirdly shaped (not just symmetric bells), we have two ways to define the 95% zone:

Equal-Tailed Interval

Leaves 2.5% in each tail. Simple, but can include low-probability regions if the distribution is skewed.

Highest Density Interval (HDI)

The "narrowest" possible interval that contains 95% of the mass. Every point inside the HDI has a higher probability density than any point outside it.

3. ML Connection: Intuitive Reporting

Product Management Dashboards

Why Stakeholders Love Bayes

If you tell a CEO: "Our model's accuracy is 85%, and the 95% Confidence Interval is [82%, 88%]," they will ask: "So you're saying there's a 95% chance it's between 82 and 88?"

- Frequentist Answer: "Well, technically no. If we ran the entire world 100 times..." (Ceo stops listening). - Bayesian Answer: "Yes, exactly. Based on our evidence, we are 95% certain the real performance is in that range."

Credible Intervals align with how humans actually think and make decisions. In mission-critical ML (finance, medical diagnosis, autonomous safety), being able to give a direct probability of the result is far more valuable than a procedure-based frequency check.
