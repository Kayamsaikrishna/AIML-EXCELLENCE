03. One-Sided Limits

Approaching from the "left" vs. the "right".

1. Left and Right Hand Limits

Sometimes, a function behaves differently depending on which side you approach from. We denote these as:

  • Right-Hand Limit: Approaching from \(x > a\) (values larger than \(a\)). $$\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L$$
  • Left-Hand Limit: Approaching from \(x < a\) (values smaller than \(a\)). $$\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)=M$$

2. Existence of a Limit

For a general limit \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\) to exist, the left and right limits must be equal.

$$ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \iff \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L $$

If the one-sided limits differ, the general limit Does Not Exist (DNE).

3. AI/ML Connection: The ReLU Discontinuity

The Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU)

The most popular activation function in AI is \(ReLU(x) = \max(0, x)\). At \(x=0\), let's look at the limit of its derivative:

  • As we approach from the left (\(0^-\)): Slope is 0.
  • As we approach from the right (\(0^+\)): Slope is 1.

Because the limits of the slope are different, ReLU is technically not differentiable at x=0. In deep learning, we use a "sub-gradient" (picking 0 or 1 arbitrarily at the junction) to bypass this mathematical hurdle.

4. Python: Testing Piecewise Approach

# Piecewise function: f(x) = 1 if x >= 0 else -1
def step_fn(x):
    return 1 if x >= 0 else -1

# Approach 0 from the left
left_limit = step_fn(-0.000001) 
# Approach 0 from the right
right_limit = step_fn(0.000001)

print(f"Left Limit: {left_limit}")
print(f"Right Limit: {right_limit}")

if left_limit != right_limit:
    print("General limit as x -> 0 Does Not Exist (DNE)")
