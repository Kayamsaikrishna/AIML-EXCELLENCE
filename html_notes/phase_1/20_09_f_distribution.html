Prob 1.4 The Ratio

The F-Distribution (Fisher-Snedecor)

Comparing variances: When one spread is bigger than another.

Ratio of Chi-Squares

The F-distribution is what you get when you take the ratio of two independent Chi-Square variables, each divided by its degrees of freedom.

It is named after Sir Ronald Fisher and George Snedecor.

1. Definition

Let \(U \sim \chi^2(d_1)\) and \(V \sim \chi^2(d_2)\) be independent. $$ X = \frac{U/d_1}{V/d_2} \sim F(d_1, d_2) $$
  • \(d_1\): Numerator degrees of freedom.
  • \(d_2\): Denominator degrees of freedom.
  • Values are non-negative (\(x \ge 0\)).

2. ML Connection: Feature Selection & ANOVA

Statistics ANOVA

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

Suppose you are testing 3 different marketing strategies. You want to know if their mean conversion rates are significantly different.

Instead of comparing means directly, ANOVA compares the variance between groups to the variance within groups.

If the "between" variance is much larger than the "within" variance (leading to a high F-statistic), it implies the group means are not all equal.

F-Test in Feature Selection

In Linear Regression, the F-statistic is used to test if the model explained a significant portion of the variance compared to a model with no features. High F-score = Good features!

3. Relation to t-Distribution

A squared t-distribution variable is an F-distribution variable!

$$ (t_{\nu})^2 = F(1, \nu) $$

This shows the deep interconnection between these "sampling distributions". They all come from the same Gaussian family.
