06. Sum and Difference Rules

Differentiation is a linear operator.

1. The Rules

The derivative of a sum of functions is simply the sum of their individual derivatives. This also applies to differences.

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x) \pm g(x)] = f'(x) \pm g'(x) $$

This means we can differentiate long polynomials term-by-term.

2. Worked Example

Find the derivative of \(y = x^4 + 3x^2 - 10\).

  1. Differentiate \(x^4 \to 4x^3\)
  2. Differentiate \(3x^2 \to 6x\)
  3. Differentiate \(-10 \to 0\)

Result: \(y' = 4x^3 + 6x\)

3. AI/ML Connection: Combined Loss Functions

Regularization + Fit

In Machine Learning, we often use a Compounded Loss Function. For example, Elastic Net Regression uses:

$$ \text{Total Loss} = \text{MSE Loss} + \text{L1 Regularization} + \text{L2 Regularization} $$

Because of the Sum Rule, the optimizer can calculate the gradient for the data fit and the gradient for the regularization penalties separately and just add them together to get the total update for the model weights.

4. Python: Differentiating Expressions

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f1 = x**2
f2 = 5*x
total = f1 + f2

# The derivative of the sum is the sum of derivatives
deriv = sp.diff(total, x)

print(f"Derivative of {f1} + {f2} is: {deriv}")
