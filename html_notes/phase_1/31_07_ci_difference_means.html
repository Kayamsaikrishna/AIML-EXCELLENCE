07. CI for Difference of Means

Comparing two groups: Does the difference actually exist?

1. The Formula (Independent Samples)

When comparing the means of two independent groups ($X$ and $Y$), we estimate the difference $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ using the sample difference $\bar{x} - \bar{y}$. The CI is:

$$ (\bar{x} - \bar{y}) \pm t_{\alpha/2, df} \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}} $$

Where $df$ is calculated using the Welch-Satterthwaite equation (for unequal variances).

2. The "Overlap" Myth

It is a common error to think that if two individual CIs overlap, their difference is not significant. This is Wrong. You must calculate the CI of the *Difference* directly. If the CI of the difference contains Zero, we cannot conclude there is a real clinical or statistical difference between the two groups.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Comparison and A/B Testing

Proving Seniority

In AI research, this CI is the ultimate tool for **Algorithm Evaluation**.

When you develop a new Training Algorithm (Algorithm B) and compare it against the Industry Standard (Algorithm A), you run both 10 times with different random seeds. You then calculate the CI for the Difference in Mean Accuracy. If the resulting interval is $[0.02, 0.05]$, you have statistically proven that Algorithm B is at least 2% better than Algorithm A. In **E-commerce AI**, this is used for A/B Testing recommendation engines. If the CI for the difference in "Spend per User" includes zero, the company saves millions by not deploying a complex "New AI" that doesn't actually increase revenue.

4. Python: Comparing Two Models

# Model A Accuracies: [0.91, 0.92, 0.90, 0.91, 0.92]
# Model B Accuracies: [0.93, 0.94, 0.92, 0.93, 0.94]

a_res = [0.91, 0.92, 0.90, 0.91, 0.92]
b_res = [0.93, 0.94, 0.92, 0.93, 0.94]

# Using Scipy for the Difference CI
diff = np.mean(b_res) - np.mean(a_res)
cm = stats.CompareMeans(stats.DescrStatsW(b_res), stats.DescrStatsW(a_res))
low, high = cm.tconfint_diff(alpha=0.05, usevar='unequal')

print(f"Mean Improvement: {diff*100:.1f}%")
print(f"95% CI of Improvement: [{low*100:.2f}%, {high*100:.2f}%]")
# If the interval is entirely above zero, B is better!
