Stats 1.4 Inf. Geometry

Fisher Information

Measuring how much information about an unknown parameter is "carried" by the data.

The Sharpness of the Peak

If the Likelihood function has a very sharp peak at \(\hat{\theta}\), then small changes in the data would move the peak only slightly. We have high confidence.

If the peak is broad and "flat", then many different values of \(\theta\) are nearly as likely. We have low confidence.

Fisher Information measures this "sharpness" (the negative curvature of the log-likelihood).

1. The Formula

$$ I(\theta) = -E\left[ \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2} \ln L(\theta) \right] $$

2. Two Perspectives

  • Variance of the Score: It is the variance of the first derivative (gradient) of the log-likelihood.
  • Negative Curvature: It is the expected "steepness" of the drop-off from the maximum.

3. ML Connection: Natural Gradients

Optimization Information Theory

Natural Gradient Descent (NGD)

Standard Gradient Descent takes steps in Euclidean space. But the parameter space of a probability distribution is "curved".

NGD uses the Fisher Information Matrix as a metric to scale the gradient steps. It makes larger steps where the likelihood is flat and smaller, cautious steps where the likelihood is sharp. This is the foundation of advanced optimizers like K-FAC.

Model Sensitivity

Fisher Information is also used in Active Learning. It identifies which new data points would provide the "most information" (highest increase in Fisher Information) to the current model, allowing it to learn with fewer labeled examples.
