13. Mutually Exclusive vs. Independent Events

Clearing the most common confusion in probability.

1. The Fundamental Difference

Students often mix these up, but they are polar opposites in terms of relationship:

  • Mutually Exclusive (ME): "We cannot happen together." ($P(A \cap B) = 0$).
  • Independent: "I don't care if you happen." ($P(A|B) = P(A)$).

2. The Irony: ME implies Dependency

If two events $A$ and $B$ (with $P > 0$) are Mutually Exclusive, they are NEVER Independent. Why? Because if I tell you that $B$ happened, the probability of $A$ instantly becomes 0. Knowing about $B$ drastically changed your knowledge of $A$. Therefore, they are highly dependent!

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Assumptions

Architecture Design

When designing a Neural Network, you must decide if your output classes are ME or Independent.

If you use a Softmax layer, you are assuming the classes are Mutually Exclusive (a single object is either a dog or a cat). If you use a Sigmoid layer for each class, you are assuming the presence of one tag is Independent of another (an image can have a dog, a porch, and a sunny day simultaneously). Using the wrong assumption leads to a "Confused Model" that fails to produce valid probabilities. Distinguishing between "Competition" (ME) and "Co-occurrence" (Independence) is the first decision in model architecture selection.

4. Python: Logical Comparison

# Let A = Even roll, B = Odd roll (ME)
# Knowing B=True -> A MUST be False. They are DEPENDENT.

# Let A = Even roll, B = Roll > 3 (Indep? No.)
p_a = 0.5
p_b = 0.5
p_a_and_b = 2/6 # {4, 6} = 0.33
# 0.5 * 0.5 = 0.25. Since 0.25 != 0.33, they are Dependent.

print(f"P(A|B): {p_a_and_b / p_b:.2f}") # 0.66
print(f"P(A): {p_a:.2f}")               # 0.50
# Since P(A|B) != P(A), knowing B happened increased the chance of A.
