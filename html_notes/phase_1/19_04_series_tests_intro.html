02. Introduction to Infinite Series

Summing infinitely many things to get a finite result.

1. What is a Series?

An infinite series is the sum of the terms of a sequence:

$$ S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \dots $$

To see if it converges, we look at the limit of the Partial Sums \(S_k = \sum_{n=1}^k a_n\).

2. The Geometric Series

A classic series where each term is a multiple of the previous: \(a_n = ar^{n-1}\). It converges if and only if \(|r| < 1\):

$$ S = \frac{a}{1 - r} $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Discounted Future Rewards

In Reinforcement Learning, an AI agent tries to maximize the sum of rewards it will get in the future. Because rewards in the future are less certain than rewards now, we use a Discount Factor \(\gamma\) (usually \(0.99\)).

$$ G_t = R_{t+1} + \gamma R_{t+2} + \gamma^2 R_{t+3} + \dots = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \gamma^k R_{t+k+1} $$

This is exactly a Geometric Series. The convergence property (\(\gamma < 1\)) ensures that the total total expected reward (Value) is a finite number that the AI can actually calculate and optimize. Without this infinite sum, AI agents would have to be "Short-Sighted" and couldn't plan for the long-term.

4. Python: Summing a Geometric Series

# Sum of 1/2^n: 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8...
# a = 1/2, r = 1/2.
# Expected: (1/2) / (1 - 1/2) = 1.0

total = 0
for n in range(1, 20):
    total += 1 / (2**n)
    print(f"Partial Sum {n}: {total:.6f}")

# Reaches 0.999998 by step 19.
