09. Stationarity & Unit Root Tests

The holy grail of predictable time series.

1. What is Stationarity?

A time series is Stationary if its statistical properties (Mean, Variance, Covariance) do not change over time. It "Stays in its lane." If a series is not stationary, your model might work for January but fail completely by March.

2. Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Test

We use the ADF Test to check for a "Unit Root." If a unit root exists, the series is non-stationary (e.g., a "Random Walk").

  • Null Hypothesis ($H_0$): The series is NOT stationary.
  • P-value < 0.05: Reject the null. The series is stationary.

3. AI/ML Connection: Stability in Sequence Modeling

Avoiding Spurious Patterns

In AI, stationarity is the requirement for Convergent Learning**.

If you feed a non-stationary **Bitcoin price** directly into a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)**, the model will often "Hallucinate" a trend that doesn't exist. This is known as a **Spurious Regression**. Stationarity ensures that the distribution the AI learned during training is the same distribution it sees during inference. By performing ADF tests, AI engineers decide if they need to **Difference** the data (predicting the *change* in price rather than the price itself). This is the foundation of **Financial Engineering AI**: stationarity is why we model 'Log-Returns' instead of 'Raw Stock Prices'. It's about ensuring the AI is learning the "Physics" of the market, not just the "Coincidence" of the last 10 minutes.

4. Python: The ADF Test

from statsmodels.tsa.stattools import adfuller

result = adfuller(data)
print(f'ADF Statistic: {result[0]}')
print(f'p-value: {result[1]}')

if result[1] < 0.05:
    print("Locked and loaded: The series is stationary!")
else:
    print("Warning: We need to difference the data.")
