Stats 1.4 The Physical Limit

Efficiency & Cramer-Rao Bound

Proving that there is a mathematical speed limit on how fast we can learn.

The Minimum Variance

Some estimators are "tighter" than others. Efficiency measures how close an estimator's variance is to the theoretical minimum possible variance.

1. Cramer-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB)

For any unbiased estimator \(\hat{\theta}\): $$ \text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \ge \frac{1}{I(\theta)} $$ Where \(I(\theta)\) is the Fisher Information.

This is like a "Uncertainty Principle" for statistics. It says that no matter how clever your algorithm is, you can't have a variance lower than this bound.

2. MVUE

The Minimum Variance Unbiased Estimator (MVUE) is the estimator that achieves this lower bound exactly. If you find the MVUE, you have found the "perfect" algorithm for that parameter.

3. ML Connection: Learning Efficiency

Foundational Sample Complexity

Sample-Efficient Learning

In deep learning, we often talk about "Sample Efficiency"—how much data a model needs before it performs well. A sample-efficient model is one that extracts the maximum possible information from every training example, nearing the CRLB.

Optimization Speed

Second-order optimization methods (like Natural Gradient Descent) use the Fisher Information directly to adjust the learning rate. By accounting for the geometry of the parameter space, they can approach the limit of efficiency faster than pure SGD.
