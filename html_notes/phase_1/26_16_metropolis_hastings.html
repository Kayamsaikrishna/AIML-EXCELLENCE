Stats 1.6 Generic Sampler

Metropolis-Hastings

The "Swiss Army Knife" of MCMC. If you can define a likelihood, Metropolis-Hastings can sample from it.

The Acceptance Rule

Unlike Gibbs, Metropolis doesn't need to know the conditional math. It just makes a guess and asks: "Is this spot better than where I am now?"

- Better? Move there. - Worse? Maybe move there (to avoid getting stuck in a local spike).

1. The Formula

Acceptance Ratio (\(r\)):
$$ r = \frac{P(\theta_{new} | D)}{P(\theta_{old} | D)} $$

1. Proposed a new state \(\theta_{new}\).
2. If \(r \ge 1\), accept.
3. If \(r < 1\), accept with probability \(r\).

2. The "Proposal Distribution" Problem

Metropolis-Hastings depends heavily on how "big" your jumps are.

  • Jumps too small: You explore the space way too slowly (it takes a million years to see the whole distribution).
  • Jumps too big: You almost always land in a "bad" (low probability) zone and your proposals keep getting rejected. You never move.

3. ML Connection: Sampling Arbitrary Distributions

Generative AI Complexity

Why it's the Gold Standard for non-standard models

In many modern ML areas (like Ising Models or Energy Based Models), we have an "Energy Function" \(E(x)\) but we don't know the normalization constant (\(Z\)).

$$ P(x) = \frac{1}{Z} \exp(-E(x)) $$

Because Metropolis-Hastings only cares about the Ratio of two points (\(P_{new} / P_{old}\)), the constant \(Z\) cancels out and disappears! This allows you to sample from distributions where you can't even compute the actual probability, only "relative" probability. This is the superpower that lets us sample from things as complex as Spin Glasses and Molecular Dynamics simulations.
