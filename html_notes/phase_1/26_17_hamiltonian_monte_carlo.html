Stats 1.6 Physics-Based

Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)

Turning probability distributions into "gravity wells" and using momentum to glide through them efficiently.

The End of the Random Walk

Standard MCMC (Metropolis) is like a drunk person wandering around. It takes forever to explore far-away regions.

HMC is like a frictionless marble rolling through a landscape. It uses the Gradient (slope) of the probability distribution to pick a direction and "glid" long distances in a single step.

1. The Physics Analogy

Potential Energy

The "Negative Log-Posterior." High-probability areas are valleys; low-probability areas are peaks.

Kinetic Energy

We give the "marble" a random kick (Momentum). This allows it to roll out of the valley and up the next hill.

Leapfrog Integrator

A math trick to simulate physics in small steps while preserving total energy (Hamiltonian).

2. Why HMC is better

  • High-Dimensional Scaling: While Metropolis fails as you add more variables, HMC scales beautifully. This makes it the only viable choice for models with thousands of parameters.
  • Lower Correlation: Consecutive samples in HMC are much further apart, meaning you need fewer samples to "cover" the truth.

3. ML Connection: NUTS in PyMC

Deep Learning Hardware

The No-U-Turn Sampler

HMC is great, but it has a problem: How long should you let the marble roll before stopping and taking a sample?

NUTS is an algorithm that automatically figures this out. It lets the marble roll until it starts to turn back around ("No-U-Turn"), then it takes the sample. NUTS is the default engine in Stan and PyMC.

In deep learning, Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) is a cousin of HMC that allows us to find Bayesian distributions of Neural Network weights using the same GPUs we use for standard Backprop. It effectively bridges the gap between "Fast Optimization" and "Deep Bayesian Uncertainty."
