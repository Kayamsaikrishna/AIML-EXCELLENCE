1. Stretching the Arrow

Multiplying a vector \( \mathbf{v} \) by a scalar \( c \) scales its magnitude by \( |c| \) and, if \( c < 0 \), reverses its direction.

$$ 2 \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

This operation is Linear, meaning it preserves the structure of the space (lines remain lines).

2. NumPy Broadcasting

In classical math, you can't add a scalar to a vector \( \mathbf{v} + 5 \). In Python/AI, you CAN.

This is called Broadcasting. The scalar is automatically expanded (copied) to match the shape of the vector.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + 5 \rightarrow \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} $$

This makes code cleaner and faster (no manual loops).

3. Learning Rate Scaling

Updating weights requires scaling the gradient.

import numpy as np

# A batch of gradients (Matrix)
grads = np.array([
    [0.1, -0.2],
    [0.05, 0.3],
    [-0.1, 0.0]
])

print(f"Original Shape: {grads.shape}")

# Scalar Multiplication (Learning Rate)
lr = 0.01
update = lr * grads # Broadcasts scalar to (3, 2) matrix

print("Scaled Updates:")
print(update)

# Reversing Direction (Negative Scalar)
step = -1.0 * update
print("\nDescent Step:")
print(step)
