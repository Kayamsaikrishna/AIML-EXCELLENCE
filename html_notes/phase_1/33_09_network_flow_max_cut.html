Graph Theory Optimization

Network Flow & Max-Flow Min-Cut

Pushing the Limits. Analyzing how much 'stuff' can move through a network of pipes with varying capacities, and finding the bottlenecks that restrict it.

The Pipe Analogy

In a Flow Network, edges are "pipes" with a maximum capacity. We have a Source (where flow starts) and a Sink (where flow ends). The goal is to maximize the amount of flow from Source to Sink without exceeding the capacity of any pipe.

1. The Max-Flow Min-Cut Theorem

The maximum amount of flow in a network is exactly equal to the capacity of the Minimum Cut.

A Cut is a partition of vertices that separates the Source from the Sink. The capacity of a cut is the sum of weights of edges crossing the partition. The "tightest" cut is the bottleneck of the entire system.

2. Standard Algorithms

Ford-Fulkerson

Uses "Augmenting Paths" in a Residual Graph. It keeps adding flow as long as a path exists that isn't full.

Edmonds-Karp

An implementation of Ford-Fulkerson using BFS to find the shortest augmenting paths. Complexity: \(O(V E^2)\).

Deep Learning Ops

The Flow Logic in AI

  • Image Segmentation (GrabCut): Treating an image as a flow network where "similarity" is capacity. The Min-Cut partitions the image into "Foreground" and "Background" optimally.
  • Pipeline Optimization: In MLOps, if you have multiple data processing stages with different throughputs (capacities), Max-Flow determines the maximum data your entire pipeline can handle per second.
  • Information Bottleneck Theory: A deep learning concept suggesting that a layer's goal is to maximize information about the target while "cutting" irrelevant information from the input — analogous to finding the min-cut of information flow.
  • Resource Scheduling: Max-Flow is used to assign GPU clusters to training jobs when there are complex constraints on connectivity and memory.
