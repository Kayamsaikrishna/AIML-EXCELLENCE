Stats 1.4 Model-Based

Parametric Bootstrap

Generating "alternate realities" based on a model you've already found.

The Middle Ground

In the Non-parametric Bootstrap, we sample from the Data.
In the Parametric Bootstrap, we first fit a Model (e.g., a Normal distribution) to the data, and then we sample new synthetic data points from that model.

1. The Process

2. Why use the Parametric version?

  • Efficiency: If the model's assumptions (like normality) are actually true, the parametric bootstrap is more precise than the non-parametric one.
  • Small Samples: It can perform better when you have very little data (where resampling the data itself doesn't provide enough variation).
  • Extrapolation: It allows you to generate values that weren't in your original dataset, but are possible according to the model.

3. ML Connection: Generative AI and Simulation

Deep Learning Generative AI

Synthetic Data for Uncertainty

In high-stakes ML (like autonomous driving), we often use Simulators. A simulator is effectively a massive "Parametric Model" of the real world.

Engineers use the simulator to generate thousands of "Bootstrap" scenarios (rainy night, busy intersection, etc.). By seeing how the model behaves in these synthetic environments, they can estimate the confidence and safety of the system in the real world. This is the **Parametric Bootstrap** at an industrial scale!

Model Sensitivity

You can also use this to test how sensitive a model is to noise. Add Gaussian noise to your dataset (\(\epsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2)\)), train the model 100 times, and look at the distribution of the weights. This is a parametric bootstrap of the learning process itself.
