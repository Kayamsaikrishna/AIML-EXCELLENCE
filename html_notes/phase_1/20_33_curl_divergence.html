09. Divergence (\(\text{div } \vec{F}\))

Measuring the expansion and contraction of fields.

1. Definition

Divergence deals with Vector Fields \(\vec{F} = \langle P, Q, R \rangle\). It measures whether points act as "sources" (field spreading out) or "sinks" (field converging in).

$$ \text{div } \vec{F} = \nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial R}{\partial z} $$

Divergence is a scalar value at each point.

2. Interpretation

  • Positive Divergence: A source. Something is being added to the field at that point.
  • Negative Divergence: A sink. Something is being removed.
  • Zero Divergence: The field is incompressible. What flows in must flow out.

3. AI/ML Connection: Score-Based Generative Models

Probability Flow

Generating images with AI (like DALL·E or Stable Diffusion) works by starting with random noise and gradually "denoising" it. This process can be viewed as a vector field where every pixel is "flowing" toward the nearest realistic image.

The Divergence of this field tells us how the probability density changes over time. Researchers use the Divergence Theorem to ensure that the AI model doesn't "leak" probability into impossible states. High-quality diffusion models are built using Divergence-Free priors or strictly controlled divergence to maintain the sharpest details during generation.

4. Python: Divergence Calculation

# Field F = 
# div F = 2x + 2y + 2z

import sympy as sp

x, y, z = sp.symbols('x y z')
P = x**2
Q = y**2
R = z**2

div_F = sp.diff(P, x) + sp.diff(Q, y) + sp.diff(R, z)

print(f"Divergence of F: {div_F}")
# At point (1, 1, 1), divergence is 6.
