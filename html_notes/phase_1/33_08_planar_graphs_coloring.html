Planar Graphs & Graph Coloring

Drawing without Crossings. Exploring the elegant constraints of 2D embeddings and the power of vertex coloring to solve scheduling and allocation puzzles.

The Flattening Challenge

A graph is Planar if it can be drawn in a plane without any edges crossing. While most real-world social networks are dense and non-planar, planar graphs are the foundation of physical hardware (PCB design) and geography.

1. Euler's Formula

For any connected planar graph with \(V\) vertices, \(E\) edges, and \(F\) faces (regions):

$$ V - E + F = 2 $$

Kuratowski's Theorem: A graph is non-planar if and only if it contains a subgraph that is an expansion of \(K_5\) (complete 5-node graph) or \(K_{3,3}\) (the 3-utility house puzzle).

2. Graph Coloring

Vertex Coloring is an assignment of labels (colors) to vertices such that no two adjacent vertices share the same color. The minimum number of colors needed is the Chromatic Number \(\chi(G)\).

Four Color Theorem

Any planar graph can be colored with at most 4 colors. This was the first major theorem to be proved by a computer.

Welsh-Powell Algorithm

A greedy approach: Sort nodes by degree and color them in order to find an upper bound for the chromatic number.

Hardware Operations Research

Spatial Logic in AI

  • Register Allocation: In a compiler (like XLA for Tensor Processing Units), variables are nodes, and if two variables are "active" at the same time, they share an edge. Finding the minimum registers needed is a Graph Coloring problem.
  • Exam/Resource Scheduling: If two exams share a student, they cannot happen at the same time (edge). Finding the minimum time slots (colors) is a classic coloring application.
  • Convolutional Maps: In Computer Vision, the grid-like neighborhood of pixels forms a planar graph. CNNs leverage this planarity to perform localized operations efficiently.
  • Constraint Satisfaction (CSPs): Many AI puzzles (Sudoku, Map Coloring) are fundamentally graph coloring problems where the goal is to find a valid assignment of values under adjacency constraints.
