30. Surface Integrals

Measuring data on a curved shell.

1. What is a Surface Integral?

A Surface Integral generalizes the idea of a double integral to curved surfaces in 3D space. It calculates the net value of a scalar or vector field over a surface \(S\).

$$ \iint_S f(x, y, z) dS = \iint_D f(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| dA $$

If the field is a vector field \(\mathbf{F}\), the surface integral calculates the Flux—the net flow through the surface:

$$ \Phi = \iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} dS $$

2. Orientation

A surface must be two-sided (orientable) for the flux to be well-defined. We pick a unit normal vector \(\mathbf{n}\) to define "up" vs "down".

3. AI/ML Connection: Manifold Regularization

Integral on Feature Shells

In Unsupervised Learning, we often assume that high-dimensional data points lie on a Low-Dimensional Surface (manifold). If we want to move from one cluster to another, we are moving along that surface.

Surface Integrals are used in Geometric Deep Learning to calculate how "Smoothly" a model transforms the data. By penalizing the integral of the gradient over the Diffeomorphic Surface of the feature space, we can force the AI to learn simple, interpretable representations. It ensures that the model's "Knowledge" is evenly distributed over the curved shell of valid data, preventing the AI from "overfitting" to tiny, sharp regions of the manifold.

4. Python: Symbolic Surface Setup

import sympy as sp

u, v = sp.symbols('u v')
# Parametric Sphere: r(u, v) = [sin(u)cos(v), sin(u)sin(v), cos(u)]
r = sp.Matrix([sp.sin(u)*sp.cos(v), sp.sin(u)*sp.sin(v), sp.cos(u)])

# Find Cross Product of partials to get normal vector
ru = sp.diff(r, u)
rv = sp.diff(r, v)
n_vec = ru.cross(rv)

print(f"Normal Vector (dS) magnitude: {sp.simplify(n_vec.norm())}")
# Result for sphere is sin(u), which is the standard Jacobian factor.
