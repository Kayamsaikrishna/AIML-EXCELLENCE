12. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC)

The bridge between slopes and areas.

1. FTC Part 1 (The Accumulation Function)

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a, b]\), then the function defined by \(g(x) = \int_{a}^{x} f(t) dt\) is continuous and differentiable, and:

$$ g'(x) = f(x) $$

This means that differentiation undoes integration.

2. FTC Part 2 (The Evaluation Theorem)

If \(F\) is any antiderivative of \(f\), then:

$$ \int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a) $$

This is the most powerful tool in calculus. It allows us to calculate areas without needing to sum up millions of rectangles—we just need to find the antiderivative.

3. AI/ML Connection: Loss and Gradients

Path Independence in Optimization

The FTC is why we can calculate the Total Loss of a model by integrating its Gradient over the path of training.

In Reinforcement Learning, we often accumulate "rewards" over time. The FTC allows us to derive the relationship between the "Value Function" (total reward) and the "Policy Gradient" (the rate of change of reward). Without the FTC, we wouldn't be able to analytically optimize long-term behavior in agents like AlphaGo or ChatGPT.

4. Python: Evaluating Definite Integrals

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = sp.sin(x)

# Definite integral from 0 to pi
# F(x) = -cos(x). F(pi) - F(0) = -(-1) - (-1) = 2
result = sp.integrate(f, (x, 0, sp.pi))

print(f"Area under sin(x) from 0 to pi: {result}")
