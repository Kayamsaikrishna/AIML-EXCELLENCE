1. The Decay Formula

$$ N(t) = N_0 e^{-kt} \quad \text{or} \quad N_t = N_0 (1 - r)^t $$

AI Application: Epsilon-Greedy (RL)

In Reinforcement Learning, we want the agent to Explore a lot initially (Random actions) and then Exploit later.

$$ \epsilon_{new} = \epsilon_{old} \cdot \text{decay\_rate} $$

2. Exponential Moving Average (Momentum)

How do we smooth out jagged loss curves? We use an EMA.

$$ v_t = \beta v_{t-1} + (1-\beta) \theta_t $$

Here, \( \beta \) (often 0.9) is the "Decay Factor". It determines the "Half-Life" of the memory. A higher \( \beta \) means the model "remembers" older gradients clearly for longer.

3. Learning Rate Scheduler

Simulating Step Decay vs Exponential Decay.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Params
lr_initial = 0.1
decay_rate = 0.95
epochs = 20

lrs = []
current_lr = lr_initial

for e in range(epochs):
    lrs.append(current_lr)
    # Update Rule: Exponential Decay
    current_lr = current_lr * decay_rate

print(f"Start LR: {lrs[0]}")
print(f"Final LR: {lrs[-1]:.5f}")
# Output decreases smoothly without hitting 0 instantly 
# (Zeno's Paradox nature of decay)
