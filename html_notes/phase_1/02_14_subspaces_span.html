14. Subspaces and Span

Finding the limits of what vectors can reach.

1. What is a Subspace?

A subset \( S \) of a vector space \( V \) is a Subspace if it is itself a vector space. Crucially, every subspace must contain the Zero Vector \(\mathbf{0}\).

$$ \text{span}(\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \dots, \mathbf{v}_k) = \{ c_1 \mathbf{v}_1 + c_2 \mathbf{v}_2 + \dots + c_k \mathbf{v}_k \} $$

The Span of a set of vectors is the collection of all possible linear combinations of those vectors.

2. Low-Rank Approximations in AI

The Manifold Hypothesis

AI researchers believe that real-world data (like images of faces) occupies a small Subspace within the massive space of all possible pixels. This is the Manifold Hypothesis.

  • Column Space: The span of the columns of a matrix \( X \). If your target \( y \) lies outside this span, your model will always have error.
  • LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation): A modern technique to fine-tune massive LLMs by only training a small "Subspace" of weights.

3. Python: Rank & Span Detection

import numpy as np

# Two vectors that are parallel (Linear Dependent)
v1 = np.array([1, 2])
v2 = np.array([2, 4])

# The span is just a line, not a 2D plane
matrix = np.array([v1, v2])
rank = np.linalg.matrix_rank(matrix)

print(f"Dimension of Span (Rank): {rank}") # Output: 1
