02. Marginal Probability Distribution

Slicing high-dimensional data into individual variables.

1. Definition

The Marginal Distribution of a subset of random variables is the probability distribution of the variables in the subset, without reference to the values of the other variables. To find it, we "average out" the unwanted variables.

  • **Discrete:** $P(X=x) = \sum_{y} P(X=x, Y=y)$
  • **Continuous:** $f_X(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x, y) dy$

2. Intuition: The Shadow

Imagine the Joint Distribution as a 3D hill. The Marginal Distribution of $X$ is the "shadow" cast by that hill onto the $X$-axis. It tells you everything about $X$ while ignoring the existence of $Y$.

3. AI/ML Connection: Global Stats and Attention Mechanisms

Ignoring the Noise

Marginalization is how AI handles Missing or Irrelevant Data.

In **Data Analysis**, when you calculate the `mean()` or `std()` of a single column in a massive dataset, you are essentially calculating a statistic of the Marginal Distribution. In **Generative Modeling**, like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), we often care about the "Marginal Likelihood" of our data—the probability of an image existing regardless of the hidden "latent" factors the AI used to create it. In **Transformer models** (like ChatGPT), the **Attention Mechanism** can be seen as a way of dynamically marginalizing over tokens—the model decides which tokens are relevant to the current word and "averages out" the influence of the rest. Understanding marginals allows you to reason about how AI models summarize complex datasets into individual feature insights.

4. Python: Marginalizing with Numpy

# Example: Marginalizing a Joint PMF
# [X=0, X=1] x [Y=0, Y=1]
joint_pmf = np.array([[0.1, 0.2],
                      [0.3, 0.4]])

# Marginal of X (summing across axis 1 / Y)
marginal_x = np.sum(joint_pmf, axis=1)

# Marginal of Y (summing across axis 0 / X)
marginal_y = np.sum(joint_pmf, axis=0)

print(f"P(X): {marginal_x}") # [0.3, 0.7]
print(f"P(Y): {marginal_y}") # [0.4, 0.6]
