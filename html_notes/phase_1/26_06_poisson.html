06. Poisson Distribution

Modeling rare events in fixed intervals.

1. Definition

The Poisson Distribution models the probability of a given number of events occurring in a fixed interval of time or space, provided these events occur with a known constant mean rate ($\lambda$) and independently of the time since the last event.

$$ P(X=k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!} \quad \text{for } k=0, 1, 2, \dots $$

2. Unique Property

The Poisson distribution is unique because its Mean equals its Variance:

$$ E[X] = Var(X) = \lambda $$

This is often used as a diagnostic check: if your data's variance is much higher than its mean, the Poisson model might be too simple, and you should switch to Negative Binomial.

3. AI/ML Connection: Demand Forecasting and Anomaly Detection

The Math of Arrival Rates

Poisson is the foundation of **Poisson Regression**, used when the AI needs to predict "event counts."

In **Predictive Maintenance**, the number of machine failures in a month acts as a Poisson random variable. In **Network Security AI**, the number of packets arriving at a server is modeled as a Poisson process; if the actual count $k$ is significantly higher than $\lambda$, the AI flags an Anomaly (DDoS Attack). It is also used in **Log-Linear Models** for Natural Language Processing (NLP) to model the frequency of rare words. Understanding Poisson allows you to design AI systems that reason about "When the next event will happen."

4. Python: Poisson PMF

from scipy.stats import poisson

# Average rate of website visits: λ=10 per minute
lam = 10

# Probability of exactly 15 visits
print(f"P(X=15): {poisson.pmf(15, lam):.4f}")

# Simulate 5 minutes of data
print(f"Arrivals: {poisson.rvs(lam, size=5)}")
