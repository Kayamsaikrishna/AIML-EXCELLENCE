Stats 1.8 Pre-processing

Differencing & Transformations

Taming the Signal. The surgical techniques to remove trends and stabilize the chaos in your data.

The Correction Kit

Most time series data in the wild is Non-Stationary. Before we can model it, we have to "squeeze" it until the mean and variance become constant.

- Differencing fixes the Mean (removes Trend). - Transformations fix the Variance (stabilizes Volatility).

1. Differencing (The "Delta" Method)

First Differencing (\(d=1\))

$$ Y'_t = Y_t - Y_{t-1} $$

Removes a Linear Trend. Instead of predicting the absolute price, you are predicting the *change* in price.

Seasonal Differencing (\(D=1\))

$$ Y'_t = Y_t - Y_{t-s} $$

Removes Seasonality. If \(s=120\) (monthly), you compare this March to last March. This isolates the true underlying growth.

2. Mathematical Transformations

1. Log Transformation

\( \log(Y_t) \). Converts exponential growth into linear growth. Essential for "Fan-shaped" data where variance grows over time.

2. Box-Cox Transformation

A generalized transformation that uses a parameter \(\lambda\) to find the best way to make the data look "Normal" and stationary.

3. Variance Stabilizing (VST)

Often used in genomics and sensor data to ensure that higher values don't have higher proportionally noise.

3. ML Connection: Feature Pipelines

Deep Learning Data Ops

The Pre-processing Paradox

In many ML models, differencing is the difference between a "Random Guess" and a "Winning Trade."

  • Information Loss: When you difference data, you lose the "Level" (the actual price). If your goal is to know the total revenue, you must remember to Integrate (Inverse-Difference) the predictions back to the original scale!
  • Fractional Differencing: A modern technique used in Quantitative Finance that preserves some "Memory" of the original series while still achieving stationarity. Standard differencing (\(d=10\)) often throws away too much useful information.
  • Deep Learning Inputs: Transformers and LSTMs perform significantly better when the inputs are normalized. Feeding a raw stock price (\(2000\)) is much harder for a network than feeding a returns percentage (\(+0.5\%\)).
Engineer's Secret: Never difference more than twice (\(d > 2\)). If the data isn't stationary after two rounds of differencing, you probably have a structural break in the dataset that needs a more complex model (like Regime Switching).
