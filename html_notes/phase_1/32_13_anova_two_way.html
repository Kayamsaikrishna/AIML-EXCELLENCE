13. Two-way ANOVA

Analyzing multiple factors and their hidden interactions.

1. Beyond Single Factors

While One-way ANOVA compares several groups of one factor, **Two-way ANOVA** looks at two factors simultaneously. For example, if you are testing model performance, Factor 1 could be **Architecture** (ResNet vs. ViT) and Factor 2 could be **Dataset Resolution** (224x224 vs. 512x512).

2. Interaction Effects: The Critical Insight

The most important part of a Two-way ANOVA is the Interaction Effect. This asks: "Does the effect of one factor depend on the other?". For example, maybe ResNet works better at low resolution, but ViT only works when resolution is high. If an interaction is present, you cannot talk about one factor without mentioning the other.

3. AI/ML Connection: Hyperparameter Synergy and Feature Crosses

When A + B != C

In AI, Two-way ANOVA is the core of **Advanced Ablation Studies**.

When you are tuning a **Neural Network**, you might tune the **Learning Rate** and the **Batch Size**. If you tune them one-by-one, you might miss the "Sweet Spot" because these factors have a massive **Interaction**. A high learning rate might be disastrous with a small batch, but perfect with a large one. In **Feature Engineering**, this concept drives **Feature Crosses**: when you combine "Gender" and "Income" into a single feature because the AI's behavior for income differs wildly depending on gender. Use Two-way ANOVA to prove that these combinations are statistically meaningful and not just adding unnecessary complexity to your model.

4. Python: statsmodels.formula.api

import statsmodels.api as sm
from statsmodels.formula.api import ols

# DataFrame with results from Architecture and Resolution
# 'score ~ C(Arch) + C(Res) + C(Arch):C(Res)'
model = ols('score ~ C(Arch) * C(Res)', data=df).fit()
anova_table = sm.stats.anova_lm(model, typ=2)

print(anova_table)
# Look for the 'PR(>F)' of the interaction term 'Arch:Res'. 
# If < 0.05, you have a breakthrough insight into your model's synergy!
