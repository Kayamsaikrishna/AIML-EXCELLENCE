1. The Determinant Formula

Unlike the Dot Product (Scalar), the Cross Product returns a Vector.

$$ \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} $$

Features:

  • Magnitude: \( ||\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}|| = ||\mathbf{a}|| ||\mathbf{b}|| \sin \theta \). (Area of Parallelogram).
  • Direction: Determined by the Right-Hand Rule.
  • Orthogonality: The result is always perpendicular to both inputs.

2. Defining Planes

In 3D Graphics and Robotic Vision, we define a "surface" by its Normal Vector. To find the normal of a triangle mesh (e.g. in a game engine or simulator), we take the cross product of two edges.

In High-Dim AI, we generalize this to finding the "Null Space" (Kernel) of a matrix, which gives us vectors perpendicular to every row.

3. Calculating Normals

Computing the vector perpendicular to a surface.

import numpy as np

# Two Vectors on a plane (e.g. x-axis and y-axis)
a = np.array([1, 0, 0])
b = np.array([0, 1, 0])

# Cross Product
normal = np.cross(a, b)

print(f"Vector A: {a}")
print(f"Vector B: {b}")
print(f"Normal (A x B): {normal}") 
# Result: [0, 0, 1] (The z-axis)

# Check Orthogonality (Dot product should be 0)
print(f"Dot(A, Normal): {np.dot(a, normal)}")
print(f"Dot(B, Normal): {np.dot(b, normal)}")
