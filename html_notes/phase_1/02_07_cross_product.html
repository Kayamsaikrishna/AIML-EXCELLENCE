07. Cross Product (Vector Product)

Generating orthogonality in 3D space.

1. Definition & Determinant Form

Unlike the dot product, the cross product only exists in 3D Space (or 7D). It returns a vector that is perpendicular to both inputs.

$$ \mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ u_1 & u_2 & u_3 \\ v_1 & v_2 & v_3 \end{vmatrix} $$

Properties

  • Anti-Commutative: \( \mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v} = -(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{u}) \).
  • Magnitude: \( \|\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{v}\| = \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \sin \theta \).
  • Self-Parallel: \( \mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0} \).

2. Applications in Robotics & Graphics AI

Surface Normals & Torque

In 3D Computer Vision (like NeRFs or LiDAR processing), we need to find the orientation of a surface. The cross product of two vectors on a plane gives the Surface Normal.

  • Robotics: Calculating the torque \( \mathbf{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F} \) for arm movements.
  • Ray Casting: Determining if a point is "inside" or "outside" a mesh during AI-driven 3D reconstruction.

3. Python: Computing Cross Product

import numpy as np

u = np.array([1, 0, 0]) # i vector
v = np.array([0, 1, 0]) # j vector

# The cross product should be k (0, 0, 1)
cp = np.cross(u, v)

print(f"Cross Product (u x v): {cp}") # Output: [0 0 1]
