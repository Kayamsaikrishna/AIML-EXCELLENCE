Z-Test for Proportions

Testing if the "Success Rate" of a process matches a claimed value.

The Requirements

We use the Normal approximation for Binomial proportions if:
- \(np_0 \ge 10\) and \(n(1-p_0) \ge 10\)
(Basically, you need enough successes and failures in your sample).

1. The Test Statistic

$$ Z = \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n}}} $$
  • \(\hat{p}\): Sample Proportion (successes / total).
  • \(p_0\): Hypothesized Population Proportion.

2. ML Connection: Accuracy Testing

Classification QA

Is the Model accurate enough?

A marketing model claims to have 90% accuracy (\(p_0 = 0.90\)). You evaluate it on a fresh batch of 400 images and it correctly identifies 348 of them (\(\hat{p} = 348/400 = 0.87\)).

The Calculation: $$ Z = \frac{0.87 - 0.90}{\sqrt{\frac{0.9 \cdot 0.1}{400}}} = \frac{-0.03}{0.015} = \mathbf{-2.0} $$

Since \(|-2.0| > 1.96\), you Reject the Null at the 95% level. The model's real-world accuracy is significantly lower than 90%.

Conversion Rates

This is the standard test for Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO). If you change a button's color and 5% of people click instead of 4.5%, you use this Z-test to see if that extra 0.5% is a real shift or just daily fluctuation.
