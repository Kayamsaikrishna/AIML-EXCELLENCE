Prob 1.4 New Universe

Conditional Probability

Updating beliefs based on evidence.

Shrinking the Sample Space

When we say \(P(A|B)\), we mean "Assuming \(B\) has happened, what is the probability of \(A\)?". Effectively, the sample space \(\Omega\) shrinks to just \(B\). \[ P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \]

The denominator \(P(B)\) acts as a normalization constant to make sure the probabilities within the new universe sum to 1.

2. ML Connection: Precision recall

Confusion Matrix

Most classification metrics are conditional probabilities:

  • Precision: \(P(\text{True Positive} | \text{Predicted Positive})\). Given the model said "Yes", how likely is it right?
  • Recall: \(P(\text{Predicted Positive} | \text{True Positive})\). Given it actually is "Yes", how likely did we find it?

3. Python: Conditional Logic

cond_prob.py
# Experiment: Roll two dice
# B: Sum is 6 {(1,5), (2,4), (3,3), (4,2), (5,1)} -> Size 5
# A: First die is 2 {(2,4)}
# P(A | B)?

# Total space size = 36
# P(B) = 5/36
# P(A and B) = 1/36 (Only outcome (2,4))

p_A_given_B = (1/36) / (5/36)
print(f"P(Die1=2 | Sum=6) = {p_A_given_B:.2f}") # 0.20
