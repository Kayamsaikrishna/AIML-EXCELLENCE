11. The Delta Method

Approximating the distribution of transformed variables.

1. Definition

The Delta Method is a result concerning the asymptotic distribution of a function of a random variable. It states that if $X_n$ is asymptotically normal around $\theta$, and $g$ is a differentiable function, then $g(X_n)$ is also asymptotically normal.

$$ \sqrt{n}(g(X_n) - g(\theta)) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, \sigma^2 [g'(\theta)]^2) $$

2. Intuition: Linear Approximation

The Delta method uses a First-Order Taylor Expansion. Near the mean, any smooth function $g(X)$ looks like a straight line. The slope of that line ($g'(\theta)$) determines how much the original variance ($\sigma^2$) is "stretched" or "compressed" in the transformed space.

3. AI/ML Connection: Log-Odds and Uncertainty Propagation

Transforming Confidence

The Delta Method is essential for **Interpreting AI Predictions**.

In **Logistic Regression**, the model predicts "Log-Odds" (logits), but humans care about "Probabilities." Since probability is a non-linear transform ($sigmoid$) of the logit, we use the Delta Method to calculate the **Confidence Interval of the probability**. Without this method, we would know our "Logit" is accurate but wouldn't be able to say for sure if our 95% probability prediction is actually 95% or potentially 80-99%. It is also used in **Financial AI** to calculate the "Value at Risk" (VaR) of a portfolio—propagating the uncertainty of individual stock prices through complex non-linear trading functions.

4. Python: Transforming Variance

# X is N(10, 1). Let g(x) = sqrt(x)
theta, sigma = 10, 1
g_prime = 1 / (2 * np.sqrt(theta)) # Derivative of sqrt(x)

# Theoretical Variance of sqrt(X) using Delta Method
transformed_var = (sigma**2) * (g_prime**2)

# Simulation
x_samples = np.random.normal(theta, sigma, 100000)
g_samples = np.sqrt(x_samples)

print(f"Empirical Var(sqrt(X)): {np.var(g_samples):.6f}")
print(f"Delta Method Var: {transformed_var:.6f}") # Matches!
