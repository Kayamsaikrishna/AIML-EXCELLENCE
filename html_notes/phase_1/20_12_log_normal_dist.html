The Log-Normal Distribution

Modeling products of small independent factors.

Sums vs. Products

The Normal distribution is for things that add up (Central Limit Theorem).
The Log-Normal distribution is for things that multiply together.

"If you take a sample and its logarithm follows a Normal distribution, the original sample is Log-Normal."

1. Definition and PDF

\(X \sim \text{Log-Normal}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) if \(\ln(X) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\). $$ f(x) = \frac{1}{x \sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left( -\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right) $$ for \(x > 0\).
  • The distribution is positively skewed (long tail to the right).
  • Always positive (useful for prices, weights, volumes).

2. When does it appear?

Any process where growth is proportional to the current size leads to a Log-Normal distribution.

  • Biology: Height of microorganisms, weight of body organs.
  • Finance: Stock prices (Black-Scholes model assumes returns are log-normal).
  • Linguistics: Length of sentences in a book.

3. ML Connection: Log-Transforms

Preprocessing Feature Engineering

Correcting Skewness

Linear models (OLS) and many Neural Networks perform best when features and targets are roughly Gaussian.

If your target variable (like House Prices) is highly skewed (Log-Normal), taking the log of the variable (\(y' = \log(y)\)) transforms it into a Normal distribution.

Training Loop: 1. Log-transform data. 2. Train model. 3. Predict. 4. Inverse-transform prediction (\(\exp(y')\)) to get final answer.

4. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \exp(\mu + \sigma^2/2)\).
    (Note: The mean is higher than \(\exp(\mu)\) because of the long tail).
  • Median: \(\exp(\mu)\).
  • Mode: \(\exp(\mu - \sigma^2)\).
