Calculus 1.5 Approximation

Maclaurin Series

Approximating from the origin.

Center at Zero

A Maclaurin Series is simply a Taylor Series centered at \(a = 0\). \[ f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(0)}{n!} x^n \]

Most standard approximations in physics and engineering (like \(\sin x \approx x\)) are actually Maclaurin series truncated after the first term.

2. AI/ML Connection: Weight Initialization

Starting at the Origin

When initializing a Neural Network (e.g., Xavier Initialization), we often set weights close to zero. Why? Because activation functions like Tanh or Sigmoid are most "linear" near zero.

Looking at the Maclaurin Series for \(\tanh(x)\): \[ \tanh(x) \approx x - \frac{x^3}{3} \dots \]

For small \(x\), \(\tanh(x) \approx x\). This means the gradient flows perfectly (slope = 1) at the start of training. If we initialized weights far from zero, the higher-order terms (\(x^3, x^5\)) would dominate, causing signal distortion or vanishing gradients.

3. Python: Visualizing Linearity

tanh_linear.py
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

x = np.linspace(-3, 3, 100)
y_tanh = np.tanh(x)
y_linear = x  # First term of Maclaurin Series

# Compare Tanh vs Linear Approximation
print("At x=0.1:")
print(f"Tanh: {np.tanh(0.1):.5f}")
print(f"Linear: {0.1:.5f}")
# Almost identical!

print("\nAt x=2.0:")
print(f"Tanh: {np.tanh(2.0):.5f}")
print(f"Linear: {2.0:.5f}")
# Diverges significantly. Maclaurin approx only good near 0.
