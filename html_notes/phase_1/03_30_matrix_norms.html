30. Matrix Norms

Measuring the magnitude of a linear transformation.

1. Common Norms

Frobenius Norm (\( ||\mathbf{A}||_F \))

The matrix equivalent of the Euclidean norm for vectors. It is the square root of the sum of the squares of all elements.

$$ ||\mathbf{A}||_F = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^m \sum_{j=1}^n |a_{ij}|^2} $$

AI Use: Used in Weight Decay (L2 Regularization) to penalize large layer weights and prevent overfitting.

Spectral Norm (\( ||\mathbf{A}||_2 \))

The maximum "amplification" a matrix can provide to a vector. It equals the largest singular value (\( \sigma_{max} \)).

$$ ||\mathbf{A}||_2 = \sigma_{max}(\mathbf{A}) $$

AI Use: Spectral Normalization is used in GANs to stabilize training by ensuring the Discriminator is Lipschitz continuous.

2. Importance in AI Generalization

The Complexity Budget

Large matrix norms often correlate with high model complexity. By constraining the norms of weight matrices, we force the model to learn smoother, more generalizable functions rather than memorizing noise.

3. Python: Calculating Norms

import numpy as np

W = np.random.randn(512, 1024)

# Frobenius Norm (Default)
fro_norm = np.linalg.norm(W)

# Spectral Norm (Max Singular Value)
spectral_norm = np.linalg.norm(W, ord=2)

# Max Absolute Row Sum (L-infinity norm)
inf_norm = np.linalg.norm(W, ord=np.inf)

print(f"Frobenius: {fro_norm:.2f}")
print(f"Spectral: {spectral_norm:.2f}")
