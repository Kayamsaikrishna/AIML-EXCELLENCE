Prob 1.4 Fat Tails

Student's t-Distribution

Modeling uncertainty when samples are small and variance is unknown.

The Guinness Origins

The t-distribution was discovered by William Sealy Gosset, a chemist for Guinness Brewery. He needed a way to monitor quality with very small samples of beer. Because he wasn't allowed to publish under his real name, he used the pseudonym "Student".

1. Definition and PDF

The t-distribution is defined by a single parameter: Degrees of Freedom (\(\nu\) or \(df\)). $$ f(t) = \frac{\Gamma(\frac{\nu+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\nu\pi} \Gamma(\frac{\nu}{2})} \left(1 + \frac{t^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}} $$

Visual Comparison:

  • Looks like a Normal distribution (bell curve).
  • It has "heavier tails" (higher probability of extreme values).
  • As \(\nu \to \infty\), it becomes exactly the Normal Distribution.

2. When to use it?

Use the t-distribution instead of Normal when:

  1. The sample size is small (\(n < 30\)).
  2. The population standard deviation (\(\sigma\)) is unknown (we use the sample standard deviation \(s\) instead).

Because we are estimating both the mean and the variance from the same small sample, our uncertainty is higher. The t-distribution's fat tails represent this extra uncertainty.

3. ML Connection: Robustness to Outliers

Robust Statistics Deep Learning

The "Laplacian" of Bells

Standard Linear Regression (Normal assumption) is very sensitive to outliers. An extreme data point can "pull" the line of best fit significantly.

T-Regression: By assuming the noise in our labels follows a t-distribution instead of a Gaussian, we make our model more Robust. Because the t-distribution expects occasional extreme values (fat tails), it doesn't overreact to outliers during training.

4. Statistical Testing (t-Tests)

In A/B testing, if we want to know if "Button A" is significantly better than "Button B" and we only have a few hundred clicks, we compute a t-statistic.

$$ t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu}{s / \sqrt{n}} $$

Standardizing by the sample error (\(s/\sqrt{n}\)) leads to a t-distribution.
