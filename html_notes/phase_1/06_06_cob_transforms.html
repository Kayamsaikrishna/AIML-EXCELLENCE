Change of Basis

\( [T]_B = P^{-1} [T]_E P \). The same map, different language.

Concept Map: The Similarity Loop

1. The Formula

If matrix \( A \) represents transformation \( T \) in the standard basis, and we want to represent \( T \) in a new basis \( B \) (whose columns form matrix \( P \)):

$$ [T]_B = P^{-1} A P $$

This is a Similarity Transformation. \( [T]_B \) and \( A \) share the same eigenvalues and determinant.

2. Diagonalization Revisited

The best basis to view a transformation is often its Eigenbasis.

If we choose \( P \) to be the matrix of eigenvectors, then \( P^{-1} A P = D \) (Diagonal Matrix). In this basis, the transformation is just simple stretching along axes. This decouples the variables, making analysis easy (e.g., in Recurrent Neural Network dynamics).

3. Rotating the Coordinate System

import numpy as np

# Transformation A (Standard Basis): Stretch x by 2, y by 3
A = np.diag([2, 3])

# New Basis B: Rotated by 45 degrees
theta = np.pi/4
P = np.array([[np.cos(theta), -np.sin(theta)],
              [np.sin(theta),  np.cos(theta)]])

# Matrix of T in Basis B
# M = P^-1 A P
M = np.linalg.inv(P) @ A @ P

print("Standard Matrix A:\n", A)
print("\nNew Basis Matrix M (Dense!):\n", M.round(2))
# The simple stretch is 'mixed' in the rotated view.

# Verify trace (Sum of eigenvalues) is invariant
print(f"\nTrace A: {np.trace(A)}")
print(f"Trace M: {np.trace(M)}")
