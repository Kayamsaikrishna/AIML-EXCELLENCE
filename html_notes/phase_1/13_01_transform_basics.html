01. Linear Transformations

Mappings that preserve the lattice.

1. The Definition

A mapping \(T: V \to W\) is Linear if it satisfies two conditions for all vectors \(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}\) and scalar \(c\):

$$ 1. \ T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v}) \quad (\text{Additivity}) $$ $$ 2. \ T(c\mathbf{u}) = cT(\mathbf{u}) \quad (\text{Homogeneity}) $$

Visually: Origin stays put and grid lines remain parallel and evenly spaced.

2. Why Linearity Matters in AI

The Linear Layer

In a Neural Network, a dense layer is \(\mathbf{y} = \sigma(\mathbf{Wx} + \mathbf{b})\). The term \(\mathbf{Wx}\) is the Linear Transformation. Linearity allows us to distribute operations, which is why we can process batches of data efficiently using Matrix Multiplication.

3. Geometric Invariants

Linear transformations can rotate, stretch, and shear space, but they cannot bend or curve it. To create complex, curved "decision boundaries" in AI, we must inject Non-linearity (like ReLU or Sigmoid) between these linear steps.
