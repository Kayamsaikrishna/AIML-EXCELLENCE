1. Area of the Parallelogram

The length of the cross product vector is exactly equal to the area of the parallelogram formed by \( \mathbf{a} \) and \( \mathbf{b} \).

$$ \text{Area} = ||\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}|| = ||\mathbf{a}|| ||\mathbf{b}|| \sin \theta $$

If the vectors are parallel (\( \theta = 0 \)), the area is 0 (it collapses to a line).

2. The Jacobian Determinant

When we transform probability distributions (like in Normalizing Flows or VAEs), we warp space. To verify the probability sum stays 1.0, we divide by the "change in volume".

In 2D, this "change in area" is exactly the magnitude of the cross product (or determinant) of the transformation matrix columns.

3. Calculating Area

Finding the area of a triangle (half a parallelogram).

import numpy as np

# Triangle vertices
A = np.array([0, 0, 0])
B = np.array([3, 0, 0])
C = np.array([0, 4, 0])

# Edge Vectors
AB = B - A
AC = C - A

# Cross Product
cross_prod = np.cross(AB, AC)
area_parallelogram = np.linalg.norm(cross_prod)
area_triangle = 0.5 * area_parallelogram

print(f"Area of Triangle: {area_triangle}") 
# 0.5 * 3 * 4 = 6.0
