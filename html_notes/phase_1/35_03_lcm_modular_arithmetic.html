LCM & Modular Arithmetic

Thinking in Circles. Understanding the relationship between shared multiples and the mathematics of remainders—the engine behind pseudo-randomness and hashing.

The Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The LCM of $a$ and $b$ is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by both. It relates to the GCD via the elegant identity:

$$ gcd(a, b) \cdot lcm(a, b) = |a \cdot b| $$

1. Introduction to Modular Arithmetic

Often called "Clock Arithmetic," modular arithmetic deals with the remainders of division. We say $a \equiv b \pmod n$ if $n$ divides $(a - b)$.

The Modulo Operator

In code, `a % n` returns the remainder. Properties: $(a+b) \pmod n = ((a \pmod n) + (b \pmod n)) \pmod n$.

Residue Classes

The set $\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$ represents all possible remainders. This finite set forms the basis of all modern digital security.

2. Mathematical Properties

  • Addition: $a \equiv b \pmod n, c \equiv d \pmod n \implies a+c \equiv b+d \pmod n$.
  • Multiplication: $ac \equiv bd \pmod n$.
  • Exponentiation: $a^k \equiv b^k \pmod n$. This is used in RSA to sign and encrypt large data blocks.
Systems Optimization

The Infrastructure of AI

  • Data Shuffling: When training on multiple GPUs, we often use modular logic to index data batches (e.g., `batch_id % num_gpus`) to ensure balanced workloads.
  • Cycle Detection: Finding the period of a random numbers generator or a cyclic pattern in a time-series model relies on LCM calculations.
  • Feature Hashing: High-cardinality features (like user IDs) are often hashed as `hash(id) % 1000` to create a fixed-size embedding vector.
  • Distributed Memory: Segmenting large tensors across nodes often uses modular addressing to ensure $O(1)$ lookup time.
