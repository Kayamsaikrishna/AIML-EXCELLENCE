Conjugate Priors Concept

Simplifying Bayesian math so you can calculate posteriors with a pencil instead of a supercomputer.

The Mathematical Match

Normally, calculating the Posterior is hard because the denominator (\(P(D)\)) is an impossible integral.

However, if you choose a Prior that "matches" your Likelihood, the math cancels out perfectly. The resulting Posterior is in the same family as the Prior.

1. Definition

A Prior is Conjugate to a Likelihood if the Posterior distribution is in the same probability distribution family as the Prior.

Family X (Prior) + Family Y (Likelihood) \(\rightarrow\) Family X (Posterior)

2. Common Conjugate Pairs

Likelihood (Data) Conjugate Prior Posterior
Bernoulli (Coin Flips) Beta Beta
Poisson (Arrivals) Gamma Gamma
Normal (with known \(\sigma\)) Normal Normal
Multinomial (Dice/Words) Dirichlet Dirichlet

3. ML Connection: Topic Modeling (LDA)

Unsupervised NLP

Dirichlet-Multinomial Conjugacy

In Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), we assume: 1. A document is a Multinomial distribution of words. 2. The distribution of topics follows a Dirichlet distribution.

Because Dirichlet is the conjugate prior for Multinomial, the algorithm can update its "topic belief" for every word in a giant corpus very quickly. Without conjugacy, LDA would be too slow to run on modern web-scale text data.
