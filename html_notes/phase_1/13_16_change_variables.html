Calculus 1.6 Transformation

Change of Variables

Warping space to simplify the math.

The General Formula

Sometimes a region \( R \) is ugly in \( \int f(x,y) dx dy \), but beautiful in \( u, v \) coordinates. To switch variables, we must pay a price for "squishing" or "stretching" the grid. That price is the Jacobian Determinant. \[ \iint_{R} f(x, y) dx dy = \iint_{S} f(g(u,v), h(u,v)) \, \left| \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} \right| \, du dv \]

The term \( \left| \det(J) \right| \) is the local magnification factor. It ensures that mass/probability is conserved during the warp.

2. AI/ML Connection: Normalizing Flows

Generative Modeling with Jacobians

Normalizing Flows (like Glow, RealNVP) are state-of-the-art Generative Models. They work by taking a simple distribution (like a Gaussian Blob) and applying a sequence of invertible transformations \( f_1, f_2, \dots \) to warp it into a complex distribution (like the pixels of a face).

To train these models, we need to calculate the exact log-likelihood of real data. The Change of Variables formula gives us the exact rule: \[ \log p_x(x) = \log p_z(z) - \log \left| \det \frac{\partial f}{\partial z} \right| \] We optimize the model by maximizing this likelihood. The Jacobian determinant tracks the "change in volume" of probability density as it flows through the network.

3. Python: Computing the Correction Factor

jacobian_correction.py
import sympy as sp

# Define transformation: u = x + y, v = x - y
# Inverse: x = (u + v)/2, y = (u - v)/2
u, v = sp.symbols('u v')
x = (u + v) / 2
y = (u - v) / 2

# Compute Jacobian Matrix of Inverse Transformation
J = sp.Matrix([x, y]).jacobian([u, v])

# Compute Determinant
det_J = sp.simplify(J.det())

print("Jacobian Matrix:")
sp.pprint(J)
print(f"\nDeterminant (Correction Factor): {abs(det_J)}")
# Expected: |-1/2| = 0.5. 
# The map shrinks area by half. So to integrate, we multiply by 0.5.
