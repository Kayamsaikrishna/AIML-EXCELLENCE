06. Properties of the CDF

Mathematical constraints on cumulative probability.

1. Core Properties

Any valid CDF $F_X(x)$ must satisfy the following four properties:

  • Monotonicity: $F_X(x)$ is a non-decreasing function. If $x_1 < x_2$, then $F_X(x_1) \le F_X(x_2)$.
  • Lower Limit: $\lim_{x \to -\infty} F_X(x) = 0$.
  • Upper Limit: $\lim_{x \to \infty} F_X(x) = 1$.
  • Right-Continuity: $F_X(x)$ is continuous from the right at every $x$.

2. Relation to PMF/PDF

For a continuous RV, the PDF is the derivative of the CDF:

$$ f_X(x) = \frac{d}{dx} F_X(x) $$

For a discrete RV, the PMF at $x$ is the "jump" in the CDF at $x$:

$$ p_X(x) = F_X(x) - \lim_{t \to x^-} F_X(t) $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Probability Integral Transform

Generating Artificial Data

One of the most powerful properties of the CDF in AI is the **Probability Integral Transform**.

This theorem states that if $X$ is a continuous RV with CDF $F_X$, then the variable $Y = F_X(X)$ is uniformly distributed between 0 and 1. Conversely, if you have a uniform random number $U$, you can generate a value from any distribution by applying the inverse CDF: $X = F_X^{-1}(U)$. This is how modern AI systems perform **Sampling**. When a generative model like a **GAN** or **VAE** produces a synthetic face or a line of text, it often starts by generating uniform noise and "warping" it through a complex learned CDF property. Understanding these constraints ensures that the data an AI generates follows the laws of probability.

4. Python: Verifying Monotonicity

import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

x = np.linspace(-5, 5, 20)
cdf_vals = norm.cdf(x)

# Check if non-decreasing
is_monotonic = np.all(np.diff(cdf_vals) >= 0)
print(f"Is CDF Monotonic? {is_monotonic}")
print(f"Max value: {cdf_vals[-1]:.4f}") # Should be near 1.0
