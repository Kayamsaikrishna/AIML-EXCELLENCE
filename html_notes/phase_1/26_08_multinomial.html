08. Multinomial Distribution

Generalizing success/failure to multiple categories.

1. Definition

The Multinomial Distribution is the generalization of the Binomial distribution. It models the count of outcomes for $n$ independent trials where each trial can result in one of $k$ categories, with probabilities $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_k$ (where $\sum p_i = 1$).

$$ P(X_1=x_1, \dots, X_k=x_k) = \frac{n!}{x_1! \dots x_k!} p_1^{x_1} \dots p_k^{x_k} $$

2. Relation to Softmax

While the Bernoulli distribution produces a single probability $p$, the Multinomial requires a vector of probabilities. In Deep Learning, the **Softmax** layer takes raw scores (logits) and converts them into this exact probability vector, ensuring they are non-negative and sum to 1.

3. AI/ML Connection: Multi-class Classification and NLP

Beyond Binary

The Multinomial distribution is the foundation of **Multi-class Classification**.

When an AI categorizes an image as one of 1000 categories (like in ImageNet), it is modeling a Multinomial trial where $n=1$. The **Cross-Entropy Loss** for multi-class tasks is the Negative Log-Likelihood of this Multinomial distribution. In **Large Language Models (LLMs)** like GPT, the "Next Token Prediction" is a Multinomial sampling task where the categories are every word in the dictionary (the vocabulary). The model predicts a vector of 50,000+ probabilities, and we sample from that Multinomial distribution to get the next word. Understanding this allows you to reason about "Temperature" and "Top-P" sampling in AI text generation.

4. Python: Multinomial Sampling

from scipy.stats import multinomial

# n=10 trials, 3 categories with probs [0.2, 0.5, 0.3]
n = 10
p = [0.2, 0.5, 0.3]

# Probability of getting [2, 5, 3] successes
print(f"P(X=[2,5,3]): {multinomial.pmf([2, 5, 3], n, p):.4f}")

# Simulate 5 experiments
print(f"Sample Outcomes:\n{multinomial.rvs(n, p, size=5)}")
