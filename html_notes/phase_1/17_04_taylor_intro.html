04. Taylor Series Foundations

Approximating the complex with polynomials.

1. The Big Idea

Any "smooth" function can be approximated near a point \(a\) as an infinite sum of polynomials. The more terms we add, the more accurate the approximation.

$$ f(x) \approx f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2 + ... $$

2. Orders of Approximation

  • First-Order (Linear): \(L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a)\). This is just the Tangent Line. It's what standard Gradient Descent uses.
  • Second-Order (Quadratic): This captures Curvature. It's what Newton's Method uses to find the minimum in fewer steps.

3. AI Connection: Linearization

Deep Neural Networks are non-linear. However, when we perform a single step of optimization, we are Linearizing the network locally. We're saying "In this tiny region, the network behaves like a line (Tangent)". Taylor series is the mathematical justification for why small steps in the direction of the gradient actually lead to lower loss.

4. Python: Taylor Polynomial Viz

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def f(x): return np.exp(x)
def taylor2(x): return 1 + x + (x**2)/2

x = np.linspace(-1, 1, 100)
plt.plot(x, f(x), label='Exact exp(x)', lw=3)
plt.plot(x, taylor2(x), '--', label='2nd Order Taylor')
plt.title("Taylor Approximation near x=0")
plt.legend()
plt.show()
