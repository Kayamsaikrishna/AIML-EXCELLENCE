02. Gradient-based Optimization Algorithms

Descent into the depths of the loss landscape.

1. Vanilla Gradient Descent

The core update rule for modern AI. We update weights by moving in the opposite direction of the gradient, scaled by a Learning Rate \(\eta\):

$$ \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \cdot \nabla_{\theta} J(\theta_t) $$

2. Advanced Variations

  • Momentum: Adds a fraction of the previous update to the current one, helping "bounce" out of local minima.
  • Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD): Calculates the gradient on a single data point (or minibatch) rather than the whole dataset to speed up training.
  • Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation): Adjusts the learning rate for each specific weight based on how much its gradient is oscillating.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Vanishing Gradient Problem

Calculus in Deep Networks

In very deep neural networks, we multiply many partial derivatives together (due to the chain rule).

If these derivatives are all small (e.g., between 0 and 1), the final product becomes effectively zero. This is the Vanishing Gradient Problem. When this happens, the optimization algorithms stop updating the early layers of the network, and the AI "stops learning". This is why optimization isn't just about the formula; it's about the Calculus of Signal Persistence. Understanding this led to breakthroughs like ReLU activations and Residual Connections, which keep gradients "alive" during descent.

4. Python: SGD with Momentum

# Simplified Momentum Update
velocity = 0
momentum = 0.9
learning_rate = 0.01

for epoch in range(100):
    grad = get_gradient(params)
    # v = m*v - lr*grad
    velocity = momentum * velocity - learning_rate * grad
    params += velocity
