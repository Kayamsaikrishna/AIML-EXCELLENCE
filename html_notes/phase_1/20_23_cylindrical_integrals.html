07. Triple Integrals in Cylindrical Coordinates

Integration for pipes, wires, and rotating rods.

1. The Transformation

Cylindrical coordinates are just polar coordinates (\(r, \theta\)) with a vertical \(z\) axis added.

$$ x = r \cos \theta, \quad y = r \sin \theta, \quad z = z $$ $$ dV = \mathbf{r} \, dz \, dr \, d\theta $$

Use this when the region of integration has Axial Symmetry (symmetry around the z-axis).

2. Why use it?

Integrating a function like \(f(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2\) over a cylinder is trivial in cylindrical coordinates (\(f = r^2\)), but involves painful square roots in rectangular coordinates.

3. AI/ML Connection: Industrial IoT and Fluids

Pipeline Diagnostics

AI models used in Smart Factories often monitor the flow of liquids or gas through pipes. To predict a leak or a blockage, the AI must integrate the velocity profile across the cross-section of the cylinder.

Calculations for Reynolds Numbers (which tell an AI if flow is stable or turbulent) rely on cylindrical integration. Furthermore, in Hardware AI, simulating the thermal heat dissipation along a copper wire (a long cylinder) requires these integrals to ensure that the AI chip doesn't melt during intense training loads.

4. Python: Cylindrical Volume of a Paraboloid

# Volume under z = 1 - r^2 above r=0..1, theta=0..2pi
# Integral [0 to 2pi] [0 to 1] [0 to 1-r^2] r dz dr d_theta

import sympy as sp
r, theta, z = sp.symbols('r theta z')

# Volume = int (r dz dr d_theta)
# The innermost integral int(r, z=0..1-r^2) = r * (1 - r^2)
volume = sp.integrate(sp.integrate(sp.integrate(r, (z, 0, 1 - r**2)), (r, 0, 1)), (theta, 0, 2*sp.pi))

print(f"Volume of the paraboloid: {volume}")
# Result: pi/2
