08. Power Series & Convergence

Turning functions into infinite polynomials.

1. Definition

A Power Series is a series of the form:

$$ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (x - a)^n = c_0 + c_1(x-a) + c_2(x-a)^2 + \dots $$

Where \(x\) is a variable, \(c_n\) are coefficients, and \(a\) is the center of the series.

2. The Radius of Convergence (R)

For any power series, there is a radius \(R\) such that the series converges if \(|x-a| < R\) and diverges if \(|x-a|> R\). We find \(R\) by applying the Ratio Test to the entire term.

3. AI/ML Connection: Approximating Non-Linearity

Series Expansion of Neural Gates

Neural networks use non-linear functions like Sigmoid (\(1/(1+e^{-x})\)) or Tanh. In modern hardware AI chips, these functions are actually very slow to calculate directly.

To speed things up, engineers represent these functions as a Power Series. However, the AI must stay within the Interval of Convergence. If the input weights push the value of \(x\) outside of the radius \(R\), the approximation fails and the AI becomes wildly inaccurate. Understanding the "Safe Zone" of a power series allows hardware researchers to design ultra-fast AI processors that use simple polynomial additions instead of complex exponential math.

4. Python: Determining Convergence Interval

# Series: x^n / n
# Ratio Test: |x^(n+1)/(n+1) * n/x^n| = |x * n/(n+1)| -> |x|
# Converges for |x| < 1. Radius R = 1.

import sympy as sp
x, n = sp.symbols('x n')
an = x**n / n

# Using SymPy's formal limit
ratio_limit = sp.limit(sp.Abs(x**(n+1)/(n+1) * (n/x**n)), n, sp.oo)
print(f"Convergence condition: {ratio_limit} < 1")
