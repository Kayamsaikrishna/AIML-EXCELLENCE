11. Moment Generating Functions (MGF)

Generating statistical information through Taylor series expansion.

1. Definition

The Moment Generating Function (MGF) of a random variable $X$, denoted $M_X(t)$, is the expected value of $e^{tX}$:

$$ M_X(t) = E[e^{tX}] $$

It "encapsulates" all moments of the distribution in a single analytic function.

2. The "Generating" Property

The $k$-th raw moment $E[X^k]$ can be found by taking the $k$-th derivative of $M_X(t)$ and evaluating it at $t=0$:

$$ E[X^k] = \left. \frac{d^k M_X(t)}{dt^k} \right|_{t=0} $$

This is extremely useful when the direct summation/integration for moments is complex, but the MGF has an elegant closed-form expression.

3. AI/ML Connection: Uniqueness and Convergence

Theoretical Guarantees

MGFs are essential for **Proving that two distributions are the same**.

The **Uniqueness Theorem** states that if two random variables have the same MGF (in a neighborhood around zero), they have the same distribution. In AI Research, MGFs are used to prove the **Central Limit Theorem**, which guarantees that the sum of many small independent errors follows a Gaussian curve—a core assumption in almost every model. Furthermore, in **Statistical Learning Theory**, MGFs of the loss function are used to derive **Generalization Bounds** (Chernoff Bounds), which tell us the maximum probability that an AI will fail on new data.

4. Python: MGF of a Normal Distribution

# MGF of Standard Normal is exp(t^2 / 2)
def standard_normal_mgf(t):
    return np.exp(t**2 / 2)

# Verify 2nd Moment (Variance + Mean^2)
# Derivative of exp(t^2/2) is t * exp(t^2/2)
# 2nd derivative is exp(t^2/2) + t^2 * exp(t^2/2)
# At t=0, 2nd deriv = 1. (Which matches Var=1 for Z-dist)
print(f"MGF at t=0.5: {standard_normal_mgf(0.5):.4f}")
