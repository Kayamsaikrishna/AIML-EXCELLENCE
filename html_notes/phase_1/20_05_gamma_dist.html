Prob 1.4 The Sum

The Gamma Distribution

Waiting for the \(k\)-th event to occur.

Sum of Exponentials

Just as the Negative Binomial is the sum of Geometric variables, the Gamma Distribution is the sum of independent Exponential variables.

If we wait for one bus, it's Exponential. If we wait for the 5th bus to arrive, the total time follows a Gamma distribution.

1. The Formula

\(X \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)\) where \(\alpha\) is the shape and \(\beta\) is the rate (or \(\theta = 1/\beta\) is the scale). $$ f(x) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} x^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta x} $$ for \(x > 0\).

Key Players:

  • \(\alpha\): The number of events we are waiting for.
  • \(\Gamma(\alpha)\): The Gamma Function. It is a continuous version of the factorial: \(\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!\) for integers.

3. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\beta}\).
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = \frac{\alpha}{\beta^2}\).

4. ML Connection: Conjugate Priors

Bayesian ML Regularization

The Gamma Prior

In Bayesian statistics, we need to choose a "prior" distribution to represent our belief about a parameter before seeing data.

The Gamma distribution is the Conjugate Prior for the rate parameter \(\lambda\) of a Poisson distribution. This means if you start with a Gamma prior and observe Poisson data, your "posterior" (updated belief) is also a Gamma distribution. This makes the math extremely efficient.

Precision in Linear Models

In some advanced linear models, instead of fixing the noise variance \(\sigma^2\), we model the "precision" \(\tau = 1/\sigma^2\) as a Gamma-distributed random variable.
