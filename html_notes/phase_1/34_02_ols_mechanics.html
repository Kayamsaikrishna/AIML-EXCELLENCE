02. OLS Mechanics

Calculating the best-fit line by minimizing the squares.

1. The Minimization Problem

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) finds the line that minimizes the Residual Sum of Squares (RSS):

$$ RSS = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - (\beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i))^2 $$

We use calculus (partial derivatives) to find the $\beta$ values that make this sum as small as possible.

2. The Normal Equations

For the simple case, the optimal slope is:

$$ \hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} $$

The line effectively balances the covariance (relationship) against the variance (spread) of the data.

3. AI/ML Connection: Loss Functions and Convergence

The Prototype of Gradient Descent

In AI, OLS is the **Direct Solution** to the world's most popular loss: **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**.

When you train a model with Gradient Descent**, you are iteratively looking for the same point that OLS finds in one "Matrix inversion" step. However, OLS is the foundation of Adaptive Filtering** (used in noise-canceling headphones). These systems use a "Recursive Least Squares" approach to update weights as new sound arrives. Understanding OLS mechanics is essential because it is the "Perfect World" scenario: it tells us what the absolute best possible linear model is. If Gradient Descent is taking too long to converge, AI engineers compare its current weights to the OLS analytical solution to measure how far they are from the global minimum.

4. Python: Manual OLS Calculation

# Direct Math instead of sklearn
c = np.cov(X, y)[0, 1]
v = np.var(X)

beta_1 = c / v
beta_0 = np.mean(y) - beta_1 * np.mean(X)

print(f"Optimal Prediction: y = {beta_0:.2f} + {beta_1:.2f}*x")
