Stats 1.4 The Meta-Distribution

Sampling Distributions

Why your guess changes if you pick a different sample.

The Distribution of Results

An estimator is a random variable. If you take 100 different samples from the same population and calculate the mean for each, you will get 100 different numbers. The distribution of these 100 numbers is the Sampling Distribution.

1. Standard Error (SE)

Standard Error is the Standard Deviation of the Sampling Distribution. $$ SE(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} $$

It tells you how much your "guess" is expected to fluctuate from one sample to another.

2. Central Limit Theorem Revisited

The Sampling Distribution of the mean always becomes Normal as \(n \to \infty\). This is why the Normal distribution is the "gold standard" for calculating confidence in our statistics.

3. ML Connection: Variance of the Model

Robustness Cross-Validation

Why we use K-Fold CV

When we run K-Fold Cross-Validation, we get \(K\) different values for the accuracy/loss. This is a small-scale empirical look at the Sampling Distribution of our model's performance.

If the accuracy is 90% in one fold and 60% in another, the Standard Error is high. This tells us that our model is sensitive to the specific sample it was trained on (High Variance). A robust model should have a "tight" sampling distribution with low SE.
