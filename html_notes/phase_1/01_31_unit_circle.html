31. The Unit Circle & Periodicity

Where Trigonometry meets the infinite loop.

1. The Geometry of Cycles

A circle with radius \( r = 1 \). Any point on its boundary has coordinates \( (x, y) = (\cos \theta, \sin \theta) \).

$$ x^2 + y^2 = 1 \implies \cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta = 1 $$

2. Periodic Activation in AI

Oscillations in Learning

Periodicity is the heart of Seasonal Forecasting and Audio Pattern Recognition. In Neural Networks, periodic activation functions (like SIRENs - Sinusoidal Representation Networks) are used to represent complex signals like 3D shapes or textures with infinite detail.

  • Continuity: Unlike ReLU, \( \sin(x) \) is infinitely differentiable, which is critical for solving Differential Equations using Neural Networks (PINNs).
  • Normalization: The bounded nature of the unit circle (values between -1 and 1) provides a natural normalization scheme for hidden layers.

3. Python: Unit Circle Mapping

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Generate 100 points around the circle
theta = np.linspace(0, 2*np.pi, 100)
x = np.cos(theta)
y = np.sin(theta)

# Plotting the circle
plt.figure(figsize=(5,5))
plt.plot(x, y, label='Unit Circle')
plt.axhline(0, color='black', lw=1)
plt.axvline(0, color='black', lw=1)
plt.grid(True, linestyle='--')
plt.axis('equal')
plt.title("The Loop of Trigonometry")
plt.show()
