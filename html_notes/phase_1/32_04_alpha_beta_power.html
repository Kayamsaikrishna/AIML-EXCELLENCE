04. Significance ($\alpha$), $\beta$, and Power

The sensitivity of your statistical detector.

1. The Parameters of Choice

  • $\alpha$ (Significance Level): The maximum risk you are willing to take of a False Positive. Usually set to 0.05. It's the "Size" of the rejection region.
  • $\beta$ (Type II Error): The probability of missing a true effect.
  • Power ($1 - \beta$): The probability that the test correctly rejects $H_0$ when $H_0$ is false. It is the "Sensitivity" of the test. A power of 0.80 is generally the target.

2. What Influences Power?

1. **Sample Size ($n$):** More data always increases power.
2. **Effect Size:** A massive change is easier to detect than a tiny one.
3. **Significance Level ($\alpha$):** If you make $\alpha$ smaller (conservative), power decreases.

3. AI/ML Connection: Data Budgeting and Model Deployment

Knowing when to stop collecting data

In AI, Power Analysis is how you decide your **Data labeling budget**.

If you are developing a **Fraud Detection AI**, you need to know how many transactions to "audit" to prove that your new deep learning model is better than the legacy heuristic. If your "Power" is only 0.20, it means that even if your model is 2x better, you only have a 20% chance of *proving* it statistically. This leads to Model Under-Evaluation**. Professional AI teams do a "Pre-Study Power Analysis" to determine: "We need exactly 14,000 labeled images to achieve a Power of 0.90." This prevents wasted compute over training sets that are too small to yield definitive conclusions.

4. Python: Visualizing Power

from statsmodels.stats.power import TTestIndPower

# 1. Initiate power analysis
obj = TTestIndPower()

# 2. Calculate sample size needed
# effect_size=0.5 (Medium), alpha=0.05, power=0.8
n = obj.solve_power(effect_size=0.5, alpha=0.05, power=0.8, ratio=1)

print(f"Sample size needed PER GROUP: {n:.2f}")
# Result: ~64. Sample size determines your budget!
