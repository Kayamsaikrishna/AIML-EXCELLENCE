Derangements & The Pigeonhole Principle

Guaranteed Collisions and Absolute Chaos. Using simple numerical constraints to prove that certain patterns must exist, and calculating the probability that everything is in the wrong place.

The Power of Constraint

Some truths are true simply because there isn't enough room for them to be false. If you have 10 pigeons and only 9 holes, at least one hole *must* contain at least two pigeons. This seemingly trivial fact is the foundation of complex existence proofs in computer science.

1. The Pigeonhole Principle (PHP)

If \(n > mk\), then at least one pigeonhole must contain at least \(k+1\) pigeons.

Example: In a city with 1 million people, are there at least two people with the exact same number of hairs on their head?

Maximum human head hairs \(\approx 150,000\). Since Population (1M) > Max Hairs (150K), the answer is a mathematical YES.

2. Derangements (\(!n\))

A derangement is a permutation where no element appears in its original position.

$$ !n = n! \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{(-1)^k}{k!} $$

As \(n \to \infty\), the probability that a random permutation is a derangement approaches \(\frac{1}{e} \approx 36.8\%\).

Example: 4 students leave their phones in a box. They each pick one at random. What is the probability that none of them gets their own phone?

\(!4 = 4! (1 - 1 + 1/2 - 1/6 + 1/24) = 9\). Total permutations = \(4! = 24\). Probability = \(9/24 = 37.5\%\).

Hashing Cryptography

The Logic of Collisions in ML

  • Hash Collisions: The Pigeonhole Principle explains why hash collisions are inevitable. If your hash space (e.g., 256 bits) is smaller than the input data space, two different inputs *must* map to the same hash.
  • Feature Hashing (Hashing Trick): In NLP, we often hash words into a fixed-size vector. PHP tells us how much "information loss" or collision we can expect given the vocabulary size and vector dimension.
  • Randomized Algorithms: Derangements help analyze "Wait Time" and "Failure Rates" in randomized optimization or shuffling algorithms (like SGD mini-batch sampling).
  • Adversarial Attacks: Finding "Universal Adversarial Perturbations" is essentially finding a point in input space that "pigeonholes" multiple classes into the same error region.
