11. Paired t-test

Measuring change within the same subjects.

1. The Concept of Matching

The Paired t-test is used when the two samples are **Related**—usually because the same subject is measured twice (Before vs After). Because we are measuring the same thing, we filter out "Individual differences," making this test much more powerful than the independent version.

2. The Math of Differences

We don't look at the raw means of Group 1 and Group 2. Instead, we calculate the difference ($d_i$) for every pair. We then perform a one-sample t-test on these differences, testing against $\mu_d = 0$.

$$ t = \frac{\bar{d} - 0}{s_d / \sqrt{n}} $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Hyperparameter Tuning and Model Finetuning

Before and After Optimization

In AI, the Paired t-test is the gold standard for **Optimization Validation**.

If you implement a new **Weight Initialization** or a **Learning Rate Scheduler**, you test it across 50 different datasets. You have 50 scores "Before" the change and 50 scores "After." These are **Paired** because you are comparing performance on the *same dataset*. By using a Paired t-test, you cancel out the fact that some datasets are "Hard" and some are "Easy." You only see the **Net delta** caused by your code change. This is also how we evaluate **Transfer Learning**: we measure a model's performance on a task "As-is" vs "After fine-tuning" on 100 sample images. The paired t-test tells us if the fine-tuning actually moved the needle, separate from the model's base intelligence.

4. Python: stats.ttest_rel

# Performance of 10 users on a task
before = [75, 82, 68, 90, 85, 78, 80, 72, 88, 70]
# After 'AI Assistance' is enabled
after = [79, 85, 72, 92, 84, 83, 85, 76, 91, 74]

t_stat, p_val = stats.ttest_rel(before, after)

print(f"p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
# Average improvement: ~4 points. Is it significant?
# If p < 0.05, YES, the AI assistance helped.
