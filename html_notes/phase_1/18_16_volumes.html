16. Volumes of Revolution

Turning 2D shapes into 3D models.

1. The Core Idea

If you take a 2D area and spin it around an axis (like a pottery wheel), you create a 3D solid. Calculus allows us to calculate the volume of these solids by summing up infinitely thin "slices".

2. The Methods

  • Disk Method (\(V = \int \pi [R(x)]^2 dx\)): Use when the slices are solid circles.
  • Washer Method (\(V = \int \pi ([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2) dx\)): Use when the solid has a hole in the middle.
  • Shell Method (\(V = \int 2\pi x f(x) dx\)): Use when you "wrap" thin cylindrical shells around an axis.

3. AI/ML Connection: 3D Generation & NeRFs

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)

Generating 3D objects from 2D photos is a massive field in AI. Models like NeRFs represent 3D space as a continuous function of density and color.

To render an image from a NeRF, the AI must "Integrate" the volume along a ray of light. This is effectively calculating a volume integral. If the model is generating a symmetrical object (like a vase or a car wheel), it might use Rotational Symmetry priors based on these volume integration rules to fill in missing parts of the object that the camera couldn't see. Understanding volumes of revolution is the first step toward understanding 3D AI and Digital Twins.

4. Python: Calculating a Sphere's Volume

# Rotate y = sqrt(r^2 - x^2) around x-axis
# Integral of pi * (sqrt(r^2 - x^2))^2 from -r to r
# Result: 4/3 * pi * r^3

import sympy as sp

x, r = sp.symbols('x r')
f_squared = r**2 - x**2
volume = sp.pi * sp.integrate(f_squared, (x, -r, r))

print(f"Volume of a sphere with radius r: {volume}")
# Output: 4*pi*r**3/3
