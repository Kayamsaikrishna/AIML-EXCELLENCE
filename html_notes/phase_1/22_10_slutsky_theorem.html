Prob 1.4 Algebra of Limits

Slutsky's Theorem

Performing algebra on random variable sequences that converge in different ways.

The Problem with Convergence

In calculus, if \(a_n \to a\) and \(b_n \to b\), then \(a_n + b_n \to a + b\).

In probability, this is not always true if the variables converge in different ways (e.g., one in distribution and one in probability). Slutsky's Theorem provides the crucial exceptions where algebra still works.

1. The Formula

Let \(X_n \xrightarrow{d} X\) (Distribution) and \(Y_n \xrightarrow{P} c\) (Probability to a constant). Then:
  • \(X_n + Y_n \xrightarrow{d} X + c\)
  • \(X_n Y_n \xrightarrow{d} cX\)
  • \(X_n / Y_n \xrightarrow{d} X/c\) (if \(c \neq 0\))

2. Why the Constant "c" Matters

If \(Y_n\) converged to another random variable \(Y\), we wouldn't be able to say much without knowing their joint relationship (correlation). But because \(Y_n\) collapses to a single number, Slutsky proves that the limit behavior is well-behaved.

3. ML Connection: Variance Estimation

Statistics Inference

Why we can use the Sample Variance

When we perform a Z-test, we ideally need the true population variance \(\sigma\). But we only have the sample variance \(s\).

The Central Limit Theorem says: \(\frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\).
The Law of Large Numbers says: \(s \xrightarrow{P} \sigma\).

Slustky's Theorem allows us to combine these: $$\frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{s/\sqrt{n}} = \left(\frac{\bar{X} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{\sigma}{s}\right) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \cdot 1 = \mathcal{N}(0, 1) $$ This is the mathematical proof that for large \(n\), using the sample variance in your formulas is asymptotically valid.
