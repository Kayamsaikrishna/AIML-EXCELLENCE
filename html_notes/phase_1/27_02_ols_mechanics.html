Stats 1.7 Optimization

OLS Mechanics

Ordinary Least Squares: The engine that minimizes the "Square of Errors" to find the perfect fit.

Why "Squares"?

We square the errors for two reasons:
1. Positivity: It ensures that positive and negative errors don't cancel each other out. 2. Penalty: It punishes large errors much more than small ones (a 2m error is 4x worse than a 1m error).

1. The Objective Function

We want to minimize the Residual Sum of Squares (RSS):

$$ \min \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 $$ $$ \hat{y}_i = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 x_i $$

2. The Analytical Solution

Using calculus (taking partial derivatives and setting to zero), we get the "best" estimates:

$$ \hat{\beta}_1 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(x,y)}{\text{Var}(x)} $$ $$ \hat{\beta}_0 = \bar{y} - \hat{\beta}_1 \bar{x} $$

Key Insight: The slope is simply the relationship (covariance) normalized by the spread of the input.

3. ML Connection: Normal Equation vs Gradient Descent

Optimization Computational Complexity

How Scikit-Learn works

When you run `LinearRegression().fit()`, the computer has two choices:

Method Complexity Best For...
Normal Equation (Formula) \(O(n^3)\) Small/Medium datasets. Exact solution in one step.
Gradient Descent \(O(kn^2)\) Massive data. Iteratively "walks" towards the minimum error.

For most tabular data, OLS (Normal Equation/SVD) is preferred because it's deterministic and requires no "learning rate" tuning.
