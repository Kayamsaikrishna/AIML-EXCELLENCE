Stats 1.6 Probabilistic Truth

Bayesian Hypothesis Testing

Asking "What is the probability that my hypothesis is true?" instead of "How weird is this data?"

The P-Value Problem

A p-value measures the probability of the *Data* given the Null (\(P(data|H_0)\)).
But you actually want the probability of the Hypothesis given the Data (\(P(H_a|data)\)).

Bayesian testing gives you exactly what you asked for.

1. The Bayesian Workflow for Testing

1. Define \(H_0\) (e.g., Conversion Rate \(< 5\%\)).
2. Calculate the Posterior Distribution \(P(\theta|data)\).
3. Calculate the shaded area under the posterior for the region of interest.

If the area for "Effect > 0" is 0.98, then there is a 98% probability that the feature works. You don't need "Significance" levels; you just need to decide if 98% is high enough to risk deployment.

2. Regions of Practical Equivalence (ROPE)

In Frequentist stats, any tiny difference can be "significant." In Bayesian stats, we define a ROPE.

graph LR A[ROPE: -0.1 to 0.1] --> B{Does Posterior overlap?} B -->|Yes| C[Effect is Negligible] B -->|No| D[Effect is Real and Meaningful]

ROPE helps avoid the "Large N trap" where tiny, useless differences are touted as discoveries.

3. ML Connection: Early Stopping in A/B Tests

Product Optimization

Peeking is Allowed

In Frequentist A/B testing, "peeking" at the results before the end of the test is a huge sin (it inflates Alpha).

In Bayesian A/B Testing, you can look as often as you want! Since you are maintaining a continuous Posterior, you can check the "Probability of B > A" every 10 minutes. If the probability hits 99.9% early, you can Stop the test immediately and save thousands of dollars. Companies like Google and Optimizely use Bayesian engines for exactly this reason: speed of decision-making.
