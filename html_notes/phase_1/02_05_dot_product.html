The Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

\( |\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}| \le ||\mathbf{u}|| ||\mathbf{v}|| \). The rigorous proof that similarity is bounded, and why correlation sits between -1 and 1.

1. The Most Important Inequality in Math

The absolute value of the dot product can never exceed the product of the magnitudes.

$$ |\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}| \le ||\mathbf{u}|| ||\mathbf{v}|| $$

Equality holds ONLY if \( \mathbf{u} \) and \( \mathbf{v} \) are parallel (linearly dependent).

Correction: This inequality is why "Cosine Similarity" \( \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{||\mathbf{u}|| ||\mathbf{v}||} \) is mathematically guaranteed to be between -1.0 and 1.0.

2. Pearson Correlation

In Data Science, Correlation is just the Cosine Similarity of Centered Vectors (Mean = 0).

When training a model to output a score (e.g. Tanh activation), we rely on Cauchy-Schwarz logic to ensure our bounded outputs map correctly to bounded targets.

3. Verification

Checking the inequality for random high-dimensional vectors.

import numpy as np

# Random Vectors in 100-dim space
u = np.random.randn(100)
v = np.random.randn(100)

# 1. Dot Product
dot_val = np.abs(np.dot(u, v))

# 2. Product of Norms
norm_prod = np.linalg.norm(u) * np.linalg.norm(v)

print(f"|u . v| : {dot_val:.4f}")
print(f"||u||*||v|| : {norm_prod:.4f}")

if dot_val <= norm_prod:
    print("Cauchy-Schwarz holds.")
else:
    print("Math is broken.")
