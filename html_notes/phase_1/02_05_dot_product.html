05. Dot Product (Scalar Product)

Measuring alignment and similarity.

1. Algebraic Definition

The dot product takes two vectors of the same dimension and returns a single number (a scalar).

$$ \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \sum_{i=1}^n u_i v_i = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + \dots + u_n v_n $$

Key Properties

  • Commutative: \( \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{u} \).
  • Distributive: \( \mathbf{u} \cdot (\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{w} \).
  • Magnitude Relation: \( \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{u} = \|\mathbf{u}\|^2 \).

2. The Core of Transformers

Dot Product Attention

Modern Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4 work by calculating "Attention". When a query vector \(Q\) meets a key vector \(K\), their Dot Product determines how much "Attention" to pay to that specific word.

  • High Dot Product: Signals strong relevance or semantic similarity.
  • Efficiency: Dot products are extremely fast to compute on GPUs, making them the engine of modern AI.

3. Python: Vectorized Dot Product

import numpy as np

u = np.array([1, 2, 3])
v = np.array([4, 5, 6])

# Algebraic calculation
dot_algebraic = np.dot(u, v) # (1*4 + 2*5 + 3*6) = 32

# Alternative syntax (The AI/ML standard)
dot_standard = u @ v 

print(f"Dot Product result: {dot_standard}")
