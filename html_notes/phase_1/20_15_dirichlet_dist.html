Prob 1.4 Multivariate Prior

The Dirichlet Distribution

Distributions of categorical distributions.

The Beta of many Categories

The Beta distribution models uncertainty about a Bernoulli parameter (2 outcomes).
The Dirichlet distribution models uncertainty about a Multinomial parameter (K outcomes).

"A sample from a Dirichlet distribution is itself a valid probability distribution (a set of numbers that sum to 1)."

1. Definition and Simplex

Let \(X = (x_1, \dots, x_K)\) take values on the (K-1)-Simplex (meaning \(x_i \ge 0\) and \(\sum x_i = 1\)).
The parameters are \(\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_K)\) where \(\alpha_i > 0\). $$ f(x_1, \dots, x_K; \alpha) = \frac{1}{B(\alpha)} \prod_{i=1}^K x_i^{\alpha_i-1} $$

The Simplex: Because the components must sum to 1, a Dirichlet over 3 variables can be visualized as a 2D triangle. Each point inside the triangle represents a specific probability distribution (e.g., 40% A, 30% B, 30% C).

2. Shape and Concentration

  • If all \(\alpha_i = 1\): Uniform distribution over the simplex.
  • If all \(\alpha_i > 1\): Density concentrates in the middle (Balanced probabilities).
  • If all \(\alpha_i < 1\): Density pushes towards the corners (Sparse probabilities—the sample will have one element close to 1 and others close to 0).

3. ML Connection: Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)

NLP Topic Modeling

Topic Modeling

In LDA, we assume a document is a mixture of "Topics".

  • Each Document is a sample from a Dirichlet distribution over topics.
  • Each Topic is a sample from a Dirichlet distribution over words.

By setting the \(\alpha\) parameters to be small (e.g., 0.1), we encode the prior belief that each document mostly belongs to just a few topics, and each topic is mostly made of just a few key words (Sparsity).

Bayesian Categorical Inference

Dirichlet is the Conjugate Prior for Multinomial. If you see word counts from a website (Multinomial), you update your Dirichlet prior by simply adding the observed word counts to your \(\alpha\) parameters.

4. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[x_i] = \frac{\alpha_i}{\sum \alpha_j}\).
  • Interpretation: The "expected" probability for category \(i\) is its share of the total concentration.
