04. Riemann Sums and Definite Integrals

The area under the curve.

1. Estimating with Rectangles

To find the area under \(f(x)\) from \(a\) to \(b\), we divide it into \(n\) rectangles of width \(\Delta x = \frac{b-a}{n}\).

$$ \text{Area} \approx \sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^*) \Delta x $$

Using the **Left Endpoint**, **Right Endpoint**, or **Midpoint** changes the estimate accuracy.

2. The Definite Integral

The **Definite Integral** is the limit of the Riemann sum as \(n \to \infty\):

$$ \int_a^b f(x) dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i) \Delta x $$

3. AI Connection: Expectations & PDF

Probability Density Functions

In Statistics, a **PDF** \(p(x)\) tells us the density of probability at a point. To find the Probability that a variable falls in range \([a, b]\), we must integrate: $$ P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b p(x) dx $$ The Riemann sum is the discrete version (summing probabilities of bins), and the integral is the continuous truth. This is the heart of Expectation Values in every stochastic model.

4. Python: Numerical Riemann Sum

import numpy as np

def f(x): return x**2
a, b = 0, 1
n = 1000

x = np.linspace(a, b, n)
dx = (b-a)/n

# Riemann sum (Right endpoint)
area = np.sum(f(x[1:])) * dx

print(f"Estimated Area: {area:.6f}")
print(f"Exact Area (1/3): {1/3:.6f}")
