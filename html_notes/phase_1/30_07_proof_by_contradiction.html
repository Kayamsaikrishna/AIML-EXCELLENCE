Proofs Indirect

Indirect Proof: Contradiction & Contraposition

Reductio ad Absurdum. When a straight path is blocked, we prove the truth by showing that the alternatives lead to a world of impossible madness.

The Indirect Advantage

Sometimes proving \(P \rightarrow Q\) directly is hard. Indirect proofs give us two clever detours:

  • Contraposition: Prove that "Not Q" implies "Not P". This is logically identical to the original statement.
  • Contradiction: Assume the statement is False, and show that this assumption breaks the laws of math. Since math can't be broken, the original statement must have been True.

1. Proof by Contraposition

$$ (P \rightarrow Q) \equiv (\neg Q \rightarrow \neg P) $$

Example: Prove that if \(n^2\) is even, then \(n\) is even.

  1. Assume the opposite of the conclusion: \(n\) is odd.
  2. Then \(n = 2k + 1\).
  3. \(n^2 = (2k+1)^2 = 4k^2 + 4k + 1 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1\).
  4. This means \(n^2\) is odd (\(\neg P\)).
  5. By contraposition, if \(n^2\) is even, \(n\) must be even. \(\square\)

2. Proof by Contradiction

One of the most powerful tools in history. Used to prove things that "don't exist" or are irrational.

Example: Prove that \(\sqrt{2}\) is irrational.

  1. Assume \(\sqrt{2}\) is rational (Assume \(\neg S\)).
  2. Then \(\sqrt{2} = a/b\) in lowest terms.
  3. \(2 = a^2/b^2 \rightarrow a^2 = 2b^2\). This means \(a\) is even.
  4. If \(a\) is even, \(a = 2k\), so \(a^2 = 4k^2\).
  5. \(4k^2 = 2b^2 \rightarrow b^2 = 2k^2\). This means \(b\) is also even.
  6. Contradiction: If both \(a\) and \(b\) are even, the fraction was not in lowest terms! \(\therefore\) Our assumption was wrong. \(\sqrt{2}\) is irrational. \(\square\)
Cybersecurity Complexity

Why AI needs Contradiction

  • Complexity Lower Bounds: In Computer Science, we prove that certain problems (like the Halting Problem) are undecidable using proof by contradiction. We assume a machine exists that solves it, move logically, and reach an impossible paradox.
  • Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP): In blockchain and private AI, ZKPs rely on the idea that if a person didn't know the secret, the probability of them passing the challenge is zero. If they pass, the assumption they were lying leads to a contradiction.
  • Security Verification: To prove a system is "secure," we often prove that "If an adversary can break the system, they could also solve a known impossible math problem." Since they can't solve the problem, they can't break the system.
