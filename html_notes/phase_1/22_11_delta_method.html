The Delta Method

Extending the Central Limit Theorem to smooth functions of sample averages.

Beyond Simple Means

The CLT tells us that the average \(\bar{X}\) is Normally distributed. But what if we are interested in \(\sqrt{\bar{X}}\), or \(\log(\bar{X})\), or \(1/\bar{X}\)?

The Delta Method allows us to find the distribution of any smooth function \(g(\bar{X})\) by linearizing it around the mean using a Taylor expansion.

1. The Formula

If \(\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}_n - \mu) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) and \(g\) is differentiable at \(\mu\), then: $$ \sqrt{n}(g(\bar{X}_n) - g(\mu)) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, [g'(\mu)]^2 \sigma^2) $$

Intuition: The variance of the function is the original variance scaled by the squared derivative (slope) of the function at that point. If the function is very steep, the fluctuations of \(\bar{X}\) are magnified. If it's flat, they are dampened.

2. ML Connection: Odds and Logistic Units

Deep Learning Classification

Confidence in Logits

In Logistic Regression, we often interpret the output as a probability \(P = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x})\). But what if we want to know the Standard Error of this probability?

The model gives us the variance of the linear scores (\(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}\)). We use the Delta Method (with the Sigmoid function as our \(g\)) to propagate that variance through the non-linearity and find the confidence interval of the final probability.

3. Second-Order Delta Method

If the first derivative \(g'(\mu)\) is zero, we look at the second derivative. In this case, the resulting distribution is not Normal but usually related to a Chi-Square distribution.
