1. Adding Forces

Vector addition is commutative: \( \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{a} \).

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} $$

Geometrically, this forms the diagonal of the parallelogram extended by the two vectors.

2. The Learning Step

Training a Neural Network is literally just vector subtraction.

We want to move our weights \( W \) in the direction opposite to the Gradient \( \nabla L \).

$$ W_{new} = W_{old} + (-\eta \cdot \nabla L) $$

Here, \( -\eta \cdot \nabla L \) is a vector that points "downhill". We add this vector to our current position to arrive at a better solution.

3. Displacement Vectors

Finding the vector pointing from A to B.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Current Weights (Position in Landscape)
w_old = np.array([2.0, 3.0])

# Gradient (Slope direction)
gradient = np.array([0.5, 1.0])

# Learning Rate (Step Size)
lr = 0.1

# Calculate Step Vector (The Update)
step = -lr * gradient # [-0.05, -0.1]

# Apply Update (Vector Addition)
w_new = w_old + step

print(f"Old Weights: {w_old}")
print(f"Update Step: {step}")
print(f"New Weights: {w_new}")
# The model has 'walked' slightly downhill.
