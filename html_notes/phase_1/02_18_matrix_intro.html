1. What is a Matrix?

While often taught as a "box of numbers", a Matrix is best understood as a Linear Transformation.

The columns of \( A \) tell you where the basis vectors \( \hat{i} \) and \( \hat{j} \) land after the transformation.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} | & | \\ T(\hat{i}) & T(\hat{j}) \\ | & | \end{bmatrix} $$

2. Tensors (N-Dimensional Arrays)

In Deep Learning, we generalize this structure.

  • 0D: Scalar (Loss value).
  • 1D: Vector (Audio signal, one row of data).
  • 2D: Matrix (Grayscale Image, Weight Layer).
  • 3D: Tensor (RGB Image, Video Frame).
  • 4D: Batch (Batch of RGB Images).

Libraries like PyTorch and TensorFlow are essentially "Tensor Algebra" engines.

3. Tensor Shapes

Understanding dimensionality in NumPy/PyTorch.

import numpy as np

# 1. Matrix (2D Tensor) representing a Rotation
theta = np.radians(90)
R = np.array([
    [np.cos(theta), -np.sin(theta)],
    [np.sin(theta),  np.cos(theta)]
])

# 2. Vector (1D Tensor)
v = np.array([1, 0])

# Transform
v_prime = np.dot(R, v)

print(f"Original: {v}")
print(f"Rotated:  {v_prime}") # Should be [0, 1]

# 3. 3D Tensor (e.g. 2 images of 2x2 pixels)
T = np.random.rand(2, 2, 2)
print(f"\nTensor Shape: {T.shape}")
print(f"Rank (NDIM): {T.ndim}")
