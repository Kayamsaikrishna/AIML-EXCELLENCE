03. Geometric Interpretation of Regression

Linear Algebra meets Statistics: The Projection.

1. The Vector View

In statistics, we see data as points. In geometry, we see $Y$ as a vector in an $n$-dimensional space. Regression is the process of finding a vector $\hat{Y}$ that is a linear combination of our input vectors ($X$'s) that is **Closest** to the actual $Y$.

2. The Orthogonal Projection

The "Closest" point is found by an **Orthogonal Projection**. The residuals $\epsilon = Y - \hat{Y}$ must be perpendicular (orthogonal) to the plane spanned by our feature vectors $X$.

$$ X^T(Y - X\beta) = 0 $$

This is why the Mean of residuals is always zero in OLS.

3. AI/ML Connection: Vector Spaces and Embeddings

The Subspace of Reality

In AI, this geometric view is the foundation of Vector Space Modeling.

When we perform **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** or construct **Word Embeddings (Word2Vec)**, we are essentially doing high-dimensional geometry similar to regression. This view explains *why* we can compress data: we are "Projecting" the high-dimensional world onto a lower-dimensional **Latent Subspace**. This is also how **Modern Recommendatons** work: the AI projects users and items onto the same coordinate system. If a user is "Close" to a movie's feature vector in that subspace, the AI recommends it. Understanding regression as a "Projection" is the first step toward understanding the complex internal geometry of **Large Language Models**.

4. Python: Visualizing the Subspace

# Proof that residuals are orthogonal to X
residuals = y - model.predict(X)
correlation = np.dot(X.flatten(), residuals.flatten())

print(f"Dot Product (Close to zero): {correlation:.10f}")
# In a perfect geometric world, this is exactly 0!
