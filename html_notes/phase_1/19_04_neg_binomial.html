Prob 1.4 The Extension

The Negative Binomial Distribution

Waiting not just for one, but for \(r\) successes.

Generalizing the Wait

The Geometric distribution is "Trials until 1st success".
The Negative Binomial distribution is "Trials until \(r\)-th success".

Alternatively (and more commonly in data science), it is defined as the number of failures before \(r\) successes.

1. The Formula (Failures Definition)

Let \(k\) be the number of failures. Let \(r\) be the required successes. $$ P(X=k) = \binom{k+r-1}{k} (1-p)^k p^r $$ Why? The last trial must be a success. So we need to arrange \(r-1\) successes and \(k\) failures in the first \(k+r-1\) trials.

2. ML Connection: Overdispersion

NLP Count Data

Better than Poisson?

We often use Poisson Regression for count data (e.g., number of clicks per day). However, Poisson assumes Mean = Variance.

Real-world data is often "bursty" or clumpy, meaning the variance is much higher than the mean (Overdispersion).

Negative Binomial has an extra parameter that allows the variance to exceed the mean: $$ \text{Var}(X) = \mu + \frac{\mu^2}{r} $$ This makes it the standard choice for modeling:

  • Word counts in text corpus (some words appear in bursty clusters).
  • RNA-seq read counts in bioinformatics.
  • Traffic accidents.

3. Relation to Geometric

Just as Binomial is a sum of Bernoullis, Negative Binomial is a sum of \(r\) independent Geometric variables.
You wait for the 1st success (Geo 1), reset, wait for the 2nd (Geo 2)... until the r-th.
