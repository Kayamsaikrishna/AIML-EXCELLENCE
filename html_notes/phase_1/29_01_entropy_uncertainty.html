01. Entropy & Uncertainty

Measuring the pure information content of a signal.

1. What is Entropy?

Shannon Entropy ($H$) is a measure of the average level of "Surprise" or "Uncertainty" in a random variable. If an event is 100% predictable, its entropy is 0. If it's a random coin toss, its entropy is high.

$$ H(X) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i) $$

2. Bits as Information

Entropy tells you the minimum number of Bits required to transmit a signal. If a message is repetitive, we can compress it because its entropy is low. AI is essentially a compression engine that tries to find the lowest possible entropy representation of data.

3. AI/ML Connection: Decision Trees and Loss Energy

Reducing Impurity

In AI, Entropy is the fundamental metric for Decision Tree Learning (ID3, C4.5)**.

When an XGBoost model splits a node, it chooses the feature that results in the largest Information Gain (reduction in Entropy). It "Squeezes out" uncertainty until the clusters are pure. Furthermore, in Deep Learning**, Entropy is related to the "Certainty" of a model's prediction. If the Softmax output is $[0.5, 0.5]$, the entropy is maximized—the model doesn't know what it's doing. By minimizing entropy during training, we force the model to commit to a specific label. It is the mathematical bridge between Uncertainty and **Deep Learning Certainty**.

4. Python: Calculating Shannon Entropy

from scipy.stats import entropy

# p(x) for three categories
p = [0.1, 0.2, 0.7]

# Calculate entropy in bits (base 2)
h = entropy(p, base=2)

print(f"Entropy: {h:.4f} bits")
# If p=[0, 0, 1], entropy will be 0.0 (Zero surprise!)
