Properties of KL Divergence

Rules of the Manifold. The mathematical properties that distinguish KL from a simple distance metric and make it a sovereign tool for probability theory.

The Non-Metric Distance

In our 3D world, the distance from A to B is the same as B to A. In the world of probability, this is not true. Measuring the distance from a "Real" source to a "Model" is different from measuring the distance from a "Model" to a "Real" source.

1. Fundamental Axioms

1. Gibbs' Inequality

$$ D_{KL}(P \| Q) \ge 0 $$

Divergence is always non-negative. This is the "Thermodynamic Law" of information: an approximation always adds bits; it never subtracts them.

2. The Identity Principle

$$ D_{KL}(P \| Q) = 0 \iff P = Q $$

The only way to reach zero divergence is to be identical. This is why minimizing KL is a valid way to train models to match data.

3. Asymmetry

$$ D_{KL}(P \| Q) \neq D_{KL}(Q \| P) $$

This is why we must be extremely careful which variable is the "Reference" (\(P\)) and which is the "Approximation" (\(Q\)).

2. Advanced Mathematical Traits

Convexity

KL divergence is a convex function of the pair \((P, Q)\). This is mathematically vital because it guarantees that a global minimum exists when we optimize distributions.

Additivity

If sources are independent, the total KL divergence is simply the sum of individual divergences. This allows us to scale information theory to multiple independent features.

$$ D_{KL}(P_x P_y \| Q_x Q_y) = D_{KL}(P_x \| Q_x) + D_{KL}(P_y \| Q_y) $$
Deep Learning Fisher Information

Why Symmetry Fails Deep Learning

In standard SGD, we step in "Euclidean" weight space. But the relationship between weights and probabilities is highly non-linear.

  • Natural Gradient Descent: Instead of taking a fixed step in weight space, we take a fixed step in KL Divergence space. This ensures that the model's actual *behavior* (its output distribution) changes at a constant rate, regardless of how complex the weights are.
  • Fisher Information Matrix: The "Hessian" of the KL Divergence is the Fisher Information Matrix. It measures the curvature of the probability space and tells us which weights are "sensitive" and which are "robust."
