ML Calc 1.9 Geometry

Loss Convexity in ML

Why Linear Models are safe and Neural Nets are dangerous.

Convex = Solvable

A problem is convex if the line segment between any two points on the loss surface lies above the curve. This implies a single global minimum. Linear Regression and SVMs are convex optimization problems. You simply cannot get "stuck".

Neural Networks are Non-Convex. They have symmetries (permuting neurons) and complex interactions, creating a landscape full of hills, valleys, and saddle points.

2. ML Connection: Mode Connectivity

Finding Valleys

Recent research shows that while Neural Nets are non-convex globally, good solutions are often connected by "low-loss valleys".

This means if you find two different good models (Model A and Model B), you can often find a path between them where the error never goes up. This is the geometric basis for Ensembling and Weight Averaging (SWA).

3. Python: Ensembling

swa_demo.py
# Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)
# If we average the weights of a model at different steps of SGD,
# we often end up in a flatter, more robust minimum.

import torch

def swa_update(swa_model, model, alpha):
    for swa_param, param in zip(swa_model.parameters(), model.parameters()):
        swa_param.data = swa_param.data * (1.0 - alpha) + param.data * alpha

# This simple arithmetic average of weights exploits the 
# local convexity of the solution space.
