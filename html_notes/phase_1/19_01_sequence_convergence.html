01. Sequences and Convergence

Predicting the end of an infinite list.

1. What is a Sequence?

A sequence \(\{a_n\}\) is an ordered list of numbers followed by a rule. We are primarily interested in the Limit as \(n \to \infty\).

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = L $$

If the limit exists, the sequence is Convergent. If not, it is Divergent.

2. Monotonic & Bounded

  • Monotonic: The sequence only goes in one direction (up or down).
  • Bounded: There is a "ceiling" or "floor" that the sequence never crosses.
  • Monotonic Convergence Theorem: Every bounded monotonic sequence is convergent.

3. AI/ML Connection: Optimization Trajectories

Will the AI stop learning?

When training an AI, the sequence of weights \(w_1, w_2, w_3, \dots\) is a sequence. We need to prove that this sequence "Converges" to a fixed point (the optimal weight).

If the sequence of Loss values \(L_n\) is Monotonic Decreasing (meaning the error always goes down or stays same) and Bounded (Loss can't be less than zero), then the Monotonic Convergence Theorem guarantees that the AI will eventually reach a stable state. Understanding the properties of sequences prevents us from wasting time on AI models that will oscillate forever and never finish training.

4. Python: Simulating Sequence Limits

# a_n = (n + 1) / n
# As n->inf, a_n -> 1

n_values = [10, 100, 1000, 10000, 100000]
sequence = [(n + 1) / n for n in n_values]

for n, val in zip(n_values, sequence):
    print(f"n={n:6}: {val:.6f}")

# The values get closer to 1.0
