24. Higher Dimensional Spaces (\(\mathbb{R}^n\))

Where intuition goes to die.

1. The Curse of Dimensionality

As dimensions increase, the "volume" of the space grows exponentially. This has counter-intuitive effects on data distributions.

Strange Phenomena

  • Everything is an Outlier: In 1000D, almost all data points lie on the "shell" of a sphere, not the center.
  • Orthogonality is the Norm: If you pick two random vectors in high dimensions, they are almost definitely orthogonal (90°).
  • Sparsity: Data becomes so spread out that the concept of a "neighbor" loses its meaning.

2. Why AI Needs High Dimensions

Linear Separation Power

The Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma tells us that high-dimensional data can be projected into lower dimensions while preserving distances. However, the power of models comes from lifting data into higher dimensions where complex patterns become linear.

  • Llama-3: Uses 4096-dimensional vectors for its hidden layers.
  • Image Feature Maps: A CNN output might have 512 channels, each representing a unique dimension of visual feature.

3. Python: Orthogonality in High Dim

import numpy as np

def random_angle(dim):
    u = np.random.randn(dim)
    v = np.random.randn(dim)
    cos_theta = np.dot(u, v) / (np.linalg.norm(u) * np.linalg.norm(v))
    return np.degrees(np.arccos(np.clip(cos_theta, -1, 1)))

print(f"Angle in 2D: {random_angle(2):.2f}°")
print(f"Angle in 1000D: {random_angle(1000):.2f}°") # Usually 89-91°
