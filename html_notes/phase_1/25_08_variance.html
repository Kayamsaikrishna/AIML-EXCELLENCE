08. Variance

Measuring the spread of uncertainty.

1. Definition

The Variance $Var(X)$, often denoted by $\sigma^2$, measures how much the values of a random variable differ from its expected value (mean). It is defined as the expected value of the squared deviation from the mean:

$$ Var(X) = E[(X - E[X])^2] $$

A more practical formula for calculation is:

$$ Var(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 $$

2. Key Properties

  • Non-negativity: $Var(X) \ge 0$.
  • Scaling: $Var(aX + b) = a^2 Var(X)$. (Notice how the shift $b$ disappears and the scale $a$ is squared).
  • Zero Variance: $Var(X) = 0$ if and only if $X$ is a constant.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Bias-Variance Tradeoff

Instability in Learning

Variance is the mathematical reason your AI might perform well on training data but fail in the real world.

In the **Bias-Variance Tradeoff**, "High Variance" means the model is inconsistent; small changes in the training data lead to wildy different predictions (Overfitting). In **Deep Learning**, we use techniques like Weight Decay (L2 Regularization) to "squeeze" the variance of the model's weights, making its predictions more stable. Furthermore, the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function, used in almost all regression tasks, is literally the definition of variance for the model's error. Understanding variance allows you to diagnose whether your AI is "Dumb" (High Bias) or "Eratic" (High Variance).

4. Python: Calculating Variance

data = np.array([10, 12, 23, 23, 16, 23, 21, 16])

# Manual calc: Average(x^2) - (Average(x))^2
var_manual = np.mean(data**2) - np.mean(data)**2

# Numpy calc
var_numpy = np.var(data)

print(f"Variance: {var_numpy:.4f}")
