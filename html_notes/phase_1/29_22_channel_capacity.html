Channel Capacity

The Speed Limit of Information. Shannon's Second Theorem defines the ultimate ceiling on how fast we can transmit data error-free through a noisy world.

The Miraculous Discovery

Before Shannon, engineers thought that if you wanted to reduce errors in a noisy channel, you had to slow down to a crawl. Shannon proved them wrong: you can transmit data at a **fixed rate** (the capacity) and make the error rate approach zero, provided you use a clever enough code.

1. The Formal Definition

Channel Capacity (\(C\)) is the maximum Mutual Information between the input \(X\) and the output \(Y\), optimized over all possible input distributions \(P(X)\).

$$ C = \max_{P(X)} I(X; Y) $$

Intuition

Mutual Information measures how much \(Y\) tells us about \(X\). Capacity is the "best case scenario" where we choose an input style that survives the channel noise most effectively.

Units

Usually measured in bits per channel use or bits per second. It represents the "throughput" of the information pipe.

2. The Shannon-Hartley Theorem

For a continuous channel with Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN), the capacity is determined by bandwidth and the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).

$$ C = B \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{S}{N} \right) $$
  • B: Bandwidth in Hertz.
  • S/N: Signal power over Noise power.

This formula is the reason why your 5G is faster than 4G (more bandwidth) and why your WiFi is slower in the kitchen (more noise).

Deep Learning Distributed AI

Capacity in Machine Learning

Information capacity isn't just for radios; it defines the limits of neural architectures:

  • Dropout as a Noisy Channel: We can view a neural layer as a channel. Dropout adds noise. The network must learn to encode information robustly so that it can be decoded even when parts of the signal are missing.
  • Information Bottleneck: The capacity of a latent layer determines how much information from the input can be "remembered" for the output. Too much capacity leads to overfitting; too little leads to underfitting.
  • Communication in Distributed ML: In Large Language Model training (e.g., across thousands of GPUs), the "Channel Capacity" of the network interconnect is the primary bottleneck for scaling.
