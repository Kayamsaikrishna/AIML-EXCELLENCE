Stats 1.5 Ranks

Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test

Testing paired differences without assuming that the data follows a Normal distribution.

The Distribution-Free Alternative

The Paired T-test requires the differences to be Normally distributed. What if they aren't? What if your data has massive outliers or is highly skewed?

Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test ignore the raw values and looks at their Ranks.

1. The Logic

1. Calculate differences (After - Before). 2. Ignore the signs and Rank the differences by magnitude. 3. Sum the ranks for the positive differences (\(W^+\)) and negative differences (\(W^-\)). 4. If the Null is true, \(W^+\) and \(W^-\) should be roughly equal.

2. When to use it?

  • Non-Normal Data: Your "Differences" histograms looks like a mess.
  • Ordinal Data: Your ratings aren't numbers (e.g., "Good", "Better", "Best").
  • Small Samples with Outliers: One crazy data point can ruin a T-test; it only shifts one rank in Wilcoxon.

3. ML Connection: Ranking Models

Search/Recommend Robustness

Comparing Rank-Based Systems

In Search Engine optimization, we often use NDCG (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain). This metric is notoriously non-normal.

If you want to prove Model A is better than Model B across 20 search queries: - Why T-test fails: With only 20 queries, one "easy" query where both models score 1.0 would skew the mean and variance. - Why Wilcoxon wins: It only cares if Model A was *relatively* better than Model B for each query.

Wilcoxon is the "Gold Standard" for comparing algorithms in ML research papers when the metric doesn't follow a clean bell curve.
