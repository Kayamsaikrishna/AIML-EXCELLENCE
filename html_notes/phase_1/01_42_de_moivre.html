42. De Moivre's Theorem

Scaling complexity through powers and roots.

1. The Scaling Power

De Moivre's Theorem simplifies raising a complex number to an integer power \(n\):

$$ [r(\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)]^n = r^n (\cos n\theta + i \sin n\theta) $$

Intuition: Squaring a vector in the complex plane doubles its angle and squares its length.

2. Roots & Fractal Geometry

The Infinite Detail of Repetitive Rules

Generating the \(n\)-th roots of unity (\(z^n = 1\)) creates point sets perfectly spaced on a circle. In Generative AI, these recursive rules are used to create "Fractal" textures and geometric patterns that never lose detail when zoomed.

  • Stability Analysis: In Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), checking if the powers of the weight matrix explode or vanish is analogous to checking if De Moivre's \(r^n\) goes to \(\infty\) or \(0\).
  • Cyclic Encoding: High-dimensional circular embeddings rely on these roots to ensure tokens are spaced with maximum entropy.

3. Python: Finding the roots

import numpy as np

# Find the 5th roots of unity
n = 5
roots = []

for k in range(n):
    theta = 2 * np.pi * k / n
    z = complex(np.cos(theta), np.sin(theta))
    roots.append(z)

for i, r in enumerate(roots):
    print(f"Root {i}: {r:.2f}")

# All roots are spaced 72 degrees (360/5) apart!
