1. Powers are Scaled Angles

$$ (\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)^n = \cos(n\theta) + i \sin(n\theta) $$

Raising a complex number to the power \( n \) keeps the magnitude the same (for unit vectors) but multiplies the angle by \( n \). This allows us to jump around the unit circle instantly.

2. The Cooley-Tukey Algorithm

To compute the Fourier Transform quickly, we need to evaluate a polynomial at the "N-th Roots of Unity" (points equally spaced on the circle).

$$ \omega_{N}^k = e^{-i \frac{2\pi k}{N}} $$

De Moivre's theorem tells us these roots have a symmetric structure: \( \omega^2_{2N} = \omega_N \). This allows us to break a size \( N \) problem into two size \( N/2 \) problems recursively.

3. Visualize Roots of Unity

Plotting the points used in an 8-point FFT.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

N = 8
k = np.arange(N)

# N-th Roots of Unity
roots = np.exp(2j * np.pi * k / N)

print(f"Angle Step: {360/N} degrees")
for i, root in enumerate(roots):
    print(f"Root {i}: {root:.2f}")

# These 8 points are where we sample the signal frequency.
