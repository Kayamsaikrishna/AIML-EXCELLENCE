14. Seasonal ARIMA (SARIMA)

Handling the cycles of the calendar.

1. The Seasonal Extension

Standard ARIMA is great for trends, but it struggles with patterns that repeat every week or every year (e.g., higher sales on Saturdays). SARIMA adds seasonal counterparts to the $(p, d, q)$ parameters:

$$ \text{SARIMA}(p, d, q) \times (P, D, Q)_s $$

where $s$ is the number of time steps in a season (e.g., $s=12$ for monthly data with annual seasonality).

2. Why this matters

If you don't use SARIMA, your residuals will show a strong correlation every $s$ steps. The model will "Miss" the fact that it's currently December and instead just guess based on November. SARIMA explicitly checks: "What happened last December?"

3. AI/ML Connection: Demand Forecasting and Multi-scale Patterns

Temporal Attention across Scales

In AI, SARIMA is the statistical equivalent of Dilated Convolutions** or **Multi-scale Temporal Attention**.

When you build an AI for **Inventory Management** (like those used by Walmart or Amazon), the model must understand three scales: Today vs Ahead, This Week vs Next, and This Season vs Last. SARIMA's $(P, D, Q)_s$ logic provides this "Long-term Memory." In TFT (Temporal Fusion Transformers)**, we use "Static Metadata" (like 'Day of the week') to explicitly help the AI learn these seasonal gates. SARIMA remains the most robust way to handle **Cold-Start problems**: if you have a new store with only 1 year of data, SARIMA can reliably guess the holiday peak better than a data-hungry LSTM. It is the "Smart Baseline" for all modern retail AI.

4. Python: SARIMA Modelling

# Monthly data (s=12)
model = sm.tsa.statespace.SARIMAX(data, 
                                 order=(1, 1, 1), 
                                 seasonal_order=(1, 1, 1, 12))
results = model.fit()

# Forecast the next 24 months
forecast = results.get_forecast(steps=24)
