11. Properties of Maximum Likelihood Estimators

Why MLE is the gold standard of inference.

1. The Big Three

Under general regularity conditions, MLE estimators possess three exceptional mathematical properties:

  • Consistency: As $n \to \infty$, $\hat{\theta}_{MLE}$ hits the truth $\theta$ with probability 1.
  • Invariance: If $\hat{\theta}$ is the MLE of $\theta$, then $g(\hat{\theta})$ is the MLE of $g(\theta)$. (e.g., if you estimate $\sigma$, you've also estimated $\sigma^2$ optimally).
  • Asymptotic Normality: For large $n$, the distribution of $\hat{\theta}_{MLE}$ becomes Gaussian, centered at the truth.

2. Asymptotic Efficiency

MLE is an Asymptotically Efficient estimator. This means that as you get more data, no other unbiased estimator can have a smaller variance than the MLE. It reaches the **Cramér-Rao Lower Bound**, making it the "tightest" possible guess allowed by the laws of mathematics.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Stability of Neural Learning

Mathematical Robustness

MLE properties are the reason AI models are Predictable at scale.

Because of Asymptotic Normality, we know that the "Error" in a trained model's weights will follow a Bell Curve. This allows AI engineers to calculate **Confidence Intervals** for a model's coefficients. The Invariance Property is used every time we apply a non-linear activation (like Softmax) to a model's output—we are guaranteed that if the raw scores (logits) were MLE-optimal, the resulting probabilities are also MLE-optimal. This mathematical "handshake" between different layers of an AI ensures that the training signal isn't lost or distorted as it propagates through complex mathematical transforms.

4. Python: Observing Gaussian Error

# Repeatedly estimate 'p' for a Bernoulli trial with n=100
true_p = 0.5
estimates = []

for _ in range(1000):
    sample = np.random.binomial(1, true_p, 100)
    estimates.append(np.mean(sample)) # MLE

# Plotting the sampling distribution of the MLE
plt.hist(estimates, bins=20, density=True)
plt.title("Asymptotic Normality of MLE")
plt.show() # Will look like a Normal distribution centered at 0.5
