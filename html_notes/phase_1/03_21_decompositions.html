21. Matrix Decompositions (Factorization)

Finding the prime factors of a matrix.

1. Why Decompose?

Just as \( 12 = 3 \times 4 \) tells us about the properties of 12, factoring a matrix into a product of simpler matrices (Symmetric, Orthogonal, Triangular) makes complex problems trivial to solve.

The Three Main Goals

  • Computational Speed: Solving \( \mathbf{Ax} = \mathbf{b} \) is \( O(n^3) \). Using LU or Cholesky makes it much faster for large batches.
  • Numerical Stability: Directly calculating \( \mathbf{A}^{-1} \) introduces huge rounding errors. Decompositions avoid this.
  • Interpretability: SVD or Eigendecomposition tells us the "principal directions" of data variance (PCA).

2. The AI Toolkit Overview

Decomposition Factors Typical AI Use Case
LU L (Lower), U (Upper) Fast inference and system solving.
QR Q (Orthogonal), R (Triangular) Least squares and stable regression.
Cholesky L · LT Sampling from Gaussian distributions.
SVD U · Σ · VT Rank reduction (LoRA), PCA, Compression.

3. Python: Native Scipy Methods

import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import lu, qr, cholesky

A = np.array([[4, 12, -16], [12, 37, -43], [-16, -43, 98]])

# LU
P, L, U = lu(A)

# QR
Q, R = qr(A)

# Cholesky (Requires positive definite symmetric)
L_chol = cholesky(A, lower=True)

print("LU and QR are the workhorses of numerical AI.")
