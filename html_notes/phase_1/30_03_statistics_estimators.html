03. Statistics & Estimators

Converting raw data into meaningful intelligence.

1. Definitions

  • Statistic: Any function of the sample data that does not depend on unknown parameters. (e.g., $\bar{x}$ is a statistic).
  • Estimator ($\hat{\theta}$): A specific rule or formula used to guess the value of a population parameter $\theta$.
  • Estimate: The actual numerical value produced by the estimator for a specific sample.

2. The "Recipe" Metaphor

An **Estimator** is like a recipe (e.g., "Add all numbers and divide by count"). The **Statistic** is the resulting dish. We want our "Recipe" to be robust, so that no matter what fresh ingredients (data) we get, the resulting dish is always close to the "Master Chef's" original intent (the Population Parameter).

3. AI/ML Connection: The Model as an Estimator

Guessing the Global Truth

In Machine Learning, a Model is essentially a collection of Nested Estimators.

When you call `model.fit(data)`, you are asking the computer to find an **Estimator** for the relationship between inputs ($X$) and outputs ($y$). The **Weights** of your model are estimates of the true, hidden weights of the universe. In **Supervised Learning**, we use the **Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE)** to find the parameters that make our training data most likely. In **Bayesian Deep Learning**, we use the **MAP (Maximum A Posteriori)** estimator to combine our data with prior knowledge. Understanding that your AI's weights are just "Samples" of a larger truth helps you realize that every time you refresh your data, your weights should change slightly—this is not a bug, but the nature of estimation.

4. Python: Estimator vs. Parameter

# Population Parameter (The Truth)
true_std = 15.0

# Estimator formula: sqrt( sum( (x - avg)^2 ) / (n-1) )
def unbiased_std_estimator(data):
    return np.std(data, ddof=1)

# Take a sample and generate an 'Estimate'
sample = np.random.normal(170, true_std, 50)
estimate = unbiased_std_estimator(sample)

print(f"True Parameter: {true_std}")
print(f"Our Estimate: {estimate:.2f}")
