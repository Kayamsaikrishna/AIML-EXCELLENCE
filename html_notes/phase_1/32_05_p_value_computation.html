05. p-value: Definition and Computation

The probability of "Winning" by pure luck.

1. What a p-value IS

The p-value is the probability of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the one observed, **assuming the Null Hypothesis ($H_0$) is true**.

It is a measurement of **Surprise**:

  • Low p-value ($< 0.05$): "This result is too weird to be luck. $H_0$ is likely wrong."
  • High p-value ($\ge 0.05$): "This result could easily have happened by chance. Keep $H_0$."

2. What a p-value IS NOT

1. It is **NOT** the probability that $H_0$ is true.
2. It is **NOT** the probability that the finding is "Important" (Effect size matters more).
3. It **IS** a measure of how incompatible the data is with the model of "No effect."

3. AI/ML Connection: Significance and Model Quality

Filtering out Flukes

In AI, p-values are used for Experiment Validation and Pruning.

If you run a Grid Search** for hyperparameters and find that Configuration A is 1% better than Configuration B, you shouldn't just pick A. You calculate the p-value of that 1% difference. If $p=0.45$, it means there is a 45% chance that B is actually better and you just got a "Lucky" shuffle of the validation set. In **Feature Engineering**, p-values are used to prune inputs. If a feature has a p-value of 0.8 against the output label, it's essentially garbage—it has no statistically significant relationship with what you're trying to predict. Removing it reduces the search space and prevents **Overfitting to Noise**.

4. Python: Calculating p from Z

# Observed Z-score: 2.1
z_obs = 2.1

# Probability of seeing Z > 2.1 or Z < -2.1
p_val = 2 * (1 - stats.norm.cdf(abs(z_obs)))

print(f"p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
# p = 0.0357. Since p < 0.05, we reject the null!
