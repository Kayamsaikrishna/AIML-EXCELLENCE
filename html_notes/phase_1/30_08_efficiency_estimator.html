08. Efficiency

Extracting the maximum information from every data point.

1. Definition

The Efficiency of an unbiased estimator is its variance compared to other possible unbiased estimators. An estimator $\hat{\theta}_1$ is more efficient than $\hat{\theta}_2$ if $Var(\hat{\theta}_1) < Var(\hat{\theta}_2)$.

Relative Efficiency is defined as:

$$ e(\hat{\theta}_1, \hat{\theta}_2) = \frac{Var(\hat{\theta}_2)}{Var(\hat{\theta}_1)} $$

2. The "Waste Management" of Data

An inefficient estimator "wastes" data. If Method A needs 1,000 samples to reach a certain precision, but Method B only needs 500 samples to reach that same precision, then Method B is twice as efficient. Highly efficient estimators allow us to build reliable AI with smaller, more curated datasets rather than "brute-forcing" with trillions of rows.

3. AI/ML Connection: Sample Efficiency and Architecture

Doing More with Less

In AI research, Sample Efficiency is one of the most important metrics for comparison.

In **Reinforcement Learning**, we compare algorithms (like PPO vs. SAC) based on how many "Environment Steps" they need to learn a task. An efficient RL agent learns to fly a drone in 2 hours, while an inefficient one takes 2 weeks. In **Active Learning**, we choose which data points to label to maximize the "Efficiency" of the human expert's time. Theoretically, the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (which we will study in Series 30.12) tells us the "Absolute Maximum Efficiency" possible for any unbiased estimator. Models that hit this bound are called MVU (Minimum Variance Unbiased) estimators. Researchers use this benchmark to prove that their new AI architecture is "Optimal" and cannot be mathematically improved further given the same amount of data.

4. Python: Mean vs. Median Efficiency

# For a Normal distribution, both Mean and Median are unbiased estimators of mu.
# Which is more efficient?

means = []
medians = []

for _ in range(1000):
    sample = np.random.normal(0, 1, 100)
    means.append(np.mean(sample))
    medians.append(np.median(sample))

print(f"Variance of Mean: {np.var(means):.6f}")
print(f"Variance of Median: {np.var(medians):.6f}")
# The Mean is significantly more efficient for Normal data!
