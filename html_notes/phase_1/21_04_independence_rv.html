Prob 1.4 The Divorce

Independence of Random Variables

When knowledge of one variable provides zero information about another.

The Vanishing Condition

Two variables are independent if their Joint Distribution is simply the product of their Marginal Distributions.

"Knowing \(X\) doesn't change my belief about \(Y\)." Mathematically, the conditional distribution is just the marginal.

1. Definition

RVs \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent if and only if: $$ f(x, y) = f_X(x) f_Y(y) $$ Equivalent to: $$ f_{X|Y}(x|y) = f_X(x) $$

2. Independent and Identically Distributed (I.I.D.)

The Foundation of Statistics

Most ML algorithms start with the I.I.D. Assumption: We assume our training data points are sampled independently from the same underlying distribution.

If your samples are Independent, the likelihood of the whole dataset is just the product of the likelihoods of each individual point. This simplifies the math for Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) tremendously: $$ P(\mathcal{D}) = \prod_{i=1}^n P(x_i) $$

3. ML Connection: Naive Bayes

Classic ML Supervised

The "Naive" Assumption

In classification, we want to compute \(P(Y \mid X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n)\).
The Naive Bayes classifier makes a massive simplification: It assumes all features \(X_i\) are conditionally independent given the class label \(Y\).

Instead of trying to learn a complex joint relationship between all features, we just learn the relationship between each individual feature and the class: $$ P(X_1, \dots, X_n \mid Y) \approx \prod_{i=1}^n P(X_i \mid Y) $$ Even though this assumption is almost always wrong (features are usually correlated), Naive Bayes works surprisingly well for tasks like Spam filtering.

4. Independence vs. Uncorrelated

Warning: Independence implies zero correlation, but **Zero correlation does NOT necessarily imply independence.**

Correlation only measures linear relationships. Two variables could be perfectly related (e.g., \(Y = X^2\)) but have zero correlation if the relationship is symmetric. However, they are definitely not independent!
