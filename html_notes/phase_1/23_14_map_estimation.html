Stats 1.4 Bayesian Bridge

Maximum A Posteriori (MAP)

Combining the evidence of the data with our prior knowledge of the world.

The Bayesian Upgrade

MLE asks: "What \(\theta\) makes the data most likely?"
MAP asks: "What \(\theta\) is most likely, given the data AND what I believed before seeing the data?"

1. The Formula

Using Bayes' Rule: $$ P(\theta \mid \text{Data}) \propto P(\text{Data} \mid \theta) P(\theta) $$ $$ \hat{\theta}_{MAP} = \text{arg max}_{\theta} \left[ \ln L(\text{Data} \mid \theta) + \ln P(\theta) \right] $$

MAP is just MLE plus an extra term based on your Prior distribution.

2. Uniform Priors

If you use a Flat (Uniform) Prior (meaning you think all values of \(\theta\) are equally likely), then MAP gives the exact same result as MLE. MLE is essentially MAP with a "don't care" attitude about the prior.

3. ML Connection: L2 Regularization

Deep Learning Regularization

Weight Decay is MAP

When we add an L2 penalty term \(\lambda ||w||^2\) to our loss function, we are performing MAP estimation with a Gaussian Prior centered at zero.

Our "Prior" is the belief that small weights are more likely than large weights. - The Likelihood term (\(L\)) wants to fit the data perfectly. - The Prior term (\(P\)) wants to keep weights small. - The model settles on the Posterior Mode (The MAP estimate) which balances these two forces.

Similarly, L1 Regularization (Lasso) is MAP estimation with a Laplace Prior.
