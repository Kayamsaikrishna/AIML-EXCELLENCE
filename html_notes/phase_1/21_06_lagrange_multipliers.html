06. Lagrange Multipliers

Where gradients touch, the optimum is found.

1. The Geometric Intuition

Suppose you want to maximize \(f(x, y)\) subject to \(g(x, y) = k\). As you move along the constraint curve \(g=k\), you cross level curves of \(f\). The optimum value occurs at the tangency point—where the level curve of \(f\) and the constraint curve \(g=k\) have the same tangent line.

This means their Gradient Vectors must be parallel!

$$ \nabla f(x, y) = \lambda \nabla g(x, y) $$

The variable \(\lambda\) is called the Lagrange Multiplier.

2. The Lagrangian Function

We can combine the objective and the constraint into a single function, the Lagrangian (\(\mathcal{L}\)):

$$ \mathcal{L}(x, y, \lambda) = f(x, y) - \lambda (g(x, y) - k) $$

Finding the optimum of the constrained problem is equivalent to finding the critical points of \(\mathcal{L}\) (where \(\nabla \mathcal{L} = 0\)).

3. AI/ML Connection: Dual Problems and SVMs

The Support Vector Machine (SVM)

One of the most famous applications of Lagrange Multipliers in AI is the Support Vector Machine. We want to maximize the "Margin" (the distance between two classes of data) subject to the constraint that no data points are inside the margin.

By using the Lagrangian Dual, we transform a hard problem about "Weights" into an easier problem about "Multipliers" (\(\alpha\)). The data points where the Lagrange multipliers are non-zero are the Support Vectors. They are the only points that actually define the model's boundary. Lagrange multipliers allow AI to ignore 99% of the noisy data and focus only on the critical "Border" examples.

4. Python: Solving with SymPy

import sympy as sp

x, y, L = sp.symbols('x y L')
f = x * y # Objective
g = x**2 + y**2 - 8 # Constraint x^2 + y^2 = 8

# Lagrangian: f - L*g
Lag = f - L*g

# System of equations
grad_L = [sp.diff(Lag, var) for var in (x, y, L)]
sol = sp.solve(grad_L, (x, y, L))

print(f"Solutions: {sol}")
# Points (2, 2) and (-2, -2) yield the max value 4.
