08. Triple Integrals in Spherical Coordinates

Integrating across orbits, planets, and atoms.

1. The Transformation

Spherical coordinates use a distance from the origin (\(\rho\)), an angle in the xy-plane (\(\theta\)), and an angle from the positive z-axis (\(\phi\)).

$$ x = \rho \sin \phi \cos \theta, \quad y = \rho \sin \phi \sin \theta, \quad z = \rho \cos \phi $$ $$ dV = \mathbf{\rho^2 \sin \phi} \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta $$

The "stretching factor" \(\rho^2 \sin \phi\) is crucial because volume expands much faster in 3D spheres than in 2D circles.

2. Applications

Use spherical coordinates whenever your region is a ball, a cone, or a sphere. It is the language of gravity, electromagnetism, and light radiation.

3. AI/ML Connection: High-Dimensional Geometry

The Concentration of Measure

In High-Dimensional AI, we often deal with "Normalized" vectors (embeddings). These live on the surface of a multi-dimensional sphere. One of the strangest facts in AI is that in high dimensions (like 1000-D), almost all the volume of a sphere is located in a thin shell near the surface.

By calculating spherical integrals in higher dimensions, AI researchers discovered the "Bizarre Geometry" of high-dimensional space. This explains why random vectors in AI are almost always perpendicular to each other. Understanding spherical integration allows you to build models that respect the natural geometry of global data, like Weather Forecasting AI or Satellite Image Analysis.

4. Python: Volume of a Unit Ball

# Integral of rho^2 * sin(phi)
# rho: 0->1, phi: 0->pi, theta: 0->2pi
# Result: 4/3 * pi

import sympy as sp
rho, phi, theta = sp.symbols('rho phi theta')

# Innermost integral: rho^2/3 * sin(phi)
# Middle: [-cos(phi)] from 0 to pi = 2
# Outer: 2 * 2pi / 3 = 4pi/3
integrand = rho**2 * sp.sin(phi)
volume = sp.integrate(sp.integrate(sp.integrate(integrand, (rho, 0, 1)), (phi, 0, sp.pi)), (theta, 0, 2*sp.pi))

print(f"Volume of Unit Ball: {volume}")
