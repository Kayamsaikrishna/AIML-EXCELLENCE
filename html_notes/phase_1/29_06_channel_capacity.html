06. Channel Capacity & Information Rate

The speed limit of information.

1. The Shannon Limit

Channel Capacity ($C$) is the maximum rate at which information can be transmitted over a communication channel with an arbitrarily low error probability. Even in a noisy environment, Shannon proved there is always a way to communicate perfectly, provided you don't exceed the capacity limit.

$$ C = B \log_2(1 + \frac{S}{N}) $$

where $B$ is bandwidth, $S$ is signal power, and $N$ is noise power.

2. Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)

If the noise ($N$) is too high, the capacity drops. To maintain the same information rate, you must increase the power of the signal or use more clever error-correcting codes.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Capacity and Learning Rates

The Information Throughput

In AI, Channel Capacity is analogous to Model Capacity (VC Dimension)**.

A smaller model (e.g., MobileNet) has a lower "Channel Capacity" than a giant model (e.g., ResNet-152). It can only "Transmit" (learn) so much complexity from the training data. If your dataset has high variance (Noise), a low-capacity model will fail to capture the signal. In Distributed AI Training**, we use Shannon's theory to optimize how much "Gradient Information" we send over the network. By using Quantization (e.g., 8-bit gradients)**, we reduce the bandwidth required, effectively trying to push the signal right up against the Channel Capacity to speed up training across thousands of GPUs. It is the math of Communication Efficiency.

4. Mathematical Note

If you try to transmit information *above* the capacity $C$, errors are guaranteed**. In AI, if you try to fit a 1,000,000 parameter dataset into a 100 parameter model, you are exceeding the capacity, and your error rate will never reach zero. This is the information-theoretic root of Underfitting.
