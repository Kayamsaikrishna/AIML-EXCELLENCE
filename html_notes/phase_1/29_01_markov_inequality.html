01. Markov's Inequality

Setting the upper bound on probability using only the mean.

1. Definition

Markov's Inequality gives an upper bound on the probability that a non-negative random variable $X$ is greater than or equal to some positive constant $a$. It requires nothing about $X$ except its expected value $E[X]$.

$$ P(X \ge a) \le \frac{E[X]}{a} \quad \text{for } X \ge 0, a > 0 $$

2. Intuition: The Weight of the Tail

If the average income (E[X]) is $50,000, how many people can earn more than $500,000? Markov's Inequality says at most 10% ($50k / 500k$). It's a "Loose" bound, but it's powerful because it works for **any** non-negative distribution, no matter how weird or skewed.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Stability and Safety

Theoretical Guardrails

Markov's Inequality is the "First Responder" for **Theoretical Proofs** in AI.

In **Optimization**, we use Markov's inequality to prove that the "Loss" of a model won't exceed a certain threshold with high probability, ensuring **Algorithm Stability**. In **System Monitoring**, if an AI is controlling a robot arm, we use this bound to guarantee that the "Error Magnitude" (which is non-negative) stays within safe limits. Most importantly, it is the foundation for proving more complex theorems like Chebyshev's Inequality and the Law of Large Numbers. While an AI engineer might rarely calculate it by hand, the very fact that your model "converges" is mathematically guaranteed by the existence of these bounds.

4. Python: Verifying the Bound

import numpy as np

# Sample from an Exponential Distribution (X >= 0)
x = np.random.exponential(scale=10, size=100000)
mean_x = np.mean(x)

# Probability X >= 30?
actual_prob = np.mean(x >= 30)
markov_bound = mean_x / 30

print(f"Actual P(X >= 30): {actual_prob:.4f}")
print(f"Markov Upper Bound: {markov_bound:.4f}")
# Markov bound is always higher than or equal to the actual probability.
