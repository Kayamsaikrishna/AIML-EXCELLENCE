10. Methods to find Inverse

From hand-calculation to high-performance solvers.

1. Method 1: The Adjoint Method (\( 2 \times 2 \) Case)

For a small matrix, the analytical solution is easy to remember:

$$ \mathbf{A}^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc} \begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix} $$

Where \( (ad - bc) \) is the determinant.

2. Method 2: Elementary Row Operations

Also known as the Gauss-Jordan Method. You append the Identity matrix to \( \mathbf{A} \):

$$ [\mathbf{A} | \mathbf{I}] \xrightarrow{\text{Row Ops}} [\mathbf{I} | \mathbf{A}^{-1}] $$

3. Numerical Computing & AI Frameworks

Pseudoinverse (Moore-Penrose)

What if a matrix is not square or is singular? In AI, we use the Pseudoinverse (\( \mathbf{A}^+ \)). It provides the "best-fit" inverse using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).

$$ \mathbf{A}^+ = (\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^T $$
  • Stability: Calculating the exact inverse for large matrices is numerically unstable. We usually solve \( \mathbf{Ax} = \mathbf{b} \) using LU Decomposition or QR instead of multiplying by \( \mathbf{A}^{-1} \).

4. Python: Pseudoinverse

import numpy as np

# A non-square matrix (Cannot have a standard inverse)
A = np.array([[1, 2], [3, 4], [5, 6]])

# Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse
A_pinv = np.linalg.pinv(A)

print(f"Pseudoinverse shape: {A_pinv.shape}") # (2, 3)
print(f"Check: np.allclose(A_pinv @ A, I) -> \n{np.round(A_pinv @ A)}")
