16. Transformation of Random Variables

Warping probability density through mathematical functions.

1. The Problem

If we have a random variable $X$ with a known PDF $f_X(x)$, and we define a new variable $Y = g(X)$, what is the PDF of $Y$? Simply plugging the function in is not enough—the "probability mass" must be squeezed or stretched to maintain the area of 1.

2. The Change of Variables Formula

For a strictly monotonic and differentiable function $g(X)$, the PDF of $Y$ is given by:

$$ f_Y(y) = f_X(g^{-1}(y)) \left| \frac{d}{dy} g^{-1}(y) \right| $$

The term $\left| \frac{d}{dy} g^{-1}(y) \right|$ is the **Jacobian**, which acts as a correction factor for the "stretching" of the probability space.

3. AI/ML Connection: Normalizing Flows

Reshaping Data Distributions

Variable transformation is the fundamental math behind **Normalizing Flows**, a powerful class of generative models.

Instead of trying to learn a complex data distribution (like the pixels of a face) directly, Normalizing Flows start with a simple Gaussian distribution and "warp" it through a series of invertible, differentiable functions. By applying the **Change of Variables Formula** at each step, the model calculates how the probability density changes. This allows the AI to generate high-fidelity data while still being able to calculate the exact log-likelihood of a data point. Furthermore, **Softmax** is a transformation that maps $N$ continuous random variables into a discrete PMF. Understanding these mappings is essential for creating models that "shape" noise into useful features.

4. Python: Transforming a Distribution

# Log-Normal is the result of Y = exp(X), where X is Normal
x = np.random.normal(0, 1, 1000)
y = np.exp(x) # Transformation

print(f"Mean of X: {np.mean(x):.4f}")
print(f"Mean of Y: {np.mean(y):.4f} (Not exp(0)!)") # E[exp(X)] != exp(E[X])
