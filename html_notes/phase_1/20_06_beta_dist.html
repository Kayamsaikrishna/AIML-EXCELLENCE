Prob 1.4 Bounded Uncertainty

The Beta Distribution

Distributions of probabilities: Modeling a parameter that must be between 0 and 1.

Probability of a Probability

We know that a Bernoulli parameter \(p\) must stay in \([0, 1]\). How do we express uncertainty about \(p\)?

"I think the conversion rate is roughly 0.1, but I could be wrong."
We don't use a Normal distribution (which can go to \(\infty\)). We use the Beta Distribution because it is naturally bounded between 0 and 1.

1. The Formula

\(X \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)\) with \(\alpha, \beta > 0\). $$ f(x) = \frac{x^{\alpha-1} (1-x)^{\beta-1}}{B(\alpha, \beta)} $$ where \(B(\alpha, \beta)\) is the Beta Function (normalization constant).

Intuition:

  • Think of \(\alpha-1\) as the number of "Successes" you've seen.
  • Think of \(\beta-1\) as the number of "Failures" you've seen.

2. Shape and Flexible Forms

By changing \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), the Beta distribution can take many shapes:

  • \(\alpha=1, \beta=1\): Uniform Distribution (Flat).
  • \(\alpha > \beta\): Skewed towards 1.
  • \(\alpha < \beta\): Skewed towards 0.
  • \(\alpha, \beta > 1\): Unimodal (Hump in the middle).
  • \(\alpha, \beta < 1\): U-shaped (High at both 0 and 1).

3. ML Connection: Bayesian Updating

Bayesian Inference A/B Testing

The Conjugate Prior for Bernoulli

The Beta distribution is the Conjugate Prior for the Bernoulli and Binomial distributions.

Suppose you start with a "Flat" belief: \(\text{Beta}(1, 1)\).
You perform 10 trials and see 7 Successes and 3 Failures.
Your new belief (Posterior) is simply: \(\text{Beta}(1+7, 1+3) = \text{Beta}(8, 4)\).

Why this matters: You don't need complex calculus to update your model. You just add the new counts to the existing parameters!

Multi-Armed Bandits (Thompson Sampling)

In Reinforcement Learning, Thompson Sampling uses the Beta distribution to model the expected reward of different ads/buttons. We sample from each arm's Beta distribution and pick the one with the highest sample. This naturally handles the Exploration-Exploitation trade-off.

4. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta}\).
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = \frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)}\).
