Prob 1.4 The Atom

The Bernoulli Distribution

The building block of all probability: The Coin Flip.

The Bit of Probability

A Bernoulli random variable is the simplest possible random variable. It has exactly two outcomes: 0 (Failure) or 1 (Success).

In Machine Learning, this is the language of Binary Classification (Cat vs. Dog, Spam vs. Not Spam). Every Logistic Regression model is essentially predicting the parameter \(p\) of a Bernoulli distribution.

1. Definition and PMF

A Random Variable \(X\) follows a Bernoulli distribution with parameter \(p\) (where \(0 \le p \le 1\)): $$ X \sim \text{Bern}(p) $$ The Probability Mass Function (PMF) is: $$ P(X=k) = \begin{cases} p & \text{if } k=1 \\ 1-p & \text{if } k=0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$ Compact form: \(P(X=k) = p^k (1-p)^{1-k}\) for \(k \in \{0,1\}\).

2. Moments (Mean & Variance)

Expectation (Mean)

$$ E[X] = 1 \cdot p + 0 \cdot (1-p) = p $$

The "average" outcome is the probability of success. If \(p=0.8\), the expected value is 0.8 (even though you never see a 0.8, only 0s and 1s).

Variance

$$ \text{Var}(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 $$

Since \(1^2=1\) and \(0^2=0\), \(E[X^2] = p\).

$$ \text{Var}(X) = p - p^2 = p(1-p) $$

Variance Intuition

When is variance highest?
\(\frac{d}{dp}(p - p^2) = 1 - 2p = 0 \Rightarrow p = 0.5\).

Implication: Unpredictability is maximized when the coin is fair (\(p=0.5\)). If \(p=0.99\) (highly biased), the variance is low because we are almost certain it will be a 1.

3. ML Connection: Binary Cross-Entropy

Loss Functions Logistic Regression

Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

Suppose we have a dataset of labels \(y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n \in \{0, 1\}\) and our model predicts probabilities \(\hat{y}_i\). We assume \(y_i \sim \text{Bern}(\hat{y}_i)\).

The likelihood of the data is:

$$ L = \prod_{i=1}^n \hat{y}_i^{y_i} (1-\hat{y}_i)^{1-y_i} $$

Taking the negative log-likelihood gives us the Binary Cross-Entropy Loss:

$$ J = - \sum_{i=1}^n [y_i \log(\hat{y}_i) + (1-y_i) \log(1-\hat{y}_i)] $$

Minimizing this loss is mathematically equivalent to finding the Bernoulli parameter that makes the observed data most probable.

4. Another ML Connection: Dropout

Dropout describes a regularization technique where neurons are "dropped" (set to 0) during training with probability \(p\).

Mathematically, this involves multiplying the activation vector \(h\) by a mask vector \(m\), where \(m_j \sim \text{Bern}(p)\).
