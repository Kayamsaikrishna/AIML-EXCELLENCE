1. What makes a Product "Inner"?

An Inner Product is a generalization of the dot product that satisfies three axioms: symmetry, linearity, and positive-definiteness.

$$ \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \mathbf{u}^T W \mathbf{v} $$

If \( W = I \), it's the standard dot product. If \( W \) is diagonal, it's a Weighted Inner Product (caring more about some dimensions than others).

2. Kernel Trick

In SVMs, we map data to infinite dimensions. We can't actually calculate the vectors there.

However, we only need their dot products! A Kernel Function \( K(x, y) \) computes the inner product in that high-dimensional space without ever going there.

$$ K(x, y) = \langle \phi(x), \phi(y) \rangle $$

3. Weighted Similarity

Measuring similarity when one feature is more important.

import numpy as np

# Two profiles (Age, Income)
u = np.array([30, 50000])
v = np.array([35, 55000])

# Just calculating dot product is dominated by Income (50k vs 30)
print(f"Standard Dot: {np.dot(u, v):.2e}")

# Weight Matrix (Normalize importance)
# 1.0 for Age, 1e-6 for Income
W = np.diag([1.0, 1e-6])

# Weighted Inner Product: u^T W v
similarity = u.T @ W @ v

print(f"Weighted Inner Product: {similarity:.2f}")
# Now Age matters!
