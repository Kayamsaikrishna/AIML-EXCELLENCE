19. Inner Product Spaces

Generalizing "Similarity" for functions and signals.

1. Generalizing the Dot Product

An Inner Product \(\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle\) is a function that generalizes the dot product. It must satisfy linearity, symmetry, and positive-definiteness.

$$ \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \mathbf{u}^T \mathbf{M} \mathbf{v} $$

Where \(\mathbf{M}\) can be used to "weight" different dimensions or features differently.

2. Kernel Methods & Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS)

Implicit High Dimensions

In Kernelized AI, we don't calculate the coordinates in high dimensions. We simply define an Inner Product function (Kernel) that tells us the similarity between two input points.

  • RBF Kernel: \( K(x, y) = \exp(-\gamma \|x-y\|^2) \). This is an inner product in an infinite-dimensional space!
  • Custom Metrics: Defining how "close" two sentences are using an inner product of their embedding vectors.

3. Python: Weighted Inner Product

import numpy as np

u = np.array([1, 2])
v = np.array([3, 4])

# A diagonal weight metric (Importance of feature 1 is 10x)
M = np.diag([10, 1])

# Weighted Inner Product
inner_product = u.T @ M @ v 

print(f"Standard Dot: {u @ v}")      # 11
print(f"Weighted Inner: {inner_product}") # 30 + 8 = 38
