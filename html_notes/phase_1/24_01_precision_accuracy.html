Stats 1.4 Measurement Quality

Precision vs. Accuracy

Understanding the difference between getting the right answer and getting a consistent answer.

The Target Analogy

Imagine throwing darts at a bullseye:
- Accuracy: How close you are to the bullseye (the center).
- Precision: How tightly clustered your darts are (even if they are far from the center).

Accuracy (Validity)

  • Relates to Bias.
  • The ability of a measurement to match the "True" value.
  • Improved by better calibration or model unbiasedness.

Precision (Reliability)

  • Relates to Variance.
  • The consistency of measurements when repeated.
  • Improved by increasing sample size or reducing noise.

2. ML Connection: Error Analysis

Foundational Model Evaluation

Why Accuracy is Not Precision

In Classification, Accuracy counts total correct predictions. But if you have a massive class imbalance (99% A, 1% B), a model that always predicts "A" has 99% accuracy but 0 Precision for class B.

In Regression: - If your model's predictions are always +10 higher than the truth, it has high Precision (low variance) but low Accuracy (high bias). This is often caused by a missing feature or a bad intercept. - If your model's predictions are scattered but the mean error is zero, it is Accurate (unbiased) but not Precise.

3. Key Takeaway

In Statistics, we often prefer a Biased (Low Accuracy) but Precise (Low Variance) model over an accurate but wildly varying one, especially when the bias is predictable. This is the logic behind Regularization!
