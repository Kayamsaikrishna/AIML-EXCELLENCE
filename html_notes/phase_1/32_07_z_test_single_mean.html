07. Z-test for a Single Mean

Testing the mean when we know the population spread.

1. When to use a Z-test

The Z-test is the simplest and most common hypothesis test. It requires two conditions:

  • The data follows a Normal distribution (or $n > 30$ due to CLT).
  • The population standard deviation ($\sigma$) is **Known**. (This is rare in science but common in engineering/manufacturing).

2. The Formula

$$ Z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} $$

We compare this $Z$ value to critical values (like $\pm 1.96$) to decide if the sample mean $\bar{x}$ is significantly different from the target $\mu_0$.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Drift and Online Monitoring

Is the model breaking?

In AI Production, the Z-test is the core of Model Drift Detection.

When you deploy a **Speech-to-Text AI**, you know that on average, its "Confidence Score" should be 0.85 with a standard deviation of 0.05 (based on massive historical data). If today's batch of 1,000 requests shows a mean confidence of only 0.80, you run a **Z-test**. Because $n=1,000$ (Large Sample) and $\sigma=0.05$ (Known from history), the Z-test is extremely fast and accurate. If the Z-score is -10.0, the AI can automatically trigger an alert: "Model performance is collapsing—likely new microphone hardware or background noise in the data." The Z-test provides the mathematical proof needed to stop an AI before it starts making errors that hurt the business.

4. Python: Manual Z-test

# Current batch mean accuracy: 0.91
# Historical population mean: 0.94, SD: 0.03
n = 100
sample_mean = 0.91
pop_mean = 0.94
pop_sd = 0.03

z_stat = (sample_mean - pop_mean) / (pop_sd / np.sqrt(n))
p_val = stats.norm.cdf(z_stat) # Left-tailed test

print(f"Z-statistic: {z_stat:.2f}")
print(f"p-value: {p_val:.5f}")
# p < 0.01. Strong evidence of performance drift!
