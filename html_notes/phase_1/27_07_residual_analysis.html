Residual Analysis

Decoding the signal in the noise. If your residuals have a pattern, your model is missing the point.

The Ideal Residual

A perfect model should leave behind residuals that look like "White Noise":
- Mean of zero. - No visible patterns (No U-shapes, no fan-shapes). - Normal distribution (QQ-plot should be a straight line).

1. The Four Horsemen of Regression Failure

1. Non-Linearity

Symptom: Residual Plot has a "U" or "S" curve.
Fix: Add polynomial terms (\(X^2\)) or transform X.

2. Heteroscedasticity

Symptom: Residual plot looks like a "Fan" (Variance grows).
Fix: Use Log-transform on Y or Weighted Least Squares.

3. Outliers

Symptom: 1 or 2 points are far away from the Zero-line.
Fix: Check for data entry errors or use Robust Regression.

4. Autocorrelation

Symptom: Residuals cluster (positive follows positive).
Fix: Add a Time-series component or a Lag variable.

2. ML Connection: Error Distribution Logic

Deep Learning Monitoring

Why Residuals drive Loss Functions

Why do we use MSE (L2) vs MAE (L1)? It's all about how we view residuals!

- MSE: Assumes residuals are Normal. It penalizes large misses heavily. - MAE: Assumes residuals are Laplace (Longer tails). It is robust to outliers. - Poisson Loss: Assumes residuals follow a Poisson distribution (Used for count data).

In Model Monitoring, if you see the mean of your residuals drifting away from zero over time, your model is suffering from Concept Drift. The world has changed, and your weights are now biased.
