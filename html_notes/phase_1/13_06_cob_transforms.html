06. Change of Basis for Transformations

Same map, different viewpoint.

1. The Goal

A transformation \(T\) is represented by matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) in the standard basis. What is its matrix \(\mathbf{B}\) in a different basis \(\mathcal{V}\)?

$$ \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{P} $$

Where \(\mathbf{P}\) is the basis-change matrix. Matrices \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(\mathbf{B}\) are Similar—they represent the exact same geometric action, just measured in different units/directions.

2. Why Basis Change is Hard but Worth It

Making sense of Complexity

Imagine a complex shearing transformation. By choosing a basis aligned with the transformation's Eigenvectors, the matrix becomes Diagonal. Diagonal matrices tell you exactly how many times the space is stretched in each natural direction.

3. AI Analogy: Representation Learning

In Deep Learning, we don't just transform data; we look for a Basis (the latent space) where the data is "easier to read". Answering a question in the "raw pixel basis" is hard; switching to a "feature basis" (encoded space) makes the transformation to a class label much simpler.

4. Python: Diagonalization as Basis Change

import numpy as np

# A matrix in standard basis
A = np.array([[3, 1], [0, 2]])

# Change to a basis where transformation is simple (Eigen basis)
vals, P = np.linalg.eig(A)

# B = P^-1 * A * P
B = np.linalg.inv(P) @ A @ P

print(f"Matrix in Eigenbasis (Should be diagonal):\n{np.round(B, 5)}")
