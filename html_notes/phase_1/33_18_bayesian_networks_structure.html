18. Bayesian Networks: The Structure

Representing complex worlds through Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs).

1. Why Graphical Models?

A full joint distribution for 100 variables requires $2^{100}$ parameters—more than the number of atoms in the universe. Bayesian Networks (BNs) solve this by assuming that most things are only locally dependent. They use a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) to represent "Who causes whom."

2. The Core Principle: Factorization

The joint probability of a BN is simply the product of the conditional probabilities of each node given its parents:

$$ P(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(x_i | \text{parents}(x_i)) $$

This "Modularization" reduces the number of parameters from exponential to linear, making complex reasoning possible.

3. AI/ML Connection: Causal AI and Expert Systems

Modeling "Why", not just "What"

In AI, Bayesian Networks are the foundation of Explainable Causal Reasoning.

Standard Neural Networks are "Black Boxes." In contrast, a **Bayesian Network** for Medical Diagnosis explicitly shows that "Smoking" causes "Cancer" which causes a "Positive X-ray." If a doctor asks the AI: "Why did you suggest this treatment?", the BN provides a clear, human-readable trace of evidence. They are also used in Cyber-Physical Systems** (like power grids) to perform **Root Cause Analysis**. If a sensor fails, the BN can backtrack through the graph to identify the most likely fault point, even if that point isn't directly monitored. It is the language of logic in a probabilistic world.

4. Python: Defining a Structure

from pgmpy.models import BayesianNetwork

# Define the causal edges
# Burglary -> Alarm, Earthquake -> Alarm, Alarm -> JohnCalls
model = BayesianNetwork([('B', 'A'), ('E', 'A'), ('A', 'J')])

# The structure alone implies certain independence rules!
# e.g., John calls ONLY depends on the Alarm, not directly on the Burglary.
