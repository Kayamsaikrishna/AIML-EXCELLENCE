05. Curve Sketching and Motion

The complete visual analysis of functions.

1. The Curve Sketching Checklist

  1. Domain/Intercepts: Where does it live and where does it cross zero?
  2. Asymptotes: Behavior at infinity or division by zero.
  3. Monotonicity (\(f'\)): Where is it increasing/decreasing? (Find Critical Points).
  4. Concavity (\(f''\)): Where is it curved up/down? (Find Inflection Points).

2. Physics of Motion

$$ s(t) = \text{Position} $$ $$ v(t) = s'(t) = \text{Velocity} $$ $$ a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t) = \text{Acceleration} $$

Note: Speed is the absolute value of velocity \(|v(t)|\).

3. AI Connection: Trajectory Prediction

Kalman Filters and Tracking

In Computer Vision, when an AI tracks a moving person, it doesn't just look at the current frame. it uses the First Derivative (current velocity) and the Second Derivative (acceleration) to predict where the person will be in the next frame. This allows the AI to "handle" occlusions (when the person walks behind a tree) by trusting the calculus of motion.

4. Python: Automated Analysis

import sympy as sp

t = sp.Symbol('t')
s = t**3 - 6*t**2 + 9*t # Position function

v = sp.diff(s, t)
a = sp.diff(v, t)

print(f"Velocity: {v}")
print(f"Acceleration: {a}")

# Find when particle is at rest (v=0)
rest_times = sp.solve(v, t)
print(f"Rest at t = {rest_times}")
