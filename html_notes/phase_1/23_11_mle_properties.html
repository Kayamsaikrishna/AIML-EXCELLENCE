Stats 1.4 The Gold Standard

Properties of MLE

Why MLE is the most widely used estimation method in science and engineering.

The Winning Streak

Under very general conditions (regularity), MLE achieves nearly every desirable property an estimator can have.

1. The Four Pillars

  • Consistency: As \(n \to \infty\), \(\hat{\theta}_{MLE} \xrightarrow{P} \theta\). It always finds the truth eventually.
  • Asymptotic Normality: For large \(n\), the distribution of the estimator becomes Normal. This allows for Z-tests even if the original data was very non-normal.
  • Asymptotic Efficiency: As \(n \to \infty\), MLE achieves the Cramer-Rao Lower Bound. It is the most precise estimator possible in the limit.
  • Invariance: If \(\hat{\theta}\) is the MLE for \(\theta\), then \(g(\hat{\theta})\) is the MLE for \(g(\theta)\). (Calculus magic!)

2. Note on Bias

MLE is NOT always unbiased! For example, the MLE for variance is \(\frac{1}{n} \sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2\), which we know is biased. However, it is Consistent, meaning the bias disappears as \(n\) grows.

3. ML Connection: Transfer Learning

Deep Learning Fine-tuning

Invariance in Action

The Invariance property of MLE is what allows us to transform model parameters without losing their optimal "likelihood" status.

If we train a model to predict Log-Odds (\(z\)), we can pass those through a Sigmoid to get probabilities (\(p\)). Because of MLE Invariance, the \(p\) we get is automatically the Maximum Likelihood estimate for the probability. We don't need to re-train the model to "switch units".
