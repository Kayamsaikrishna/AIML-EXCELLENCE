Prob 1.4 Eve's Law

The Law of Total Variance

Breaking down total uncertainty into two distinct sources.

Where does Variance come from?

The total variation in a variable \(X\) can be split into two parts:
1. Within-Group Variance: The average spread inside each group.
2. Between-Group Variance: The spread between the group averages.

1. The Formula (EVVE)

$$ \text{Var}(X) = E[\text{Var}(X|Y)] + \text{Var}(E[X|Y]) $$
  • \(E[\text{Var}(X|Y)]\): The Noise (residual variance that \(Y\) cannot explain).
  • \(\text{Var}(E[X|Y])\): The Signal (variance explained by the relationship between \(X\) and \(Y\)).

2. Intuitive Example: Exam Scores

Imagine students in two different cities, A and B, taking the same exam.

  • The Total Variance in scores across all students is influenced by:
    • The average difference between City A and City B (Between-group).
    • The individual differences between students within City A (Within-group).

3. ML Connection: Bias-Variance Decomposition

Generalization Optimization

Why Models Fail

In ML, we decompose the total error of a model into: - Bias: Error from the $E[X|Y]$ part (getting the mean wrong). - Variance: Error from the individual fluctuations (getting the spread around the mean wrong).

The Law of Total Variance is the mathematical "grandfather" of the Bias-Variance tradeoff. It tells us that even the "perfect" model will still have an error equal to the Irreducible Noise ($E[\text{Var}(X|Y)]$), which represents information missing from the features.

Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)

ANOVA is a statistical test that literally uses this law to check if group means are different. If the "Between-group" variance is much larger than the "Within-group" variance, we conclude that the grouping variable \(Y\) has a significant effect on \(X\).
