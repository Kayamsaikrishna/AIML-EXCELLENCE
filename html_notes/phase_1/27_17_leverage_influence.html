Stats 1.7 Outliers

Leverage & Influence

Identifying the "Power Players". Why one single bad data point can make your entire model lie to you.

The Outlier vs The Influencer

- Outlier: A point with a large residual (far from the line). - Leverage: A point with an unusual X-value (far from the "mass" of the data). - Influence: A point that moves the line. Not all outliers are influential; only those with leverage usually change the weights significantly.

1. Measuring Influence: Cook's Distance

$$ D_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^n (\hat{y}_j - \hat{y}_{j(i)})^2}{p s^2} $$

Intuition: "If I delete this one point, how much do ALL my predictions change?"

If \(D_i > 1\) (or \(4/n\)), that point is a Red Flag. It's holding your model hostage.

2. The Leverage Score (\(h_{ii}\))

X-Space Extremes

Leverage lives in the Design Matrix. It measures how far a point is from the mean of all other features. These are the diagonal elements of the Hat Matrix.

The Rule of Thumb

High leverage points act like a Fulcrum. They have the power to pull the regression line towards them, even if they are wrong.

3. ML Connection: Robustness & Data Cleaning

Data Quality Robustness

Protecting the Model

In a production pipeline with millions of rows, you can't manually look at scatter plots. You need automated influence detection.

  • Data Cleaning: We use influence metrics to automatically flag rows that might be sensor errors or edge cases that don't represent the general population.
  • Robust Regression: Algorithms like RANSAC or Theil-Sen regression are designed to ignore high-influence outliers automatically.
  • Deep Learning: In Neural Networks, high-influence points can cause "Gradient Explosion" or "Memory Overwriting" in certain layers. We use Gradient Clipping to counteract the physical effect of high-influence samples during training.
Engineer's Philosophy: A model that is 95% accurate but breaks when a single data point changes is Dangerous. Always check your Cook's Distance before deploying to production.
