05. Multi-armed Bandits (MAB)

A/B Testing with continuous optimization.

1. The Slot Machine Dilemma

Imagine you are in a casino with 10 slot machines (Arms). Some pay out more than others, but you don't know which. Do you keep playing the machine that worked once (**Exploitation**) or try a new one (**Exploration**)?

2. Bandits vs. A/B Testing

In A/B testing, you waste half your traffic on the "Loser" until the test ends. Bandits dynamically shift traffic toward the "Winner" as results come in, minimizing "Regret" (lost potential revenue).

  • $\epsilon$-Greedy: Explore randomly $\epsilon$% of the time, exploit the best arm otherwise.
  • Thompson Sampling: A Bayesian approach using probability distributions per arm.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Core of Reinforcement Learning

Learning by Doing

In AI, MAB is the simplest form of Reinforcement Learning (RL)**.

Unlike standard ML which needs a fixed dataset, Bandits learn in **Real-time**. They are used in Ad Serving Systems (Google/Facebook Ads)** to decide which ad to show a user. If an ad gets clicks, the AI shows it more. MAB is also the secret behind Dynamic Pricing**: the AI "Explores" different price points to find the maximum profit margin. By mastering Bandits, you learn how to build systems that **Adapt** to changing user preferences without human intervention. It is the bridge to advanced RL concepts like "Policy Gradients" and "Q-Learning."

4. Python: Thompson Sampling Logic

# Arms' record: (successes, failures)
arms = [(12, 100), (45, 100)] 

# Sample from Beta distribution (Bayesian belief)
samples = [np.random.beta(s+1, f+1) for s, f in arms]

# Play the arm with the highest sample
chosen_arm = np.argmax(samples)
print(f"Algorithm decides to play arm: {chosen_arm}")
