Stats 1.4 Measuring the Data

Statistics & Estimators

Converting a pile of data into a single, meaningful number.

What is a Statistic?

A Statistic is any function of the sample data. Crucially, a statistic cannot depend on unknown parameters. It must be something you can calculate purely from the numbers in your hand.

Statistic

Any summary of data.

  • The sum of all values.
  • The maximum value.
  • The average.

Estimator (\(\hat{\theta}\))

A statistic used specifically to guess a population parameter.

  • \(\bar{x}\) (Estimates \(\mu\)).
  • \(s^2\) (Estimates \(\sigma^2\)).

1. Estimator vs. Estimate

  • Estimator: The mathematical rule (the "code" or function). It is a Random Variable.
  • Estimate: The specific numerical value obtained from a specific dataset. It is a Number.

2. ML Connection: Learning is Estimation

Supervised Loss Functions

Model Training as Iterative Estimation

A Neural Network is a massive Estimator. When you run `model.fit(X, y)`, you are calculating a specific Estimate (the final weights) that hopefully reflects the true relationship in the data.

Evaluation Metrics

Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-score are all Statistics. They are summaries of the model's performance on the sample. We use them to infer the model's "True Accuracy" on the population of all possible future inputs.

3. Desirable Qualities

We don't just want any estimator; we want ones that are:

  • Unbiased: On average, they hit the mark.
  • Consistent: They get more accurate with more data.
  • Efficient: They require the least amount of data to hit a target precision.
