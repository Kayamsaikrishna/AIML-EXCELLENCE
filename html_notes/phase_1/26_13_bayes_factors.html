Stats 1.6 Model Comparison

Bayes Factors

Quantifying exactly how much more likely one model is than another, without the binary "Reject/Accept" trap.

The Ratio of Evidence

A Bayes Factor (\(BF\)) tells you: "The Evidence is [X] times stronger for Model A than for Model B."

1. Definition

$$ BF_{10} = \frac{P(D|M_1)}{P(D|M_0)} $$

Where \(P(D|M)\) is the Marginal Likelihood (the probability of the data averaged over all possible parameter values in that model).

2. Jeffreys' Scale

How do we interpret the number? We use a standard scale of "Evidence Strength."

\(BF_{10}\) Value Strength of Evidence
1 to 3 Barely worth mentioning (Anecdotal).
3 to 10 Substantial evidence.
10 to 30 Strong evidence.
30 to 100 Very Strong evidence.
> 100 Decisive (Extreme) evidence.

3. ML Connection: Bayesian Occam's Razor

Research Model Choice

Why complex models are penalized

Frequentist metrics like \(R^2\) can always be "gamed" by adding more features.

Bayes Factors have an inherent penalty for complexity. Because the Marginal Likelihood integrates over the *entire* parameter space, a complex model (with many parameters) spreads its "probability mass" very thin.

If the complex model doesn't explain the data *significantly* better than a simple model, the Bayes Factor will automatically favor the simple model. In ML research, this is called the Bayesian Occam's Razor—it mathematically proves that simpler models should be preferred unless the complex one is overwhelmingly better.
