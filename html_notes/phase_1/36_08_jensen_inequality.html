08. Jensen's Inequality

The secret geometric engine of probabilistic bounds.

1. The Core Geometric Concept

Jensen's Inequality** states that for a **Convex Function** $f(x)$, the function of the average is less than or equal to the average of the function. For a **Concave Function** (like Logarithm), it's the opposite.

$$ f(E[X]) \le E[f(X)] \quad \text{(Convex)} $$ $$ E[\log(X)] \le \log(E[X]) \quad \text{(Concave)} $$

Geometrically, a chord connecting two points on a convex curve always lies above the curve.

2. Why this matters in Probability

Jensen's inequality allows us to bound complex expectations that are impossible to calculate directly. It is the bridge between "Hard Math" and "Computable Approximations."

3. AI/ML Connection: ELBO and Variational Inference

The Magic of the Log-Evidence

In AI, Jensen's Inequality is how we derive the ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound)** used in **VAEs** and **LDA (Latent Dirichlet Allocation)**.

We want to maximize the probability of our data $\log p(x)$. However, $p(x)$ is often a complex integral over "Hidden Variables" (Latents). By applying Jensen's Inequality to the concave $\log$ function, we create a "Lower Bound" (the ELBO) that is much easier for an optimizer like **Adam** to handle. Minimizing the KL divergence between our model and the truth is equivalent to maximizing the ELBO provided by Jensen. In Expectation-Maximization (EM)** algorithm, Jensen's inequality ensures that every step we take *guarantees* an improvement in the model's likelihood. It is the geometric proof that the AI is "Getting warmer."

4. Python: Visualizing the Bound

import numpy as np

# A list of values
x = np.array([1, 2, 3, 4, 5])

# Convex function: f(x) = x^2
f_of_expect = np.mean(x)**2
expect_of_f = np.mean(x**2)

print(f"Function of Mean: {f_of_expect}")
print(f"Mean of Function: {expect_of_f}")
# Jensen: 9.0 <= 11.0 (True!)
