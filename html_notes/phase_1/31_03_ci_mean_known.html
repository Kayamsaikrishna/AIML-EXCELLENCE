03. CI for Population Mean (Known Variance)

The Z-Interval: Our first analytical uncertainty bound.

1. The Formula

When the population variance $\sigma^2$ is known (a rare but illustrative case), we use the Z-statistic to construct our interval. The formula is:

$$ \bar{x} \pm Z_{\alpha/2} \left( \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \right) $$
  • $\bar{x}$: Sample Mean.
  • $Z_{\alpha/2}$: Critical value from the Z-table (e.g., 1.96 for 95% confidence).
  • $\sigma / \sqrt{n}$: Standard Error of the mean.

2. Assumptions

  • The data are sampled independently.
  • The population is Normal, **OR** the sample size is large enough ($n > 30$) for the Central Limit Theorem to apply.

3. AI/ML Connection: Standardized Evaluation and Power Analysis

Precision under Constraints

In AI research, we often work with Benchmarks where the variance of the testing environment is known.

On a standardized test like ImageNet-mini, we might know from thousands of previous trials that the standard deviation of "Top-1 Accuracy" is roughly 2%. When you report your new model's performance on this benchmark, you can use the Known-Variance CI to prove that your 1% improvement isn't just a fluke. This formula is also used for Power Analysis—it helps you calculate exactly how many images you need to label to detect a 0.5% improvement in accuracy with 95% confidence. Without this, you might waste weeks training on too little data, only to find your final result is "Insignificant" because your error bars are too wide.

4. Python: Calculating the Z-Interval

from scipy import stats

# Data: Test scores for 50 users
data = np.random.normal(70, 5, 50)
mu_hat = np.mean(data)
sigma = 5 # Known population variance
n = len(data)

# 95% Confidence (alpha = 0.05)
z_crit = stats.norm.ppf(0.975) # 1.96

margin_of_error = z_crit * (sigma / np.sqrt(n))
ci = (mu_hat - margin_of_error, mu_hat + margin_of_error)

print(f"Sample Mean: {mu_hat:.2f}")
print(f"95% Confidence Interval: ({ci[0]:.2f}, {ci[1]:.2f})")
