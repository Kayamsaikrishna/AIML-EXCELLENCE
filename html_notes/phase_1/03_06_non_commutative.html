06. Matrix Multiplication is NOT Commutative

Why the order of operations changes everything.

1. The Proof by Counter-Example

Unlike regular numbers (\( 2 \times 3 = 3 \times 2 \)), for matrices \( \mathbf{A} \) and \( \mathbf{B} \):

$$ \mathbf{AB} \neq \mathbf{BA} $$

In fact, if \( \mathbf{AB} \) is defined, \( \mathbf{BA} \) might not even be defined (if dimensions don't align).

Numerical Example

Let \( \mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \) and \( \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \).

$$ \mathbf{AB} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 7 \\ 8 & 15 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{BA} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 11 & 16 \end{bmatrix} $$

2. Why this matters in AI Architecture

Sequential Layers

Modern AI architectures like Transformers or ResNets are built as sequences of linear transformations (Matrix Multiplications). Swapping the order of layers completely changes the features extracted.

  • Layer Composition: \( f(g(x)) \neq g(f(x)) \). If \( f \) is a convolution and \( g \) is a pooling layer, the result depends strictly on the order.
  • Gradient Flow: In Backpropagation, we multiply Jacobian matrices. The order of these multiplications must strictly follow the chain rule in reverse.

3. Python: Verifying Order Impact

import numpy as np

A = np.array([[1, 2], [3, 4]])
B = np.array([[0, 1], [2, 3]])

AB = A @ B
BA = B @ A

if not np.array_equal(AB, BA):
    print("Multiplication order matters!")
    print(f"AB:\n{AB}")
    print(f"BA:\n{BA}")
