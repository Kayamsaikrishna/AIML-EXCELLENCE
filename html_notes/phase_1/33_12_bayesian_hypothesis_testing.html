12. Bayesian Hypothesis Testing

Beyond p-values: Direct probability of effects.

1. The Bayesian Alternative

Frequentist testing asks: "Could the null hypothesis produce this data?". Bayesian testing asks: "Given this data, what is the probability that the effect is real?". It is far more direct and avoids the logical "Double Negative" of rejecting the null.

2. ROPE (Region of Practical Equivalence)

Instead of a "Point Null" ($p = 0.5$), Bayesians define a small window around zero (say $-0.01$ to $0.01$) where the effect is so small it doesn't matter. We then look at the Posterior.

  • If the entire 95% Credible Interval is outside the ROPE, the effect is **Significant**.
  • If it is entirely inside, we have **Proved the Null**. (Something a p-value can never do!)

3. AI/ML Connection: Verifying Model Superiority

Is Algorithm A truly better?

In AI, Bayesian testing is the gold standard for Benchmarking.

When comparing **GPT-4** vs. **Claude 3**, a t-test might give $p=0.04$. But does that mean GPT-4 is 100% better? A Bayesian test calculates the Probability of Direction (pd)**: the probability that the difference is positive. It might say: "There is an 88% probability that GPT-4 is better than Claude on this task." This allows researchers to make nuanced claims: "The evidence moderately supports GPT-4's superiority." Furthermore, **Bayesian A/B Testing** is used in dynamic platforms (like Netflix) to "Stop early." If the ROPE test shows that a new UI change is definitely useless after 2 hours, the AI kills the experiment immediately, saving millions of "Poor" user experiences.

4. Python: Testing with Medians

# Calculate probability that Effect > 0
prob_better = np.mean(posterior_diff > 0)
print(f"Probability that Model A is better: {prob_better:.2%}")

# Check against ROPE [-0.1, 0.1]
in_rope = np.mean((posterior_diff > -0.1) & (posterior_diff < 0.1))
print(f"Probability of 'No Meaningful Difference': {in_rope:.2%}")
