Formal Logic Quantification

Predicate Logic & Quantifiers

From Facts to Formulas. Moving beyond simple fixed statements to logical templates that describe whole categories of objects and behaviors.

The Template Problem

Propositional logic can say "Socrates is a man." But it can't say "All men are mortal" without a infinite list. Predicate Logic introduces variables, allowing us to define properties that apply to many subjects.

1. Predicates & Variables

A Predicate \(P(x)\) is a statement containing a variable \(x\). It is not a proposition until \(x\) is replaced by a specific value.

  • \(P(x)\): "\(x\) is greater than 3."
  • \(P(4)\): "4 is greater than 3" (True proposition).
  • \(P(2)\): "2 is greater than 3" (False proposition).

2. Quantifiers: The Logic of Sets

Universal (\(\forall\))

"For All": The statement \(P(x)\) is true for every element in the domain.

\(\forall x P(x)\)

Example: "Everyone in this room is a student."

Existential (\(\exists\))

"There Exists": There is at least one element in the domain for which \(P(x)\) is true.

\(\exists x P(x)\)

Example: "Someone in this room is wearing a hat."

3. Negating Quantifiers

When you negate a quantifier, the quantifier flips, and the negation moves inside. This is essential for counterexamples.

$$ \neg (\forall x P(x)) \equiv \exists x \neg P(x) $$ $$ \neg (\exists x P(x)) \equiv \forall x \neg P(x) $$

Intuition: Saying "Not everyone is happy" is the same as saying "There exists at least one person who is not happy."

Programming Knowledge Graphs

Predicates in AI Systems

  • Database Queries (SQL): `SELECT * FROM students WHERE grade > 90` is essentially evaluating a predicate \(P(x) = (\text{grade}(x) > 90)\) for all \(x\) in the domain of students.
  • Assert Statement: In Python, `assert all(x > 0 for x in data)` is a direct implementation of \(\forall x (x > 0)\).
  • Semantic Networks: Modern Knowledge Graphs (like Google's Search graph) store information as predicates: `BornIn(Einstein, Germany)`. By using quantifiers, these systems can infer new knowledge: "If someone is born in Germany, they are European." (\(\forall x (\text{BornIn}(x, \text{Germany}) \rightarrow \text{European}(x))\)).
