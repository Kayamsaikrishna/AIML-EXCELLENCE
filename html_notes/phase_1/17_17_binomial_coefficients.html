Prob 1.4 Expansion

Binomial Coefficients

Weights of the distribution.

The Theorem

The coefficient \(\binom{n}{k}\) appears in the expansion of \((x+y)^n\). This directly relates to the probability of getting exactly \(k\) heads in \(n\) coin flips: \[ P(k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} \]

2. ML Connection: Binomial Distribution

Classification Uncertainty

If we classify 100 images, and our model has 90% accuracy, what is the probability we get exactly 90 correct? It's given by the Binomial Distribution. As \(n \to \infty\), this approximates a Gaussian (Normal) Distribution (Central Limit Theorem). This is why we assume errors are normally distributed in Regression.
