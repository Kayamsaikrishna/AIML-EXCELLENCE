Bayesian ML Inference

KL in Variational Inference

Bridging the Gap. How we turn the impossible task of finding evidence into an optimization problem using the KL Divergence.

The Bayesian Problem

In Bayesian ML, we want the posterior \(P(z|x)\) (what are the hidden factors \(z\) given data \(x\)). Calculating this exactly is often impossible. Variational Inference says: Let's pick a simple distribution \(Q(z)\) and force it to be as close to \(P(z|x)\) as possible using KL Divergence.

1. Forward vs. Reverse KL

Forward KL \(D_{KL}(P \| Q)\)

Mean-Seeking: If \(P\) has two peaks, \(Q\) will stretch itself to cover both. This leads to high-entropy, "blurred" approximations.

$$ E_{z \sim P} [ \ln P(z) - \ln Q(z) ] $$

Reverse KL \(D_{KL}(Q \| P)\)

Mode-Seeking: \(Q\) will pick one peak of \(P\) and fit it very tightly. This is used in VI because we can calculate the gradient with respect to \(Q\).

$$ E_{z \sim Q} [ \ln Q(z) - \ln P(z) ] $$

2. The ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound)

Since we don't know the posterior \(P(z|x)\), we can't minimize \(D_{KL}(Q \| P)\) directly. However, we can minimize a different term that is mathematically equivalent:

$$ \ln P(x) = \text{ELBO}(Q) + D_{KL}(Q(z) \| P(z|x)) $$

Because KL is always \(\ge 0\), the ELBO is a "Lower Bound" on the "Evidence" (\(\ln P(x)\)).

Maximize ELBO

By pushing the ELBO as high as possible, we automatically push the KL Divergence to be as low as possible.

Intuition

We are "fitting a balloon inside a box." The box is the true distribution, and the balloon is our approximation. We expand the balloon until it matches the box shape as closely as possible.

VAEs Latent Space

The VAE Loss Function

In a Variational Autoencoder, the loss function has two competing terms:

  1. Reconstruction Loss: Force the model to generate accurate data (Maximize Likelihood).
  2. KL Divergence Loss: Force the latent codes \(z\) to follow a simple Prior (usually a Standard Gaussian).

The KL term acts as a regularizer: it prevents the model from "cheating" by assigning a unique point in space to every data point, forcing the latent space to be continuous and structured.
