Prob 1.4 The King

The Normal (Gaussian) Distribution

The "natural" shape of uncertainty.

The Ubiquity of the Bell Curve

Why does the Normal distribution appear everywhere? Height, weight, IQ, noise in sensors, financial returns...

The reason is the Central Limit Theorem (CLT): If you sum many independent random variables (of almost any distribution), the result approaches a Normal distribution. Since most real-world phenomena are the result of many small independent factors, they naturally become Gaussian.

1. The PDF Formula

A Random Variable \(X\) is Normally distributed with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\): $$ X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) $$ The Probability Density Function (PDF) is: $$ f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left( -\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right) $$

Anatomy of the bell:

  • \(\mu\): The center (location). In a normal distribution, Mean = Median = Mode.
  • \(\sigma\): The spread (scale). Determines how "fat" or "skinny" the bell is.
  • The factor \(\frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}}\) is the normalization constant that ensures the total area under the curve is exactly 1.

2. The 68-95-99.7 Rule (Empirical Rule)

For any Normal distribution:

  • ~68.2% of data falls within \(1\sigma\) of the mean.
  • ~95.4% of data falls within \(2\sigma\) of the mean.
  • ~99.7% of data falls within \(3\sigma\) of the mean.

"Six Sigma"

In manufacturing, "Six Sigma" quality means the process is so precise that a defect only occurs if an outcome falls more than 6 standard deviations from the mean. This happens roughly 3.4 times per million opportunities.

3. ML Connection: The Gaussian Assumption

Statistical ML Regression

Why MSE?

In Linear Regression, we minimize Mean Squared Error (MSE). This choice isn't arbitrary.

If we assume the target \(y\) follows a Normal distribution centered at our prediction \(\hat{y}\) with constant variance (noise), then the Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) of the parameters leads directly and mathematically to the minimization of the squared distance.

Gaussian Naive Bayes

In classification, if we assume that the features for each class are Normally distributed, we can compute the probability of a data point belonging to a class using the Gaussian PDF.

4. Maximum Entropy

Just as the Uniform distribution has the maximum entropy for a bounded interval, the Normal distribution has the maximum entropy among all distributions with a fixed mean and variance.

Implication: If you only know the mean and variance of a dataset, the most "honest" (least biased) assumption you can make about its distribution is that it is Gaussian.

5. Python Visualization

Python: PDF vs. Sample Histogram

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from scipy.stats import norm

# Parameters
mu, sigma = 0, 1
x = np.linspace(-4, 4, 100)

# Theoretical PDF
pdf = norm.pdf(x, mu, sigma)

# Samples
samples = np.random.normal(mu, sigma, 1000)

plt.hist(samples, bins=30, density=True, alpha=0.6, color='g', label='Samples')
plt.plot(x, pdf, 'r', lw=2, label='PDF')
plt.title(f'Normal Distribution ($\mu$=0, $\sigma$=1)')
plt.legend()
plt.show()
