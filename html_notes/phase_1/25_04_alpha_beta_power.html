Stats 1.5 Sensitivity

Alpha, Beta & Power

Quantifying the strength of your statistical lens: how clearly can you see a real effect?

The Big Three

  • \(\alpha\) (Alpha): The probability of a Type I error. Your tolerance for being a "false alarm."
  • \(\beta\) (Beta): The probability of a Type II error. Your chance of being "blind" to a real discovery.
  • Power (\(1 - \beta\)): The probability of correctly rejecting a false Null. This is the Sensitivity of your test.

1. What Affects Power?

A high-power test is one that almost never misses a real effect. You increase Power by:

  1. Increasing Sample Size (\(n\)): More data gives clearer evidence.
  2. Increasing Alpha (\(\alpha\)): Being more liberal with discovery makes you miss fewer real ones (but increases false alarms).
  3. Effect Size: Large differences (like a 50% performance boost) are easy to see; small differences (0.5% boost) require a very "powerful" lens.
  4. Reducing Noise: Lower variance (\(\sigma\)) makes the signal pop out.

3. ML Connection: SOTA Benchmarking

Product Research

Why "Slightly Better" isn't "Significantly Better"

In many ML competitions, people chase "0.1% improvement." If your test doesn't have enough Power, you can't tell if that 0.1% is real or just a lucky test set split.

Underpowered Studies: If you only test your "Groundbreaking" model on 10 images, your Power is effectively zero. Even if the model is genuinely 2x better, the test lacks the resolution to prove it. This is why reviewers demand large test sets and cross-validation—to ensure the study has enough Power to be believable.
