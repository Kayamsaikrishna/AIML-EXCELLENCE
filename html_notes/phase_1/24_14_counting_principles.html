14. Counting Principles

The math of "How many ways?"

1. Multiplication Principle

The Multiplication Principle (Fundamental Counting Principle) states that if one event can occur in \(m\) ways and another in \(n\) ways, the total number of ways both can occur is \(m \times n\).

2. Addition Principle

The Addition Principle states that if two events are mutually exclusive, and the first can occur in \(m\) ways while the second can occur in \(n\) ways, the total number of ways either can occur is \(m + n\).

3. AI/ML Connection: Hyperparameter Grid Search

The Explosion of Possibilities

In Machine Learning, we often need to find the best settings (hyperparameters) for our models.

If you have:

  • 3 Learning Rates
  • 4 Batch Sizes
  • 2 Types of Optimizers

The Multiplication Principle tells us there are $3 \times 4 \times 2 = 24$ unique combinations to test. While 24 sounds small, modern AI models have dozens of hyperparameters, leading to Combinatorial Explosion. This is why we use Random Search or Bayesian Optimization instead of checking every single combination. Counting principles allow AI Engineers to estimate the "Search Space" of a problem, helping them decide how much compute power they need to find the optimal solution.

4. Python: Cartesisan Product Counting

h_params = {
    "lr": [0.1, 0.01, 0.001],
    "batch": [16, 32, 64, 128],
    "opt": ["SGD", "Adam"]
}

total_ways = 1
for key in h_params:
    total_ways *= len(h_params[key])

print(f"Total Grid Search points: {total_ways}")
