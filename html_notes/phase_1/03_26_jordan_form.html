26. Jordan Normal Form

The closest form to diagonalization for general matrices.

1. What happens when Diagonalization fails?

A matrix is diagonalizable only if it has a complete set of linearly independent eigenvectors. When eigenvalues have Algebraic Multiplicity higher than their Geometric Multiplicity, the matrix is "defective". The Jordan Normal Form (JNF) is the most simplified representation we can achieve for such matrices.

$$ \mathbf{J} = \mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{AP} = \begin{bmatrix} J_1 & 0 & \dots \\ 0 & J_2 & \dots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{bmatrix} $$

Each Jordan Block \( J_i \) looks like this:

$$ J_i = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_i & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_i & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda_i \end{bmatrix} $$

2. Importance in System Dynamics

Stability Analysis

In Robotics and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), the growth of a signal over time is governed by powers of the weight matrix \( \mathbf{W}^t \). The Jordan form allows us to see that if a Jordan block has \( \lambda = 1 \), the "1" on the super-diagonal leads to linearly increasing signals (Polynomial growth), which can cause drift in control systems.

  • Generalized Eigenvectors: Used to construct the basis for JNF.
  • Computing matrix functions: It is easy to calculate \( e^{\mathbf{A}t} \) or \( \mathbf{A}^n \) using the Jordan form.

3. Python: Attempting JNF

import sympy as sp

# A defective matrix
A = sp.Matrix([[5, 4, 2, 1], [0, 1, -1, -1], [-1, -1, 3, 0], [1, 1, -1, 2]])

# Jordan Form returns (P, J)
P, J = A.jordan_form()

print("Jordan Form J:")
sp.pprint(J)
