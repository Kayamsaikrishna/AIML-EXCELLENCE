09. Standard Deviation

The squareroot of variance: bringing spread back to reality.

1. Definition

The Standard Deviation $\sigma$ is the positive square root of the variance. While variance is in "squared units" (like $\text{meters}^2$), the standard deviation is in the same units as the random variable itself.

$$ \sigma = \sqrt{Var(X)} = \sqrt{E[(X - \mu)^2]} $$

2. Why Use Standard Deviation?

Because it shares the same scale as the data, $\sigma$ is much more intuitive for interpretation. In a **Normal Distribution**:

  • ~68% of data falls within $\pm 1 \sigma$.
  • ~95% of data falls within $\pm 2 \sigma$.
  • ~99.7% of data falls within $\pm 3 \sigma$.

3. AI/ML Connection: Standardization (Z-Score)

Leveling the Playing Field

Standard deviation is the key to **Feature Scaling**, which is mandatory for almost all Deep Learning algorithms.

If one feature (like "House Area") has values in the thousands, and another (like "Number of Rooms") has values between 1 and 5, a Neural Network will struggle to learn because the gradients will be dominated by the larger feature. We fix this by **Standardizing** the data: subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation. This transforms every feature to have $\mu=0$ and $\sigma=1$. This process, known as **StandardScaler** in Scikit-Learn, ensures that the AI pays equal attention to all features, regardless of their original scale.

4. Python: Scaling with Standard Deviation

# Example of Z-score normalization
data = np.array([1000, 2500, 3100, 4200, 1500])

mean = np.mean(data)
std = np.std(data)

standardized_data = (data - mean) / std

print(f"Original: {data}")
print(f"Standardized: {standardized_data}")
print(f"New Std Dev: {np.std(standardized_data):.1f}") # Should be 1.0
