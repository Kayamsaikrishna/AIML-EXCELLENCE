Entropy for Discrete Distributions

Exploring the landscape of uncertainty across probability mass functions (PMFs).

The Peak of Ignorance

In a discrete system, entropy is the measure of how much we don't know. If every outcome is equally likely, we know nothing about what will happen next—this is the peak of entropy.

1. The Bernoulli Case: Binary Entropy

The simplest discrete distribution is the Bernoulli trial (e.g., a coin flip). Let \(P(X=1) = p\) and \(P(X=0) = 1-p\).

$$ H(p) = -p \log_2 p - (1-p) \log_2 (1-p) $$

Characteristics of the Binary Entropy Function:

  • Maximized at \(p = 0.5\): \(H(0.5) = 1\) bit. A fair coin is the most uncertain.
  • Zero at the edges: \(H(0) = 0\) and \(H(1) = 0\). If we know the outcome for sure, there is no surprise.
  • Symmetry: \(H(p) = H(1-p)\).

2. Uniform Distribution: Maximum Spread

For a discrete random variable with \(n\) possible outcomes, the distribution with the **highest entropy** is the Uniform Distribution.

$$ P(X = x_i) = \frac{1}{n} \implies H(X) = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{n} \log_2 n = \log_2 n $$
Insight: This is why "Maximum Entropy" is a principle in Bayesian statistics. If you have no prior information, the most honest distribution to pick is the Uniform one, as it assumes the least about the outcomes.

3. Categorical Distribution & ML

In Machine Learning classification (e.g., MNIST digit recognition), the model outputs a Softmax vector, which is a Categorical PMF.

$$ \hat{y} = [\hat{p}_1, \hat{p}_2, \dots, \hat{p}_{10}] $$

We measure the Distance between the predicted distribution and the actual "One-Hot" label using Cross-Entropy. The "Perfect" model would have zero entropy on the correct class and infinite entropy (high surprise) if it miscalculates.

Reinforcement Learning Maximum Entropy

Entropy Regularization in RL

In algorithms like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), we add the Entropy of the policy to the Reward function:

$$ J(\pi) = \sum_{t=0}^T E_{(s_t, a_t) \sim \rho_\pi} [r(s_t, a_t) + \alpha H(\pi(\cdot|s_t))] $$

Why? This prevents the agent from converging too quickly to a single "Best" action. It forces the agent to keep its choices "Disordered" enough to continue exploring the environment.
