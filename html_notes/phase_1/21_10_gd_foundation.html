10. Gradient Descent Math

The engine of modern machine learning.

1. The Update Rule

Gradient Descent is an iterative optimization algorithm for finding the local minimum of a differentiable function. Since the gradient \(\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\) points in the direction of steepest ascent, we move in the opposite direction to descend:

$$ \mathbf{w}_{t+1} = \mathbf{w}_t - \eta \nabla f(\mathbf{w}_t) $$

Where \(\eta\) (eta) is the Learning Rate (or step size).

2. The Learning Rate Dilemma

  • Too Large: The steps are too big, and the algorithm may "overshoot" the minimum or even diverge (get further away).
  • Too Small: Convergence will be painfully slow, requiring thousands of unnecessary iterations.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Core of Backpropagation

Training Millions of Weights

Everything you see in modern AI—from ChatGPT to image generators—relies on this single equation. During Backpropagation, we calculate the gradient of the loss function with respect to every single "Weight" in the neural network.

Then, we apply Gradient Descent to slightly adjust those weights. A typical training run might repeat this simple calculus operation billions of times. The mathematical guarantee that the negative gradient points towards the quickest decrease in error is what allows a computer to "Learn" from data. It's not magic; it's just very high-dimensional multivariable calculus.

4. Python: Manual Gradient Descent

# Minimize f(x) = x^2. Gradient f'(x) = 2x.
def gradient_descent(start_x, lr, epochs):
    x = start_x
    for i in range(epochs):
        grad = 2 * x
        x = x - lr * grad
        print(f"Step {i}: x={x:.4f}, f(x)={x**2:.4f}")
    return x

final_x = gradient_descent(start_x=10, lr=0.1, epochs=10)
