1. What is a Radian?

One radian is the angle where the arc length equals the radius.

$$ \theta_{rad} = \frac{\text{arc length}}{\text{radius}} $$

This makes Calculus beautiful: \( \frac{d}{dx} \sin(x) = \cos(x) \) ONLY works in Radians. In Degrees, you get an ugly constant factor.

2. Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)

In LLaMA 2 (and other modern LLMs), words obtain their position information by being rotated in vector space.

The angle of rotation depends on the position index \( m \) and a frequency \( \theta \).

$$ f(x, m) = x \cdot e^{i m \theta} $$

This allows the model to understand "relative distance" naturally, just by subtracting angles.

3. Implementing Rotation

Rotating a vector using complex numbers (euler's formula).

import numpy as np

def rotate_vector(vector, angle_rad):
    # Treat 2D vector as complex number: x + iy
    z = vector[0] + 1j * vector[1]
    
    # Rotate by multiplying by e^(i*theta)
    rotation = np.exp(1j * angle_rad)
    z_rotated = z * rotation
    
    return np.array([z_rotated.real, z_rotated.imag])

vec = np.array([1.0, 0.0])
angle = np.pi / 2 # 90 degrees in Radians

new_vec = rotate_vector(vec, angle)
print(f"Original: {vec}")
print(f"Rotated (Radiens): {new_vec}") 
# [0.0, 1.0] (Vertical)
