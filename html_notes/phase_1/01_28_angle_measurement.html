28. Angle Measurement (Degrees & Radians)

Why math prefers Radians while humans prefer Degrees.

1. The Geometry of a Revolution

While Degrees are a historical artifact (360 is roughly the number of days in a year), Radians are a natural mathematical unit based on the arc length of a unit circle.

$$ \theta_{rad} = \frac{\pi}{180} \times \theta_{deg} $$ $$ \theta_{deg} = \frac{180}{\pi} \times \theta_{rad} $$

2. Engineering & AI Context

Phase Shifts & Periodicity

In AI models dealing with signals (Audio, Time Series) or Positional Embeddings (Transformers), we use radians because they simplify the derivative of trigonometric functions.

  • Rotary Embeddings (RoPE): Used in Llama 3 to rotate token vectors in high-dimensional space. These rotations are always calculated in radians to maintain rotational invariance.
  • Complex exponentials: \( e^{i\theta} \) expects \( \theta \) in radians for the Taylor expansion to hold.

3. Python Implementation

Handling angle conversions in Large Scale Vectorized Operations.

import numpy as np

# A batch of angles in degrees
degrees = np.array([0, 30, 45, 60, 90, 180])

# Direct conversion (Faster in Numpy than manual math)
radians = np.deg2rad(degrees)

# Inverse conversion
back_to_deg = np.rad2deg(radians)

print(f"Degrees: {degrees}")
print(f"Radians: {radians}")
# Output: [0.         0.52359878 0.78539816 1.04719755 1.57079633 3.14159265]
