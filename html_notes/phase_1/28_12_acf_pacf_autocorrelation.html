Stats 1.8 Correlation

ACF, PACF & Autocorrelation

Measuring the memory of data. How past events echo into the present.

The Echo Effect

In a Time Series, data points are almost never independent. Today's temperature is strongly correlated with yesterday's.

Autocorrelation measures the strength of this "Echo" at different time lags.

1. ACF vs. PACF

Autocorrelation Function (ACF)

The total correlation between \(Y_t\) and \(Y_{t-k}\).
Note: This includes both the *direct* relationship and the *indirect* relationship (e.g., \(t\) correlates with \(t-2\) because they both correlate with \(t-1\)).

Partial Autocorrelation (PACF)

The "Pure" correlation between \(Y_t\) and \(Y_{t-k}\) after we remove the effect of all intermediate lags.
Analogy: It's like a Multiple Regression where we control for the values in between.

2. Identifying the Model

These plots are the specific "fingerprints" used to build ARIMA models:

AR(p) Process

PACF: Spikes at first \(p\) lags, then shuts off.
ACF: Dies out slowly (Exponentially).

MA(q) Process

ACF: Spikes at first \(q\) lags, then shuts off.
PACF: Dies out slowly.

3. ML Connection: Lag Feature Engineering

Deep Learning Feature Eng

Why "Lags" are your best features

If you are building a Supervised ML model (like XGBoost) to predict time series, ACF/PACF plots tell you exactly which features to build.

  • Lag Selection: If the PACF spike at Lag 7 is significant, it means there is a "Weekly Pattern". You should create a feature `target_lag_7`.
  • Window Statistics: If the ACF dies out very slowly over 30 days, a simple lag won't work. You should create a Rolling Mean or Rolling Std feature representing the "Month's momentum".
  • Attention Mechanisms: In Transformers (like the Informer or Temporal Fusion Transformer), the "Self-Attention" weights effectively learn a dynamic version of the ACF/PACF, deciding which past historical points are most relevant to the current prediction.
Engineer's Pro-Tip: If all your ACF/PACF values are within the "Blue Confidence Band," your data is White Noise. There is zero predictability in the patterns, and no model (not even a Transformer) can find a signal.
