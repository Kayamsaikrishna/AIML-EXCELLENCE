Prob 1.4 Marginalization

Law of Total Probability

Summing out the unknowns.

Divide and Conquer

To find the probability of event \(B\), we can partition the sample space into disjoint events \(A_1, A_2, \dots A_n\) (scenarios) and sum the probability of reaching \(B\) via each scenario: \[ P(B) = \sum_{i} P(B|A_i)P(A_i) \]

This is also known as Marginalization (summing out variable \(A\)).

2. ML Connection: Mixture Models

GMMs

A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) assumes data comes from one of \(K\) hidden clusters (components). The probability of observing a data point \(x\) is the sum of probabilities of generating \(x\) from each cluster, weighted by the cluster's size: \[ P(x) = \sum_{k=1}^K P(x | z=k) P(z=k) \]

3. Python: Marginalization

total_prob.py
# Factory A makes 20% of widgets (1% defective)
# Factory B makes 80% of widgets (5% defective)
# What is total defective rate? P(Defect)

p_A = 0.20
p_B = 0.80

p_def_A = 0.01
p_def_B = 0.05

# Law of Total Prob
p_total_defect = (p_def_A * p_A) + (p_def_B * p_B)

print(f"Total Defect Rate: {p_total_defect:.4f}")
# 0.002 + 0.04 = 0.042 (4.2%)
