01. Precision vs. Accuracy

The two pillars of reliable estimation.

1. Definitions

In Statistical Estimation, we distinguish between two types of errors:

  • Accuracy (Unbiasedness): How close the *average* of your estimates is to the true value. High accuracy means low Bias.
  • Precision (Reliability): How close your individual estimates are to each other. High precision means low Variance.

2. The Bullseye Metaphor

Imagine a target:

  • **Accurate but not Precise:** Shots are scattered, but their average center is the bullseye.
  • **Precise but not Accurate:** Shots are tightly clustered together, but they are 5 inches away from the bullseye.
  • **Both:** The gold standard—all shots are in the bullseye.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Reliability and Error Metrics

Knowing Why You Failed

In AI, the Prec/Acc distinction helps us choose the right fix for a failing model.

If your model has High Accuracy but Low Precision, it means it is "Learning the truth" on average, but it is too sensitive to the specific training data (High Variance). The fix is Regularization** or Getting more data. If your model has High Precision but Low Accuracy, it means its internal logic is consistent but systematically wrong (High Bias). The fix is Selecting a more complex architecture (e.g., switching from Linear to Non-linear). Understanding this distinction prevents you from wasting time tuning a model's complexity when the real problem is just a "shaky" training dataset.

4. Python: Quantifying Prec vs Acc

# Experiment: 1000 trials to estimate 'TrueValue = 100'
true_val = 100

# Estimator A (Accurate, Unreliable)
est_a = np.random.normal(100, 10, 1000)

# Estimator B (Precise, Inaccurate)
est_b = np.random.normal(90, 1, 1000)

print(f"Est A: Bias={np.mean(est_a)-100:.2f}, Std={np.std(est_a):.2f}")
print(f"Est B: Bias={np.mean(est_b)-100:.2f}, Std={np.std(est_b):.2f}")
