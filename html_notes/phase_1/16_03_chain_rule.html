03. The Chain Rule

The engine of deep learning backprop.

1. Definition

When functions are nested like Russian dolls, \(y = f(g(x))\), the derivative is the product of individual derivatives:

$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} $$

Or in prime notation: \( f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \).

2. The "Peeling the Onion" Analogy

To differentiate \( \sin(x^2) \):

  1. Outer layer: \(\sin(\cdot) \to \cos(\cdot)\)
  2. Inner layer: \(x^2 \to 2x\)
  3. Combine: \(\cos(x^2) \cdot 2x\)

3. AI Connection: Backpropagation

A Neural Network is just a massive composition of functions: \( y = L(f_n(...f_1(x)))\). To find how the final Loss \(L\) changes with respect to a weight in layer 1 (\(w_1\)), we multiply the "local" gradients of every layer in between. This is literally the Chain Rule implemented in a computational graph.

4. Python: Nested Differentiation

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# f(x) = exp(sin(x^2))
f = sp.exp(sp.sin(x**2))

df = sp.diff(f, x)
print(f"d/dx [exp(sin(x^2))] =\n{df}")
# Output involves derivatives of exp, sin, and x^2
