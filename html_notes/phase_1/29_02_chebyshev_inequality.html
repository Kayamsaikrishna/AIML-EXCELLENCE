02. Chebyshev's Inequality

Bounding deviations using variance.

1. Definition

Chebyshev's Inequality states that for any random variable $X$ with finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$, the probability that $X$ deviates from its mean by more than $k$ standard deviations is at most $1/k^2$.

$$ P(|X - \mu| \ge k\sigma) \le \frac{1}{k^2} \quad \text{for } k > 0 $$

2. The "Distribution-Free" Power

Unlike the **Empirical Rule** (which assumes a Normal Distribution and says 99.7% of data is within $3\sigma$), Chebyshev applies to any distribution. It guaranteed that at least 75% of data is within $2\sigma$ and at least 88.8% is within $3\sigma$, no matter how skewed the data is.

3. AI/ML Connection: Outlier Filtering and Model Stability

Robustness Guarantees

Chebyshev's Inequality is used to establish Reliability Bounds for AI predictions.

In **Anomaly Detection**, if you don't know the distribution of your data, you can use Chebyshev to define "Outliers"—any data point more than $5\sigma$ from the mean has a less than 4% chance of being "natural" under any distribution. In **Active Learning**, when an AI is deciding which data points to ask a human to label, it often uses these bounds to identify "high-uncertainty" samples. Furthermore, it is the fundamental tool for proving the **Weak Law of Large Numbers**, which guarantees that as you add more data, your AI's estimated average will eventually hit the true population average.

4. Python: The Chebyshev Bound

# k = 3 standard deviations
k = 3
bound = 1 / (k**2)

# Sample from a highly non-normal distribution (Pareto)
data = np.random.pareto(3, 10000) 
mu, sigma = np.mean(data), np.std(data)

# Actual percentage outside 3-sigma
outside = np.mean(np.abs(data - mu) >= k * sigma)

print(f"Prob outside 3-sigma: {outside:.4f}")
print(f"Chebyshev Bound: {bound:.4f}")
# Actual prob will be significantly lower than 0.1111
