Mutual Information Estimation

From Calculus to Computation. How to actually measure MI when you don't know the underlying probability densities.

The Integration Wall

In theory, calculating MI requires integrating over the joint density of your variables. In practice, we only have samples (data points).

Estimating MI from finite data is one of the most challenging problems in statistical Machine Learning.

1. The Discrete Case: Frequency Counting

If your variables are discrete (e.g., "City" and "Product Category"), you can use the **Plug-in Estimator**:

$$ \hat{I}(X; Y) = \sum_{x, y} \frac{count(x, y)}{N} \log \frac{count(x, y) \cdot N}{count(x) count(y)} $$
The Finite Sample Bias: With small datasets, this estimator tends to overestimate MI. If every user is unique, the formula will think the "User ID" perfectly predicts the "Outcome," which is a false correlation.

2. Continuous Case: KSG (K-Nearest Neighbors)

For two continuous variables (e.g., "Age" and "Income"), we use the Kraskov-Stögbauer-Grassberger (KSG) estimator. It doesn't bin the data; instead, it looks at the distance to the \(k\)-th neighbor.

$$ I(X; Y) \approx \psi(k) - \langle \psi(n_x + 1) + \psi(n_y + 1) \rangle + \psi(N) $$
  • \(\psi\): The Digamma function.
  • Why it's better: It adapts to the local density of the data, working well even in areas where data is sparse.

3. The Modern Era: MINE (Neural Estimation)

What if the relationship is incredibly complex (e.g., Image Pixels and a Concept)? We use a **Neural Network** to estimate the MI. This is known as MINE (Mutual Information Neural Estimator).

$$ I(X; Y) \ge E_{P(X, Y)}[T_\theta(x, y)] - \log E_{P(X)P(Y)}[\exp(T_\theta(x, y))] $$

By training a network \(T_\theta\) to maximize this bound, the output of the loss function becomes a direct estimate of the Mutual Information. This scales to high-dimensional images and language embeddings where traditional statistics fail.

Deep Learning Representation Learning

Why we need Neural Estimators

  • Self-Supervised Learning: Models like SimCLR or CPC (Contrastive Predictive Coding) don't use labels. Instead, they maximize the mutual information between different slices or views of the same data.
  • Information Bottleneck Analysis: Researchers use MINE to plot how much information about the training labels is kept at each layer of a neural network, proving that networks "learn by forgetting" irrelevant details.
  • GAN Evaluation: Measuring the MI between the GAN latent space and the output helps quantify if the model is suffering from Mode Collapse.

4. Estimation Methods Comparison

Binning

Fast, easy. Fails in high dimensions (empty bins).

KSG (KNN)

Very accurate for 1D-5D. Slow for millions of points (\(O(N^2)\)).

Neural (MINE)

Scales to any dimension. Requires GPU and training time.
