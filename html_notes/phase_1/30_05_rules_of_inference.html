Formal Logic Reasoning

Rules of Inference

The Engine of Proof. Valid logical templates that allow us to derive new truths from existing facts without needing to build a truth table every time.

The Legal Moves

In a chess game, you can't move a knight however you want. In a proof, you can't jump to a conclusion however you want. Rules of Inference are the "legal moves" of logic. They ensure that if the premises are true, the conclusion is guaranteed to be true.

1. Classic Inference Rules

Modus Ponens

Affirming the Antecedent: If you have "If \(p\) then \(q\)" and you know \(p\) is true, you can conclude \(q\).

$$ p \rightarrow q $$ $$ p $$ $$ \therefore q $$

Modus Tollens

Denying the Consequent: If you have "If \(p\) then \(q\)" and you know \(q\) is false, you can conclude \(p\) is false.

$$ p \rightarrow q $$ $$ \neg q $$ $$ \therefore \neg p $$

Hypothetical Syllogism

Transitivity: If \(p \rightarrow q\) and \(q \rightarrow r\), then \(p \rightarrow r\).

$$ p \rightarrow q $$ $$ q \rightarrow r $$ $$ \therefore p \rightarrow r $$

2. Rules for Quantifiers

Universal Specialization

If something is true for everyone, it is true for Socrates.

$$ \forall x P(x) $$ $$ \therefore P(c) $$

Existential Generalization

If Socrates has a property, then someone exists who has that property.

$$ P(c) $$ $$ \therefore \exists x P(x) $$
Expert Systems Reasoning

Automated Reasoning

Before Deep Learning, AI was largely about "Inference Engines."

  • Prolog: A programming language where you provide facts and rules (Hypothetical Syllogisms), and the computer uses rules of inference to answer queries.
  • Forward Chaining: Start with what you know and use Modus Ponens to reach a goal. (Used in diagnostic systems).
  • Backward Chaining: Start with a goal and search for premises that could prove it via Modus Tollens. (Used in legal/policy expert systems).
  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting: When an LLM says "Let's think step by step," it is attempting to build a sequence of Modus Ponens steps. If one step is logically invalid, the whole reasoning "hallucinates."
