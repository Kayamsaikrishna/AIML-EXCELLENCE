08. R-squared (Coefficient of Determination)

Measuring how much of the story your model is telling.

1. Variation Accounting

$R^2$ measures the proportion of the total variance in $Y$ that is explained by $X$ in the model.

$$ R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_{Res}}{SS_{Total}} $$
  • $SS_{Total}$: Total variation in data (variance from the mean).
  • $SS_{Res}$: Unexplained variation (variance from the line).

2. Interpretation

$R^2 = 0.85$ means your model explains 85% of why $Y$ changes. The remaining 15% is noise or features you haven't included yet. In Simple Linear Regression, $R^2$ is exactly the square of the Pearson Correlation Coefficient ($r^2$).

3. AI/ML Connection: The universal Regression Metric

Contextualizing Performance

In AI, $R^2$ is the Standardized Accuracy for Regression**.

Unlike MAE or RMSE, which depend on the scale of your data (is $100 error a lot or a little?), $R^2$ is always between 0 and 1. This allows AI engineers to compare models across different datasets. For instance, in **Ad Click-Through Rate (CTR) prediction**, an $R^2$ of 0.1 might be a massive breakthrough because human behavior is so chaotic. In **Physics Simulation**, we might demand an $R^2$ of 0.999. It is also used to evaluate **Transfer Learning**: if we take a model trained on ImageNet and use its features for regression, the $R^2$ tells us exactly how much "Visual Information" was successfully transferred to the new task.

4. Python: Calculating R2

from sklearn.metrics import r2_score

y_true = [3, -0.5, 2, 7]
y_pred = [2.5, 0.0, 2, 8]

score = r2_score(y_true, y_pred)
print(f"R-squared: {score:.2f}")
# Higher is better!
