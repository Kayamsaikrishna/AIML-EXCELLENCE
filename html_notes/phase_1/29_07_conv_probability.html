07. Convergence in Probability

The math of "getting closer" to the truth.

1. Definition

A sequence of random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots$ converges in probability to a random variable $X$ if, for any arbitrarily small positive number $\epsilon$, the probability that the difference between $X_n$ and $X$ is greater than $\epsilon$ goes to zero as $n$ goes to infinity.

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(|X_n - X| \ge \epsilon) = 0 $$

Notation: $X_n \xrightarrow{p} X$.

2. Intuition: Shrinking Error

Imagine you are throwing darts. Convergence in probability doesn't mean you hit the bullseye every single time. It means that as you practice more ($n$), the "Chance" of you missing by more than an inch ($\epsilon$) becomes infinitely small. You might still have a "Freak Accident" once every trillion years, but the probability of that event is vanishing.

3. AI/ML Connection: Consistency in Estimators

The Mark of a Good Model

Convergence in probability is the mathematical definition of Consistency.

In **Deep Learning**, we say that a training algorithm is consistent if the weights it learns ($W_n$) converge in probability to the "Optimal Weights" ($W^*$) as the dataset size $n$ increases. If an algorithm is not consistent, it doesn't matter how much data you throw at it—it will never find the true underlying pattern. Developers use this concept when comparing different **Optimization Algorithms** (like SGD vs. Adam). A theoretically sound algorithm must guarantee convergence in probability to ensure the AI's predictions become more "Trustworthy" as the company collects more user data.

4. Python: Simulating WLLN (P-Convergence)

# Checking how often the sample mean is "Very Far" from the truth
trials = 1000
true_mean = 0.5
n_sizes = [10, 100, 1000, 10000]
epsilon = 0.05

for n in n_sizes:
    # 1000 experiments for each sample size n
    means = [np.mean(np.random.uniform(0, 1, n)) for _ in range(1000)]
    prob_far = np.mean(np.abs(np.array(means) - true_mean) >= epsilon)
    print(f"n={n}: P(|Xn - mu| >= 0.05) = {prob_far:.4f}")

# You will see the probability dropping to 0!
