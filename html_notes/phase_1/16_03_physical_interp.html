03. Physical Interpretation of the Derivative

The rate at which reality changes.

1. Velocity: The First Derivative

If \(s(t)\) is a position function that tells you where an object is at time \(t\), the derivative \(v(t) = s'(t)\) tells you its velocity (how fast its position is changing).

$$ v(t) = \frac{ds}{dt} = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{s(t+\Delta t) - s(t)}{\Delta t} $$

2. Acceleration: The Second Derivative

If velocity is changing, we have acceleration. This is the derivative of velocity, or the second derivative of position:

$$ a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t) = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2} $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Optimizers with "Momentum"

Velocity in Gradient Descent

In standard Gradient Descent, we update weights based on the current gradient (position change). However, modern optimizers like SGD with Momentum actually maintain a "velocity" vector. Instead of just looking at the slope at the current step, the optimizer "remembers" previous slopes, allowing it to accelerate through flat regions of the loss landscape.

Thinking of optimization as a physical ball rolling down a hill with acceleration and velocity makes it easier to understand why Momentum and Adam optimizers converge faster than basic SGD.

4. Python: Calculating Instantaneous Velocity

import sympy as sp

t = sp.Symbol('t')
# Position function: s(t) = 5t^2 + 2t
s = 5*t**2 + 2*t

# Velocity = ds/dt
v = sp.diff(s, t)
# Acceleration = dv/dt
a = sp.diff(v, t)

print(f"Position: {s}")
print(f"Velocity: {v}")      # Output: 10*t + 2
print(f"Acceleration: {a}")  # Output: 10
