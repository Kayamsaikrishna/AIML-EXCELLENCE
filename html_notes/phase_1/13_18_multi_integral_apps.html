Calculus 1.6 Moments

Applications: Centroids & Probability

Where is the center of the data?

Center of Mass

In physics, the Center of Mass \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\) is the balance point of an object. To find it, we calculate the "Moments" (weighted torque) and divide by total mass. \[ \bar{x} = \frac{1}{M} \iint_D x \cdot \rho(x, y) \, dA \] where \( \rho \) is the density.

In Statistics, this corresponds exactly to the Expected Value \( E[X] \) of a joint distribution.

2. AI/ML Connection: K-Means & Tracking

Finding the "Middle"

K-Means Clustering works by iteratively re-calculating the Centroid of a cluster of points. This is effectively calculating the center of mass of a discrete cloud of points.

In Computer Vision (Object Tracking), once an object is segmented (binary mask), we calculate its "Image Moment" to find its center \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\). This coordinate is fed into a Kalman Filter to predict where the object will move next. Any instability in calculating this integral leads to "jittery" tracking.

3. Python: Image Moments

find_centroid.py
import numpy as np
from scipy import ndimage

# Create a 10x10 "image" with a block of "mass"
image = np.zeros((10, 10))
image[2:5, 3:6] = 1 # A 3x3 square of pixels

# Calculate center of mass
# ndimage.center_of_mass uses numeric integration of array indices
cy, cx = ndimage.center_of_mass(image)

print(f"Centroid (y, x): ({cy}, {cx})")
# Expected: Center of 2,3,4 is 3.0; Center of 3,4,5 is 4.0
# Result: (3.0, 4.0)
