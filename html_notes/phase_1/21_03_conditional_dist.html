Prob 1.4 The Slice

Conditional Distributions

What happens to one variable when we fix the value of another?

Slicing the Mountain

Integration/Marginalization is like looking at the mountain's shadow. Conditioning is like taking a sharp knife and slicing through the mountain at a specific point (e.g., \(Y = y\)).

The shape of that slice is the conditional distribution. To make it a valid probability distribution, we must renormalize it so that the area under the slice is 1.

1. Discrete Case

The conditional PMF of \(X\) given \(Y=y\): $$ p_{X|Y}(x|y) = \frac{p(x, y)}{p_Y(y)} $$

Calculation: 1. Find the row/column for \(Y=y\). 2. Take the probability mass in that row/column. 3. Divide by the total probability of that row/column (the marginal).

2. Continuous Case

The conditional PDF of \(X\) given \(Y=y\): $$ f_{X|Y}(x|y) = \frac{f(x, y)}{f_Y(y)} $$

This is essentially Bayes' Theorem for continuous variables. The denominator \(f_Y(y)\) ensures that \(\int f_{X|Y}(x|y) dx = 1\).

3. ML Connection: Supervised Learning

Supervised Learning Deep Learning

Modeling $P(Y \mid X)$

Almost all supervised machine learning (Classification, Regression) is an attempt to model the Conditional Distribution of the label \(Y\) given the features \(X\).

- Regression: We predict the mean of the conditional distribution \(E[Y \mid X]\).
- Classification: We predict the probability of a class label given inputs \(P(Y=k \mid X)\) using a Softmax function.

Recommender Systems

"What is the probability that a user likes Movie B, given they liked Movie A?"
This is \(P(\text{Like}_B \mid \text{Like}_A)\). Collaborative filtering models spend their entire existence trying to approximate these conditional densities.

4. The Relation to Joint and Marginal

The Product Rule: $$ f(x, y) = f_{X|Y}(x|y) f_Y(y) $$

This is the fundamental building block of Probabilistic Graphical Models (like Bayesian Networks). It allows us to build complex joint distributions by specifying simple conditional dependencies.
