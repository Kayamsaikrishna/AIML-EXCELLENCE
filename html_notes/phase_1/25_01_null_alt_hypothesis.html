Null & Alternative Hypothesis

Setting the stage for a statistical "trial" where we decide which explanation for the data is more likely.

The Philosophical Stance

In Statistics, we start by assuming the "boring" explanation is true. We only change our minds if the evidence (data) is overwhelmingly against it.

Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\))

  • The "Status Quo" or "Boring" view.
  • Assumes no effect, no difference, or no change.
  • Example: "The new model is NOT better than the old one."
  • Symbol: \(H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2\)

Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_a\) or \(H_1\))

  • The researcher's claim or "Exciting" view.
  • Assumes there is an effect or difference.
  • Example: "The new model IS better than the old one."
  • Symbol: \(H_1: \mu_1 \neq \mu_2\) (or \(>\) or \(<\)).< /li>

1. The Two Outcomes

IMPORTANT TERMINOLOGY: Statisticians never say they "Accept the Null Hypothesis".

You only have two valid conclusions:
  1. Reject the Null: We found enough evidence for the Alternative.
  2. Fail to Reject the Null: We didn't find enough evidence to change our minds. (This doesn't mean the Null is true, just that we haven't proven it's false).

2. ML Connection: Model Testing

Research Evaluation

The Innovation Barrier

When a PhD student claims a new neural architecture is faster than the SOTA (State of the Art):

  • \(H_0\): The mean inference time of New Arch \(\ge\) Mean inference time of SOTA.
  • \(H_a\): The mean inference time of New Arch \(<\) Mean inference time of SOTA.

The burden of proof is on the student. If the data is only slightly faster, they "Fail to Reject the Null"—the difference might just be random noise in the GPU hardware.
