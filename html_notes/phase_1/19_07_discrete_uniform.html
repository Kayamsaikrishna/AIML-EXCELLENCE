Prob 1.4 Equality

The Discrete Uniform Distribution

When you know nothing, assume everything is equal.

The Principle of Insufficient Reason

If we have \(N\) possible outcomes and absolutely no reason to favor one over another (a "fair" die), logic dictates we assign equal probability to each.

This is the Maximum Entropy distribution. It assumes the least amount of information.

1. Definition

Let \(X\) take values in the set \(\{a, a+1, \dots, b\}\). The total number of outcomes is \(N = b - a + 1\). $$ P(X=k) = \frac{1}{N} $$

Example: Rolling a standard 6-sided die. \(P(X=k) = 1/6\) for \(k \in \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\).

2. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \frac{a+b}{2}\) (The exact middle).
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = \frac{N^2 - 1}{12}\).
    (Compare to Continuous Uniform variance \(\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}\)).

3. ML Connection: Initialization & Hashing

Cryptography Hash Maps

Ideally Uniform

Hash Functions: A good hash function maps independent inputs to buckets such that the distribution of items in buckets follows a Discrete Uniform distribution. If it's not uniform, we get collisions (clustering).

Random Seeds: When setting a random seed `random.seed(42)`, we are effectively selecting a starting point in a pseudo-random sequence that approximates a Uniform distribution.
