05. Convexity in Loss Functions

The geography of optimization.

1. Convex vs. Non-Convex

A function is Convex if a line segment between any two points on the graph lies above the graph. In simple terms: it's "Bowl-shaped".

$$ f(tx_1 + (1-t)x_2) \le tf(x_1) + (1-t)f(x_2) \quad \text{for } t \in [0, 1] $$

2. The Second Derivative Test

Mathematically, we check for convexity using the Hessian Matrix \(H\). If the Hessian is Positive Semi-Definite (all eigenvalues $\ge 0$), the surface is convex. For a convex function, any local minimum is guaranteed to be the Global Minimum.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Optimization Trap

Linear vs. Neural Networks

Optimization is "Easy" for convex problems. Linear Regression and SVMs (with certain kernels) are convex, meaning optimization will always find the "Best" possible model given enough time.

Neural Networks, however, are notoriously non-convex. Their loss surfaces are filled with local minima, saddle points, and plateaus. Calculus helps AI researchers design "Convex Relaxations" or utilize second-order information (like the Hessian) to steer through these traps. Understanding convexity is why we know that training a Neural Network is a "Heuristic" process that depends heavily on initialization, whereas training a Linear Regressor is a "Guarantee" process.
