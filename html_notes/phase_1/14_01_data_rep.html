01. Feature Space & Data Representation

Translating reality into vectors.

1. The Design Matrix (\(\mathbf{X}\))

In AI/ML, we organize data as follow:

$$ \mathbf{X} = \begin{bmatrix} x_{1,1} & x_{1,2} & \dots & x_{1,n} \\ x_{2,1} & x_{2,2} & \dots & x_{2,n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ x_{m,1} & x_{m,2} & \dots & x_{m,n} \end{bmatrix} $$
  • Rows (\(m\)): Observations (Samples).
  • Columns (\(n\)): Features (Variables).

2. Vectors in High-Dimensional Space

Every sample is a point in an \(n\)-dimensional Feature Space. Learning is mathematically finding a Hyperplane or a Manifold in this space that separates or represents the points accurately.

3. Scaling and Normalization

Because features usually have different units (e.g., age vs salary), their variances differ wildly. Using raw vectors often causes Gradient Descent to fail. Linear Algebra tools like Standard Scaling (Z-score) transform the feature space so that all axes have commensurate lengths.

4. Python: Loading a Feature Matrix

import numpy as np

# Height (cm), Weight (kg), Age
data = [
    [170, 70, 25],
    [180, 85, 30],
    [160, 60, 22]
]

X = np.array(data)

# Mean centering (Linear shift)
X_centered = X - np.mean(X, axis=0)

print(f"Centered Matrix:\n{X_centered}")
