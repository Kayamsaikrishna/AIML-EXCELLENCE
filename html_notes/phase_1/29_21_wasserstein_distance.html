Optimal Transport Stability

Wasserstein Distance (EMD)

The Earth Mover's Metric. Measuring distance in terms of the work required to transform one distribution into another, creating the smoothest gradients for AI.

The Vanishing Gradient Problem

Both KL and JS Divergence fail when distributions don't overlap. Their gradients become zero (or infinity), and your model stops learning. Wasserstein Distance fixes this: even if there is zero overlap, it provides a linear, meaningful "distance" that tells you exactly how far away you are.

1. The Intuition: Earth Mover's Distance

Imagine two distributions are piles of dirt. The Wasserstein distance is the minimum "cost" to move the dirt from pile \(P\) to pile \(Q\).

  • Work = Amount of Dirt \(\times\) Distance Moved.
  • If the piles are far apart, the cost is high. If they are close, the cost is low.
$$ W(P, Q) = \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(P, Q)} E_{(x,y) \sim \gamma} [\|x - y\|] $$

2. Mathematical Power

Continuity Everywhere

Unlike JSD, which jumps to a constant value when distributions are disjoint, Wasserstein is continuous. This means gradients are always available to guide the model.

Kantorovich-Rubinstein Duality

Calculating the "infimum" is hard. But we can solve the "dual" problem: finding a function that maximizes the difference in expected values while being 1-Lipschitz (smooth).

WGAN Stability

Wasserstein GANs

In a regular GAN, the discriminator outputs a probability. In a Wasserstein GAN (WGAN), the "critic" outputs a real number and must follow the 1-Lipschitz constraint (often enforced via weight clipping or gradient penalty).

  • Benefit 1: Training is vastly more stable.
  • Benefit 2: The loss value of the critic actually correlates with the quality of the generated images.
  • Benefit 3: Prevents "Mode Collapse" much more effectively than JS-based GANs.
