Graph Theory in AI (GNNs & Beyond)

From Adjacency to Intelligence. Exploring how modern AI systems leverage structural priors to learn from complex, non-Euclidean data.

The Non-Euclidean Frontier

Standard Deep Learning (CNNs, RNNs) works on grids or sequences. But the world is a graph. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) allow models to learn directly from the topology of molecules, social networks, and recommendation graphs by generalizing convolution to arbitrary connections.

1. Google's PageRank

The algorithm that built the web. It treats every webpage as a node and every hyperlink as a directed edge. The importance (rank) of a node is determined by the rank of the nodes pointing to it.

$$ PR(u) = \frac{1-d}{N} + d \sum_{v \in B_u} \frac{PR(v)}{L(v)} $$

Where \(d\) is a "damping factor" (probability of jumping to a random page). This is fundamentally an Eigenvector Centrality problem on a giant adjacency matrix.

2. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)

Message Passing (MPNNs)

Every node updates its hidden state by "aggregating" information from its immediate neighbors. After \(k\) layers, a node has information from its \(k\)-hop neighborhood.

Node Embeddings

Converting a node's structural and feature-based identity into a dense vector (like Word2Vec, but for Graphs). Famous examples: Node2Vec, GraphSAGE.

Bio-ML Recommender

Why AI researchers study Graphs

  • Drug Discovery: Molecules are graphs. GNNs predict if a "graph" (molecule) is toxic or can bind to a specific protein (another graph).
  • Dynamic Routing: AI for traffic prediction (Google Maps) uses graph theory to predict flow and congestion on the global road graph.
  • Knowledge Representation: LLMs are increasingly being augmented with **Knowledge Graphs (RAG)** to provide factual, structured context that a "bag of words" approach misses.
  • Causal Inference: Causal models are represented as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs). Understanding graph structure is the only way to answer "Why" something happened.
