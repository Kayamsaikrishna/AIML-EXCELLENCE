02. Test Statistic and Critical Value

The math that turns "Maybe" into "Yes" or "No."

1. The Test Statistic

A Test Statistic is a single numerical value calculated from your sample data. It measures how far your observation deviates from what we expect under the Null Hypothesis. The common forms are:

  • Z-score: Used for large samples or known variance.
  • t-score: Used for small samples with unknown variance.
  • Chi-Square: Used for categorical frequencies.

2. The Critical Value & Rejection Region

The Critical Value is the "Red Line" on the distribution curve. If your Test Statistic falls beyond this value (into the Rejection Region), you reject $H_0$. This value is determined by your chosen **Significance Level ($\alpha$)** (usually 0.05).

3. AI/ML Connection: Hard Constraints and Triggers

Setting the Threshold

In AI Engineering, Critical Values are the **Triggers** for automated actions.

In **Cybersecurity AI**, the Test Statistic might be the "Unexpected Volume of Outbound Traffic." The **Critical Value** is the threshold above which the AI automatically locks the network port. If the threshold is too low, you disrupt business (False Positives). If it's too high, you miss the hack (False Negatives). In **Self-Driving Cars**, the "Test Statistic" is the proximity to an obstacle. The **Critical Value** is the distance at which the emergency brake is triggered. Statistical theory ensures these thresholds aren't just arbitrary numbers—it allows us to say: "We set the brake threshold such that the probability of a false brake (due to sensor noise) is less than 0.1%."

4. Python: Calculating critical Z

from scipy import stats

# Goal: 95% Confidence (alpha = 0.05)
alpha = 0.05

# Two-tailed test critical values
z_crit_low = stats.norm.ppf(alpha/2)
z_crit_high = stats.norm.ppf(1 - alpha/2)

print(f"Rejection Regions: Z < {z_crit_low:.2f} or Z > {z_crit_high:.2f}")
# Result: Z < -1.96 or Z > 1.96
