04. Integration by Substitution (U-Substitution)

Reversing the Chain Rule.

1. The Concept

U-Substitution is a technique to solve integrals that contain a function and its derivative. It is based on the Chain Rule for differentiation.

$$ \int f(g(x))g'(x) dx = \int f(u) du \quad \text{where } u = g(x) $$

2. The Checklist

  1. Identify an "inner" function \(u\).
  2. Calculate its derivative \(\frac{du}{dx}\).
  3. Solve for \(dx\) or notice if \(du\) is already present in the integrand.
  4. Rewrite the whole integral in terms of \(u\).
  5. Integrate and then substitute back to get the final answer in terms of \(x\).

3. AI/ML Connection: Change of Variables

Normalizing Flows & Reparameterization

In Generative AI (Variational Autoencoders), we often need to transform a simple probability distribution (like a Normal/Gaussian distribution) into a complex one that matches real-world data (like a face or a cat). This is called a Change of Variables.

Mathematically, this involves the Jacobian determinant, which is the multi-dimensional version of the "u-substitution" rule. By performing substitution inside our probability integrals, we can "stretch" and "squeeze" distributions until they fit our training data perfectly. This is the secret sauce behind Normalizing Flows.

4. Python: U-Substitution Example

# Integral of 2x * exp(x^2)
# Let u = x^2, du = 2x dx
# Result should be exp(x^2) + C

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = 2*x * sp.exp(x**2)

# SymPy solves this using substitution internally
result = sp.integrate(f, x)

print(f"Integral is: {result} + C") 
# Output: exp(x**2) + C
