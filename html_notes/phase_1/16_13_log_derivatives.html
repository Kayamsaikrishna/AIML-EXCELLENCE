13. Derivatives of Logarithmic Functions

Turning multiplicative change into additive change.

1. The Natural Log Rule

The derivative of the natural logarithm is the reciprocal of the input.

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[\ln x] = \frac{1}{x} $$

With the Chain Rule (the most common form in AI):

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[\ln(u(x))] = \frac{u'(x)}{u(x)} $$

2. General Log Rule

For a logarithm with base \(a\):

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[\log_a x] = \frac{1}{x \ln a} $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Log-Loss & Cross Entropy

Gradient of Choice

Most classification models use Cross-Entropy Loss, which is defined using the natural log: \(L = -\sum y_{true} \ln(y_{pred})\).

When we calculate the gradient of this loss to train the model, we use the rule \(\frac{d}{dx}\ln(x) = 1/x\). This reciprocal derivative is why models penalize "overconfident" wrong answers so heavily—as the predicted probability \(y_{pred}\) approaches 0, the derivative \(1/y_{pred}\) approaches infinity, creating a massive push to correct the weights.

This is why Logarithmic Derivatives are the "brakes" and "accelerators" of probabilistic AI.

4. Python: Differentiating the Log

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# Derivative of ln(x^2 + 1)
f = sp.log(x**2 + 1)
f_prime = sp.diff(f, x)

print(f"Function: {f}")
print(f"Derivative: {f_prime}") 
# Output: 2*x/(x**2 + 1)
