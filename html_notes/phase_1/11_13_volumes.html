Calculus 1.4 3D Calculus

Volumes of Revolution

From 2D curves to 3D objects.

Slicing the Space

Just as area is a sum of thin rectangles, volume is a sum of thin 3D slices. If we rotate a function \(f(x)\) around an axis, the slices become circles ("disks").

1. The Disk Method

To find the volume formed by rotating the area under \(f(x)\) around the x-axis: \[ V = \int_a^b \pi [f(x)]^2 dx \]

Why? Because the area of each circular slice is \(\pi r^2\), where the radius \(r = f(x)\). Summing these areas (\(\int A(x) dx\)) gives Volume.

Washer Method: If there is a hole (outer graph \(R\), inner graph \(r\)): \[ V = \int \pi (R^2 - r^2) dx \]

2. AI/ML Connection: NeRFs & 3D Vision

Neural Radiance Fields

In NeRFs (Neural Radiance Fields), an AI model learns to represent a 3D scene as a continuous function \(F(x, y, z) \to (Color, Density)\).

To render a 2D image from this 3D model, the AI performs Volumetric Rendering, which is mathematically identical to a line integral of density along a viewing ray. While NeRFs integrate along a line, Rotational Volumes are the foundational geometric concept for 3D generative models that create symmetrical objects (like vases or faces) from a single profile curve.

3. Python: Volume of a Paraboloid

volume_calc.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')

# Function: y = sqrt(x)
# Rotate around x-axis from 0 to 4
# This creates a "Paraboloid" (like a satellite dish)

radius = sp.sqrt(x)
# Disk Area = pi * r^2 = pi * x
disk_area = sp.pi * radius**2

volume = sp.integrate(disk_area, (x, 0, 4))

print(f"Volume of Paraboloid: {volume}")
# Output: 8*pi
