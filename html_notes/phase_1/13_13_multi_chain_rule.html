05. Multivariable Chain Rule

Propagating influence across dependencies.

1. The Formula

If \(z = f(x, y)\) depends on \(x\) and \(y\), and both \(x\) and \(y\) depend on another variable \(t\), the total rate of change of \(z\) with respect to \(t\) is the sum of changes along all paths:

$$ \frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} \frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} \frac{dy}{dt} $$

2. Tree Diagrams

To solve complex chain rule problems involving many variables (e.g., \(x, y, u, v, t\)), physicists and AI researchers draw Dependency Trees. Each edge in the tree represents a partial derivative. To find the total derivative, you multiply along each path and add the paths together.

3. AI/ML Connection: Modern Backpropagation

Information Flow in Networks

Neural networks are not single lines; they are complex graphs with many parallel paths (like ResNets or Transformers). When computing the gradient of the loss, the AI must use the Multivariable Chain Rule to sum up the contributions from every single path that leads from a weight to the final outcome.

If a weight influences two different neurons in the next layer, the gradient "splits" and then "adds up" later. This Additive Property of the chain rule is why Deep Learning is possible—it allows us to attribute error correctly even in networks with billions of complex, interlocking connections.

4. Python: Symbolic Multi-Chain Rule

import sympy as sp

t = sp.Symbol('t')
x = sp.sin(t)
y = sp.exp(t)
z = x**2 + y**2

# Calculate dz/dt directly
total_deriv = sp.diff(z, t)

# Show it matches the Chain Rule sum
dz_dx = 2*x
dx_dt = sp.cos(t)
dz_dy = 2*y
dy_dt = sp.exp(t)

sum_rule = dz_dx * dx_dt + dz_dy * dy_dt

print(f"Total Deriv: {sp.simplify(total_deriv)}")
print(f"Chain Sum  : {sp.simplify(sum_rule)}")
