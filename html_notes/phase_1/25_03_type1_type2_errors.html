Stats 1.5 The Two Risks

Type I & II Errors

Understanding the cost of being wrong, whether you claim a discovery that isn't there, or miss one that is.

The Legal Analogy

The Null Hypothesis is "Innocent until proven guilty."

- Type I Error: Convicting an innocent person (False Positive).
- Type II Error: Letting a guilty person go free (False Negative).

2. The Inherent Trade-off

You cannot eliminate both errors. If you make it harder to reject the Null (to avoid Type I), you will naturally miss more real effects (increasing Type II).

Example: Spam Filter

- Type I: A valid email from your boss goes to Spam. (Very High Cost!)
- Type II: A junk email makes it to your Inbox. (Low Cost).

Because Type I is so much more expensive, we set a very strict threshold (\(\alpha\)), accepting that some spam will get through (\(\beta\)) as long as we never lose a real email.

3. ML Connection: Confusion Matrix

Classification Optimization

Precision vs. Recall

In Machine Learning, these errors are the bedrock of evaluation: - Type I (False Positive) leads to lower Precision. - Type II (False Negative) leads to lower Recall.

When you change the `threshold` of a Classifier (e.g., Logistic Regression), you are moving the Critical Value. As you slide the threshold up: 1. You get fewer False Positives (Type I \(\downarrow\), Precision \(\uparrow\)). 2. You get more False Negatives (Type II \(\uparrow\), Recall \(\downarrow\)).
