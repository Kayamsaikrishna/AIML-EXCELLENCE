08. The Multiplication Rule

Calculating the probability of sequential events.

1. Combining Dependent Events

While the addition rule handles "A OR B", the Multiplication Rule handles "A AND B". It describes the probability of two events occurring in sequence or simultaneously.

2. The General Formula

For any two events $A$ and $B$, the probability that both occur is the probability of the first times the probability of the second, *given* that the first already happened:

$$ P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B|A) $$

If the events are Independent (knowing A tells you nothing about B), then $P(B|A) = P(B)$, and the rule simplifies to: $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$.

3. AI/ML Connection: Sequential Models

Sentence Generation

How does a Large Language Model (like GPT) predict a sentence? It uses the Multiplication Rule.

The probability of a sentence "The cat sat" is:

$$ P(\text{The}, \text{cat}, \text{sat}) = P(\text{The}) \cdot P(\text{cat} | \text{The}) \cdot P(\text{sat} | \text{The cat}) $$

This "Chain Rule of Probability" (a repeated application of the multiplication rule) is the mathematical bedrock of Autoregressive Generation. Every time AI generates a token, it is multiplying the conditional probability of that token given all preceding tokens. Understanding this rule is essential for grasping how sequential data (speech, time-series, text) is modeled across all of machine learning.

4. Python: Dependency Demo

# Experiment: Two cards drawn WITHOUT replacement
# A: First is Red (26/52)
# B: Second is Red (25/51, given first was red)

p_a = 26/52
p_b_given_a = 25/51

p_both_red = p_a * p_b_given_a

print(f"P(Both Red): {p_both_red:.4f}")
print(f"P(Independent Approx): {(26/52)**2:.4f} (Incorrect!)")
