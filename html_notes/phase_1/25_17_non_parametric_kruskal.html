Stats 1.5 Multi-Rank

Kruskal-Wallis H-Test

Comparing three or more independent groups using ranks instead of means.

The Robust ANOVA

Just as the Wilcoxon is the alternative to the T-test, Kruskal-Wallis is the non-parametric alternative to One-Way ANOVA.

It tests if the groups come from the same distribution without assuming that distribution is Normal.

1. The Logic

1. Combine all data from all groups. 2. Rank them from 1 to \(N\). 3. Sum the ranks for each group. 4. Calculate the \(H\) statistic (measures the "gap" between rank sums).

If one group consistently has the largest values in the dataset, its rank sum will be uniquely high, resulting in a low P-value.

2. Why choose Kruskal-Wallis?

  • Heteroscedasticity: Your groups have very different variances.
  • Skewed Data: Your data has "Long Tails" (e.g., income, house prices, latency).
  • Small Datasets: When you can't verify Normality at all.

3. ML Connection: Benchmark Aggregation

Research NLP

Aggregating across heterogeneous tasks

You are comparing 3 Large Language Models (GPT-4, Claude 3, Llama 3) across 50 different coding tasks. Each task has a different difficulty (some take 10s to solve, some take 300s).

The Problem: You can't just average the times, because the 300s tasks will dominate the mean, drowning out the performance on the 10s tasks.

The Solution: Use Kruskal-Wallis. It converts the times into Ranks within each task. It doesn't matter if a task took 10s or 300s; being 1st place always gives the best rank. This provides a fair, robust comparison of the 3 models across the entire suite of tasks.
