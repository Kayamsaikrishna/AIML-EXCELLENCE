Graph Theory Hierarchy

Trees, Forests & Graph Isomorphism

Order without Chaos. Exploring the unique class of graphs that represent minimal connectivity and the deep mathematical question of when two graphs are 'the same'.

The Tree Constraint

A Tree is a connected graph with no cycles. This single property creates a rigid, hierarchical structure where any two nodes are connected by exactly one path. A collection of disjoint trees is a Forest.

1. Tree Properties

A tree with \(n\) vertices must have exactly \(n-1\) edges.

Rooted Trees

A tree where one node is designated as the 'root'. This introduces the concept of Parent, Child, and Leaf nodes.

Distance & Diameter

The Distance between two nodes is the length of the unique path. The Diameter is the maximum distance in the tree.

2. Graph Isomorphism

Two graphs \(G\) and \(H\) are Isomorphic (\(G \cong H\)) if there is a bijection between their vertex sets that preserves adjacency. In simple terms: they are the same graph, just drawn differently.

Checking for Isomorphism: It is difficult to prove two graphs *are* isomorphic (this is a complex computational problem), but easy to prove they are *not* by looking for Invariants:

  • Do they have the same number of vertices/edges?
  • Do they have the same degree sequence?
  • Do they both have cycles of the same length?
NLP ML Architectures

Hierarchies in AI

  • Decision Trees: The most famous application. Using a tree structure to split data until a leaf (prediction) is reached.
  • Hierarchical Clustering: Building a "Dendrogram" (a binary tree) to represent the hierarchy of data clusters based on distance.
  • Tree-LSTMs: A variation of RNNs that operates on tree-structured data (like natural language parse trees) rather than linear sequences.
  • Prompt Engineering: When we organize complex prompts into "nested instructions," we are building a cognitive tree for the LLM to follow.
  • Isomorphism in Databases: Graph Databases use subgraph isomorphism to find patterns (e.g., "Find all users who bought the same 3 items as User A").
