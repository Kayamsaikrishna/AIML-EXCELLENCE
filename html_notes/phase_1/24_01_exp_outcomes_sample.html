01. Experiments, Outcomes, and Sample Spaces

Defining the boundaries of possibility.

1. The Random Experiment

A Random Experiment is a process that leads to one of several possible observations. While we know what *could* happen, we don't know exactly what *will* happen until the experiment is complete.

  • Outcomes: The individual results of an experiment.
  • Sample Space (\(S\)): The set of all possible outcomes.

2. Formal Definition

If we toss a coin, the Sample Space is \(S = \{H, T\}\).

If we roll a six-sided die, \(S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}\).

Sample spaces can be Discrete (finite or countably infinite) or Continuous (like the range of possible temperatures).

3. AI/ML Connection: The Feature Space

The Data Universe

In Machine Learning, we don't just "toss coins". Our "Experiment" is Sampling a Data Point from the real world.

If we are building a model to detect spam, our Sample Space \(S\) is the set of all possible email text strings. If we are predicting house prices, our Sample Space involves all possible combinations of square footage, location, and age. Understanding the Sample Space allows AI Engineers to define the Domain of their model. If a model encounters an input outside its defined sample space (an "Out-of-Distribution" sample), it will likely fail. Probability starts by defining exactly what exists in our "Data Universe".

4. Python: Defining a Discrete Space

import itertools

# Experiment: Roll two 4-sided dice
sides = [1, 2, 3, 4]
sample_space = list(itertools.product(sides, repeat=2))

print(f"Sample Space Size: {len(sample_space)}")
print(f"First 5 Outcomes: {sample_space[:5]}")
