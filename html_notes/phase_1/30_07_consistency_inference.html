07. Consistency

Will you eventually find the truth if you have enough data?

1. Definition

An estimator $\hat{\theta}_n$ is Consistent if it converges in probability to the true parameter $\theta$ as the sample size $n$ goes to infinity.

$$ P(|\hat{\theta}_n - \theta| > \epsilon) \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty $$

In other words, the "shakiness" of the estimator must vanish as we collect more information.

2. The "Perfect Guess" Limit

Consistency is a Minimum Requirement for any good estimation rule. If an estimator is not consistent, it is "systematically broken"—no matter how many billions of rows of data you have, it will never yield the correct result. Consistency ensures that our AI models actually learn the underlying patterns rather than getting stuck on irrelevant noise.

3. AI/ML Connection: Convergence and Data Scaling

The Justification for Big Data

In AI, Consistency is the bridge between the Training Set and the World.

When you train a Logistics Regression or a Linear Model, the standard training algorithms (like MLE) are mathematically proven to be Consistent. This means that as your dataset grows, your model's weights must eventually hit the "True" weights of the data-generating process. This gives research scientists the confidence to invest in "Large-Scale Crawling" or "Data Mining"—the theorem guarantees that the more data we see, the more "Accurate" our model's internal representation becomes. Consistency also applies to Performance Evaluation: it ensures that your validation score becomes a more and more reliable estimate of the real-world deployment performance as you increase the size of your test set.

4. Python: Scaling Data, Reducing Error

# Demonstrating that the Error of the Mean goes to zero
true_mu = 50.0
sample_sizes = [10, 100, 1000, 10000]

errors = []
for n in sample_sizes:
    sample = np.random.normal(true_mu, 10, n)
    estimate = np.mean(sample)
    errors.append(abs(estimate - true_mu))

plt.plot(sample_sizes, errors, marker='o')
plt.xscale('log')
plt.title("Consistency: Error vanishes as n increases")
plt.show()
