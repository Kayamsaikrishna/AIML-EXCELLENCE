Prob 1.4 Tighter Bounds

Chebyshev's Inequality

Using variance to quantify how "tightly" a variable clusters around its mean.

Adding Variance to the Mix

Markov's inequality only used the mean. Chebyshev's Inequality uses both the Mean and the Variance.

It works for any distribution (even those that take negative values) and provides a much stronger ceiling on the probability of "extreme" events.

1. The Formula

For any random variable \(X\) with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), and any \(k > 0\): $$ P(|X - \mu| \ge k\sigma) \le \frac{1}{k^2} $$

The \(k\)-sigma rule: - The probability of being more than 2 standard deviations away is \(\le 1/2^2 = 25\%\). - The probability of being more than 3 standard deviations away is \(\le 1/3^2 \approx 11\%\). - The probability of being more than 10 standard deviations away is \(\le 1/10^2 = 1\%\).

2. Comparison with Normal Distribution

For a Normal Distribution, the probability of being outside \(3\sigma\) is only 0.3%. Chebyshev says it is \(\le 11\%\).

Universal Guarantee

Chebyshev is much looser than the Normal rule, but it is always true. Even if the distribution is skewed, multimodal, or has long tails, it cannot break this rule as long as it has a finite variance.

3. ML Connection: Anomaly Detection

Unsupervised Robustness

The Outlier Ceiling

When we don't know the distribution of our data (which is almost always the case in real-world ML), we can't use Z-scores reliably.

However, we can use Chebyshev to provide a "safety threshold". If we flag any data point that is more than $10\sigma$ from the mean as an "Outlier", we are guaranteed that we will flag at most 1% of the data, regardless of how "weird" the distribution is.

4. Proof Sketch (Markov's Child)

Chebyshev is actually just Markov's Inequality applied to the squared deviation: $$ P(|X-\mu| \ge a) = P((X-\mu)^2 \ge a^2) \le \frac{E[(X-\mu)^2]}{a^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{a^2} $$ Setting \(a = k\sigma\) gives the familiar \(1/k^2\) result.
