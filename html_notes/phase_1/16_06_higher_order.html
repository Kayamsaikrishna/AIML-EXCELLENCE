06. Higher-Order Derivatives

Differentiation of derivatives.

1. Definitions and Notation

If \(f'(x)\) is the "rate of change" (velocity), then the derivative of the derivative \(f''(x)\) is the "rate of change of the rate" (acceleration/curvature).

$$ f''(x) = \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}, \quad f'''(x) = \frac{d^3y}{dx^3}, \quad f^{(n)}(x) = \text{n-th derivative} $$

2. Curvature Interpretation

  • \(f''(x) > 0\): Function is Concave Up (holding water).
  • \(f''(x) < 0\): Function is Concave Down (shedding water).

3. AI Connection: Second-Order Optimizers

The Hessian Road

Standard Gradient Descent only uses the first derivative. It's like walking downhill while looking purely at your feet. Newton's Method and L-BFGS use the second derivative (Curvature) to jump further where the surface is flat and slow down where it's curvy. Higher-order derivatives provide the "topography" needed for efficient training.

4. Python: N-th Order Diff

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = sp.exp(x) * x**4

# 4th derivative
df4 = sp.diff(f, x, 4)

print(f"f(x)   = {f}")
print(f"f''''(x) = {sp.simplify(df4)}")
