04. Differentiation Notation

Different ways to say "rate of change".

1. Common Notations

Calculus was co-invented, leading to multiple ways of writing a derivative. In AI/ML, you will encounter all of these.

A. Leibniz Notation ($\frac{dy}{dx}$)

Uses fractions to suggest "change in y over change in x". Highly useful for the chain rule and integration variables.

$$ \frac{dy}{dx}, \quad \frac{d}{dx}[f(x)] $$

B. Lagrange Notation ($f'(x)$)

Uses a "prime" symbol. Very concise, great for simple functions and single-variable problems.

$$ f'(x), \quad f''(x) $$

C. Newton Notation ($\dot{x}$)

Uses a dot on top of the variable. Almost exclusively used in Physics and Control Theory (robotics) for derivatives with respect to time.

$$ \dot{x} = \frac{dx}{dt} $$

D. Euler Notation ($D_x f$)

Uses a capital D. Often used in operator theory and differential equations.

$$ D_x f(x) $$

2. Why Notation Matters

Leibniz notation is usually preferred when performing complex derivation because the "fractional" look helps you track units and verify the Chain Rule via "cancellation" of differentials.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Nabla ($\nabla$)

The Gradient Operator

In Deep Learning, we deal with thousands of variables. We don't write \(\frac{df}{dx_1}, \frac{df}{dx_2}, \dots\). Instead, we use the Nabla operator \(\nabla\). This single symbol represents a vector of all possible first derivatives (the Gradient).

$$ \nabla f(w) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_1} \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_2} \end{bmatrix} $$

Understanding notation is often the biggest hurdle to reading AI research papers!
