15. Gibbs Sampling

Sampling one dimension at a time.

1. The "Conditional" Strategy

Gibbs Sampling is a specific MCMC algorithm designed for multivariate problems. Instead of proposing a move in all directions at once (which is likely to fail), Gibbs updates each parameter one by one, sampling from its Conditional Distribution while keeping the others fixed.

$$ \theta_1^{(t+1)} \sim P(\theta_1 | \theta_2^{(t)}, \theta_3^{(t)} \dots, D) $$

2. The Advantage

Gibbs is incredibly efficient when the conditional distributions are easy to sample from (e.g., in hierarchical models). It has an acceptance rate of 100%—every single move is accepted because it respects the current local structure of the posterior.

3. AI/ML Connection: Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)

Uncovering Hidden Themes

In AI, Gibbs Sampling is the heartbeat of Probabilistic Topic Modeling.

In **Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)**, we want to know two things: which "Topic" a word belongs to, and which "Topic Mixture" a document has. This is a massive high-dimensional problem. **Gibbs Sampling** solves it by iterating: "Assuming Topic 2 is 'Politics', how likely is the word 'Vote'? Given that 'Vote' appeared 10 times, how much of this document is 'Politics'?". By cycling millions of times, the AI creates a coherent map of human knowledge from raw text. This is how Content Recommendation Systems** understand the "Spirit" of an article without just looking at keywords. It's the engine of automated cataloging and knowledge discovery.

4. Python: The Gibbs Loop

# Sampling a Bivariate Normal using Gibbs
theta1, theta2 = 0, 0
samples = []

for i in range(1000):
    # Sample theta1 | theta2
    theta1 = np.random.normal(rho * theta2, np.sqrt(1 - rho**2))
    # Sample theta2 | theta1
    theta2 = np.random.normal(rho * theta1, np.sqrt(1 - rho**2))
    samples.append((theta1, theta2))

# This sequence effectively maps the 2D joint distribution.
