Calculus 1.3 Language

The Language of Change

Calculus was discovered independently by two rival geniuses. We inherited both of their languages.

Why multiple notations?

Different notations stress different aspects. Some stress logical flow (Chain Rule), some stress brevity, and some stress vectorization. In AI, you must be fluent in Leibniz and Vector Calculus notation.

1. The Big Four

Leibniz: \(\frac{dy}{dx}\)

The Engineer's Choice. Treats derivatives like fractions (which they are, in the limit). Makes the Chain Rule intuitive via "cancellation".

\[ \frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} \]

Lagrange: \(f'(x)\)

The Mathematician's Choice. Clean, uncluttered. Best for generic single-variable functions where the input variable is obvious.

Newton: \(\dot{x}\)

The Physicist's Choice. The dot specifically means "derivative with respect to time". Used in Robotics and Reinforcement Learning dynamics.

Partial: \(\partial\) vs \(d\)

The AI Standard. \(\partial\) (curly 'd') means "change one input, hold others constant." \(d\) means total change.

2. AI/ML Connection: The Nabla ($\nabla$)

The Gradient Vector

In Deep Learning, our "function" is a Neural Net with 1 Billion weights. Writing 1 Billion partial derivatives is impossible.

We use the Nabla operator \(\nabla\) to pack all those partials into a single vector (or tensor).

\[ \nabla f(\mathbf{w}) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_1} \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_2} \\ \vdots \\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial w_n} \end{bmatrix} \]

This allows us to write the update rule for all 1 Billion weights in one line: \[ \mathbf{w}_{new} = \mathbf{w}_{old} - \alpha \nabla f(\mathbf{w}) \]
