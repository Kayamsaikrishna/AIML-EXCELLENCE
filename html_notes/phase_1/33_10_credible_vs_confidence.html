10. Credible vs. Confidence Intervals

Why the Bayesian interval says what you actually want it to say.

1. The Semantic Gap

One of the biggest sources of confusion in statistics is the interpretation of intervals.

  • Frequentist Confidence Interval: "If I repeat this experiment 100 times, 95 of the intervals will contain the true (fixed) parameter." You *cannot* say there is a 95% chance the parameter is in *this specific* interval.
  • Bayesian Credible Interval: "There is a 95% probability that the (random) parameter lies within this specific range." This is exactly what most people think a confidence interval is.

2. Highest Posterior Density (HPD)

A common type of Credible Interval is the HPD Interval. It is the shortest possible interval that contains 95% of the posterior mass. Every point inside an HPD interval has a higher probability density than any point outside of it.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Trust and Safety Critical Systems

Explaining Uncertainty to Stakeholders

In AI, the Credible Interval is the Human-Centric uncertainty metric.

If you are building an AI for Medical Diagnosis, showing a Confidence Interval is confusing for doctors. They don't care about "Repeating the experiment 100 times." They care about the current patient. A **Credible Interval** allows the AI to say: "I am 95% certain that this patient's probability of disease is between 0.62 and 0.75." This is intuitive, actionable, and legally defensible. In Self-Driving Cars**, Credible Intervals are used to calculate "Collision Probability." The car creates an HPD interval for the future position of a pedestrian. If the road overlaps with that 95% Credible Interval, the car brakes. It is the mathematical way of saying: "The pedestrian is *probably* in this box."

4. Python: Calculating HPD

import arviz as az

# Samples from our Posterior distribution (e.g., from MCMC)
samples = np.random.normal(5, 1, 1000)

# Calculate 95% Highest Density Interval (HDI)
hdi = az.hdi(samples, hdi_prob=0.95)

print(f"95% Credible Interval (HDI): {hdi}")
# This literally means we are 95% sure the parameter is here.
