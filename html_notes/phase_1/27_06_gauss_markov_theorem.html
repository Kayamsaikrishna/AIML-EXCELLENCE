Stats 1.7 The Blue Proof

Gauss-Markov Theorem

Proving that OLS is the "Best Linear Unbiased Estimator" (B.L.U.E.) under specific conditions.

What is "Best"?

In statistics, "Best" simply means Efficiency. An estimator is Best if it has the **lowest possible variance** among all unbiased linear estimators.

1. The 5 Core Assumptions

For the theorem to hold, the world must look like this:

1. Linearity: The relationship is theoretically linear.
2. Full Rank: No independent variable is a perfect combo of others (No multicollinearity).
3. Exogeneity: \(E[\epsilon | X] = 0\). The error is random, not correlated with features.
4. Homoscedasticity: Var(\(\epsilon\)) is constant. The noise doesn't grow as X grows.
5. No Autocorrelation: Errors are independent of each other (Critical for Time Series).

2. BLUE Explained

Best

Minimum variance (Tightest interval).

Linear

A linear combination of the data (\(\mathbf{y}\)).

Unbiased

On average, it gets the true parameters exactly right (\(E[\hat{\beta}] = \beta\)).

Estimator

A mathematical rule applied to data.

3. ML Connection: Why we optimize Bias vs Variance

Deep Learning Optimization

The End of BLUE

In modern Machine Learning, we often Reject BLUE.

Wait, why? Because Gauss-Markov only says OLS is best among unbiased estimators. In ML (Random Forests, XGBoost, Neural Nets), we often introduce a little bit of Bias (Regularization) because it drastically reduces Variance.

The Gauss-Markov theorem is the "Golden Age" of classic stats, but ML is about searching for the Minimum MSE (Mean Squared Error), which can often be found by a biased model that generalizes better to new data than a BLUE model.
