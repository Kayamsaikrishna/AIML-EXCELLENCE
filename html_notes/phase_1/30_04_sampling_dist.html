04. Sampling Distributions

Understanding the probability of your "Guess."

1. Definition

A Sampling Distribution is the probability distribution of a statistic (like the sample mean or sample variance) calculated from a large number of independent samples drawn from the same population.

$$ \hat{\theta} \sim f(\hat{\theta} \mid \theta) $$

While the **Population Distribution** describes individuals, the **Sampling Distribution** describes how much our "Estimate" would wiggle if we repeated our experiment 1,000 times.

2. Standard Error (SE)

The standard deviation of a sampling distribution is called the Standard Error. It measures the precision of our estimator. A small SE means that no matter which sample we pick, the result will be almost the same; a large SE means our estimator is "shaky" and unreliable.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Evaluation and Cross-Validation

Is your AI just lucky?

In AI, the Sampling Distribution is why we use Cross-Validation.

When you train a model on a dataset, the "Accuracy" you get is a Statistic. If you had a different dataset (a different sample from the same population), you would get a different accuracy. The distribution of these accuracies across different Folds of Cross-Validation is the Sampling Distribution of the model's performance. If your accuracy ranges from 70% to 95% across folds, your sampling distribution has high variance, meaning your model is not stable. Understanding this helps you move beyond reporting a single "Lucky" number and instead provide a Mean and Standard Deviation of performance, which is the standard for professional AI research.

4. Python: Simulating the Sampling Dist

# Population: 10,000 values
pop = np.random.uniform(0, 100, 10000)

# Goal: Estimate the mean
# Repeatedly take samples of size 30 and store their means
sample_means = []
for _ in range(1000):
    s = np.random.choice(pop, size=30)
    sample_means.append(np.mean(s))

plt.hist(sample_means, bins=30, color='skyblue', edgecolor='black')
plt.title("Sampling Distribution of the Mean")
plt.xlabel("Sample Mean Value")
plt.show()
