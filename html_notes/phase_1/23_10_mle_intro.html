Stats 1.4 The Best Fit

Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

Asking the question: "Which parameter makes the data I just saw most probable?"

The Likelihood Perspective

In probability, we calculate the chance of data given a parameter: \(P(\text{Data} \mid \theta)\). In MLE, we reverse this. We observe the data, and we treat the result as a function of \(\theta\). This is the Likelihood Function \(L(\theta)\).

1. The Goal

Find the \(\theta\) that maximizes the Likelihood: $$ \hat{\theta}_{MLE} = \text{arg max}_{\theta} L(\theta) $$

2. The Log-Likelihood Trick

Calculating products of thousands of tiny probabilities leads to numerical underflow. To solve this, we maximize the Log-Likelihood \(\ell(\theta) = \ln L(\theta)\).

Why Log?

  1. Log turns products into sums (easier to differentiate).
  2. Log is a monotonic function, so the max of \(\ell(\theta)\) is at the same location as the max of \(L(\theta)\).

3. ML Connection: Cost Functions

Deep Learning Loss Minimization

Binary Cross Entropy = MLE

In Deep Learning, we don't usually talk about "maximizing likelihood". We talk about minimizing Loss.

It turns out that Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss is just the Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) of a Bernoulli distribution. - Maximizing \(L(\theta)\) is the same as minimizing \(- \ln L(\theta)\).

Every time you train a Neural Network with BCE or MSE, you are performing Maximum Likelihood Estimation!
