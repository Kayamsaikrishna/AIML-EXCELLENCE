1. Definition

Every square matrix \( A \) can be unitarily triangulated:

$$ A = Q T Q^H $$
  • \( Q \): Unitary Matrix (Complex Orthogonal).
  • \( T \): Upper Triangular. The diagonal entries of T are the eigenvalues of A.

Unlike diagonalization (which fails for defective matrices), Schur always exists.

2. The QR Algorithm

The standard algorithm to find eigenvalues (the QR Algorithm) actually computes the Schur Decomposition.

It iteratively converges \( A \) to the triangular form \( T \). Once we have \( T \), the eigenvalues represent the natural frequencies or stability modes of the system.

3. SciPy Schur

Computing T and Q.

import numpy as np
import scipy.linalg as la

# A non-symmetric matrix
A = np.array([
    [5, 2],
    [-2, 5]
]) # Eigenvalues are 5+2i, 5-2i

# Compute Schur
T, Z = la.schur(A, output='complex')

print("Upper Triangular T:")
print(T.round(2))
# Diagonal should be [5+2j, 5-2j]

print("\nUnitary Z:")
print(Z.round(2))

print("\nCheck A = Z T Z.H:")
print((Z @ T @ Z.conj().T).real.round(2))
