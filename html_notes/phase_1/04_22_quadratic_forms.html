22. Quadratic Forms

The multi-variable version of \(ax^2\).

1. Algebraic Structure

A Quadratic Form is a scalar-valued function defined by a symmetric matrix \(\mathbf{A}\):

$$ q(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} $$

Example in 2D:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} x & y \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = ax^2 + 2bxy + cy^2 $$

2. The Principal Axes Theorem

Every quadratic form can be diagonalized into a "sum of squares" by changing to the eigenvector basis (\(\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{Py}\)). This removes the cross-term (\(2bxy\)).

$$ q(\mathbf{y}) = \lambda_1 y_1^2 + \lambda_2 y_2^2 + \dots + \lambda_n y_n^2 $$

3. Visualization: The Topography of Loss

In Machine Learning, we often look at the level sets \(q(\mathbf{x}) = c\). These form ellipsoids in higher dimensions. The shape of these ellipsoids (determined by eigenvalues) directly affects how fast Gradient Descent converges.

4. Python: Contouring a Quadratic Form

import numpy as np

# A matrix defining the quadratic bowl
A = np.array([[3, 1], [1, 2]])

def q(x, y):
    vec = np.array([x, y])
    return vec.T @ A @ vec

# Calculate value at a point
print(f"Value at (1, 1): {q(1, 1)}")

# Eigenvalues determine the "steepness"
vals = np.linalg.eigvals(A)
print(f"Steepness along principal axes: {vals}")
