Stats 1.6 Evidence

The Likelihood Function

Measuring how well a specific set of parameters explains the data we actually saw.

Probability vs. Likelihood

They look the same (\(P(D|\theta)\)), but the perspective is flipped:

- Probability: \(\theta\) is constant. We predict different Data outcomes. (Area under curve = 1). - Likelihood: Data is constant. We try different \(\theta\) values to see which one "fits" best. (Does NOT sum to 1).

1. Definition

$$ L(\theta|D) = P(D|\theta) $$

If you flip a coin 10 times and get 8 heads, the Likelihood function tells you which coin bias (\(\theta = 0.5, 0.8, 1.0\)) makes that "8 heads" result most probable. - For \(\theta = 0.5\), the chance is low. - For \(\theta = 0.8\), the likelihood is at its peak.

2. The Multiplication Rule

For independent data points (\(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\)), the total likelihood is the product:

$$ L(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(x_i | \theta) $$

Because products of small probabilities turn into tiny numbers (0.00000001), computers use the Log-Likelihood instead:

$$ \log L(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log P(x_i | \theta) $$

3. ML Connection: Cross-Entropy Loss

Supervised Optimization

Loss is Negative Log-Likelihood

In Classification, why do we use Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)?

$$ \text{Loss} = -[y \log(\hat{y}) + (1-y) \log(1-\hat{y})] $$

This isn't just a random formula. It is the Negative Log-Likelihood of a Bernoulli distribution! When your model minimizes Cross-Entropy, it is mathematically Maximizing the Likelihood of the training data. Every time you run `model.compile(loss='categorical_crossentropy')`, you are doing Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) under the hood.
