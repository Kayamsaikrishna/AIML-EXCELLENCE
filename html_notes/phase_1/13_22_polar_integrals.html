04. Double Integrals in Polar Coordinates

Solving circular problems with circular tools.

1. Why Polar?

If your domain of integration is a circle or a sector, using \(x\) and \(y\) is extremely difficult. Converting to Polar Coordinates (\(r, \theta\)) makes the limits simple constants.

$$ x = r \cos \theta, \quad y = r \sin \theta, \quad x^2 + y^2 = r^2 $$

2. The Formula (Don't forget the r!)

When you switch to polar, the area element \(dA\) changes. Because the grid "stretches" as you move further from the origin, you must include an extra r factor:

$$ \iint_{R} f(x, y) dA = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \int_{r_1}^{r_2} f(r, \theta) \, \mathbf{r} \, dr \, d\theta $$

3. AI/ML Connection: The Gaussian Integral

The Heart of Statistics

The Normal (Gaussian) Distribution is defined by the function \(e^{-x^2}\). This integral is actually "unsolvable" using standard single-variable rules. To solve for the constant that makes the total probability 1, we must square the integral, move to 2D, and then switch to Polar Coordinates.

$$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-(x^2+y^2)} dx dy = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-r^2} \mathbf{r} dr d\theta = \pi $$

Without the trick of Polar Integration, we wouldn't be able to define the Standard Normal distribution, meaning we couldn't perform Standardization (Scaling features to Mean 0, Variance 1) which is a crucial pre-processing step for almost every AI model.

4. Python: Radial Integration

import sympy as sp

r, theta = sp.symbols('r theta')
# Area of a unit circle: integrate r dr d_theta 
# r: 0->1, theta: 0->2*pi
# Result should be pi * 1^2 = pi

area = sp.integrate(sp.integrate(r, (r, 0, 1)), (theta, 0, 2*sp.pi))

print(f"Area of unit circle using Polar Integration: {area}")
