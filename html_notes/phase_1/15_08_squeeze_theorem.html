08. The Squeeze (Sandwich) Theorem

Solving difficult limits by trapping them.

1. The Theorem

Suppose that for all \(x\) near \(a\) (except possibly at \(a\)):

$$ g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x) $$

If \(\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = L\) and \(\lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L\), then:

$$ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L $$

2. Classic Example

Find \(\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin(1/x)\). Since \(-1 \leq \sin \leq 1\), we can "squeeze" it:

$$ -x^2 \leq x^2 \sin(1/x) \leq x^2 $$

Since both \(-x^2\) and \(x^2\) go to 0 as \(x \to 0\), the middle function must also go to 0.

3. AI Connection: Convergence Rate Bounds

In Optimization Theory, we often can't calculate the exact error at every step. Instead, we prove that the error is Bounded above and below by simpler sequences (like \(1/t\) or \(e^{-t}\)). By showing both bounds go to zero, we "squeeze" the optimizer's error to zero, proving it works reliably.

4. Python: Visualizing the Squeeze

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

x = np.linspace(-0.5, 0.5, 500)
x = x[x != 0]

f = x**2 * np.sin(1/x)
upper = x**2
lower = -x**2

plt.plot(x, f, label='f(x) = x^2 sin(1/x)')
plt.plot(x, upper, 'r--', label='Upper Bound x^2')
plt.plot(x, lower, 'r--', label='Lower Bound -x^2')
plt.title("Squeeze Theorem Visualization")
plt.legend()
plt.show()
