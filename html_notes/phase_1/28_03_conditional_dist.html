03. Conditional Probability Distribution

Focusing on what happens given specific information.

1. Definition

The Conditional Distribution of a random variable given another is the probability distribution of $X$ when $Y$ is fixed at a particular value $y$.

$$ f(x \mid y) = \frac{f(x, y)}{f_Y(y)} \quad \text{provided } f_Y(y) > 0 $$

Essentially, it is a "slice" of the joint distribution, re-normalized so that its area equals 1.

2. Intuition: The Constrained View

If $f(x, y)$ is a map of where people live (X=Latitude, Y=Longitude), $f(x \mid Y=40)$ is the distribution of people along a specific line of latitude. It answers: "Given that we are at this exact Y, how is X distributed?"

3. AI/ML Connection: The Core of Prediction

Prediction is Conditional Probability

Every Supervised Learning model is an attempt to estimate a Conditional Distribution.

When you provide an AI with features $\mathbf{x}$ and ask for a label $y$, you are asking the AI for $P(y \mid \mathbf{x})$. In **Image Classification**, the AI calculates the probability of "Cat" given the "Pixels." In **Language Models** (like GPT), the entire model is a massive conditional probability engine predicting $P(\text{next token} \mid \text{previous context})$. Every "Prompt" you write is just a way of setting the "Given" $(y)$ in a conditional distribution, forcing the AI to sample its response from a specific, narrowed-down subspace of everything it knows. Understanding this makes "Prompt Engineering" a mathematical exercise in conditioning.

4. Python: Slicing a Joint Matrix

# Assuming the same joint_pmf from 28_02
#        Y=0   Y=1
# X=0 [[0.1, 0.2],
# X=1  [0.3, 0.4]]

# Given Y=1, what is the distribution of X?
# f(x | Y=1) = f(x, 1) / f_Y(1)
f_Y1 = 0.2 + 0.4 # 0.6
dist_given_Y1 = joint_pmf[:, 1] / f_Y1

print(f"P(X | Y=1): {dist_given_Y1}") # [0.333, 0.666]
print(f"Sum: {np.sum(dist_given_Y1)}") # Should be 1.0
