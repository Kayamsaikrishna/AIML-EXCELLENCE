05. Expectation of Multiple Random Variables

Linearity: The superhuman power of expectations.

1. Expected Value of a Function

For a function $g(X, Y)$ of two random variables, the expected value is calculated by weighting the function output by the joint probability:

$$ E[g(X, Y)] = \iint g(x, y) f(x, y) dx dy $$

2. Linearity of Expectation

The most important property in multivariate probability: The expectation of a sum is the sum of the expectations, regardless of independence.

$$ E[aX + bY + c] = aE[X] + bE[Y] + c $$

This property is the foundation for scaling calculations in complex systems.

3. AI/ML Connection: Layer Sums and Dropout

Scaling Up AI Training

Linearity of Expectation is what allows us to train Deep Neural Networks efficiently.

In a **Neural Network Layer**, the output is a weighted sum of inputs: $z = \sum w_i x_i + b$. Because of linearity, we can calculate the expected activation of a neuron even if the inputs are complex and noisy. This is also how **Dropout** regularization works: we randomly "drop" neurons during training, but we scale the remaining weights by $1/p$ (the probability of survival) so that the Expected Value of the layer sum remains constant. Without linearity of expectation, the math of backpropagation would be impossibly complex to compute for models with billions of variables.

4. Python: Simulating Linearity

# Even if X and Y are correlated, E[X+Y] = E[X] + E[Y]
x = np.random.normal(5, 1, 1000)
y = x * 2 + np.random.normal(0, 1, 1000) # Heavy correlation

e_x = np.mean(x)
e_y = np.mean(y)
e_sum = np.mean(x + y)

print(f"E[X]: {e_x:.2f}, E[Y]: {e_y:.2f}")
print(f"E[X + Y]: {e_sum:.2f}")
print(f"E[X] + E[Y]: {e_x + e_y:.2f}") # They match!
