09. Bayesian Inference Workflow

The cycle of probabilistic programming.

1. The Four Steps of Bayes

Doing Bayesian Statistics professionally involves a structured loop known as Bayesian Data Analysis (BDA):

  • 1. Model Specification: Define the Prior $P(\theta)$ and the Likelihood $P(D|\theta)$.
  • 2. Technical Computation: Calculate the Posterior $P(\theta|D)$ (via Conjugacy, MCMC, or Variational Inference).
  • 3. Model Checking: Use Posterior Predictive Checks (PPC)** to see if the model's "Dream" data matches the real-world data.
  • 4. Inference & Action: Make decisions based on the full posterior distribution, not just a point estimate.

2. Box's Loop

George Box famously said: "All models are wrong, but some are useful." The Bayesian workflow is designed to find *where* the model is wrong and improve it through iteration.

3. AI/ML Connection: MLOps and Model Monitoring

The Lifecycle of a Probabilistic Model

In AI, this workflow is the foundation of Production MLOps.

When you deploy a **Fraud Detection AI**, the "Model Checking" step is continuous. If the AI's posterior distributions start looking very different from the incoming data distributions, we have **Model Drift**. In a Bayesian workflow, the AI doesn't just "Perform worse"—it produces larger **Uncertainty Intervals**. MLOps engineers use this signal to trigger an automatic Bayesian update or a manual review. This makes Bayesian AI "Fail Gracefully": it identifies its own obsolescence before it starts making confident mistakes.
