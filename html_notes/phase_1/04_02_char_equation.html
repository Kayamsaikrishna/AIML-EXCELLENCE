02. The Characteristic Equation

Unlocking the hidden eigenvalues.

1. Deriving the Solution

From the definition \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}\), we can subtract \(\lambda\mathbf{v}\) from both sides:

$$ \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v} - \lambda\mathbf{v} = 0 $$ $$ (\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I})\mathbf{v} = 0 $$

For a non-zero solution \(\mathbf{v}\) to exist, the matrix \((\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I})\) must be singular (non-invertible). This means its determinant must be zero.

$$ \det(\mathbf{A} - \lambda\mathbf{I}) = 0 $$

This is the Characteristic Equation. Expanding this determinant results in a polynomial in \(\lambda\) of degree \(n\), called the Characteristic Polynomial.

2. Solving for \(\lambda\)

Example: 2x2 Matrix

Let \(\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\). The equation is:

$$ \begin{vmatrix} a-\lambda & b \\ c & d-\lambda \end{vmatrix} = 0 $$ $$ (a-\lambda)(d-\lambda) - bc = 0 $$ $$ \lambda^2 - (a+d)\lambda + (ad-bc) = 0 $$

Note: The middle term \((a+d)\) is the Trace and the last term is the Determinant!

3. Determinant and Trace Property

For any \(n \times n\) matrix:

  • Sum of Eigenvalues: \(\sum \lambda_i = \text{Tr}(\mathbf{A})\)
  • Product of Eigenvalues: \(\prod \lambda_i = \det(\mathbf{A})\)

4. Python: Symbolic Solving

import sympy as sp

# Define matrix symbolically
lam = sp.symbols('lambda')
A = sp.Matrix([[4, 2], [1, 3]])

# Characteristic Polynomial
char_poly = A.charpoly(lam)
print(f"Char Poly: {char_poly.as_expr()}")

# Solve for Eigenvalues
eigenvals = sp.solve(char_poly, lam)
print(f"Eigenvalues: {eigenvals}")
