08. Limit Comparison Test (LCT)

Ignoring the noise to see the soul of a series.

1. The Test

If you have two positive series \(\sum a_n\) and \(\sum b_n\), calculate the limit of their ratio:

$$ L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} $$

If \(L\) is a positive finite number (\(0 < L < \infty\)), then both series share the same fate. If one converges, both converge. If one diverges, both diverge.

2. Why it is better than Direct Comparison

Direct Comparison requires you to prove \(a_n \leq b_n\), which is annoying if there are negative constants in the denominator. LCT allows you to just look at the leading terms (highest powers) and ignore everything else as "noise".

3. AI/ML Connection: Big-O Complexity

Asymptotic Analysis

In Computer Science and AI Algorithm Design, we use Big-O notation (\(O(n^2)\), \(O(n \log n)\)). Big-O is essentially the Limit Comparison Test.

When we say an AI training step takes \(O(n^2)\) time, we mean that as \(n\) (the number of neurons) goes to infinity, the complexity "behaves like" \(n^2\). Just as LCT ignores small constants to find convergence, AI engineers ignore small terms to find Scalability. If the "Limit Ratio" between your algorithm's time and \(n^2\) is a constant, your algorithm is \(O(n^2)\). This determines if your AI can run on a phone or if it needs a supercomputer.

4. Python: Leading Term Check

# a_n = (4n + 7) / (n^3 - 4n)
# Leading terms: 4n / n^3 = 4/n^2.
# We compare with b_n = 1/n^2 (Convergent p-series)

import numpy as np

n = 1000000
an = (4*n + 7) / (n**3 - 4*n)
bn = 1 / (n**2)

limit_ratio = an / bn
print(f"Limit Ratio: {limit_ratio:.6f}")
print("Ratio is finite and positive (~4.0). Fate shared!")
