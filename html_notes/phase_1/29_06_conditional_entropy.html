Conditional Entropy

Filtering the Noise. How much uncertainty remains in \(Y\) given that we already know \(X\)?

The Value of a Hint

Imagine you are trying to guess a word. \(H(Y)\) is your initial uncertainty. Now, I give you a hint \(X\): "The word starts with 'S'."

Conditional Entropy \(H(Y|X)\) is the amount of uncertainty that is left over after the hint. A good hint reduces entropy significantly; a bad hint leaves it unchanged.

1. Definition

Conditional entropy \(H(Y|X)\) is the weighted average of the entropy of \(Y\) for each possible value of \(X\):

$$ H(Y|X) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) H(Y|X=x) $$

Mathematically, it can be written as:

$$ H(Y|X) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} P(x, y) \log_2 P(y|x) $$

2. The Subtraction Rule

Conditional entropy is most easily understood through its relationship with Joint and Individual entropy:

$$ H(Y|X) = H(X, Y) - H(X) $$

Interpretation: "The uncertainty of the whole system minus the uncertainty of the part we already know is equal to the uncertainty that remains."

3. Key Properties

Conditioning NEVER Increases Entropy

$$ H(Y|X) \le H(Y) $$

Even if \(X\) is completely irrelevant to \(Y\), it won't make you *more* confused. It just keeps entropy the same.

Conditioning on Constant

If \(X\) is a constant value, then \(H(Y|X) = H(Y)\). Information that is always the same provides zero bits of context.

Generative AI LLMs

Entropy in Language Modeling

When we say an LLM is a "next-token predictor," we are asking it to minimize the **Conditional Entropy** of the next token \(T_{n+1}\) given the context window \(C = \{T_1, \dots, T_n\}\).

  • Predictability: A low \(H(T_{n+1}|C)\) means the model is very confident about the next word (e.g., "The capital of France is Paris").
  • Creativity: A higher \(H(T_{n+1}|C)\) occurs when there are many valid next tokens (e.g., "Once upon a time..."). Increasing the "Temperature" in an LLM effectively artificially increases the entropy of the prediction.
  • Loss Function: The Negative Log-Likelihood loss used to train GPT models is an estimator of the conditional entropy of human language.
