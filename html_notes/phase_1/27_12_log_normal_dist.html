12. Log-Normal Distribution

The result of multiplicative growth and positive skewness.

1. Definition

A random variable $X$ follows a Log-Normal Distribution if the natural logarithm of $X$ follows a normal distribution. That is, $Y = \ln(X) \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$.

$$ f(x) = \frac{1}{x\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \quad \text{for } x > 0 $$

It is always positive and typically has a "long tail" to the right.

2. Multiplicative Process

While the Normal distribution appears when independent effects are added (Central Limit Theorem), the Log-Normal distribution appears when independent effects are multiplied. This makes it the natural choice for modeling growth processes, sizes, and durations that cannot be negative.

3. AI/ML Connection: Natural Language and Finance

Modeling Positive Real-World Values

Log-Normal distributions are the standard for Skewed Positive Data in AI.

In **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**, the lengths of sentences in a corpus or the time a user spends reading an article are typically log-normally distributed—most are short, but some are very long. In **Algorithmic Trading AI**, stock prices are often assumed to follow a log-normal distribution (the foundation of the Black-Scholes model) because prices can't go below zero and growth is multiplicative. Understanding this allows you to **Normalize your Targets**; if you're training an AI to predict prices, taking the `log()` of your target variable converts it into a Normal distribution, which is much easier for an optimizer like Adam or SGD to handle.

4. Python: Transforming to Gaussian

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Generate log-normal data (Positive & Skewed)
data = np.random.lognormal(mean=0, sigma=1, size=1000)

# The 'Cheat' for AI training: Log-Transform
log_data = np.log(data)

print(f"Original skew: {np.mean(data):.2f}, {np.std(data):.2f}")
print(f"Log-transformed mean: {np.mean(log_data):.4f}") # (Should be close to 0)
