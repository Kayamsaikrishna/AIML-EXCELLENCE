Primitive Roots & Discrete Logs

The Generators of Chaos. Understanding how simple modular exponentiation creates the "one-way functions" that secure the entire internet.

The Primitive Root

A primitive root modulo $n$ is an integer $g$ such that every number coprime to $n$ can be expressed as a power of $g$. In group theory terms, $g$ is a generator of the multiplicative group $\mathbb{Z}_n^*$.

$$ \{ g^1, g^2, \dots, g^{\phi(n)} \} \equiv \mathbb{Z}_n^* \pmod n $$

1. Order and Generators

The order of an element $a$ modulo $n$ is the smallest positive integer $k$ such that $a^k \equiv 1 \pmod n$.

  • By Euler's Theorem, the order always divides $\phi(n)$.
  • If the order is exactly $\phi(n)$, then $a$ is a primitive root.
  • Example (Mod 7):
    • $2^1=2, 2^2=4, 2^3=1$. Order = 3. Not a primitive root.
    • $3^1=3, 3^2=2, 3^3=6, 3^4=4, 3^5=5, 3^6=1$. Order = 6. Primitive root!

2. The Discrete Logarithm Problem (DLP)

This is the cornerstone of modern cryptography. Given a prime $p$, a primitive root $g$, and a value $h$, finding $x$ such that:

$$ g^x \equiv h \pmod p $$

This is called the Discrete Logarithm of $h$ base $g$.

Asymmetry: Computation is easy ($O(\log x)$ using Modular Exponentiation). Inversion is hard (Subject of intense cryptoanalysis, roughly exponential time for general groups).
Cryptography Key Exchange

Application: Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange

How do two AI agents (or browsers) agree on a secret key over an insecure channel? They use the hardness of DLP.

  1. Setup: Publicly agree on large prime $p$ and generator $g$.
  2. Alice: Picks secret $a$, sends $A = g^a \pmod p$.
  3. Bob: Picks secret $b$, sends $B = g^b \pmod p$.
  4. Secret: Alice computes $S = B^a = (g^b)^a = g^{ab}$. Bob computes $S = A^b = (g^a)^b = g^{ab}$.

An eavesdropper sees $g, p, A, B$ but cannot feasibly compute $g^{ab}$ without solving the Discrete Log problem to find $a$ or $b$. This enables Secure Multi-Party Computation and privacy-preserving AI training.
