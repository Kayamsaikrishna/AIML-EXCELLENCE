The Substitution Rule

Simplifying the complex by changing the point of view.

The Reverse Chain Rule

Differentiation is linear, but integration is messy. Substitution is our primary weapon for untangling functions inside functions. It's simply the Chain Rule in reverse.

1. The Process

Integration of \(\int f(g(x)) g'(x) dx\):

  1. Choose \(u\): Pick the "inner" function \(g(x)\).
  2. Find \(du\): Differentiate \(u\) to get \(du = g'(x) dx\).
  3. Substitute: Replace all \(x\) terms with \(u\) terms.
  4. Integrate: Solve the simpler Integral in \(u\).
  5. Back-Substitute: Replace \(u\) with \(g(x)\).

$$ \int 2x(x^2+1)^4 dx \xrightarrow{u=x^2+1} \int u^4 du $$

2. AI/ML Connection: Change of Variables

Normalizing Flows

In deep generative models called Normalizing Flows, we transform a simple distribution (like a Gaussian) into a complex one (like an image) using a series of invertible functions.

To calculate the probability density of the new variable, we use the multivariate version of U-Substitution, which involves the Determinant of the Jacobian Matrix (the equivalent of the \(dx/du\) term). \[ p_x(x) = p_z(z) \left| \det \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} \right| \] Without understanding substitution, this formula is magic. With it, it's just correcting for the "stretching" of space.

3. Python: Symbolic Substitution

u_sub_solver.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# Problem: Integrate 2x * e^(x^2)
# Let u = x^2, then du = 2x dx. The integral becomes e^u du.
f = 2*x * sp.exp(x**2)

# SymPy handles substitution automatically internally
result = sp.integrate(f, x)

print(f"Original: {f}")
print(f"Integrated: {result}")
# Output: exp(x**2)

# Verify by differentiating
deriv = sp.diff(result, x)
print(f"Differentiation Check: {deriv == f}") # True
