Calculus 1.4 The Bridge

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Connecting the instantaneous with the eternal.

The Grand Unification

Before Isaac Newton and Gottfried Leibniz, "Finding Tangents" (Derivatives) and "Finding Areas" (Integrals) were totally separate branches of math. The FTC proved they are inversions of the same process.

1. The Two Parts

Part 1: Accumulation
The derivative of an integral is the original function. \[ \frac{d}{dx} \int_a^x f(t) dt = f(x) \]
Part 2: Evaluation
To calculate a definite integral, just subtract the antiderivative values. \[ \int_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a) \]

2. AI/ML Connection: Loss vs. Gradient

Accumulating Knowledge

Think of a training run.

  • \(f(t)\) Gradient at Step \(t\): The instantaneous learning ("Move left!").
  • \(F(T)\) Model Weights at Step \(T\): The sum total of all learning steps.

The Model State today \(F(T)\) is literally the integral of all past gradients \(f(t)\): \[ W_{final} = W_{init} - \eta \int_0^T \nabla Loss(t) dt \] The Fundamental Theorem says "The model you have now is just the accumulation of all the updates you applied."

3. Python: Verifying the Link

verify_ftc.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = x**2

# 1. Integrate f(x) to get F(x) = x^3/3
F = sp.integrate(f, x)
print(f"Antiderivative F(x): {F}")

# 2. Differentiate F(x)
f_recovered = sp.diff(F, x)
print(f"Derivative of F(x): {f_recovered}")

if f == f_recovered:
    print("SUCCESS: Differentiation reversed Integration!")

# Part 2 Checking: Area under x^2 from 0 to 3
# Geometric area = x^3/3 | 0->3 = 27/3 - 0 = 9
area_ftc = F.subs(x, 3) - F.subs(x, 0)
print(f"Area via FTC: {area_ftc}")
