Prob 1.4 The Life Cycle

The Weibull Distribution

Generalizing the Exponential distribution into the world of aging and mechanical failure.

Beyond Memorylessness

The Exponential distribution assumes a constant failure rate (memoryless). But in real life, things age.
A lightbulb is more likely to fail as it gets older (Aging).
A software system is more likely to crash in its first hour before settling (\*\*Infant Mortality\*\*).

The Weibull Distribution uses a shape parameter \(k\) to model these changing rates.

1. Definition and PDF

\(X \sim \text{Weibull}(\lambda, k)\) where \(\lambda > 0\) is the scale and \(k > 0\) is the shape. $$ f(x) = \frac{k}{\lambda} \left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{k-1} e^{-(x/\lambda)^k} $$ for \(x \ge 0\).

2. The Hazard Function (The Failure Rate)

The "Hazard" is the instantaneous probability of failing *now*, given you have survived until now.

graph LR A[k < 1] -->|Hazard Decreases| B[Early Failure / Learning / Burning-in] C[k = 1] -->|Hazard Constant| D[Exponential Distribution / Random Chance] E[k > 1] -->|Hazard Increases| F[Aging / Wear-out]
  • If \(k = 2\), the rate increases linearly with time (Rayleigh Distribution).
  • If \(k = 3.4\), the Weibull looks strikingly like a Normal distribution.

3. ML Connection: Reliability Engineering

Predictive Maintenance Systems

Modeling Time-to-Failure

In IoT and Industrial AI, we predict the Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of engines/batteries.

Because hardware inevitably wears out, we fit a Weibull distribution to the historical lifespan of parts. This allows us to calculate the probability of failure in the next 24 hours and schedule maintenance before a catastrophic breakdown occurs.

4. Extreme Value Theory

The Weibull is one of the three distributions that arise in Extreme Value Theory. It models the distribution of the minimum of a large number of independent samples.
"A chain is only as strong as its weakest link." If links have i.i.d. strengths, the strength of the chain follows a Weibull distribution.
