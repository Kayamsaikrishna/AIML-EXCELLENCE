Stats 1.5 Large Samples

Z-Test (Single Mean)

Testing if your sample mean significantly differs from a known population value.

The Requirements

You can use a Z-test if:
1. The population standard deviation (\(\sigma\)) is Known.
2. The sample size is Large (\(n \ge 30\)).

1. The Test Statistic

$$ Z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} $$
  • \(\bar{x}\): Sample Mean.
  • \(\mu_0\): The value claimed by the Null Hypothesis.
  • \(\sigma / \sqrt{n}\): The Standard Error.

2. Workflow Example

A factory claims their chips perform 1000 operations per second (\(\mu_0 = 1000, \sigma = 50\)). You test 100 chips and find a mean of 985.

$$ Z = \frac{985 - 1000}{50 / \sqrt{100}} = \frac{-15}{5} = \mathbf{-3.0} $$

Since \(|-3.0| > 1.96\) (95% critical value), you Reject the Null. The factory's claim is likely false.

3. ML Connection: Inference Speed

Deep Learning Hardware

Meeting the SLA

A company has a Service Level Agreement (SLA) that an API model must respond in under 200ms.

  • \(H_0: \mu \ge 200\) (Fail to meet SLA).
  • \(H_a: \mu < 200\) (Meets SLA).

You run 1,000 requests. Because \(n=1000\), the distribution of the mean response time is almost perfectly Normal (CLT). You can use a Z-test to prove to your customers that the model significantly meets the performance requirement.
