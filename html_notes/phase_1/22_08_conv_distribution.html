Prob 1.4 The Shape

Convergence in Distribution (\(D\))

When the CDF of a sequence approaches the CDF of a target distribution.

Stabilizing the Bell Curve

Convergence in distribution is "weakest" form of convergence. It doesn't say the individual values \(X_n\) get close to \(X\). It just says the population statistics (probabilities, percentiles) of \(X_n\) look exactly like the population statistics of \(X\).

Notation: \(X_n \xrightarrow{d} X\)

1. Definition

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} F_n(x) = F(x) $$ for all \(x\) where \(F(x)\) is continuous. (\(F_n\) and \(F\) are Cumulative Distribution Functions).

The Central Limit Theorem is the most famous example of convergence in distribution. We don't say the average hits the mean exactly; we say the histogram of thousands of averages looks like a Normal Distribution.

2. ML Connection: Asymptotic Normality

Deep Learning Statistical Inference

Why we can use Z-tests

Many ML estimators are Asymptotically Normal. This means that as the dataset grows, the distribution of the "Error" (\(\hat{\theta} - \theta^*\)) starts looking like a bell curve.

This allows us to perform hypothesis tests. If we know the errors are approximately Normal, we can determine if a new model's performance improvement is "statistically significant" or just random noise from the tail of the bell curve.
