15. Quantiles and Percentiles

Slicing the distribution into ordered parts.

1. Definition

Quantiles are points taken at regular intervals from the CDF of a random variable. The $p$-th quantile is the value $Q(p)$ such that $P(X \le Q(p)) = p$.

  • Quartiles: Divide data into 4 parts (25th, 50th, 75th percentiles).
  • Deciles: Divide data into 10 parts.
  • Percentiles: Divide data into 100 parts.

2. Interquartile Range (IQR)

The **IQR** ($Q3 - Q1$) measures the spread of the "middle 50%" of the data. It is a robust alternative to standard deviation and the foundation of the Box Plot.

3. AI/ML Connection: Performance Benchmarking and Calibration

Evaluating Error Distributions

Quantiles are essential for reporting **Real-World AI performance**.

In industry, we rarely look at "Mean Error" alone. An AI might have a low mean error but perform terribly for 1% of users. We look at **P95 or P99 Latency/Error** (the 95th or 99th percentile) to ensure the system is reliable for everyone. Furthermore, in **Probabilistic Forecasting**, a model doesn't just predict "One Number"; it predicts **Quantile Intervals** (e.g., "The demand will be between 10 units [P10] and 50 units [P90]"). In **Model Calibration**, we use **Q-Q Plots** (Quantile-Quantile) to check if the AI's predicted errors actually follow a normal distribution or if the model is overconfident.

4. Python: Quantile Analysis

# Example: Calculating P99 (the extreme threshold)
latency_data = np.random.lognormal(mean=2, sigma=0.5, size=1000)

p50 = np.percentile(latency_data, 50) # Median
p95 = np.percentile(latency_data, 95)
p99 = np.percentile(latency_data, 99)

print(f"Typical Latency (P50): {p50:.2f}ms")
print(f"Tail Latency (P99): {p99:.2f}ms")
