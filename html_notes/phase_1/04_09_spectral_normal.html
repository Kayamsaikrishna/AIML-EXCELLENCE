09. Spectral Theorem (Normal Matrices)

Unitary diagonalization for complex spaces.

1. What is a Normal Matrix?

A square matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) is Normal if it commutes with its conjugate transpose:

$$ \mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^H = \mathbf{A}^H\mathbf{A} $$

This category is broad and includes **Symmetric, Skew-symmetric, Orthogonal, Unitary, and Hermitian** matrices.

2. The Unitary Theorem

A matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) is unitarily diagonalizable if and only if it is normal. This means:

$$ \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{UDU}^H $$

Where \(\mathbf{U}\) is a Unitary Matrix (\(\mathbf{U}^H\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{I}\)). Unlike the real symmetric case, its eigenvalues can be Complex numbers.

3. AI Relevance

Rotation & Energy Conservation

  • Orthogonal Layers: Weight matrices that are normal (specifically orthogonal) prevent gradients from exploding or vanishing by preserving the norm of the vector during the forward pass.
  • FFT (Fast Fourier Transform): The core of Signal Processing and Audio AI. The DFT matrix is a complex normal matrix.

4. Python: Complex Eigenvalues

import numpy as np

# A Skew-Symmetric Matrix (Normal)
A = np.array([[0, 1], [-1, 0]])

# Check if Normal
print(f"A @ A.T == A.T @ A? {np.allclose(A @ A.T, A.T @ A)}")

# Eigenvalues are purely imaginary!
eigenvals = np.linalg.eigvals(A)
print(f"Eigenvalues: {eigenvals}") # [0+1j, 0-1j]
