03. One-Sided and Infinite Limits

Approaching from the left, the right, and forever.

1. One-Sided Limits

Sometimes a function behaves differently depending on which side you approach from:

  • Left-hand: \(\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)\) (Approach from \(x < a\)).
  • Right-hand: \(\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)\) (Approach from \(x > a\)).

Existence Rule: \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\) exists only if the left-hand and right-hand limits are equal.

2. Infinite Limits (Vertical Asymptotes)

If \(f(x)\) grows without bound as \(x \to a\), we say the limit is \(\infty\) or \(-\infty\).

$$ \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1}{x} = \infty $$

This identifies a **Vertical Asymptote** at \(x=0\).

3. AI Connection: Exploited Activating Functions

The Sigmoid and ReLU Saturations

Consider the Sigmoid function \(\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}\). As \(x \to \infty\), \(\sigma(x) \to 1\). As \(x \to -\infty\), \(\sigma(x) \to 0\). The "limits at infinity" are what give neural networks their squash-to-probability property.

4. Python: Visualizing Asymptotes

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

x = np.linspace(-1, 1, 400)
x = x[x != 0] # Avoid division by zero
y = 1/x

plt.plot(x, y)
plt.axvline(0, color='red', linestyle='--')
plt.title("Infinite Limit at x=0")
plt.ylim(-10, 10)
plt.show()
