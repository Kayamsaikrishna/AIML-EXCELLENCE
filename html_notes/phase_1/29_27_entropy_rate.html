Entropy Rate

Information Over Time. Measuring the density of uncertainty in a never-ending stream of symbols, the core concept behind modern language models.

Beyond the Snapshot

Shannon Entropy usually measures a single variable. But what if the next letter depends on the last 10 letters? Entropy Rate measures the average uncertainty per symbol as the sequence becomes infinitely long. It is the "real-time cost" of a conversation.

1. The Formal Limit

For a sequence of random variables \(\mathcal{X} = \{X_1, X_2, ..., X_n\}\), the entropy rate \(H(\mathcal{X})\) is the limit of the joint entropy divided by the number of symbols:

$$ H(\mathcal{X}) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} H(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) $$

Markov Chains

In a Markov process, where only the current state matters for the future, the entropy rate is elegant: it's the average of the entropy of the transitions.

$$ H(\mathcal{X}) = \sum_{ij} \mu_i P_{ij} \log \frac{1}{P_{ij}} $$

Stationarity

If the rules of the system don't change over time (stationarity), the entropy rate is guaranteed to exist and is always \(\le\) the entropy of a single symbol.

2. Asymptotic Equipartition Property (AEP)

The AEP is the "Law of Large Numbers" for information theory. It states that for a large enough \(n\), almost all sequences generated by a source will belong to a Typical Set.

  • Surprise: Every sequence in the typical set has roughly the same probability: \(2^{-nH(\mathcal{X})}\).
  • Efficiency: This is why we can compress data. We only need to assign codes to the Typical Set; the rest are so rare they essentially don't matter.
LLMs Predictability

The Entropy of Language

The primary goal of a Large Language Model (LLM) is to minimize its cross-entropy with the "true" entropy rate of human language.

  • Predictive Power: If a model has a lower entropy than another, it means it "understands" the structure of language better (it is less surprised by the next word).
  • The Context Window: As we increase the context length (the number of previous tokens the model can see), the conditional entropy of the next token decreases, approaching the true Entropy Rate of the language.
  • Infinite Monkeys: A random monkey has an entropy rate of \(\log_2(26) \approx 4.7\) bits/char. A human has an entropy rate of \(\approx 1.0\) bit/char. AI's job is to close that gap.
