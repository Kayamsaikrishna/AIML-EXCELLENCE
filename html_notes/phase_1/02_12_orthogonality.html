12. Orthogonal & Orthonormal Vectors

The ideal state for feature independence.

1. Formal Definitions

  • Orthogonal: Two vectors are orthogonal if their dot product is zero (\( \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0 \)). They are "perpendicular".
  • Orthonormal: Vectors are orthonormal if they are orthogonal AND each has a unit magnitude (\( \|\mathbf{v}\| = 1 \)).
$$ \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0, \quad \|\mathbf{u}\| = 1, \quad \|\mathbf{v}\| = 1 $$

2. Importance in AI Training

Orthogonal Weight Initialization

When training deep Neural Networks, if weights are too similar (highly correlated), gradients can vanish or explode. Orthogonal Initialization ensures that each neuron starts by looking for independent features.

  • Vanishing Gradients: Orthogonal matrices preserve the magnitude of gradients during backpropagation.
  • Redundancy: Orthonormal bases ensure that the data is represented with zero redundancy, making the model more "efficient".

3. Python: Checking Orthogonality

import numpy as np

# A standard basis is orthonormal
e1 = np.array([1, 0, 0])
e2 = np.array([0, 1, 0])

# Orthogonal Check
is_orthogonal = np.dot(e1, e2) == 0

# Orthonormal Check
is_orthonormal = is_orthogonal and np.linalg.norm(e1) == 1 and np.linalg.norm(e2) == 1

print(f"Orthonormal: {is_orthonormal}")
