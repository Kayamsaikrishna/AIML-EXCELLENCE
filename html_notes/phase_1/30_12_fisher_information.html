12. Fisher Information

Quantifying the "Quality" of information in data.

1. Definition

Fisher Information $I(\theta)$ measures the amount of information that an observable random variable $X$ carries about an unknown parameter $\theta$. It is defined as the variance of the Score (the partial derivative of the log-likelihood).

$$ I(\theta) = E\left[ \left( \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \ln f(X \mid \theta) \right)^2 \right] = -E\left[ \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \theta^2} \ln f(X \mid \theta) \right] $$

If the surface of the log-likelihood is "steep" (high second derivative), then a small change in data causes a large change in our belief—high Fisher information. If the surface is "flat," our data tells us very little.

2. Cramér-Rao Lower Bound

Fisher Information determines the Theoretic Limit of Precision. The variance of any unbiased estimator $\hat{\theta}$ is bounded by the reciprocal of the Fisher Information:

$$ Var(\hat{\theta}) \ge \frac{1}{n I(\theta)} $$

This is the "Universal speed limit" of statistics—it proves that you can never be perfectly certain about a parameter given finite data.

3. AI/ML Connection: Natural Gradient and Second-Order Optimization

Optimizing in the Space of Probability

In AI, Fisher Information is the bridge to Information Geometry.

In **Deep Learning**, the Natural Gradient algorithm uses the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) instead of the standard identity matrix to scale the learning rate. While standard Gradient Descent moves in the "Parameter Space" (which can be distorted), Natural Gradient moves in the "Distribution Space," which is invariant to how you parameterize the model. This makes training much faster and more stable, especially for complex structures like Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). Furthermore, the Fisher Information** is the expected value of the Hessian Matrix of the loss function. In **Pruning and Compression**, we use the Fisher Information to identify which weights in a neural network are "Uninformative"—if a weight has low Fisher Info, it means the model's accuracy doesn't rely on it, and we can safely delete it to make the AI smaller.

4. Python: Calculating Score Variance

# For a Bernoulli trial, I(p) = 1 / (p * (1-p))
p = 0.5
n = 100
theoretical_limit = 1 / (n * (1 / (p * (1-p))))

# Empirical variance of 1000 MLE estimates
estimates = [np.mean(np.random.binomial(1, p, n)) for _ in range(1000)]
emp_var = np.var(estimates)

print(f"Cramer-Rao Lower Bound: {theoretical_limit:.6f}")
print(f"Empirical Variance: {emp_var:.6f}")
# Empirical will be extremely close to the limit!
