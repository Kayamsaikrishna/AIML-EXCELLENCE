Information Geometry Curvature

Fisher Information Revisited

Measuring the Shape of Certainty. How the local curvature of the KL Divergence defines the geometry of probability space and the limits of learning.

The Curvature of Knowledge

We know that KL Divergence measures how far two distributions are. Fisher Information tells us how *sensitive* that distance is to small changes in our model's parameters. It is the "Hessian" of the probability manifold.

1. The Formal Definition

The Fisher Information Matrix \(I(\theta)\) is the expected value of the square of the gradient of the log-likelihood (the Score function).

$$ I(\theta) = E \left[ \left( \nabla_\theta \ln P(x|\theta) \right) \left( \nabla_\theta \ln P(x|\theta) \right)^T \right] $$

The KL Connection

Taylor expansion of KL Divergence around \(\theta\):

$$ D_{KL}(P_\theta \| P_{\theta+\epsilon}) \approx \frac{1}{2} \epsilon^T I(\theta) \epsilon $$

This proves Fisher Information is the metric tensor of the KL space.

Cramér-Rao Bound

The inverse of Fisher Information is the theoretical minimum variance any unbiased estimator can achieve. It is the "Uncertainty Principle" of statistics.

Natural Gradient EWC

Why AI Optimization Needs Geometry

Standard Gradient Descent takes a step in the direction of steepest change in *parameter values*. But we want the steepest change in *model behavior*.

  • Natural Gradient Descent: Instead of \(\Delta \theta = -\eta \nabla L\), we use \(\Delta \theta = -\eta I(\theta)^{-1} \nabla L\). This ensures a constant step in KL divergence space, making training invariant to how we parameterize our network.
  • Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC): In Continual Learning, we want to learn Task B without forgetting Task A. EWC uses the Fisher Information of Task A to identify which weights are "critical" (high curvature) and penalizes changing them while learning Task B.
