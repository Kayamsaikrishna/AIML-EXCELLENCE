Stars & Bars (Distributing Items)

Partitioning the Indistinguishable. A visualization technique to find the number of ways to distribute \(n\) identical items into \(k\) distinct bins.

The Divider Logic

Imagine you have 7 identical oranges ("stars") and you want to give them to 3 friends (distinct bins). To separate the oranges into 3 groups, you need 2 "bars" or dividers. The total arrangement is a sequence of stars and bars.

1. Case 1: Non-Negative Integers (Bins can be empty)

We need to arrange \(n\) stars and \(k-1\) bars.

$$ \binom{n + k - 1}{k - 1} = \binom{n + k - 1}{n} $$

Example: Distribute 7 identical balls into 3 distinct boxes.

\(\binom{7 + 3 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{9}{2} = 36 \).

2. Case 2: Positive Integers (Bins must have at least 1)

To ensure every bin has at least one, we give one item to each bin first. This reduces the problem to distributing \(n-k\) items into \(k\) bins.

$$ \binom{n - 1}{k - 1} $$

Example: Distribute 7 oranges to 3 kids such that everyone gets at least one.

\(\binom{7 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{6}{2} = 15 \).

Resource Allocation Probability

Resource Logic in AI

  • Load Balancing: When distributing \(n\) identical data packets across \(k\) server nodes, Stars & Bars counts the possible load configurations.
  • Hyperparameter Search Space: In Sum-Constraint Search, where hyperparameters \(\alpha_i\) must sum to 1 (like weights in an ensemble: \(\sum w_i = 1\)), if we discretize the weights into "quanta," Stars & Bars defines the size of the search space.
  • Bose-Einstein Statistics: In physics-informed machine learning, this counting method is used for particles (like photons) that are indistinguishable, unlike pixels in an image.
  • Budget Allocation: In reinforcement learning, if you have a fixed "computational budget" to allocate across different exploration paths, the number of ways to split that budget is a Stars & Bars problem.
