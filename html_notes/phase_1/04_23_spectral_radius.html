23. Spectral Radius (\(\rho\))

The limit of growth in linear systems.

1. The Definition

The Spectral Radius of a matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) is the maximum absolute value of its eigenvalues:

$$ \rho(\mathbf{A}) = \max \{ |\lambda_1|, |\lambda_2|, \dots, |\lambda_n| \} $$

2. The Convergence Theorem

Will it explode?

For any iterative process \(\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_k\), the vector \(\mathbf{x}_k\) will converge to zero for any initial vector if and only if:

$$ \rho(\mathbf{A}) < 1 $$

If \(\rho(\mathbf{A}) > 1\), the system will blow up to infinity. This is the primary concern when designing Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Echo State Networks.

3. Normalization in AI

In Weight Normalization or Spectral Normalization (used in GANs), we literally divide the weight matrix by its spectral radius (or its spectral norm, which is roughly related) to ensure the mapping is Lipschitz Continuous. This prevents gradients from wildly fluctuating.

4. Python: Checking RNN Growth

import numpy as np

# A small RNN weight matrix
W = np.random.randn(5, 5) * 0.5 

# Compute Spectral Radius
rho = np.max(np.abs(np.linalg.eigvals(W)))

print(f"Spectral Radius: {rho:.3f}")
if rho > 1:
    print("Warning: Activations may explode!")
else:
    print("System is contractive (Stable).")
