10. Two-Sample t-test (Independent)

Comparing two unrelated groups of data.

1. The Goal of Comparison

The Independent Two-Sample t-test determines if there is a statistically significant difference between the means of two independent groups. Unlike the one-sample test, we are not comparing to a fixed number, but to another variable group.

2. The Formula (Pooled Variance)

$$ t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{s_p^2 (\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2})}} $$

Where $s_p^2$ is the **Pooled Variance**, a weighted average of the variances from both groups. This assumes the groups have roughly equal variances (Homoscedasticity). If they don't, we use **Welch's t-test**, which is the more robust modern default.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Architecture Evaluation

Architecture A vs Architecture B

In AI research, the 2-Sample t-test is the "Judge" in **Competitions**.

When Google releases a new **Transformer** architecture and claims it is better than the previous one, they don't just report the average score. They take 20 different random initializations of Model A and 20 of Model B. These are independent "Groups." They run a **Two-sample t-test** on the validation metrics. If the p-value is 0.001, we can be highly confident that the architecture change *itself* caused the improvement, and it wasn't just a lucky random seed for Model B. In A/B Testing** of Recommendation Engines, this test allows us to compare "Users in Group 1 (New AI)" vs "Users in Group 2 (Baseline)" to prove that any increase in engagement is statistically valid.

4. Python: stats.ttest_ind

# Accuracy of Model A (20 runs)
group_a = np.random.normal(0.85, 0.02, 20)
# Accuracy of Model B (20 runs)
group_b = np.random.normal(0.87, 0.02, 20)

# equal_var=False performs Welch's t-test (Recommended default)
t_stat, p_val = stats.ttest_ind(group_a, group_b, equal_var=False)

print(f"t-stat: {t_stat:.2f}, p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
# If p < 0.05, Group B is significantly different (better) than Group A.
