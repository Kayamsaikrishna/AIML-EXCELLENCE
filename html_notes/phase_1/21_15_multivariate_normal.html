Prob 1.4 The Workhorse

The Multivariate Normal (Gaussian)

Generalizing the bell curve to $n$ dimensions.

The Most Important Distribution in ML

The Multivariate Normal (MVN) is unique because it is completely defined by just two parameters: a Mean Vector \(\mu\) and a Covariance Matrix \(\Sigma\).

It is the "Gold Standard" because of its elegant mathematical properties: - Marginals are Gaussian. - Conditionals are Gaussian. - Linear combinations are Gaussian.

1. The Joint PDF

For \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^k\): $$ f(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^k |\Sigma|}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x}-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf{x}-\mu) \right) $$

The Quadratic Form: The term in the exponent \((\mathbf{x}-\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf{x}-\mu)\) is the squared Mahalanobis Distance. This term determines the shape of the "ellipsoid" of density.

2. Types of Covariance Matrices

Spherical (\(\Sigma = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}\))

All features have same variance and zero correlation. Contours are circles/spheres.

Diagonal (\(\Sigma = \text{diag}(\sigma_i^2)\))

Features have different variances but zero correlation. Contours are axis-aligned ellipses.

Full (\(\Sigma\) is dense)

Features are correlated. Contours are rotated ellipses.

3. ML Connection: Gaussian Processes & VAEs

Bayesian ML Generative

Gaussian Processes (GPs)

A GP is just a Multivariate Normal over an infinite number of variables. We use it for non-parametric regression where we want to express uncertainty about the entire function shape, not just a single value.

Variational Autoencoders

In VAEs, we force the "Latent Space" to follow a Multivariate Normal distribution (usually a Standard Gaussian with \(\mu = \mathbf{0}, \Sigma = \mathbf{I}\)). This ensures the latent space is continuous and structured, allowing us to sample new data by just picking points from the bell curve.

4. Sampling: The Reparameterization Trick

How do we sample from \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)\) in a way that is differentiable?
1. Sample \(\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})\).
2. Use the Cholesky Decomposition \(\Sigma = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top\).
3. Result: \(\mathbf{x} = \mu + \mathbf{L}\epsilon\).

This is the trick that made VAEs trainable with backpropagation!
