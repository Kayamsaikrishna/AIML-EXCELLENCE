03. Likelihood Function

How well does the parameter explain the observed reality?

1. The Perspective Shift

The Likelihood Function $L(\theta | x)$ is the probability (or density) of the data $x$ as a function of the parameter $\theta$.

  • Probability: Predicts data given a fixed parameter.
  • Likelihood: Reflects on the parameter given fixed data.

It is the "Evidence" provided by the experiment. In the Bayesian formula, it is the mechanism that "pulls" the prior toward the data.

2. The Math of Observation

For $n$ independent and identically distributed (i.i.d) samples:

$$ L(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i | \theta) $$

Because products of probabilities become tiny quickly, we usually work with the **Log-Likelihood**, which turns the product into a sum.

3. AI/ML Connection: Loss Functions and Generative Modeling

Likelihood as a Objective

In AI, the Likelihood function is the core of Loss Optimization.

Every time you use Cross-Entropy Loss**, you are actually performing **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)**. You are asking the model: "Change your weights so that the observed labels in the training set become as likely as possible." In **Generative AI** (like GANs or Diffusion models), the goal is to learn the "Likelihood Landscape" of real images, so the AI can sample from regions of high likelihood to create new, realistic-looking data. Without the Likelihood function, AI would have no metric to measure its "Fit" to the world. It is the sensory input of the mathematical brain.

4. Python: Likelihood vs. Density

# Observed data: x = 5
data = 5

# We think it might come from a Normal dist with mean 'mu'
mu_candidates = np.linspace(0, 10, 100)

# Likelihood is the height of the PDF at x=5 for DIFFERENT mus
likelihoods = norm.pdf(data, loc=mu_candidates, scale=1)

# Result: Likelihood is highest at mu=5. 
# It tells us that 5 is the most 'Plausible' value for mu.
