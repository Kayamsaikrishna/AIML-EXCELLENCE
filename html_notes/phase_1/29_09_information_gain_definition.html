Information Gain Definition

Measuring the "Delta" of Uncertainty. How much clarity does a piece of evidence bring to a messy problem?

The Reduction of Ignorance

In Machine Learning, we don't just care about how much uncertainty we have (Entropy). We care about how much better we get after looking at a feature.

Information Gain is the mathematical "Price" of a hint. It's the difference between what you didn't know before and what you don't know now.

1. Definition

Information Gain (IG) of a random variable \(X\) with respect to another variable \(Y\) (the "Attribute" or "Feature") is defined as:

$$ IG(X, Y) = H(X) - H(X|Y) $$
  • H(X): Global Entropy (Initial uncertainty about the outcome).
  • H(X|Y): Conditional Entropy (Residual uncertainty about \(X\) after knowing \(Y\)).

Because conditioning can never increase entropy, **Information Gain is always non-negative (\(IG \ge 0\))**.

2. Physical Intuition

Case A: Zero Gain

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, knowing \(Y\) gives you 0 bits of help. \(H(X|Y) = H(X)\), so \(IG = 0\).

Case B: Maximum Gain

If \(Y\) perfectly predicts \(X\), then \(H(X|Y) = 0\). Your uncertainty is completely gone. Information Gain is maximized and equals the total entropy of \(X\).

3. Relationship to Mutual Information

A fundamental identity in Information Theory is:

$$ IG(X, Y) = I(X; Y) $$

Information Gain is the term used in the context of decision-making and training (e.g., Decision Trees). Mutual Information is the same concept used in communication theory and feature engineering. They both measure the shared "clues" between variables.

Decision Trees Feature Importance

The ID3 Algorithm Logic

When building a Decision Tree, the computer iterates through all possible features (Age? Income? Location?) and calculates the Information Gain for each one.

  1. Calculate entropy of the current target labels.
  2. For each feature, simulate a split and calculate the weighted average entropy of the resulting branches.
  3. The feature with the highest reduction (Max IG) becomes the root of the tree at that level.
The Bias Warning: Standard Information Gain favors features with many levels (e.g., "Customer ID" is a terrible feature but has high IG because it perfectly separates data). To fix this, we often use Gain Ratio, which penalizes features with too many branches.
