Calculus 1.5 Series

Infinite Series

Adding up forever.

Sequence vs Series

A Sequence is a list: \(1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{4}, \dots\)
A Series is the sum of that list: \(1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} + \dots\)

Does the sum blow up to infinity, or does it settle on a finite number? This is the question of Convergence.

1. Partial Sums

We cannot actually add infinite numbers. Instead, we look at the sequence of Partial Sums \(S_k\).

\[ S_k = \sum_{n=1}^{k} a_n \]

If the sequence of partial sums \(\{S_k\}\) converges to a limit \(S\), then the series converges to \(S\).

2. AI/ML Connection: Cumulative Reward

Reinforcement Learning

In RL, an agent (like a robot or game player) receives rewards \(R_t\) at each time step. The Return \(G_t\) is the total accumulated reward from now until the end of time.

\[ G_t = R_{t+1} + R_{t+2} + R_{t+3} + \dots \]

If the task never ends (infinite horizon), this sum could be infinite. To ensure it converges, we discount future rewards, turning it into a Geometric Series (covered in the next module).

3. Python: Calculating Partial Sums

partial_sums.py
# Series: Sum(1 / 2^n) = 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 ...
# We expect this to sum to 1.

partial_sum = 0
for n in range(1, 15):
    term = 1 / (2**n)
    partial_sum += term
    print(f"n={n:2} | Term: {term:.6f} | Partial Sum: {partial_sum:.6f}")

# Output shows Partial Sum approaching 1.0
