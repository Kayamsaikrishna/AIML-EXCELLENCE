Stats 1.5 Before vs After

Paired T-Test

Removing individual noise by comparing a subject to their own previous baseline.

The Dependent Sample

In a Paired T-test, the "Groups" are the same people measured twice. - Group 1: Weight before a diet. - Group 2: Weight after a diet.

Why it's better: It has much higher Power. Since we are looking at the *Difference* for each person, we ignore the fact that some people are naturally heavier than others. We only care how much they changed.

1. The Test Statistic

$$ t = \frac{\bar{d}}{s_d / \sqrt{n}} $$
  • \(d\): The difference score (\(x_{after} - x_{before}\)) for each subject.
  • \(\bar{d}\): Mean of the differences.
  • \(s_d\): Standard deviation of the differences.

A Paired T-test is mathematically identical to a Single-Sample T-test performed on the "Differences."

2. ML Connection: Model Fine-tuning

Deep Learning NLP

The Impact of RLHF

You have a Base Large Language Model. You apply **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** to make it safer.

The Test: 1. Give 100 prompts to the Base Model and rate the "Safety Score" (1-10) for each. 2. Give the same 100 prompts to the RLHF Model and score them. 3. Use a Paired T-test.

Because we used the exact same prompts, we've eliminated the noise of "hard prompts" vs "easy prompts." We are directly measuring the transformation caused by the RLHF process.
