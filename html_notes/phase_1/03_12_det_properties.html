1. The Product Rule

Since determinants represent volume scaling, applying two transformations $A$ then $B$ scales the volume by the product of their individual scalings.

$$ \det(AB) = \det(A) \cdot \det(B) $$

This explains why \( A \) must be invertible if \( \det(A) \neq 0 \): because \( \det(A)\det(A^{-1}) = \det(I) = 1 \).

2. The 'c' Trap

Common mistake: \( \det(cA) = c \det(A) \). WRONG!

If you scale a 3D box by 2, it gets 2x wider, 2x taller, 2x deeper. Volume increases by \( 2^3 = 8 \).

$$ \det(cA) = c^n \det(A) $$

In high-dimensional AI (e.g., $n=1000$), scaling weights slightly can explode determinants exponentially ($2^{1000}$).

3. Verifying Rules

Checking the Product and Scaling properties.

import numpy as np

n = 3
A = np.random.rand(n, n)
B = np.random.rand(n, n)
c = 2

# 1. Product Rule
LHS = np.linalg.det(A @ B)
RHS = np.linalg.det(A) * np.linalg.det(B)
print(f"Product Rule Match? {np.isclose(LHS, RHS)}")

# 2. Scaling Rule
LHS_scale = np.linalg.det(c * A)
RHS_scale = (c**n) * np.linalg.det(A)
print(f"Scaling Rule Match? {np.isclose(LHS_scale, RHS_scale)}")

# 3. Transpose Rule
print(f"Transpose Match? {np.isclose(np.linalg.det(A), np.linalg.det(A.T))}")
