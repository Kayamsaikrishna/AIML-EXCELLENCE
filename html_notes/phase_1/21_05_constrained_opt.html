05. Constrained Optimization

Finding the best solution within boundaries.

1. The Problem Setup

In real-world problems, we don't just want to maximize "Profit" or "Accuracy" in an infinite space. We usually have Constraints (budget, time, physical laws).

A constrained optimization problem looks like this:

$$ \text{Maximize/Minimize } f(x, y) $$ $$ \text{Subject to } g(x, y) = k $$

The solution must lie on the curve \(g(x, y) = k\). We aren't looking for the hill peak, but the highest point on the fence built around the hill.

2. Equality vs. Inequality

Constraints can be Hard Equalities (\(x + y = 1\)) or Inequalities (\(x^2 + y^2 \leq 1\)). Dealing with inequalities requires more advanced math like the KKT conditions.

3. AI/ML Connection: Fairness and Resource Limits

Fairness Constraints

In Ethical AI, we don't just want a model to be accurate; we want it to be Fair. This means we might minimize error \(f(x, w)\) subject to a constraint \(g(w) \leq \epsilon\), where \(g\) measures the "Bias" of the model against a specific group.

By treating Fairness as a mathematical constraint, we force the AI to find the "Maximum Accuracy" possible while staying within the "Safe and Ethical" boundary. Without constrained optimization, AI would simply follow the path of least resistance, often amplifying human biases found in the data.

4. Python: Simple Constrained Check

# Example: Minimize x^2 + y^2 subject to x + y = 1
# Intuition: The closest point of the line to the origin.

from scipy.optimize import minimize

def objective(v):
    return v[0]**2 + v[1]**2

cons = ({'type': 'eq', 'fun': lambda v: v[0] + v[1] - 1})

res = minimize(objective, [0, 0], constraints=cons)
print(f"Optimal Point: {res.x}")
# Result: [0.5, 0.5]
