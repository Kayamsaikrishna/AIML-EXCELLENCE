Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT)

Decomposing the Large into the Small. A structural theorem that allows us to break down arithmetic on huge integers into independent, parallel operations on smaller primes.

The Isomorphism

CRT states that if we have a set of pairwise coprime integers $n_1, n_2, \dots, n_k$, then knowing a number $x$ modulo their product $N$ is equivalent to knowing $x$ modulo each $n_i$ individually.

$$ \mathbb{Z}_N \cong \mathbb{Z}_{n_1} \times \mathbb{Z}_{n_2} \times \dots \times \mathbb{Z}_{n_k} $$

1. Theorem Statement

Let $n_1, \dots, n_k$ be integers greater than 1, which are pairwise coprime (i.e., $gcd(n_i, n_j) = 1$ for all $i \neq j$). Let $N = n_1 \times \dots \times n_k$.

Then, for any given sequence of integers $a_1, \dots, a_k$, there exists a unique integer $x$ modulo $N$ satisfying the system of simultaneous congruences:

$$ \begin{cases} x \equiv a_1 \pmod{n_1} \\ x \equiv a_2 \pmod{n_2} \\ \vdots \\ x \equiv a_k \pmod{n_k} \end{cases} $$

2. Constructive Proof & Algorithm

We don't just know $x$ exists; we can build it. The formula is a weighted sum of the remainders.

  1. Compute Partial Products: Let $N_i = N / n_i$. This is the product of all moduli except $n_i$.
  2. Find Modular Inverses: Since $N_i$ and $n_i$ share no factors, $gcd(N_i, n_i) = 1$. We can find the inverse $y_i$ such that: $$ N_i y_i \equiv 1 \pmod{n_i} $$
  3. Construct Solution: The solution is given by: $$ x = \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_i N_i y_i \pmod N $$
Cryptography Optimization

Application: Accelerated RSA Decryption

In standard RSA, decryption requires computing $c^d \pmod N$, where $N$ is a huge 2048-bit number. This is computationally expensive.

However, the owner of the private key knows the factors $p$ and $q$ of $N$. Using CRT, they can:

  • Compute $m_1 = c^{d \pmod{p-1}} \pmod p$ (which is much smaller/faster).
  • Compute $m_2 = c^{d \pmod{q-1}} \pmod q$.
  • Combine $m_1$ and $m_2$ using CRT to get the original message $m$.

Impact: This technique makes RSA decryption approximately 4 times faster, which is critical for SSL/TLS handshakes on servers handling millions of connections.
