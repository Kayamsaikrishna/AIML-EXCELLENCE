The "BAC - CAB" Rule

\( \mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \). Reducing complex 3D interactions into simple coplanar arithmetic.

1. The BAC-CAB Identity

The vector triple product is NOT associative. \( (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}) \times \mathbf{c} \neq \mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \).

$$ \mathbf{a} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{b}(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c}) - \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}) $$

Insight: The result must lie in the plane defined by \( \mathbf{b} \) and \( \mathbf{c} \), because \( \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} \) is perpendicular to that plane, and crossing it again with \( \mathbf{a} \) creates a vector perpendicular to the normal (i.e., back in the plane).

2. Computational Efficiency

In Physics Engines (Rigid Body Dynamics) and Robotics, we often encounter terms like \( \Omega \times (\Omega \times r) \) (Centripetal Acceleration).

Using the BAC-CAB rule converts expensive Cross Products (Vector ops) into cheaper Dot Products (Scalar ops) followed by simple Vector Scaling. This is faster on GPUs.

3. Verifying the Identity

Proving that the formula works numerically.

import numpy as np

a = np.array([1, 2, 3])
b = np.array([4, 5, 6])
c = np.array([7, 8, 9])

# Method 1: Direct Cross Products
b_cross_c = np.cross(b, c)
res1 = np.cross(a, b_cross_c)

# Method 2: BAC - CAB Rule
# (a . c) * b
scaler1 = np.dot(a, c)
term1 = scaler1 * b

# (a . b) * c
scaler2 = np.dot(a, b)
term2 = scaler2 * c

res2 = term1 - term2

print(f"Direct:  {res1}")
print(f"Formula: {res2}")
# They should be identical (within float error)
