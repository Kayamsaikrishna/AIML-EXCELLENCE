15. Multivariate Normal Distribution (MVN)

The bell curve in $n$ dimensions.

1. Definition

A random vector $\mathbf{X}$ follows a Multivariate Normal Distribution if every linear combination of its components is normally distributed. It is defined by a mean vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and a covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$.

$$ f(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^n |\boldsymbol{\Sigma}|}} \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^T \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu}) \right) $$

Where $|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}|$ is the determinant (volume) of the covariance matrix.

2. Geometry: Error Ellipses

While a 1D normal distribution is a curve, a 2D MVN looks like a "mountain" whose base is an ellipse. The shape and orientation of this ellipse are entirely determined by the **Covariance Matrix**. If the off-diagonal elements are zero, the ellipse is aligned with the axes; if they are non-zero, the ellipse is rotated, showing correlation between variables.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Generative Prior

Modeling the Space of Possibility

MVN is the "Gold Standard" for modeling Clustering and Latent Spaces.

In **Unsupervised Learning**, Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) assume that data is composed of several MVN "clouds." By learning the $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ of each cloud, an AI can group similar data points (like user personas) together. In **Anomaly Detection**, we fit an MVN to "normal" data; any new point that falls in the extreme tails of this high-dimensional distribution (i.e., has a low probability) is flagged as an outlier or fraudulent transaction. Most importantly, in **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**, the AI is forced to map its internal "concepts" into a standard Multivariate Normal space, ensuring that similar concepts are located close to each other in the model's "mind."

4. Python: Sampling and PDF

from scipy.stats import multivariate_normal

# Mean and Covariance
mu = [0, 0]
sigma = [[1, 0.5], [0.5, 1]] # Correlated variables

dist = multivariate_normal(mean=mu, cov=sigma)

# Probability density at [1, 1]
print(f"Density at [1,1]: {dist.pdf([1, 1]):.4f}")

# Sample 5 points
samples = dist.rvs(size=5)
print(f"Samples:\n{samples.round(2)}")
