11. Moving Averages & Smoothing

Filtering out the noise to see the soul of the data.

1. The Simple Moving Average (SMA)

An SMA averages the last $k$ data points. It "Smooths" out short-term fluctuations and highlights the underlying trend. However, it lags the actual data because it looks backward.

$$ SMA_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^{k} Y_{t-i+1} $$

2. Exponential Smoothing (EWMA)

Exponential Weighting gives more weight to recent observations and less to older ones. It reacts faster to changes than a simple average. This is the foundation of many trading indicators (EMA).

3. AI/ML Connection: Convolutional Windows and Attention

Local Feature Extraction

In AI, a Moving Average is technically a 1D Convolutional Filter**.

When you use a **CNN** for time-series (e.g., audio processing), the 'Kernel' is essentially a trainable moving average that learns which "Frequency" or "Shape" to smooth out. In Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, we use "Moving Average Rewards" to stabilize the learning process—so the AI doesn't overreact to a single lucky or unlucky step. Furthermore, the **Attention Mechanism** in Transformers can be seen as a "Learnable Weighted Average" where the model dynamically decides which historical points are important. Understanding smoothing vs. signal is what allows AI engineers to distinguish between a **Structural Change** in user behavior and a **Temporary Outlier**.

4. Python: SMA vs EWMA

# 7-day Moving Average
data['SMA_7'] = data['Sales'].rolling(window=7).mean()

# Exponential Weighting (reacts faster)
data['EWMA'] = data['Sales'].ewm(span=7).mean()

data[['Sales', 'SMA_7', 'EWMA']].plot()
# Watching the 'smooth' lines follow the 'jittery' data!
