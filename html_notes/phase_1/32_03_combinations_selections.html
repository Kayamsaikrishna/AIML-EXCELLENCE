Combinations & Selection

Choosing, Not Arranging. Defining the number of ways to pick a subset where the order doesn't matter — the mathematical engine of probability and sampling.

The Grouping Logic

A Combination is a selection of items where the order of choosing does not matter. If you are picking a team of 3 from 10 people, picking (A, B, C) is exactly the same as picking (C, B, A). Unlike permutations, we divide out the redundant orderings.

1. The Formula \(C(n, r)\)

Known as "n choose r."

$$ C(n, r) = \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} $$

Example: In a deck of 52 cards, how many different 5-card poker hands can you have?

Order doesn't matter. This is \(\binom{52}{5} = \frac{52 \times 51 \times 50 \times 49 \times 48}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = 2,598,960\).

2. Fundamental Identities

Symmetry

Choosing \(r\) items is the same as *leaving behind* \(n-r\) items.

\( \binom{n}{r} = \binom{n}{n-r} \)

Boundary Cases

There is only 1 way to pick everything, and 1 way to pick nothing.

\( \binom{n}{0} = \binom{n}{n} = 1 \)
Probability Evaluation

Why Combinations matter in AI

  • k-Fold Cross-Validation: When we split data into \(k\) folds, we are choosing 1 fold for testing and \(k-1\) for training. There are \(\binom{k}{1}\) ways to choose the test fold.
  • Hyperparameter Combinations: The total number of points in a Grid Search is a product of choices, but if we are selecting a "subset" of relevant features, we are using combinations.
  • Bernoulli Trials: The probability of exactly \(k\) successes in \(n\) trials (Binomial Distribution) is scaled by \(\binom{n}{k}\). This is fundamental to understanding error rates in classification.
  • Ensemble Methods: If you have 10 models and you want to build a "voting ensemble" of any 3, you have \(\binom{10}{3} = 120\) possible ensembles.
