04. Limit Laws and Properties

The algebraic toolkit for limit computation.

1. Algebraic Laws

If \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L\) and \(\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = M\), then:

  • Sum Rule: \(\lim (f+g) = L + M\)
  • Product Rule: \(\lim (f \cdot g) = L \cdot M\)
  • Quotient Rule: \(\lim (f / g) = L / M \quad (M \neq 0)\)
  • Power Rule: \(\lim [f(x)]^n = L^n\)

2. Indeterminate Forms (0/0 and \(\infty/\infty\))

If the Quotient Rule results in \(0/0\), the limit is not always undefined. It means more work is needed (factoring, rationalizing, or L'Hôpital's Rule later).

$$ \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2} = \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2} = \lim_{x \to 2} (x+2) = 4 $$

3. AI Analogy: Linearity of Expectation

The "Sum Rule" of limits is mirrored in Probability as the Linearity of Expectation. Since an Expectation is just an integral (a limit of sums), the property that "the limit of a sum is the sum of limits" is the reason we can average loss values across a Batch of data to get a stable gradient for the whole dataset.

4. Python: Symbolic Limit Solving

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = (x**2 - 4) / (x - 2)

# Symbolic limit
result = sp.limit(f, x, 2)

print(f"Limit of (x^2 - 4)/(x - 2) as x->2 is: {result}")
