Stats 1.6 Continuous Case

Bayes Theorem for Parameters

Moving from choosing between "Rain" or "No Rain" to choosing between infinite possible weight values.

The Shift to PDF

In basic Bayes, we deal with events (Discrete). In ML, we deal with parameters (\(\theta\)) which are usually Continuous (weights can be 0.1, 0.11, 0.111, etc.).

1. The Continuous Formula

$$ f(\theta|x) = \frac{f(x|\theta) f(\theta)}{\int f(x|\theta') f(\theta') d\theta'} $$

Where: - \(f(\theta|x)\) is the Posterior PDF. - \(f(\theta)\) is the Prior PDF.

Instead of a single probability number, we now get a Probability Density Function. This means we have a whole range of "most likely" values for our model parameters.

2. ML Connection: Parameter Estimation

Deep Learning Uncertainty

Why point estimates are dangerous

A standard Neural Network (Frequentist/MLE) gives you a single set of weights: \(w = 0.45\).

A Bayesian Neural Network gives you a distribution: \(w \sim \mathcal{N}(0.45, 0.01)\).

The Advantage: If the posterior distribution is very "wide" (high variance), it means the model is Uncertain. In medical AI, if a model says "Cancer" but has a 40% variance in its weight-belief, we shouldn't trust it! Frequentist models can be "Confidently Wrong"; Bayesian models warn you when they are guessing.

3. Key Takeaway

Bayes Theorem for parameters isn't just about finding the "best" answer. It's about finding the landscape of possible answers. The peak of the landscape is the most likely value, but the width of the landscape tells us if we should bet our lives on it.
