40. Polar Coordinates

Seeing the world in Radii and Rotation.

1. Coordinate Transformation

Instead of \((x, y)\), we use distance from origin \((r)\) and angle \((\theta)\).

$$ x = r \cos \theta, \quad y = r \sin \theta $$ $$ r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \quad \theta = \arctan(y/x) $$

2. Circular Data & Radar AI

Naturally Curved Domains

Data from Radar and Sonar arrives in Polar form (Range and Bearing). Converting this to Cartesian \((x,y)\) introduces non-linearities. Modern AI models for tracking often perform operations directly in Polar space to avoid "Grid Artifacts".

  • Log-Polar Mapping: Human vision works more like a log-polar grid (high resolution in center, low at periphery). Mimicking this allows for more efficient AI computation.
  • Directional Statistics: When predicting wind direction or protein folding angles, we use Polar distributions (like the von Mises) to account for the fact that \(360^\circ\) is the same as \(0^\circ\).

3. Python: Polar Plotting

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# A spiral in polar coordinates
theta = np.linspace(0, 4*np.pi, 1000)
r = theta**2

fig, ax = plt.subplots(subplot_kw={'projection': 'polar'})
ax.plot(theta, r)
ax.set_rticks([0.5, 1, 1.5, 2])
ax.grid(True)

plt.title("A Spiral in Polar Domain")
plt.show()
