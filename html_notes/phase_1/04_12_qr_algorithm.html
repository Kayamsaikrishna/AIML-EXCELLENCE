QR Algorithm

How computers actually find eigenvalues. Iterative factorization.

1. The Process

The QR Algorithm computes all eigenvalues at once.

1. Decompose \( A_k = Q_k R_k \).

2. Form \( A_{k+1} = R_k Q_k \) (effectively \( Q_k^T A_k Q_k \), a similarity transform).

3. Repeat. \( A_k \) converges to an upper triangular matrix whose diagonal is the eigenvalues.

2. Why this matters

Calculating Determinants and Characteristic Polynomials for large matrices is numerically unstable and slow.

The QR Algorithm (specifically Francis' implementation with shifts) is the gold standard used by NumPy, PyTorch, and SciPy to find eigenvalues robustly.

3. Manual QR Loop

import numpy as np

def basic_qr_algo(A, iter=50):
    Ak = A.copy()
    for _ in range(iter):
        Q, R = np.linalg.qr(Ak)
        Ak = R @ Q # Reverse order
    return Ak

A = np.array([
    [5, 2],
    [2, 2] # Symmetric, so it will converge to Diagonal
])

T = basic_qr_algo(A)
print("Converged Matrix T:")
print(T.round(4))

print("\nDiagonal Elements (Eigenvalues):")
print(np.diag(T).round(4))

print("\nNumpy Check:")
print(np.linalg.eigvals(A).round(4))
