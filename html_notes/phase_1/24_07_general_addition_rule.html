07. General Addition Rule

Handling the overlap between possibilities.

1. Overlapping Events

In many real-world scenarios, events are not mutually exclusive. An email can be "Spam" (A) AND "Urgent" (B). Simply adding their probabilities would count the "Spam AND Urgent" emails twice.

2. The Formula (Inclusion-Exclusion)

To find the probability of $A$ OR $B$ occurring, we add their individual probabilities and subtract the intersection once to fix the double-counting:

$$ P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Multi-Label Classification

Non-Exclusive Tags

Unlike standard image classification (where an image is ONLY one thing), Multi-Label Tagging allows overlapping events.

If an AI is tagging a news article, it might assign "Politics: 0.8" and "Crisis: 0.6". These are not mutually exclusive. If we want to calculate the probability that an article belongs to "Politics OR Crisis", we cannot just add $0.8 + 0.6 = 1.4$ (which is an impossible probability). We must use the General Addition Rule. This requires the model to also estimate the probability of the overlap. Proper multi-label probability modeling is what allows AI to understand that a single piece of data can represent multiple concepts simultaneously without breaking the mathematical bounds of probability.

4. Python: Union of Overlapping Sets

# Experiment: Card drawn is a 'Heart' (A) OR a 'Face Card' (B)
p_heart = 13/52
p_face = 12/52
p_heart_and_face = 3/52 # J, Q, K of Hearts

# General Addition Rule
p_union = p_heart + p_face - p_heart_and_face

print(f"P(Heart OR Face): {p_union:.4f}")
print(f"Direct Verification: {22/52:.4f}")
