1. Casting the Shadow

To find the part of vector \( \mathbf{v} \) that lies in the direction of \( \mathbf{u} \):

$$ \text{proj}_{\mathbf{u}} \mathbf{v} = \left( \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{u}}{||\mathbf{u}||^2} \right) \mathbf{u} $$

The scaler term \( \frac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{u}}{||\mathbf{u}||} \) is the length of the shadow. We multiply by unit vector \( \hat{u} \) to get the vector shadow.

2. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

PCA is just a sequence of projections.

  1. Find the direction \( \mathbf{u}_1 \) that maximizes the length of the projection (variance).
  2. Project all data onto \( \mathbf{u}_1 \) (Compression).
  3. Subtract the projection from the original data (find the Rejection/Residual).
  4. Repeat.

By keeping only the first few projections, we compress the image/data while preserving its "shape".

3. Projecting Noise Away

Simulating denoising by projecting onto a "signal" axis.

import numpy as np

# 'Signal' Direction (e.g. Trend Line)
u = np.array([4.0, 4.0])

# Noisy Data Point
v = np.array([4.2, 3.8])

# 1. Projection
scaler = np.dot(v, u) / np.dot(u, u) # (v . u) / ||u||^2
proj_v = scaler * u

# 2. Rejection (Noise)
noise = v - proj_v

print(f"Original: {v}")
print(f"Projected (Clean): {proj_v}") # Should be [4, 4] approximately
print(f"Noise Removed: {noise}")
