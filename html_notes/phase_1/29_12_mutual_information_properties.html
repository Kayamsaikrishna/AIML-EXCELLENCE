Info Theory Conservation Laws

Mutual Information Properties

Universal Rules of Correlation. The mathematical constraints that govern how information flows and is preserved in a system.

Information cannot be created

The most fundamental property of Information Theory is that you can only lose information through processing. Like energy in thermodynamics, information has its own "Entropy Law" called the Data Processing Inequality.

1. The Non-Negativity & Symmetry

1. Non-negativity

$$ I(X; Y) \ge 0 $$

You can't have "negative" shared information. Learning about \(X\) will never make you *more* confused about \(Y\) than you already were.

2. Symmetry

$$ I(X; Y) = I(Y; X) $$

Shared information is mutual. If I know your date of birth, I know something about your age; and if I know your age, I know something about your date of birth.

2. The Data Processing Inequality (DPI)

If we have a Markov Chain: \(X \rightarrow Y \rightarrow Z\) (meaning \(Z\) only depends on \(Y\), which in turn only depends on \(X\)):

$$ I(X; Y) \ge I(X; Z) $$
The Software Engineer's Insight: This means that no amount of clever algorithm or data processing can increase the information content about the original source \(X\). Processing an image into an embedding can only preserve or reduce information, never create it out of thin air.

3. Invariance to Re-labeling

Mutual Information is Invariant under one-to-one transformations. If \(f\) and \(g\) are invertible functions:

$$ I(X; Y) = I(f(X); g(Y)) $$

Why it matters in ML: Whether you encode "Cat" as `0` or `1`, or "Dog" as `[1, 0, 0]`, the Mutual Information between the image pixels and the labels remains identical. MI cares about the relationship, not the labels themselves.

Deep Learning Robustness

MI as a Quality Metric

  • Contrastive Learning: In models like SimCLR, we maximize the MI between two different "augmentations" of the same image. We are forcing the model to find the *invariant* features that stay the same despite rotation or color shifts.
  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): In InfoGAN, the model tries to maximize the mutual information between a small "latent code" and the generated image. This ensures that the code actually controls something in the output (like the rotation of a digit).
  • Encoding Efficiency: A good feature representation \(Z\) should have high \(I(X; Z)\) (it remembers the input) and high \(I(Y; Z)\) (it predicts the target).
