05. Bias of an Estimator

Is your "Guessing Rule" systematically wrong?

1. Definition

An Estimator $\hat{\theta}$ is a rule that we use to guess a population parameter $\theta$. The Bias of an estimator is the difference between its expected value and the true value.

$$ Bias(\hat{\theta}) = E[\hat{\theta}] - \theta $$

An estimator is **Unbiased** if $Bias(\hat{\theta}) = 0$. This means that if we repeated the experiment trillions of times, the average of all our guesses would exactly hit the bullseye.

2. Intuition: The Offset Sight

Imagine a rifle with a scope. If you are a perfect shooter but the scope is misaligned by 2 inches to the left, your shots will cluster together (low variance) but away from the bullseye. That "2-inch shift" is the **Bias**. Bias is a systematic error built into the formula itself, not a result of bad luck.

3. AI/ML Connection: Inductive Bias and Underfitting

The Cost of Assumptions

In AI, Bias represents the **Assumptions** your model makes about the world.

A **Linear Model** has high "Inductive Bias" because it assumes the relationship is a straight line. If the real world is a curve, the model will be systematically wrong (Underfitting). On the other hand, **Inductive Bias** is often necessary—without it, a model might just memorize the noise instead of learning the pattern. When we use N-1 instead of **N** in the sample variance formula ($s^2$), we are correcting a mathematical bias (Bessel's correction). In **Deep Learning**, Regularization** (like L2) intentionally introduces a small amount of bias to significantly reduce the model's variance, making it perform better on new, unseen data.

4. Python: Observing Bias in Variance

# Population Variance is 100 (std=10)
true_var = 100

# Function 1: Biased Variance (Divide by N)
# Function 2: Unbiased Variance (Divide by N-1)

results_biased = []
results_unbiased = []

for _ in range(1000):
    sample = np.random.normal(0, 10, 5) # Small sample size makes bias obvious
    results_biased.append(np.var(sample))
    results_unbiased.append(np.var(sample, ddof=1))

print(f"Biased Formula Avg: {np.mean(results_biased):.2f}")     # Will be ~80
print(f"Unbiased Formula Avg: {np.mean(results_unbiased):.2f}") # Will be ~100
