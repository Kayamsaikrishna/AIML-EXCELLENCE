Moment Generating Functions (MGF)

Encoding the distribution into a function.

Definition

The MGF \(M_X(t)\) is defined as the expected value of \(e^{tX}\): \[ M_X(t) = E[e^{tX}] \] Why care? Because if you take the \(n\)-th derivative of \(M_X(t)\) at \(t=0\), you get the \(n\)-th moment \(E[X^n]\)! \[ M_X^{(n)}(0) = E[X^n] \] Also, if two RVs have the same MGF, they have the same distribution.

2. ML Connection: Sums of RVs

Convolution

Determining the distribution of a sum of independent variables \(Z = X + Y\) is hard (requires convolution). But with MGFs, it's just multiplication! \[ M_{X+Y}(t) = M_X(t) \cdot M_Y(t) \] Disadvantage: MGFs don't always exist (e.g., Cauchy distribution). That's why we use Characteristic Functions instead.
