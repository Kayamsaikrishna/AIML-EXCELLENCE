Stats 1.4 Z-Interval

CI for Mean (Known \(\sigma\))

Calculated when we already know the variability of the population (or have a very large sample).

The Rule of Two

For a Normal distribution, ~95% of the data falls within 1.96 standard deviations of the mean. We use this fact to build our interval "around" our sample lucky guess.

1. The formula

$$ \bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2} \left( \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \right) $$
  • \(\bar{x}\): Sample Mean.
  • \(z_{\alpha/2}\): Critical value from the Z-table (Standard Normal).
  • \(\sigma / \sqrt{n}\): The Standard Error.

2. Common Critical Values (\(z^*\))

Confidence Level Alpha (\(\alpha\)) Critical Value (\(z^*\))
90% 0.10 1.645
95% 0.05 1.96
99% 0.01 2.576

3. ML Connection: Large Sample Limits

Deep Learning Central Limit Theorem

Gaussian Assumption in Big Data

In deep learning, we often have millions of samples (\(n \to \infty\)). Because of the Central Limit Theorem, the distribution of our "Mean Loss" or "Mean Accuracy" will be almost perfectly Normal.

In these cases, we can use the Z-Interval even if we don't know the exact population variance, because the sample variance (\(s\)) becomes an extremely precise substitute for \(\sigma\).
