08. Graph Representation as Adjacency Matrices

From social networks to page rank.

1. The Adjacency Matrix (\(\mathbf{A}\))

A graph with \(n\) nodes can be represented as an \(n \times n\) matrix where \(A_{ij} = 1\) if there is an edge from \(i\) to \(j\), and \(0\) otherwise.

Fact: For an undirected graph, \(\mathbf{A}\) is Symmetric. This means it has real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors!

2. Powers of Adjacency

The \(k\)-th power of the adjacency matrix, \(\mathbf{A}^k\), tells you the number of paths of length \(k\) between any two nodes. This is the basis of many graph algorithms.

3. AI Connection: PageRank & GNNs

Eigenvector Centrality

Google's PageRank algorithm defines importance as the steady-state of a Markov chain. Mathematically, the importance score vector \(\mathbf{v}\) is the Principal Eigenvector of the modified adjacency matrix (the Google Matrix).

$$ \mathbf{G}\mathbf{v} = 1 \cdot \mathbf{v} $$

4. Python: Simple Graph Spectrum

import numpy as np

# A simple triangle graph
A = np.array([
    [0, 1, 1],
    [1, 0, 1],
    [1, 1, 0]
])

# Find spectrum (eigenvalues)
vals = np.linalg.eigvalsh(A)

print(f"Graph Eigenvalues: {vals}")
# Output: [2, -1, -1] -> Sum of eigenvalues = Trace = 0!
