Prob 1.4 The Flatland

Continuous Uniform Distribution

Equal probability density across an interval.

The Density Paradox

In a discrete uniform distribution, every point has a probability. In a continuous uniform distribution over \([0, 1]\), the probability of choosing exactly \(0.5\) is zero.

Instead, we talk about Density. The density is constant, meaning the probability of landing in any sub-interval depends only on the interval's length.

1. Definition and PDF

A Random Variable \(X\) follows a Continuous Uniform distribution on \([a, b]\): $$ X \sim \mathcal{U}(a, b) $$ The Probability Density Function (PDF) is: $$ f(x) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{b-a} & \text{for } a \le x \le b \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$

Area Check: The area under the PDF is a rectangle with width \((b-a)\) and height \(\frac{1}{b-a}\). $$\text{Area} = (b-a) \times \frac{1}{b-a} = 1$$

2. Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)

The probability that \(X\) is less than \(x\):

$$ F(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & x < a \\ \frac{x-a}{b-a} & a \le x \le b \\ 1 & x> b \end{cases} $$

This is a straight line increasing from 0 to 1.

3. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \frac{a+b}{2}\).
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = \frac{(b-a)^2}{12}\).

Why 12?

The number 12 in the denominator comes from the integration of \(x^2\) over the interval, specifically \(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} x^2 dx = [x^3/3] = 1/24 - (-1/24) = 1/12\). It’s a geometric property of the square shape.

4. ML Connection: Weight Initialization

Deep Learning Xavier Init

He and Xavier Initialization

When we start training a Neural Network, we cannot set all weights to zero (Symmetry breaking problem). We often initialize them with values from a Uniform Distribution.

Xavier Uniform Initialization: $$ W \sim \mathcal{U}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{n_{in} + n_{out}}}, \frac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{n_{in} + n_{out}}}\right) $$ The bounds are specifically chosen so that the variance of the output of the layer matches the variance of the input, preventing gradients from exploding or vanishing.

Data Augmentation

In Computer Vision, we often rotate images by a random angle \(\theta \sim \mathcal{U}(-15^\circ, 15^\circ)\). This assumes we have no prior belief that some rotations are more likely than others within that range.

5. The Fundamental Theorem of Simulation

Almost every "random" number generated by a computer starts as a \(\mathcal{U}(0, 1)\) draw.

If \(U \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)\), and \(F\) is a desired CDF, then \(X = F^{-1}(U)\) has CDF \(F\).

This is called Inverse Transform Sampling. It allows us to turn a flat uniform distribution into any complex distribution (Normal, Exponential, etc.) we want.
