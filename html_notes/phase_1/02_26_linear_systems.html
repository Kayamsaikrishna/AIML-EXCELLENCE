1. Gaussian Elimination

To solve a system, we systematically subtract multiples of one row from another to introduce Zeros below the diagonal.

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 4 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_2 - 2R_1} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} $$

This transforms matrix \( A \) into an Upper Triangular matrix \( U \), which is trivial to solve from bottom to top (Back Substitution).

2. The LU Factorization

In AI libraries (PyTorch, TensorFlow), we usually store the "steps" of elimination as a Lower Triangular matrix \( L \).

$$ A = LU $$

This allows us to solve \( A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b} \) in two fast steps: \( L\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{b} \) (Forward) and \( U\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{c} \) (Backward).

3. Decomposing A

Using Scipy to factorize a matrix.

import numpy as np
import scipy.linalg

# A Matrix
A = np.array([
    [2, 1, 1],
    [4, 3, 3],
    [8, 7, 9]
])

# Perform LU Factorization
P, L, U = scipy.linalg.lu(A)

print("L (Lower Triangle):")
print(L)
# 1s on diagonal, multipliers below.

print("\nU (Upper Triangle):")
print(U)
# Pivots on diagonal: [8, 3, 2] (Scipy might reorder rows with P)

# Verify Reconstruction
Reconstructed = P @ L @ U
print(f"\nMatch? {np.allclose(A, Reconstructed)}")
