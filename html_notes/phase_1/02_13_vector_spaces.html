13. Vector Spaces Definition

The playground for data.

1. The 10 Axioms

A Vector Space \( V \) is a set of elements called vectors, together with two operations: Addition and Scalar Multiplication. For \( V \) to be a vector space, it must satisfy 10 axioms, the most critical being Closure.

Closure Rules

  • Addition Closure: If \( \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in V \), then \( \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} \in V \).
  • Scalar Closure: If \( \mathbf{v} \in V \) and \( c \in \mathbb{R} \), then \( c \mathbf{v} \in V \).

If these rules are broken, your "playground" has gaps, and AI operations will fail to produce valid outputs.

2. The Embedding Space

Bounded vs. Unbounded Spaces

In AI, we often treat Representations as vectors in a space. However, many AI "spaces" are technically not full Vector Spaces because they have boundaries (e.g., values restricted between 0 and 1).

  • Latent Space: The multi-dimensional space created by a model's hidden layers where semantically similar items are grouped.
  • The Zero Vector: Every vector space must have a zero vector (Origin). In AI, this usually represents "Silence" or "No Feature Presence".
