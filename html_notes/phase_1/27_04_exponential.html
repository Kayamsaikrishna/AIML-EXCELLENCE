04. Exponential Distribution

The wait between random events.

1. Definition

The Exponential Distribution models the time between events in a Poisson process with rate $\lambda$. It is used for continuous positive random variables.

$$ f(x) = \begin{cases} \lambda e^{-\lambda x} & \text{if } x \ge 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} $$

2. Memoryless Property

Like its discrete counterpart (the Geometric distribution), the Exponential distribution is **Memoryless**:

$$ P(X > s+t \mid X > s) = P(X > t) $$

This means if you've been waiting 10 minutes for a bus, the probability that you wait another 5 minutes is the same as it was when you first arrived (assuming arrival follows a Poisson process).

3. AI/ML Connection: User Engagement and Failure Modeling

The Decay of Interest

Exponential distributions are essential for modeling Durations.

In **Recommendation Systems**, the time a user spends on a page or watching a video often follow an exponential decay—most users leave quickly, fewer stay for a long time. In **Predictive maintenance**, it models the "Mean Time To Failure" (MTTF) of components. Furthermore, the Exponential Linear Unit (ELU) activation function in neural networks uses an exponential term to handle negative values smoothly, which can speed up learning by keeping mean activations close to zero. Understanding this distribution allows AI systems to predict not just "What" will happen, but "How long" until it happens.

4. Python: Survival and Probabilities

from scipy.stats import expon

# lambda = 0.5 (Average of 2 units of time between events)
# scipy uses scale = 1/lambda
lam = 0.5
dist = expon(scale=1/lam)

# Probability of waiting more than 4 minutes
print(f"P(X > 4): {1 - dist.cdf(4):.4f}")

# Simulating durations
print(f"Samples: {dist.rvs(size=5).round(2)}")
