10. Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

The engine of AI: Finding parameters that explain the data.

1. The Definition

Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) is a method of estimating the parameters of a statistical model by finding the parameter values that maximize the Likelihood Function. It answers: "Which parameters make the observed data most probable?"

$$ L(\theta) = f(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n \mid \theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i \mid \theta) $$ $$ \hat{\theta}_{MLE} = \arg\max_{\theta} L(\theta) $$

2. The Log-Likelihood Hack

Because maximizing a product is mathematically difficult, we almost always maximize the Log-Likelihood ($l(\theta) = \ln L(\theta)$). Logarithms turn products into sums, making the calculus significantly easier without changing the location of the maximum.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Foundation of Training

The "Why" Behind Gradient Descent

In AI, almost every training process is an MLE search.

When you minimize Cross-Entropy Loss in a Classification model, you are mathematically performing Maximum Likelihood Estimation. For Linear Regression, maximizing the Gaussian likelihood is equivalent to minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE). Every time an AI model "learns" from data, it is iterating through the parameter space to find the MLE. It is the gold standard because, for large datasets, MLE is guaranteed to be Consistent, Asymptotically Normal, and Efficient (the best possible guess). Understanding MLE is the key to understanding why specific Loss Functions (like Negative Log-Likelihood) are chosen for different types of data.

4. Python: MLE for a Bernoulli Trial

# Observed: 80 heads in 100 coin flips. Estimate 'p'.
# Likelihood = p^80 * (1-p)^20
# Log-Likelihood = 80*log(p) + 20*log(1-p)

p_values = np.linspace(0.01, 0.99, 100)
log_lik = 80 * np.log(p_values) + 20 * np.log(1 - p_values)

mle_p = p_values[np.argmax(log_lik)]
print(f"MLE Estimate of p: {mle_p:.2f}") # Result: 0.80
# For Bernoulli, MLE is simply (successes / total)
