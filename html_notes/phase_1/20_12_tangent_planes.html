08. Tangent Planes & Normal Lines

Flattening the surface at a single point.

1. The Tangent Plane

Just as a 1D curve has a tangent line, a 2D surface \(z = f(x, y)\) has a tangent plane at a point \((x_0, y_0, z_0)\). The equation is:

$$ z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0) $$

This plane gives the best linear approximation of the surface near the point.

2. The Normal Line

The Normal Vector \(\vec{n}\) is perpendicular to the tangent plane. It is given by the gradient of the surface (if expressed as \(F(x, y, z) = 0\)). The Normal Line is the line passing through the point in the direction of the normal vector.

3. AI/ML Connection: Manifold Learning & Vision

The Tangent Space

In Deep Learning, we assume that high-dimensional data (like images of faces) actually lies on a low-dimensional "folded" surface called a Manifold. To understand the relationship between two nearby faces, AI models calculate the Tangent Space at a specific face coordinate.

Furthermore, in Computer Vision, "Surface Normals" are calculated for every pixel in a 3D scene. This tells the AI how light will reflect off the surface. If an AI can predict the tangent planes of an object accurately, it can perform "Relighting" or "Style Transfer" that looks physically realistic.

4. Python: Equation of a Tangent Plane

# For f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 at point (1, 1, 2)
# f_x = 2x = 2, f_y = 2y = 2
# Plane: z - 2 = 2(x - 1) + 2(y - 1)
# Simplified: 2x + 2y - z = 2

import sympy as sp

x, y, z = sp.symbols('x y z')
f = x**2 + y**2
fx = sp.diff(f, x).subs({x:1, y:1})
fy = sp.diff(f, y).subs({x:1, y:1})

plane_eq = sp.Eq(z - 2, fx*(x - 1) + fy*(y - 1))
print(f"Tangent Plane Equation: {sp.simplify(plane_eq)}")
