05. Gamma Distribution

Generalizing the wait for multiple events.

1. Definition

The Gamma Distribution models the time until the $k$-th event occurs in a Poisson process. It is a two-parameter family ($\alpha$ as shape, $\beta$ as rate).

$$ f(x) = \frac{\beta^\alpha x^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta x}}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \quad \text{for } x > 0 $$

Where $\Gamma(\alpha) = (\alpha-1)!$ for integers. When $\alpha=1$, it simplifies to the Exponential distribution.

2. Relation to Chi-Square

The Chi-Square distribution, essential for hypothesis testing, is a special case of the Gamma distribution where $\alpha = n/2$ and $\beta = 1/2$. This makes the Gamma distribution a "parent" to many other vital statistical models.

3. AI/ML Connection: Bayesian Priors and Uncertainty

Modeling Positive Constraints

Gamma distributions are the standard choice for Conjugate Priors in Bayesian AI.

When an AI needs to estimate a "Rate" (like how often a user clicks an ad or how quickly a machine wears down), we use a Gamma distribution as our initial guess (the Prior). Because it is only defined for positive numbers, it is perfect for modeling variance, precision, or arrival rates. In **Generative AI**, Gamma distributions are used to model the "Sparsity" of activations, helping machines learn to recognize specific features rather than trying to pay attention to everything at once. Understanding Gamma is the key to creating AI that can "learn how to learn" by updating its beliefs about rates and frequencies.

4. Python: Gamma Density

from scipy.stats import gamma

# alpha (shape) = 2, beta (rate) = 0.5
# scipy uses a=alpha, scale=1/beta
a, beta = 2, 0.5
dist = gamma(a, scale=1/beta)

# Mode of the distribution
import numpy as np
x = np.linspace(0, 20, 100)
pdf = dist.pdf(x)
print(f"Max density at x = {x[np.argmax(pdf)]:.2f}")

# Sample from Gamma
print(f"Sample: {dist.rvs():.4f}")
