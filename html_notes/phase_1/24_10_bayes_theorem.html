10. Bayes' Theorem

The engine of reasoning under uncertainty.

1. Reversing the Condition

Often, we know the probability of a "Symptom" given a "Disease", but we want to know the probability of the "Disease" given the "Symptom". Bayes' Theorem provides the mathematical bridge to reverse conditional probabilities.

2. The Formula

$$ P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A) \cdot P(A)}{P(B)} $$
  • Posterior: \(P(A|B)\) - The probability of our hypothesis after seeing the evidence.
  • Likelihood: \(P(B|A)\) - How likely is the evidence if our hypothesis is true?
  • Prior: \(P(A)\) - Our initial belief before seeing any evidence.
  • Evidence: \(P(B)\) - The total probability of the evidence occurring.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Bayesian Brain

Naive Bayes and Model Training

Bayes' Theorem is the foundation of an entire branch of Machine Learning: Bayesian Inference.

In Naive Bayes Classifiers, we predict the class of a data point by calculating the posterior for every possible class and picking the highest one. It's called "Naive" because we assume all input features are independent (an assumption that simplifies the math but surprisingly works well for text).

More importantly, Machine Learning as a whole can be viewed as "Finding the Weights that maximize the Posterior". Every time you train an AI, you are essentially updating its Prior Beliefs (initial weights) based on the Evidence (training data) to arrive at a Posterior (final model weights). Bayes' Theorem isn't just a formula; it's the philosophy of how AI "Learns".

4. Python: Medical Test Bayesian Check

# Experiment: Disease testing
# P(Disease) = 0.01 (Prior)
# P(Test Positive | Disease) = 0.99 (Likelihood)
# P(Test Positive | No Disease) = 0.05 (False Positive)

p_disease = 0.01
p_no_disease = 0.99
p_pos_given_disease = 0.99
p_pos_given_no_disease = 0.05

# Evidence P(Pos) = P(Pos|D)P(D) + P(Pos|NoD)P(NoD)
p_pos = (p_pos_given_disease * p_disease) + (p_pos_given_no_disease * p_no_disease)

# Posterior P(Disease | Positive)
p_disease_given_pos = (p_pos_given_disease * p_disease) / p_pos

print(f"P(Disease | Pos): {p_disease_given_pos:.4f}") # Surprisingly low! approx 16%
