10. Moments (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th)

Describing distribution shape through powers of $X$.

1. Raw vs. Central Moments

Raw Moments: The $k$-th raw moment is $E[X^k]$. The 1st raw moment is the Mean.

Central Moments: The $k$-th central moment is $E[(X - \mu)^k]$. The 2nd central moment is the Variance.

2. The Hierarchy of Moments

  • 1st Moment: Location (Mean).
  • 2nd Moment: Width (Variance).
  • 3rd Moment: Asymmetry (Skewness).
  • 4th Moment: Peakiness/Tails (Kurtosis).

3. AI/ML Connection: Xavier and Kaiming Initialization

Matching Distribution Moments

Moments are critical for the **Initialization of Neural Networks**.

If you initialize a model's weights with values that are too large or too small, the signals will explode or vanish through the layers. Techniques like **Xavier (Glorot)** and **Kaiming (He) Initialization** are derived by calculating the relationship between the **2nd Moment (Variance)** of the inputs and the variance of the outputs. By setting initial weights such that these moments are "balanced," we ensure that signals flow smoothly during training. Furthermore, in **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**, "Moment Matching" is a loss technique where the generator tries to produce data whose lower-order moments match those of the real dataset.

4. Python: Calculating Higher Moments

import scipy.stats as stats

data = np.random.normal(0, 1, 1000)

m1 = stats.moment(data, moment=1) # Central: should be ~0
m2 = stats.moment(data, moment=2) # Central: should be ~1
m3 = stats.moment(data, moment=3) 
m4 = stats.moment(data, moment=4)

print(f"Mean (Raw 1): {np.mean(data):.4f}")
print(f"Variance (Central 2): {m2:.4f}")
