13. Pareto Distribution

The Power Law: 80% of effects come from 20% of causes.

1. Definition

The Pareto Distribution is a power-law probability distribution used to describe social, scientific, and geophysical phenomena. It is characterized by its extremely heavy "Right Tail."

$$ f(x) = \frac{\alpha x_m^\alpha}{x^{\alpha+1}} \quad \text{for } x \ge x_m $$

Where $x_m$ is the scale (minimum possible value) and $\alpha$ is the shape parameter (tail index).

2. The "80/20 Rule"

The Pareto principle suggests that for many outcomes, roughly 80% of consequences come from 20% of the causes. In the distribution, a smaller $\alpha$ leads to a "heavier" tail, meaning that extreme values (like billionaires in a wealth distribution) represent a massive portion of the total mass.

3. AI/ML Connection: Big Data and Network Modeling

Modeling the Extremes

Pareto distributions appear wherever there is a Preferential Attachment process (the rich get richer).

In **Network Analysis AI**, the number of links to a website or followers on a social network follow a Pareto distribution. In **E-commerce**, the "Long Tail" of products (many products sold once, few sold millions of times) is a Pareto phenomenon. Understanding this allows AI systems to perform **Anomaly Detection** on internet traffic—since normal traffic isn't usually Pareto-distributed, a sudden power-law shift can indicate a massive botnet or viral event. It is also used in **Reinforcement Learning** to model rewards in environments where most actions yield nothing, but a rare few yield massive returns.

4. Python: Power Law Analysis

from scipy.stats import pareto

# alpha = 2.0 (Classic 80/20 behavior), xm = 1
alpha, xm = 2.0, 1

# Probability that a value is > 5 times the minimum
print(f"P(X > 5): {1 - pareto.cdf(5, alpha, scale=xm):.4f}")

# Simulating extreme values
samples = pareto.rvs(alpha, scale=xm, size=10)
print(f"Extremes: {samples.max():.2f}")
