Prob 1.4 The Ensemble

The Binomial Distribution

What happens when you flip the coin \(n\) times?

Sum of Bernoullis

If \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) are independent Bernoulli trials with probability \(p\), their sum \(Y = \sum X_i\) follows a Binomial Distribution.

"What is the probability of getting exactly \(k\) heads in \(n\) tosses?"

1. The Formula

\(Y \sim \text{Bin}(n, p)\) represents the number of successes in \(n\) independent trials. $$ P(Y=k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} $$
Components:
  • \(p^k\): Probability of \(k\) successes.
  • \((1-p)^{n-k}\): Probability of \(n-k\) failures.
  • \(\binom{n}{k}\): The number of ways to arrange \(k\) successes among \(n\) trials (The Binomial Coefficient).

2. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[Y] = np\)
    (Derived from Linearity of Expectation: \(E[\sum X_i] = \sum E[X_i] = \sum p = np\))
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(Y) = np(1-p)\)
    (Since trials are independent, variances add up: \(\sum p(1-p) = np(1-p)\))

3. ML Connection: Ensembles & Bagging

Random Forests Ensemble Learning

The Wisdom of Crowds

Imagine you have an ensemble of \(n=100\) weak classifiers (e.g., small decision trees). Assume each classifier is independent and has an accuracy of \(p=0.6\).

The ensemble decides by majority vote (>50 votes). What is the accuracy of the ensemble?

$$ P(Y > 50) = \sum_{k=51}^{100} \binom{100}{k} (0.6)^k (0.4)^{100-k} \approx 0.97 $$

The probability jumps from 60% (single model) to 97% (ensemble)! This is the power of the Binomial Cumulative Distribution (Condorcet's Jury Theorem).

Assumption Check: Independence

The Binomial math assumes Independence. If all your classifiers make the same mistakes (high correlation), the ensemble gain vanishes. This is why "Bagging" (Bootstrap Aggregating) intentionally introduces randomness—to make the trees as independent as possible.

4. Visualization

Python: The PMF Shape

from scipy.stats import binom
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

n, p = 20, 0.5
x = range(n + 1)
pmf = binom.pmf(x, n, p)

plt.bar(x, pmf, color='cyan', alpha=0.7)
plt.title(f'Binomial PMF (n={n}, p={p})')
plt.xlabel('Number of Successes (k)')
plt.ylabel('Likelihood')
plt.show()

# As n increases, this shape approaches a Bell Curve (Normal Distribution).
# This is the De Moivre–Laplace theorem, a special case of the CLT.
