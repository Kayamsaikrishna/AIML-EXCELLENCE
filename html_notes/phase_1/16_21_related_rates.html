21. Related Rates

Tracking how changes ripple through an equation.

1. The Problem

In Related Rates problems, we want to find the rate at which one quantity is changing based on the known rate of change of another quantity. These quantities are typically related by an equation that links them (like the Pythagorean theorem or volume formulas).

2. The Strategy

  1. Identify the variables and the known rates (\(\frac{dx}{dt}, \frac{dy}{dt}, \dots\)).
  2. Write an equation relating the variables.
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to time (\(t\)) using Implicit Differentiation.
  4. Plug in the known values and solve.

3. AI/ML Connection: Dynamic Latent Spaces

Dependencies in Feature Engineering

In AI systems that model physical environments (like Autonomous Driving), variables are highly related. For example, if a car's distance from an obstacle is decreasing at rate \(R\), how fast is the "risk factor" (an implicit function of distance and speed) increasing?

Understanding related rates allows engineers to build Safety Filters that predict how a change in one feature (e.g., tire pressure) will affect a related performance metric (e.g., fuel efficiency or braking distance) over time without needing to model every single interaction explicitly.

4. Python: A Simple Simulation

# A circle is growing! dR/dt = 2 cm/s. 
# How fast is Area growing when R = 10?
# Area = pi * R^2 -> dA/dt = 2 * pi * R * dR/dt

def da_dt(R, dr_dt):
    import math
    return 2 * math.pi * R * dr_dt

radius = 10
change_in_radius = 2

growth_rate = da_dt(radius, change_in_radius)
print(f"At R={radius}, Area is growing at {growth_rate:.2f} cm^2/s")
