12. Law of Iterated Expectations (Adam's Law)

Averages of averages: The foundation of hierarchical logic.

1. Definition

The Law of Iterated Expectations (also known as Adam's Law or the Tower Property) states that the expected value of the conditional expected value of $X$ given $Y$ is the same as the expected value of $X$.

$$ E[X] = E[E[X \mid Y]] $$

Essentially, to find the "Global" average, you can first find the average for each subgroup ($Y$) and then take the average of those subgroup results.

2. Intuition: The Multi-Level Survey

If you want to find the average height of a nation (X), you can first find the average height for each city (Y), and then average the city results (weighted by population). Adam's Law proves that this shortcut yields the exact same mathematical answer as measuring every individual in the nation.

3. AI/ML Connection: Meta-Learning and Hierarchical Models

Learning to Learn

Adam's Law is the mathematical engine behind Meta-Learning and Multilevel AI.

In **Hierarchical Bayesian Modeling**, we assume that data comes from subgroups (e.g., student scores in different schools). We use Adam's Law to estimate global parameters while accounting for subgroup variance. In **Meta-Learning** (learning across different tasks), we calculate the "Inner Loop" (expected performance on a specific task) and then use the "Outer Loop" (Adam's Law) to optimize the model's global ability to learn any new task. It is also essential in **Reinforcement Learning** (specifically the Bellman Equations), where the value of a state is the expected value of the expected rewards of all future possible actions.

4. Python: Simulating Nested Averages

import numpy as np

# Populations from 3 cities
city_data = [
    np.random.normal(160, 5, 100), # City A
    np.random.normal(175, 5, 200), # City B
    np.random.normal(150, 5, 50)   # City C
]

# Method 1: Global Mean
all_data = np.concatenate(city_data)
global_mean = np.mean(all_data)

# Method 2: Mean of Means (Adam's Law)
city_means = [np.mean(c) for c in city_data]
weights = [len(c)/len(all_data) for c in city_data]
iterated_mean = np.sum(np.array(city_means) * np.array(weights))

print(f"Global: {global_mean:.2f}")
print(f"Iterated: {iterated_mean:.2f}") # Matches!
