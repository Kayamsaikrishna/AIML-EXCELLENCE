04. Rank-Nullity Theorem (Revisited)

Dimensions are never created or destroyed.

1. The Conservation Law

For any linear transformation \(T: V \to W\), the sum of the dimension of its kernel and the dimension of its image equals the dimension of its domain:

$$ \text{dim}(\text{ker}(T)) + \text{dim}(\text{im}(T)) = \text{dim}(V) $$

Or in matrix terms: \(\text{Nullity} + \text{Rank} = n\) (number of columns).

2. Importance of the Split

  • Rank: Information preserved.
  • Nullity: Information lost/collapsed.

3. AI Context: Over-parameterization

In high-dimensional AI models, we often have millions of parameters (large \(\text{dim}(V)\)) but a training set that covers only a small subspace (small \(\text{Rank}\)). This implies a massive \(\text{Nullity}\), which explains why many different weight configurations can solve the same problem.

4. Python: Verifying the Sum

import numpy as np
from scipy.linalg import null_space

A = np.random.randn(5, 10) # 5x10 Matrix

rank = np.linalg.matrix_rank(A)
nullity = null_space(A).shape[1]
n_cols = A.shape[1]

print(f"Rank ({rank}) + Nullity ({nullity}) = {rank + nullity}")
print(f"Match Domain Dim ({n_cols})? {rank + nullity == n_cols}")
