Stats 1.6 Gaussian Updates

Conjugate Priors: Normal

Updating your belief about a continuous value using Gaussian evidence.

The Precision Rule

In Bayesian Normal updates, we don't think in "Variance" (\(\sigma^2\)); we think in Precision (\(\tau = 1/\sigma^2\)).

Rule: The Posterior Precision is simply the sum of the Prior Precision and the Data Precision.

1. The Formula (Known Variance \(\sigma^2\))

Suppose your Prior is \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \tau_0)\) and you observe a single data point \(x\) from \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\).

$$ \mu_{post} = \frac{\tau_0 \mu_0 + \tau_{data} x}{\tau_0 + \tau_{data}} $$ $$ \tau_{post} = \tau_0 + \tau_{data} $$

The Posterior Mean is a Weighted Average. If you are very sure of your prior (\(\tau_0\) is huge), the mean barely moves. If the data is super precise (\(\tau_{data}\) is huge), the mean jumps to the data.

2. ML Connection: Sensor Fusion

Robotics IoT

Combining GPS and IMU

A self-driving car needs to know its exact position (\(x\)). - Prior (IMU/Dead Reckoning): "I think I'm at 100m, but my sensors are drifting (\(\sigma \approx 5m\))." - Data (GPS): "GPS says 105m, but it's noisy (\(\sigma \approx 10m\))."

The Bayesian Solution: The car performs a Normal-Normal update (the heart of a **Kalman Filter**). Because both information sources are "Normal," the car mathematically combines them to get a position that is more accurate than either sensor alone.

This is why your phone's location "snaps" to the road. It's merging a Prior (you were on the road 1 second ago) with New Data (noisy GPS) using Bayesian Normal updates.
