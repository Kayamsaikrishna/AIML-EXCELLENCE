Stats 1.8 Causality

Experimental Randomization

The "Great Equalizer". How random assignment turns a messy collection of data into a scientific proof of causality.

Correlation vs. Causality

In observational data, we see patterns. But those patterns are often "noisy" with hidden variables.

Randomization is the process that washes away these hidden variables (Confounders) so that the ONLY difference between Group A and Group B is the one thing we changed.

1. The Random Assignment Rule

Random Sampling vs. Random Assignment

Random Sampling: Picking who gets to be in the study (Ensures External Validity - generalizes to the world).
Random Assignment: Deciding who gets the Treatment vs. the Control (Ensures Internal Validity - proves the treatment works).

The Logic of Balance

By using a coin flip, we ensure that both groups have the same average age, same average income, and even same average "hidden" traits (like willpower or genetics).

3. ML Connection: Randomized Controlled Trials (RCT)

Product ML A/B Testing

The Gold Standard of Feature Launches

In Machine Learning, we often assume the data is "given." But in Product Engineering, we create the data through RCTs (A/B Tests).

  • Selection Bias: If you only show a "New Premium Filter" to users who are already spending a lot of time on your app, the filter will look successful. But is it the filter, or just the fact that they were already heavy users? Randomization solves this.
  • Washout Periods: In ML pipelines, randomization ensures that "Model Drift" or "Temporal Effects" don't skew the results of a new model deployment.
  • Simulation: In Reinforcement Learning (RL), randomization (as in Epsilon-Greedy) is essential to explore the environment without getting trapped in a local biased loop.
Statistical Secret: Randomization doesn't mean the groups will be *identical*. It means the differences between them are Random Static, which allows us to use Standard Error to calculate if the result is "Real" or "Luck".
