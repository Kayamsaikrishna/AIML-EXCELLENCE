Calculus 1.3 Simplification

Logarithmic Differentiation

Don't suffer through the Product Rule 50 times. Turn the product into a sum first.

The "Log-First" Strategy

Some functions are just too messy to differentiate directly (e.g., \(x^x\)). By taking the Natural Log of both sides before differentiating, we use the property \(\ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b\) to smash products into sums, and \(\ln(a^n) = n \ln a\) to pull exponents down.

1. The Four-Step Process

  1. Log Both Sides: Take \(\ln(y) = \ln(f(x))\).
  2. Expand: Use log laws to simplify the right side.
  3. Differentiate: Remember LHS becomes \(\frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx}\).
  4. Solve: Multiply by \(y\) to isolate \(\frac{dy}{dx}\).

2. AI/ML Connection: Maximizing Likelihood

Why Products Kill Gradients

In Bayes Nets or Language Models, the probability of a sequence is the product of token probabilities: \[ P(\text{sentence}) = P(w_1) \cdot P(w_2) \cdot ... \cdot P(w_n) \]

Differentiating a product of 100 terms would require a Product Rule with 100 components. It's a nightmare. Even worse, multiplying 100 small numbers results in Numerical Underflow (the computer thinks it's zero).

However, if we Maximize the Log-Likelihood: \[ \ln P = \ln P(w_1) + \ln P(w_2) + ... \] The derivative is just the sum of individual derivatives. This is computationally stable and trivial to parallelize.

3. Python: Solving the Unsolvable

x_power_x.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = x**x # Direct Power rule fails (base is not constant). Direct Exp rule fails (exponent is not variable-only).

# 1. Manual Log Differentiation
# ln(y) = x * ln(x)
# y'/y = 1*ln(x) + x*(1/x) = ln(x) + 1
# y' = y * (ln(x) + 1)
manual_deriv = f * (sp.log(x) + 1)

# 2. Automated SymPy
auto_deriv = sp.diff(f, x)

print(f"Function: {f}")
print(f"Manual Deriv: {manual_deriv}")
print(f"Auto Deriv:   {auto_deriv}")

# Verify
if sp.simplify(manual_deriv - auto_deriv) == 0:
    print("\n✅ Log-Diff matches SymPy.")
else:
    print("❌ Discrepancy found.")
