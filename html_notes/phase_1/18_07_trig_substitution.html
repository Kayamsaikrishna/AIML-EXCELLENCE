07. Trigonometric Substitution

Using triangles to solve square roots.

1. The Goal

Trig substitution is a specialized form of U-substitution used to integrate functions containing square roots of the following forms:

Expression Substitution Identity Used
\(\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}\) \(x = a \sin \theta\) \(1 - \sin^2 \theta = \cos^2 \theta\)
\(\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}\) \(x = a \tan \theta\) \(1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta\)
\(\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}\) \(x = a \sec \theta\) \(\sec^2 \theta - 1 = \tan^2 \theta\)

2. The Reverse Step

After integrating in terms of \(\theta\), you must draw a Reference Triangle to convert the answer back into \(x\). This step is where most errors occur.

3. AI/ML Connection: Manifold Geometry

Circular Latent Spaces

In Computer Vision, we often deal with "Rotation" and "Pose". If an AI model is trying to calculate the average orientation or area within a circular view, it must integrate over circular domains.

The \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\) form is the upper half of a unit circle. Integrating this allows us to calculate geometric priors for models. Without trig substitution, we wouldn't have the precise analytical formulas for the geometry of spheres and circles that AI models use to understand spatial dimensions in 3D reconstruction.

4. Python: Radial Integration

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# Integral of sqrt(1 - x^2) - representing a circle's area
f = sp.sqrt(1 - x**2)

# This requires substitution x = sin(theta)
result = sp.integrate(f, x)

print(f"Integral is: {result}")
# Output: x*sqrt(1 - x**2)/2 + asin(x)/2
