Stats 1.5 Modeling

Chi-Square (\(\chi^2\)) Goodness of Fit

Testing if your observed sample "fits" a specific theoretical distribution (like a fair die or a Poisson spread).

The Distribution Matcher

Does my data follow the pattern I think it does? - Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\)): The data fits the specified distribution. - Alternative (\(H_a\)): The data does NOT fit the distribution.

1. The Test Statistic

$$ \chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E} $$ $$ df = k - 1 $$
  • \(k\): Number of categories.
  • \(E\): Calculated based on the hypothesized percentages.

2. ML Connection: Cluster Analysis

Unsupervised Validation

Do clusters match reality?

You run K-Means Clustering on customer data. Based on historical research, you expect 50% "Budget," 30% "Mid," and 20% "Premium" customers.

The Test: 1. Count how many users K-Means assigned to each group. 2. Calculate the Expected counts (\(N \times 0.5\), \(N \times 0.3\), etc.). 3. Perform a Goodness of Fit test.

If the P-value is very small, it means your K-Means model is creating clusters that significantly deviate from the known business segments. This might mean your features are wrong or the customer behavior has shifted drastically!

Synthetic Data Evaluation

In Generative AI (GANs), you use this test to see if the categorical distribution of your "Fake" data matches the categorical distribution of the "Real" training data. If they match (\(P > 0.05\)), the generator is doing a good job!
