05. Hypergeometric Distribution

Sampling without replacement: when trials are dependent.

1. Definition

The Hypergeometric Distribution models the number of successes in $n$ draws from a finite population of size $N$ that contains exactly $K$ successes, without replacement.

$$ P(X=k) = \frac{\binom{K}{k} \binom{N-K}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}} $$

Unlike the Binomial distribution, the trials are not independent because the probability of success changes with each draw.

2. Relation to Binomial

When the population size $N$ is very large relative to the sample size $n$ ($N \gg n$), the Hypergeometric distribution is well-approximated by the Binomial distribution, as the change in success probability becomes negligible.

3. AI/ML Connection: Data Bias and Significance

Detecting Over-Representation

The Hypergeometric distribution is the core of **Fisher's Exact Test**, used to detect bias in datasets.

In **Fairness AI**, if you have a dataset of 1000 resumes (the population $N$) with 10% from a minority group ($K$), and an AI selects 50 candidates ($n$), you use the Hypergeometric distribution to calculate the probability of seeing $k$ minority candidates by random chance. If the actual $k$ is extremely low, the Hypergeometric PMF provides the **p-value** for proving that the AI is biased. It is also used in **Biology-focused AI** (Gene Set Enrichment Analysis) to determine if a specific functional category of genes is over-represented in a search result. Understanding this distribution allows you to mathematically prove whether a pattern in your data is a genuine discovery or just a sampling fluke.

4. Python: Sampling and Significance

from scipy.stats import hypergeom

# N=100 (Total), K=20 (Successes), n=10 (Sample)
N, K, n = 100, 20, 10

# Probability of getting exactly 2 successes
print(f"P(X=2): {hypergeom.pmf(2, N, K, n):.4f}")

# Simulating the mean
print(f"Expected successes: {hypergeom.mean(N, K, n)}") # n * (K/N) = 2.0
