Stats 1.6 The Updated Belief

Posterior Distribution

The compromise between what you thought before and what the data tells you now.

Bayes' Theorem for Models

$$ P(\theta|D) = \frac{P(D|\theta) P(\theta)}{P(D)} $$

- \(P(\theta|D)\): Posterior (Our goal). - \(P(D|\theta)\): Likelihood (Data fit). - \(P(\theta)\): Prior (Our guess). - \(P(D)\): Marginal Likelihood (Evidence).

1. The Balancing Act

The Posterior is always a weighted average of the Prior and the Likelihood.

  • If the Data is Weak (unclear), the Posterior stays close to the Prior.
  • If the Data is Strong (massive evidence), the Posterior gets pulled away from the prior and follows the Likelihood.

2. The Marginal Likelihood Trap

The denominator \(P(D)\) is the probability of the data under *all possible* parameter values.

$$ P(D) = \int P(D|\theta) P(\theta) d\theta $$

This integral is the "Big Boss" of Bayesian stats. For simple models, we can solve it. For complex models (like Deep Learning), this integral is impossible to compute. This is why we use tricks like MCMC or Variational Inference to find the posterior without ever knowing \(P(D)\).

3. ML Connection: Online Learning

Stream Processing Ops

Sequential Bayesian Updating

One of the most powerful features of the Bayesian paradigm is that today's Posterior becomes tomorrow's Prior.

graph LR A[Day 1 Prior] -->|Data 1| B[Day 1 Posterior] B --> C[Day 2 Prior] C -->|Data 2| D[Day 2 Posterior] D --> E[And so on...]

This allows for Online Learning. You don't need to retrain your model on the entire history of data every night. You just update your "Belief" (weights) using the new batch of data. This is how CTR (Click-Through-Rate) predictors in ad-tech work, updating millions of times per hour as users click on banners.
