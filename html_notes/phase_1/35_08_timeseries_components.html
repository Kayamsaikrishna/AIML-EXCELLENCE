08. Introduction to Time Series

Extracting the pattern from the flow of time.

1. The Temporal Flow

A Time Series is a sequence of data points indexed in time order. Unlike standard data, Time Series observations are Dependent: what happens today is usually related to what happened yesterday.

2. The Four Components

  • Trend: The long-term increase or decrease (e.g., Global Warming).
  • Seasonality: Patterns that repeat at regular intervals (e.g., Ice cream sales in summer).
  • Cyclic: Wavelike rises and falls of irregular length (e.g., Economic recessions).
  • Irregular: Random noise / white noise that cannot be explained.

3. AI/ML Connection: STL Decomposition and Pre-processing

Simplified Learning

In AI, understanding these components is the first step in Time-Series Feature Engineering**.

An AI model struggled to predict **Retail Demand** if the raw numbers jump around. By using **STL Decomposition (Seasonal-Trend Loess)**, we strip away the Trend and Seasonality, giving the AI a "Clean" residual signal to learn from. This is how Prophet (by Meta)** and DeepAR (by Amazon)** work: they explicitly model the trend and seasonality and use a Neural Network to handle the complex residuals. Understanding decomposition allows AI engineers to build **Multi-horizon Forecasts**: the Trend predicts the next 5 years, while the Seasonality predicts the next 5 Sundays. Separation of concerns leads to more stable and explainable AI results.

4. Python: Visualizing Decomposition

from statsmodels.tsa.seasonal import seasonal_decompose

# Decomposing the signal
result = seasonal_decompose(data, model='additive', period=12)

# result.trend, result.seasonal, result.resid
result.plot()
# Seeing the hidden patterns behind the noise!
