Stats 1.8 Stability

Stationarity & Unit Root

The requirement of predictable statistics. Why we can't predict a signal that keeps changing its rules.

The Rule of Regularity

A Time Series is Stationary if its statistical properties do not change over time.

Imagine trying to predict who will win a football game, but the field gets 10 meters longer every minute and the ball gets heavier every second. That is Non-Stationary. You can't use past data to predict the future because the future is a different game.

1. The Three Conditions

1. Constant Mean (\(\mu\))

The average value stays the same. (No Trend).

2. Constant Variance (\(\sigma^2\))

The "swing" or volatility of the data doesn't grow or shrink over time.

3. Constant Autocovariance

The relationship between time \(t\) and time \(t-k\) depends only on the gap \(k\), not on what time it is.

2. The Unit Root (The "Memory" Problem)

$$ Y_t = \phi Y_{t-1} + \epsilon_t $$

- If **\(|\phi| < 1\)**: The series is stationary. Previous shocks eventually die out. - If **\(\phi=1\)**: You have a **Unit Root** (Random Walk). Shocks last forever. The series "wanders" aimlessly.

ADF Test (Augmented Dickey-Fuller): - \(H_0\): Series has a unit root (Non-Stationary). - \(p \le 0.05\): Reject \(H_0\) \(\rightarrow\) Series is Stationary.

3. ML Connection: Spurious Correlations

Deep Learning Validation

Why AI models "hallucinate" in Finance

Non-stationary data is the #1 reason why finance models fail in production.

  • Spurious Regression: If you regress "Global Warming" against "Number of Pirates", you will find a perfect correlation just because both have a strong trend. This is a statistical illusion.
  • Feature Drift: If your model is trained on Stationary data but the environment becomes Non-Stationary (e.g., a stock market crash), your model's weights become obsolete instantly because the "distribution" of the world has shifted.
  • Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs): LSTMs and GRUs are designed to handle long-term memory, but even they struggle with unit roots. De-trending the data into a stationary format before feeding it into an LSTM often yields much better results.
Engineer's Checklist: Before you run any time series model: 1. Plot the values. 2. Run an ADF Test. 3. If it fails, use Differencing or Log-Transforms (The next topic!) to "fix" it into stationarity.
