Stats 1.8 Smoothing

Moving Averages & Smoothing

Polishing the past. How to remove "Jitter" from your data to see the underlying truth.

The Low-Pass Filter

In engineering, we use capacitors to smooth electrical signals. In statistics, we use Averages.

A smoothing function acts as a "Low-Pass Filter"—it lets the slow, meaningful trends pass through while blocking the high-frequency random noise.

1. Simple vs. Exponential

Simple Moving Average (SMA)

$$ \text{SMA}_t = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} Y_{t-i} $$

Every data point in the window has equal weight.
Pros: Easy to explain.
Cons: It "lags" significantly and reacts slowly to new trends.

Exponential Moving Average (EMA)

$$ S_t = \alpha Y_t + (1-\alpha) S_{t-1} $$

Recent data is weighted more heavily than old data.
Pros: Much more responsive to sudden price changes.
Cons: Very sensitive to outliers.

2. Complex Smoothing (Holt-Winters)

Double Exponential (Holt)

Adds a separate smoothing factor for Trend. It can predict data that is consistently rising or falling.

Triple Exponential (Holt-Winters)

Adds a third factor for Seasonality. The "Gold Standard" for non-ML forecasting of retail sales or weather.

3. ML Connection: Signal Denoising

Computer Vision Optimizer

Why "Smoothing" is everywhere in AI

We use the math of EMA/SMA even when we aren't doing Time Series work:

  • Gradient Descent with Momentum: Optimizers like SGD with Momentum or Adam use EMA to smooth out the updates. This prevents the training from "bouncing" around and helps it roll smoothly down the loss curve.
  • Batch Normalization: During inference, Batch Norm uses a Moving Average of the training mean/variance to normalize the inputs.
  • Image Kernels: A "Gaussian Blur" in Computer Vision is essentially a 2D weighted moving average. It smooths out pixel noise so the model can see larger shapes and edges.
  • Feature Engineering: When predicting user churn, we often create features like `avg_login_time_last_7_days`. This is an SMA that reduces the noise of a single bad day.
Engineer's Warning: Smoothing introduces Look-ahead Bias if not handled carefully. Ensure your window only looks at the *past* (\(t, t-1...\)) and never the *future* when calculating features for training.
