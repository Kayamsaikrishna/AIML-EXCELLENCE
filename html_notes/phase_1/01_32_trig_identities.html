32. Trigonometric Identities

The rules of equivalence that simplify the universe.

1. Fundamental Identities

$$ \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1 \quad \text{(Pythagorean)} $$ $$ 1 + \tan^2 \theta = \sec^2 \theta $$ $$ 1 + \cot^2 \theta = \csc^2 \theta $$
$$ \tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} \quad \cot \theta = \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta} $$

2. Signal Separation in AI

Fourier Analysis & De-noising

Identities are not just academic; they allow us to convert complex signals into manageable components. In Fourier Transforms (used in speech-to-text), trig identities help decompose a messy sound wave into a sum of clean sines and cosines.

  • Hardware Optimization: Some AI acceleration hardware (DSP) can compute \(\sin^2 \theta\) faster than \((1 - \cos^2 \theta)\). Using identities allows compilers to optimize execution speed.
  • Stability: Swapping identities can sometimes prevent numerical overflow/underflow in custom loss functions.

3. Python: Identity Verification

import numpy as np

theta = np.random.uniform(0, 2*np.pi, 1000)

# Verifying sin^2 + cos^2 = 1
identity_1 = np.sin(theta)**2 + np.cos(theta)**2

# All values should be extremely close to 1.0
print(f"Mean value: {np.mean(identity_1)}")
print(f"Max deviation: {np.max(np.abs(identity_1 - 1.0))}")

# Output:
# Mean value: 1.0
# Max deviation: 2.22e-16 (Floating point precision limit)
