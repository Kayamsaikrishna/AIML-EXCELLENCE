1. The Fundamental Truth

$$ \sin^2(\theta) + \cos^2(\theta) = 1 $$

This isn't just a rule to memorize for exams. It tells us that sine and cosine are Unit Norm partners. They share a fixed budget of "energy" (1.0). If sine increases, cosine MUST decrease.

2. Constraining the Weights

In Deep Learning, we often want our weight vectors \( W \) to act like unit vectors on a circle.

Cosine Similarity depends on vectors being normalized. If they aren't, "Larger" vectors dominate the dot product, biasing the model.

$$ W_{norm} = \frac{W}{\sqrt{\sum W_i^2}} $$

This ensures the model focuses on Direction (Angle), not Magnitude (Strength). Exactly like Sine/Cosine ignore radius size.

3. Enforcing the Identity

Normalizing data to lie on the Unit Circle.

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# A random vector (NOT on unit circle)
v = np.array([3.0, 4.0])

# Magnitude
mag = np.linalg.norm(v) # sqrt(9+16) = 5
print(f"Original Magnitude: {mag}")

# Normalize (Project onto Unit Circle)
v_norm = v / mag # [0.6, 0.8]

# Check Identity
identity = v_norm[0]**2 + v_norm[1]**2
print(f"Normalized Vector: {v_norm}")
print(f"Identity Check: {identity:.2f}") # 1.00
