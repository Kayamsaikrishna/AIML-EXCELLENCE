07. The Product Rule

Differentiating the interaction of two variables.

1. The Rule

If you have a function that is the product of two other functions, \(f(x) = u(x)v(x)\), the derivative is not simply the product of their derivatives. Instead:

$$ \frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot v] = u \frac{dv}{dx} + v \frac{du}{dx} $$

"First times the derivative of the second, plus the second times the derivative of the first."

2. Why is it not \(u'v'\)?

Imagine a rectangle with sides \(u\) and \(v\). If both sides grow, the area increases not just by the growth of individual sides, but by the interaction of the existing length with the new width, and vice-versa.

3. AI/ML Connection: Probability Densities

Likelihood Gradients

In Bayesian Machine Learning, we often work with the product of independent probabilities (the Joint Likelihood). When calculating the gradient of the likelihood with respect to parameters, the Product Rule is essential.

Note: We often take the logarithm specifically to turn these products into sums (Sum Rule), but the Product Rule remains fundamental to the math of interactions in neural network gates (like GRUs and LSTMs).

4. Python: Product Rule Verification

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
u = x**2
v = sp.sin(x)

# Differentiating x^2 * sin(x)
product_deriv = sp.diff(u * v, x)

print(f"u: {u}, v: {v}")
print(f"d/dx [u*v]: {product_deriv}") 
# Output: x**2*cos(x) + 2*x*sin(x)
