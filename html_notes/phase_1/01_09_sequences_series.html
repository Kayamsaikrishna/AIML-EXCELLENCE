Sequences, Series & Induction

The math of time and iteration. Dealing with data that has an order (Time Series, NLP).

24. Sequences

An ordered list of numbers: \( a_1, a_2, a_3, \dots \)

AI: Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)

Standard Neural Networks treat input as a snapshot. RNNs treat input as a Sequence.

$$ h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t) $$

The state at time \(t\) depends on the state at \(t-1\). This is a recursive sequence definition!

Arithmetic Sequence

\( a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d \). (Linear Growth)

Geometric Sequence

\( a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{n-1} \). (Exponential Growth/Decay)

25. Series

The sum of a sequence. \( S_n = \sum a_i \).

Deep Dive: Taylor Series

In Calculus, we approximate complex functions (like \(e^x\) or \(\sin x\)) as an infinite series of polynomials.

$$ f(x) \approx f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \frac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2 + \dots $$

Gradient Descent is basically just using the first two terms of the Taylor Series expansion to guess where the bottom of the hill is.

26. Summation Notation ($\Sigma$)

The "For Loop" of Mathematics.

$$ \sum_{i=1}^{N} x_i $$

This symbol is everywhere in AI. The Error function is usually a Sum of Squared Errors across $N$ examples.

27. Mathematical Induction

How do we prove something is true for all cases?

  1. Base Case: Prove it works for \(n=1\).
  2. Inductive Step: Prove that if it works for \(n=k\), it MUST work for \(n=k+1\).
Generalization

In AI, we want models that "Generalize". We train on \(k\) examples and hope it works on the \(k+1\)-th example (Test Set). While not a formal proof, the logic of learning is inductive: observing patterns and assuming they hold universally.
