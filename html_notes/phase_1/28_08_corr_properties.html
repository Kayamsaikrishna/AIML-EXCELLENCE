08. Properties of Correlation

Why correlation is robust (and where it fails).

1. Key Algebraic Properties

  • Range: $-1 \le \rho \le 1$.
  • Symmetry: $Corr(X, Y) = Corr(Y, X)$.
  • Independence: If $X, Y$ are independent, $Corr(X, Y) = 0$.
  • Linear Invariance: $Corr(aX+b, cY+d) = Corr(X, Y)$ if $a, c$ have the same sign.

2. The "Linear Only" Warning

Correlation only measures **Linear Relationships**. If $Y = X^2$, the relationship is perfect, but the linear correlation $\rho$ might be near 0. This is a common pitfall where an AI engineer might think a feature is useless because of low correlation, despite it having a powerful non-linear relationship.

3. AI/ML Connection: Scaling and Preprocessing

Robustness to Units

Correlation is the reason why **Min-Max Scaling** or **Z-Score Normalization** doesn't destroy your data relationships.

Because correlation is invariant to scale and shift ($aX+b$), transforming your features (like converting 'Feet' to 'Meters') does not change the predictive strength that the model can extract. In **Autoencoders**, we use this property to ensure that the compressed representation (latent space) preserves the "Structure" of the input even if the raw numbers are different. However, the limitation of "Linear Only" is why we need **Non-linear Activation Functions** (like ReLU) and **Kernels** (in SVMs)—they allow the AI to "see" the relationships that a standard correlation coefficient would miss.

4. Python: Linear vs Non-Linear

x = np.linspace(-10, 10, 100)
y_linear = 2 * x + 5
y_quadratic = x**2

print(f"Linear Correlation: {np.corrcoef(x, y_linear)[0, 1]:.4f}") # 1.0
print(f"Quadratic Correlation: {np.corrcoef(x, y_quadratic)[0, 1]:.4f}") # 0.0!
