04. Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN)

The guarantee that sample averages converge in probability.

1. Definition

The Weak Law of Large Numbers states that as the number of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples $n$ increases, the sample mean $\bar{X}_n$ converges in probability to the true population mean $\mu$.

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon) = 0 \quad \text{for any } \epsilon > 0 $$

This means that for any small margin of error $\epsilon$, the probability that the average is "wrong" goes to zero as we get more data.

2. Intuition: The Truth in Numbers

If you flip a fair coin 10 times, you might get 7 heads (70%). But if you flip it 1,000,000 times, the chance of getting 70% heads is effectively zero. The "Weak" law guarantees that the average will **eventually** stay within the neighborhood of the truth.

3. AI/ML Connection: Consistency and Big Data

Why More Data is Better

The WLLN is why **Supervised Learning** works at all.

In **Statistics**, we call an estimator "Consistent" if it follows the WLLN. When we calculate the "Training Error" of an AI on a dataset, we are using the dataset average as an estimate for the "True Generalization Error." The WLLN guarantees that as we increase the size of our training set, the performance we see during training will become a perfect reflection of the model's true capability. This is the mathematical justification for the "Big Data" revolution—it proves that we can find the "True" patterns of the world simply by looking at enough examples.

4. Python: Observing Convergence

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Simulate n coin flips and track the running average
n_trials = 5000
flips = np.random.binomial(1, 0.5, n_trials)
running_avg = np.cumsum(flips) / np.arange(1, n_trials + 1)

plt.plot(running_avg)
plt.axhline(y=0.5, color='r', linestyle='--')
plt.title("WLLN: Convergence of Sample Mean")
plt.xlabel("Number of Samples")
plt.ylabel("Sample Mean")
plt.show()
