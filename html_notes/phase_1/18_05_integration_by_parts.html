05. Integration by Parts

Reversing the Product Rule.

1. The Formula

Integration by Parts is used to integrate the product of two different types of functions (e.g., \(x \cdot \sin x\)).

$$ \int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du $$

Choosing which part is \(u\) and which is \(dv\) is the key. Use the LIATE priority rule:

  1. Logarithmic
  2. Inverse Trig
  3. Algebraic
  4. Trigonometric
  5. Exponential

2. Why does it work?

It is derived directly from the Product Rule for differentiation: \(\frac{d}{dx}[uv] = u v' + v u'\). By integrating both sides and rearranging, we get the IBP formula. It allows us to trade a difficult integral for a simpler one by differentiating one part (\(u\)) and integrating the other (\(dv\)).

3. AI/ML Connection: Entropy & KL Divergence

Analytic Solutions for Distributions

In Information Theory, we often calculate the "Kullback-Leibler Divergence" to measure how different two probability distributions are. For Gaussian distributions, this involves integrals like \(\int x \cdot \ln(x) \cdot e^{-x^2} dx\).

These complex integrals are solved using repeated Integration by Parts. Without these analytic solutions, we wouldn't have the closed-form loss functions used in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which would make them much slower to train.

4. Python: IBP Verification

# Integral of x * exp(x)
# Let u = x, dv = exp(x) dx
# Then du = dx, v = exp(x)
# Result: x*exp(x) - exp(x) + C

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = x * sp.exp(x)

result = sp.integrate(f, x)

print(f"Integral is: {result} + C") 
# Output: (x - 1)*exp(x) + C
