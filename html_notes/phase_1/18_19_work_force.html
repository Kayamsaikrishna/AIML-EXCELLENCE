19. Work and Fluid Force

Applying integrals to energy and pressure.

1. Work Done by Variable Force

If a force \(F(x)\) is constant, \(W = F \cdot d\). But if the force changes (like a stretching spring), we must integrate:

$$ W = \int_{a}^{b} F(x) dx $$

Common examples include Hooke's Law (\(F=kx\)) and Pumping Liquids out of a tank.

2. Hydrostatic Force

To calculate the force of water against a dam, we integrate the pressure: \(F = \int \rho g d(y) w(y) dy\), where \(\rho\) is density, \(d\) is depth, and \(w\) is width.

3. AI/ML Connection: Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

Solving Differential Equations with AI

Traditional AI learns purely from data. But Physics-Informed AI is trained with a "Loss Function" that includes these integral laws. For example, if a robot is moving through water, the AI is penalized if its predicted motion violates the laws of fluid force.

This allows AI to learn from very little data because "the laws of physics act as a guide". These models are widely used in Weather Prediction and Energy-Efficient Supercomputing (optimizing the cooling and power work required for server farms).

4. Python: Spring Work Calculation

# Hooke's Law: F = 10x
# Work to stretch from 0 to 2 meters
# Integral of 10x from 0 to 2

def spring_work(k, start, end):
    return 0.5 * k * (end**2 - start**2)

k = 10 # Spring constant
result = spring_work(k, 0, 2)

print(f"Work done: {result} Joules")
