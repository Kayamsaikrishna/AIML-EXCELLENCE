Stats 1.6 Best Practices

The Bayesian Workflow

A rigorous cycle of building, checking, and improving models using probability as the compass.

Beyond just "Running Bayes"

A professional Bayesian doesn't just calculate a posterior and quit. They constantly ask: "Does my model still make sense in the real world?"

1. The Four Steps

1. Mathematical Setup

Choosing your Prior and Likelihood. What is the generator of this data?

2. Prior Predictive Check

Simulate data from your prior. If your "prior" predicts that human heights can be -5 feet, your prior is broken! Fix it before looking at real data.

3. Inference

Merge data and prior to get the Posterior. (Usually using software like Stan, PyMC, or Edward).

4. Posterior Predictive Check (PPC)

Does the model's posterior "look" like the real data? If the real data has outliers and your model doesn't, the model is lying to you.

3. ML Connection: Probabilistic Programming

Software Automation

Stan and PyMC

You don't usually write Bayesian integrals by hand. You use **Probabilistic Programming Languages (PPLs)**.

In PyMC, you describe your model in Python code: mu = pm.Normal('mu', 0, 10) obs = pm.Normal('obs', mu, 1, observed=data)

The software then automatically chooses the best sampling algorithm (NUTS or Metropolis) to find the posterior. This "Declarative" approach to statistics allows ML engineers to build incredibly complex hierarchies (e.g., estimating conversion rates for 10,000 different stores simultaneously) with just a few lines of code.
