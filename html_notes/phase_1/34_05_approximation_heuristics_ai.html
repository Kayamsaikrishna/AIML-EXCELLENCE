Approximation & Heuristics in AI

Living with Hardness. Learning how to bypass NP-Completeness by accepting 'good enough' solutions that can be computed in practical timeframes.

The Perfection Trap

In theoretical CS, we want the *optimal* solution. In AI, an optimal solution that takes 100 years is worthless. We use Approximations (with provable bounds) and Heuristics (without provable bounds but work in practice) to make NPC problems tractable.

1. Approximation Algorithms

An algorithm that produces a solution within a factor \(\alpha\) of the optimal solution for every instance.

$$ \text{Approximation Ratio } \alpha = \max \left( \frac{C}{C^*}, \frac{C^*}{C} \right) $$

Where \(C\) is the cost of our solution and \(C^*\) is the optimal cost. Example: The 2-approximation for the Vertex Cover problem.

2. Heuristics & Metaheuristics

Strategies that "guide" the search for a solution using domain-specific knowledge or natural metaphors.

Local Search

Moving from a solution to a slightly better neighbor. Used in Hill Climbing and Simulated Annealing.

Greedy Heuristics

Making the locally optimal choice at each step. Fast but can get stuck in local optima.

Genetic Algorithms

Evolving solutions over generations using selection, crossover, and mutation. Great for non-differentiable NPC spaces.

Deep Learning CV

Why AI is "Heuristic by Nature"

  • Quantization: Approximation theory in action. We trade a small amount of model "optimality" (precision) for a massive gain in "tractability" (speed/memory).
  • Pruning: Removing 90% of weights in a neural network is a heuristic to reduce complexity while maintaining performance.
  • Approximate Nearest Neighbors (ANN): Algorithms like HNSW or FAISS solve the $O(N)$ search problem in roughly $O(\log N)$ by tolerating a small chance of missing the *absolute* closest vector.
  • Transformers & Sparsity: "Sparse Attention" is a heuristic that approximates the $O(L^2)$ dense attention matrix with an $O(L)$ or $O(L \log L)$ version to handle giant context windows.
