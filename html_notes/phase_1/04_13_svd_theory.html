1. The Definition

Any matrix \( A \) (even rectangular ones) can be factored into:

$$ A = U \Sigma V^T $$
  • \( U \): Left Singular Vectors (Orthonormal). Columns are eigenvectors of \( AA^T \).
  • \( \Sigma \): Singular Values (Diagonal, non-negative). Roots of eigenvalues of \( A^T A \).
  • \( V \): Right Singular Vectors (Orthonormal). Columns are eigenvectors of \( A^T A \).

2. The Swiss Army Knife

SVD is the engine behind:

  • PCA: Principal Component Analysis.
  • LSA: Latent Semantic Analysis (NLP).
  • Image Compression: Saving JPEGs.
  • Pseudoinverse: Solving rectangular systems.

3. SVD in NumPy

import numpy as np

A = np.array([
    [3, 1, 1],
    [-1, 3, 1]
]) # Rectangular Matrix! Eig would fail here.

# Compute SVD
U, S, Vt = np.linalg.svd(A)

print("U (Left Singular):")
print(U.round(2))

print("\nSigma (Singular Values):")
print(S.round(2))

print("\nV^T (Right Singular):")
print(Vt.round(2))

# Reconstruct
# Note: S is returned as a 1D array, need to expand it to matrix
Sigma = np.zeros((2, 3))
np.fill_diagonal(Sigma, S)

Recon = U @ Sigma @ Vt
print("\nReconstructed A:")
print(Recon.round(2))
