Prob 1.4 Consistency

Convergence in Probability (\(P\))

Formalizing the idea that outliers vanish as data points accumulate.

The Vanishing Error

A sequence of random variables \(X_n\) converges in probability to \(X\) if the probability of them being "different" than \(X\) (by any amount \(\epsilon\)) goes to zero.

Notation: \(X_n \xrightarrow{P} X\)

1. Definition

For every \(\epsilon > 0 \): $$ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(|X_n - X| \ge \epsilon) = 0 $$

2. ML Connection: Consistent Estimators

Statistics Inference

The Accuracy of Estimates

In ML, an estimator \(\hat{\theta}_n\) (like the weights of your model trained on \(n\) samples) is called Consistent if it converges in probability to the true parameter \(\theta^*\).

Consistency is the absolute minimum requirement for any valid statistical algorithm. If your model isn't consistent, even having infinite data wouldn't guarantee you find the right answer.

3. Properties

  • If \(X_n \xrightarrow{P} X\) and \(Y_n \xrightarrow{P} Y\), then \(X_n + Y_n \xrightarrow{P} X + Y\).
  • If \(g\) is a continuous function, then \(g(X_n) \xrightarrow{P} g(X)\).
