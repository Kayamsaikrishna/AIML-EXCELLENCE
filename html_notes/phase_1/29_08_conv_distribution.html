08. Convergence in Distribution

The "Weakest" convergence: Matching shapes, not values.

1. Definition

A sequence of random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots$ converges in distribution to a random variable $X$ if their cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) converge to the CDF of $X$ at every point where the target CDF is continuous.

$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} F_{X_n}(x) = F_X(x) $$

Notation: $X_n \xrightarrow{d} X$. This is also called "Weak Convergence."

2. Intuition: The Ghostly Shadow

Convergence in distribution is less strict than convergence in probability. It doesn't mean the individual values of $X_n$ are getting close to $X$ (they could be totally different random events). It only means that the **histogram** of $X_n$ eventually looks exactly like the **PDF** of $X$. The *behavior* of the system is becoming predictable, even if the specific outcomes are not.

3. AI/ML Connection: Why CLT Works

Approximating Complexity

Convergence in distribution is the mathematical engine behind the Central Limit Theorem.

In **Deep Learning Research**, we often deal with millions of parameters. Trying to calculate the exact probability distribution of a neural network's internal state is impossible. However, because these states converge in distribution to a Gaussian as the number of neurons grows, we can use simple "Gaussian Approximations" to prove that a model is stable or to calculate its **Generalization Error**. In **A/B Testing** for AI features, we use this convergence to justify using p-values and t-tests—we rely on the fact that the experimental results will converge in distribution to a known curve, allowing us to make decisions with statistical confidence.

4. Python: Shape Convergence

# Observe how Binomial(n, p) starts looking like Normal distribution
from scipy.stats import binom, norm

n = 100; p = 0.5
x = np.arange(0, 101)

# Binomial PMF
plt.bar(x, binom.pmf(x, n, p), alpha=0.5, label='Binomial')

# Normal PDF (Approximation)
mu, std = n*p, np.sqrt(n*p*(1-p))
plt.plot(x, norm.pdf(x, mu, std), color='red', label='Normal (Target)')

plt.title("Convergence in Distribution Demo")
plt.legend()
plt.show()
