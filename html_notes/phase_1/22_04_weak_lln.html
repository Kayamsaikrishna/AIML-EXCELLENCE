Prob 1.4 Consistency

Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN)

Proving that averages eventually stop fluctuating and settle on the truth.

The Stability of Averages

The WLLN states that if you take the average of many independent trials, the result is "probably" very close to the true theoretical average (the expectation).

"The more data you have, the less your average jumps around."

1. The Formal Statement

Let \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) be i.i.d. random variables with mean \(\mu\). Let \(\bar{X}_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum X_i\). Then for any \(\epsilon > 0 \): $$ \lim_{n \to \infty} P(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon) = 0 $$

This is called Convergence in Probability. It means that as \(n\) grows, the chance of the sample mean being "far" (by even a tiny amount \(\epsilon\)) from the true mean drops to zero.

2. Simple Proof (using Chebyshev)

Recall Chebyshev: \(P(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon) \le \frac{\text{Var}(\bar{X}_n)}{\epsilon^2}\).
Since \(\text{Var}(\bar{X}_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\), the bound becomes \(\frac{\sigma^2}{n\epsilon^2}\).

As \(n \to \infty\), this bound clearly goes to 0.

3. ML Connection: Unbiased Estimators

Optimization Estimation

Why More Data is Always Better

Most ML metrics (like Accuracy, MSE, or Cross-Entropy) are calculated as averages over the dataset.

The WLLN guarantees that the Training Loss we calculate on 1 million samples is a consistent estimator of the true population loss. This gives us the mathematical "permission" to optimize on a finite sample and expect the results to generalize.

Batch Size Intuition

In Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), using a larger batch size gives you a "weaker" fluctuation in the gradient estimate. This is the WLLN in action—larger batches drive the noise in the gradient down towards the "true" gradient direction.
