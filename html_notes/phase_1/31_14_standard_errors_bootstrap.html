14. Standard Errors via Bootstrap

Quantifying precision when formulas fail.

1. The Problem: Formula-less Statistics

For the sample mean, we have a formula for standard error: $SE = \sigma / \sqrt{n}$. But what if you want the standard error of the **Correlation Coefficient**, the **Ratio of Two Means**, or the **Eigenvalues of a Matrix**? Most statistics do not have a simple analytical formula for their variance.

2. The Bootstrap Solution

The Standard Error is simply the standard deviation of the sampling distribution. The Bootstrap allows us to *simulate* that sampling distribution.

$$ SE_{boot} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{B-1} \sum_{b=1}^{B} (\hat{\theta}^*_b - \bar{\theta}^*)^2} $$

Where $\hat{\theta}^*_b$ is the estimate from the $b$-th bootstrap sample, and $\bar{\theta}^*$ is the average of all $B$ bootstrap estimates.

3. AI/ML Connection: Model Uncertainty and Feature Selection

Is the Weight Significant?

In AI, Bootstrap SEs are used for **Feature Importance Ranking**.

When you train a Logistic Regression** or a **Linear Model, the library gives you "Coefficients." But are those coefficients stable? By bootstrapping the training data and re-calculating the coefficients, you get a Standard Error** for every weight in your neural network. If the weight is $w=5.0$ but the Bootstrap SE is $10.0$, it means that weight is pure noise—the model is just guessing. This allows AI engineers to Prune Features that have high variance, leading to models that generalize much better to new data. It transforms a "Black Box" model into an interpretable system where we know exactly which parts of the model are reliable and which are shaky.

4. Python: SE of Correlation

# Correlation between two variables
x = np.random.normal(0, 1, 50)
y = x + np.random.normal(0, 0.5, 50)

boot_corrs = []
for _ in range(2000):
    indices = np.random.choice(range(50), 50, replace=True)
    boot_corrs.append(np.corrcoef(x[indices], y[indices])[0,1])

se_corr = np.std(boot_corrs)
print(f"Sample Correlation: {np.corrcoef(x, y)[0,1]:.3f}")
print(f"Bootstrap Standard Error: {se_corr:.4f}")
