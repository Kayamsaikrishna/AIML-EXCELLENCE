Calculus 1.3 Min/Max

Critical Points & Extrema

To find the highest peak, first find where the ground is flat.

The Search for Zero

In optimization (and life), significant things happen when change stops. If a quantity is maximized or minimized, its rate of change must momentarily be zero.

1. Definition of Extrema

  • Absolute (Global) Maximum: The highest point on the entire domain. "The summit of Everest."
  • Relative (Local) Maximum: The highest point in a neighborhood. "The summit of a small hill."
  • Critical Point: Any \(c\) where \(f'(c) = 0\) OR \(f'(c)\) is undefined.

Fermat's Theorem: If \(f\) has a local extremum at \(c\), then \(c\) is a Critical Point.

2. AI/ML Connection: The Loss Landscape

Navigating the Terrain

Training a Neural Network is literally just finding the global minimum of a high-dimensional function called the Loss Surface using Gradient Descent.

The Challenge:
In 1D, finding \(f'(x)=0\) is easy. In 1,000,000 dimensions, there are billions of Critical Points.

  • Local Minima: Traps where the model thinks it's done but can do better.
  • Saddle Points: Points where it's flat, but slopes up in one direction and down in another. These are surprisingly common in Deep Learning and kill training speed.
  • Plateaus: Vast flat regions where gradients vanish.

3. Python: Symbolic Hunt

find_critical_points.py
import sympy as sp
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# A polynomial with peaks and valleys: f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3 + 10
f = x**4 - 4 * x**3 + 10

# 1. Calculate Derivative
df = sp.diff(f, x)
print(f"Function: {f}")
print(f"Derivative: {df}")

# 2. Solve df/dx = 0
critical_points = sp.solve(df, x)
print(f"Critical Points found at x = {critical_points}")

# 3. Evaluate f(x) at these points
for cp in critical_points:
    val = f.subs(x, cp)
    print(f"Value at x={cp}: {val}")

# Interpretation:
# x=0: f(0)=10. Is it a max, min, or inflection?
# x=3: f(3)= -17. 
# We need the 2nd Derivative Test to know for sure.
