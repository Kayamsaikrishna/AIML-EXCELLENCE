07. Continuity Definition

The smooth flow of mathematical functions.

1. What makes a function "Continuous"?

Intuitively, a function is continuous if you can draw its graph without lifting your pencil. Mathematically, for a function \(f\) to be continuous at a point \(a\), three conditions must be met:

  1. Value Exists: \(f(a)\) is defined.
  2. Limit Exists: \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\) exists.
  3. Link Established: \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)\).

2. Continuity on Intervals

A function is continuous on an interval if it is continuous at every point in that interval. Most elementary functions (sin, cos, exp, polynomials) are continuous everywhere in their domain.

3. AI/ML Connection: The Smooth Loss Landscape

Gradient Descent needs Continuity

For Gradient Descent to work, the Loss Landscape (the surface representing the error for all possible weights) should ideally be continuous. If there are "holes" or sudden jumps (discontinuities) in the error surface, the optimizer might "fall through" or get trapped in unreachable regions.

Ensuring that our network layers and activation functions are continuous (like Sigmoid, Tanh, or ReLU) is essential for a stable training process.
