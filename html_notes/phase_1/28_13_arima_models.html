Stats 1.8 Forecasting

ARIMA Models

Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average. The "All-in-One" solution for time-series forecasting.

The Modular Forecasting Machine

ARIMA is not one model; it is a blend of three separate mathematical components. By tuning its three parameters \((p, d, q)\), you can model almost any industrial time series.

1. The Three Components

AR (Autoregressive) - \(p\)

Uses the relationship between an observation and a number of lagged observations.
*Equation:* \( Y_t = \phi_1 Y_{t-1} + ... + \epsilon_t \)

I (Integrated) - \(d\)

The number of times the raw observations are differenced to make the series Stationary.
(Subtracting previous values from current values).

MA (Moving Average) - \(q\)

Uses the dependency between an observation and a residual error from a moving average model applied to lagged observations.

2. The Box-Jenkins Method

How to find the perfect \((p, d, q)\) for your data:

graph TD A[Plot Data] --> B{Stationary?} B -- No --> C[Difference Data (d=1 or 2)] C --> B B -- Yes --> D[Examine ACF and PACF] D --> E[Identify p and q] E --> F[Estimate Parameters] F --> G[Diagnostic Check (Residuals)] G --> H[Forecast]

3. ML Connection: Baseline Benchmarking

Deep Learning Monitoring

Why ARIMA is your "Standard"

In the world of time series, a simple ARIMA model is surprisingly hard to beat.

  • The Baseline Rule: Before you deploy a massive Transformer or an LSTM for forecasting, you MUST run an ARIMA model. If the Transformer only gives you 1% more accuracy, it's not worth the technical debt.
  • Small Data: ARIMA excels when you have limited data (e.g., only 50 historical points). Deep Learning models usually fail or overfit in these scenarios.
  • Explainability: If a manager asks "Why did the forecast drop?", you can point to the AR coefficient. You can't do that easily with a Black-Box model.
  • Hybrid Models: Some of the most accurate modern systems use ARIMA-LSTM hybrids. They use ARIMA to model the linear part of the signal and the LSTM to model the non-linear "residual" errors.
Engineer's Fact: ARIMA assumes the data is Linear. If your data has complex non-linear swings (like Crypto prices or viral social media trends), ARIMA will often produce a flat horizontal line for its forecast. That's your cue to switch to Deep Learning.
