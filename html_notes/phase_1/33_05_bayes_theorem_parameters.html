05. Bayes' Theorem for Parameters

The mathematical engine of learning.

1. The Formal Equation

Bayes' Theorem is the fundamental law for updating beliefs. For a parameter $\theta$ and data $D$:

$$ P(\theta | D) = \frac{P(D | \theta) P(\theta)}{P(D)} $$

Or in simple terms:

$$ \text{Posterior} = \frac{\text{Likelihood} \times \text{Prior}}{\text{Marginal Likelihood}} $$

2. The denominator: P(D)

The **Marginal Likelihood** (or Evidence) is the probability of the data averaged over all possible parameter values: $P(D) = \int P(D|\theta)P(\theta) d\theta$. This integral is often impossible to solve analytically, which is why we need **MCMC** or **Conjugate Priors**. However, for many purposes, we say:

$$ P(\theta | D) \propto P(D | \theta) P(\theta) $$
The posterior is **Proportional** to Likelihood times Prior.

3. AI/ML Connection: Incremental Learning and Stream Processing

Learning never stops

In AI, Bayes' Theorem enables Streaming Intelligence.

Unlike Frequentist models that need to be "Retrained from scratch" on the whole dataset, Bayesian models are **Incremental**. Today's **Posterior** becomes tomorrow's **Prior**. When new data arrives, we don't look at the old data—we only update our current belief. This is used in Recommendation Systems** (like TikTok or YouTube): as you watch a 5-second video, the model performs a Bayesian update on your "Interest" parameters. It's a continuous, real-time conversation between the AI and the world. This efficiency makes Bayesian methods ideal for Edge Computing** and IoT devices with limited memory that cannot store massive historical training sets.

4. Python: Proportional Update

# The Core Bayesian Logic Loop
prior = current_belief_dist()
likelihood = calculate_evidence(new_batch)

# The learning step
posterior = (likelihood * prior) / normalization_constant

# Repeat for the next batch!
save_as_prior(posterior)
