1. The Matrix P

If we have a new basis \( B = \{ \mathbf{b}_1, \dots, \mathbf{b}_n \} \), we construct the Change of Basis matrix \( P \) by putting these vectors as columns.

$$ P_B = \begin{bmatrix} | & & | \\ \mathbf{b}_1 & \dots & \mathbf{b}_n \\ | & & | \end{bmatrix} $$

This matrix \( P \) converts New Coordinates to Standard Coordinates: \( \mathbf{x}_{std} = P \mathbf{x}_{new} \).

To go the other way (Standard to New), we invert it: \( \mathbf{x}_{new} = P^{-1} \mathbf{x}_{std} \).

2. Rotating Data

In PCA, we change the basis from the "Pixel Axis" to the "Eigenface Axis".

This is simply a Change of Basis where the new basis vectors are the Eigenvectors of the Covariance Matrix. In this new basis, the data features become uncorrelated.

3. Switching Coordinates

Converting a point from Standard Frame to a Rotated Frame.

import numpy as np

# Standard Point (3, 4)
v_std = np.array([3, 4])

# New Basis Vectors (Rotated 45 degrees, scaled)
b1 = np.array([1, 1])
b2 = np.array([-1, 1])

# Change of Basis Matrix P
P = np.column_stack((b1, b2))

print("Change of Basis Matrix P:")
print(P)

# Convert Standard -> New (Using Inverse)
# P * v_new = v_std  =>  v_new = P_inv * v_std
# Or better: solve Px = b
v_new = np.linalg.solve(P, v_std)

print(f"\nCoordinates in New System: {v_new}")
# Check: v_new[0]*b1 + v_new[1]*b2 should equal v_std
reconstruction = v_new[0]*b1 + v_new[1]*b2
print(f"Reconstruction: {reconstruction}")
