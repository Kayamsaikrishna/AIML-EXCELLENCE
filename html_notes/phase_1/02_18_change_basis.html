18. Change of Basis

Looking at the same problem from a different angle.

1. Coordinate Transformations

A vector's components are relative to its Basis. If you change the basis from \( \mathcal{B} \) to \( \mathcal{B}' \), you need a Transition Matrix \( P \).

$$ [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}} = P [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}'} $$

2. Kernels & Linear Separation

The Kernel Trick

In Support Vector Machines (SVMs), we often have data that isn't linearly separable in its raw basis. We change the basis (often to an infinite-dimensional one) where the data becomes perfectly separable by a straight line or plane.

  • Coordinate Shifts: Useful in Image Processing to align images from different cameras.
  • Diagonalization: Changing basis to the eigenvectors of a matrix to simplify operations (Matrix Exponentiation).

3. Python: Change of Basis Computation

import numpy as np

# Standard Basis Vector
v_std = np.array([3, 4])

# New Basis B' = {[1, 1], [1, -1]}
B_prime = np.array([[1, 1], [1, -1]]).T

# Transition Matrix (Standard to B') = inverse(B')
P_inv = np.linalg.inv(B_prime)

# Coordinates in new basis
v_new = P_inv @ v_std

print(f"Standard coords: {v_std}")
print(f"New basis coords: {v_new}") # [3.5, -0.5]
