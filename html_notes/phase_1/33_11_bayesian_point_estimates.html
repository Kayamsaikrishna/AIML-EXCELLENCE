11. Bayesian Point Estimates

Reducing a distribution to a single number.

1. Why summarize?

While the full Posterior is the "Correct" Bayesian answer, business and software systems often require a single number (e.g., "What is the expected delivery time?"). Bayesian statistics offers several ways to pick this number, each minimizing a different type of "Loss."

2. The Three Musketeers

  • Posterior Mean: The "Balanced" choice. Minimizes the **Squared Error Loss** ($L_2$). It is the center of mass.
  • Posterior Median: The "Robust" choice. Minimizes the **Absolute Error Loss** ($L_1$). Less sensitive to outliers in the posterior.
  • Posterior Mode (MAP): The "Most Likely" choice. Minimizes 0-1 Loss. It is simply the peak of the distribution.

3. AI/ML Connection: Regularization and MAP

The Link to Tradition

In AI, the MAP (Maximum a Posteriori) estimate is the bridge to standard training.

When you train a Neural Network with Weight Decay (L2), you are technically finding the Posterior Mode (MAP)** of the weights under a Gaussian Prior. You aren't doing "Full Bayes"—you are just picking the highest point on the hill. However, in **Probabilistic Robotics**, we often use the **Posterior Mean** because it accounts for the "Tail Risks." If a path has a 90% chance of being safe but a 10% chance of total disaster (a heavy tail), the Mean will shift away from the danger, while the Mode might ignorantly steer right into it. Choosing the right Bayesian Point Estimate is essentially choosing the **Risk Profile** of your AI.
