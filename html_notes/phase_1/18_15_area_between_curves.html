15. Area Between Curves

Quantifying the "Gap" between two models or functions.

1. The Formula

To find the area between two continuous functions \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) on an interval \([a, b]\) where \(f(x) \geq g(x)\):

$$ A = \int_{a}^{b} [f(x) - g(x)] dx $$

Think of this as Area(Top Function) - Area(Bottom Function).

2. Finding the Bounds

Often, the interval \([a, b]\) isn't given. You must first find where the curves intersect by solving \(f(x) = g(x)\). These intersection points become your limits of integration.

3. AI/ML Connection: Performance Gaps & AUC

Benchmarking Models

When comparing two AI models (e.g., GPT-4 vs. Claude 3), we often plot their learning curves. The Area Between the Curves represents the "Total Performance Advantage" one model has over the other across all training steps.

In classification, the Area Under the Curve (AUC-ROC) is a measure of how well a model separates classes. If you have two different ROC curves, the area between them tells you exactly how much "Better" one classifier is at handling trade-offs between precision and recall than the other. It is the mathematical proof of model superiority.

4. Python: Calculating the Inter-Curve Area

# Area between y = x^2 and y = x
# 1. Intersection: x^2 = x -> x=0, x=1
# 2. Top is y=x (between 0 and 1)
# 3. Integral (x - x^2) from 0 to 1

from scipy.integrate import quad

def integrand(x):
    return x - x**2 # Top - Bottom

area, error = quad(integrand, 0, 1)

print(f"Area Between Curves: {area:.4f}") 
# Result: 1/6 approx 0.1667
