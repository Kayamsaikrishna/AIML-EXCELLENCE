21. Distance Metrics

Quantifying "unlikeness" in data.

1. Relationship to Norms

A distance metric \(d(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})\) is induced by a norm. It measures the "gap" between two points in space.

$$ d(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\| $$
  • Euclidean Distance: Induced by the L2 norm. Best for physical space.
  • Manhattan Distance: Induced by the L1 norm. Best for grid-based data.
  • Minkowski Distance: The generalized form for any p-norm.

2. Similarity Search & KNN

K-Nearest Neighbors

In KNN and Clustering, the choice of distance metric is the most important hyperparameter. If features have different scales, Euclidean distance will be biased towards the feature with the largest range.

  • Search Engines: Finding similar images involves Calculating distance between feature vectors.
  • Anomaly Detection: Data points that have a very high distance from all others are flagged as outliers.

3. Python: Vectorized Distance

import numpy as np
from scipy.spatial import distance

u = np.array([1, 2, 3])
v = np.array([4, 5, 6])

# Euclidean
d_euc = np.linalg.norm(u - v)

# Manhattan
d_man = np.linalg.norm(u - v, ord=1)

# Cosine Distance (1 - Cosine Similarity)
d_cos = distance.cosine(u, v)

print(f"Euclidean: {d_euc:.2f}, Manhattan: {d_man}, Cosine: {d_cos:.4f}")
