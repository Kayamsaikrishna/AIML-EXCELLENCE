14. Weibull Distribution

The math of aging, reliability, and failure.

1. Definition

The Weibull Distribution is a versatile distribution used to model extreme values and the time to failure of components. Its shape parameter $k$ allows it to model processes with increasing, constant, or decreasing failure rates.

$$ f(x) = \frac{k}{\lambda} \left( \frac{x}{\lambda} \right)^{k-1} e^{-(x/\lambda)^k} \quad \text{for } x \ge 0 $$

2. The Three Phases of Failure

Depending on $k$, the Weibull distribution models different life stages:

  • **$k < 1$ (Infant Mortality):** Failure rate decreases over time (e.g., defective software discovered early).
  • **$k = 1$ (Random Failure):** Failure rate is constant (Exponential Distribution).
  • **$k > 1$ (Wear-out):** Failure rate increases over time (e.g., mechanical friction or aging).

3. AI/ML Connection: Predictive Maintenance

Knowing when something will break

The Weibull distribution is the foundation of Reliability AI.

In **Predictive Maintenance**, an AI model isn't just predicting "if" a factory robot will fail, but "When." By fitting sensor data to a Weibull distribution, the AI can calculate the **Remaining Useful Life (RUL)** of a component. In **Customer Analytics**, it models "Lifespan"—predicting exactly when a user's subscription will expire based on their usage patterns. Furthermore, in **Gradient Boosting** (like XGBoost), Weibull loss functions are used for "AFT" (Accelerated Failure Time) regression, allowing the AI to handle "Censored Data" (records where we know the subject hasn't failed yet, but we don't know when they will).

4. Python: Weibull Sampling

from scipy.stats import weibull_min

# k (shape) = 1.5, lambda (scale) = 1000 hours
k, lam = 1.5, 1000
dist = weibull_min(k, scale=lam)

# Probability of failure before 500 hours
print(f"P(X < 500): {dist.cdf(500):.4f}")

# Median life span
print(f"Median RUL: {dist.median():.2f} hours")
