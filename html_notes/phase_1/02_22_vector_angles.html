1. The Most Important Inequality

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality states that the magnitude of the inner product is bounded by the product of the norms:

$$ |\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle| \le ||\mathbf{u}|| \cdot ||\mathbf{v}|| $$

This implies that the ratio \( \frac{\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle}{||\mathbf{u}|| ||\mathbf{v}||} \) is always between -1 and 1, which allows us to define it as \( \cos \theta \).

2. Correlation is Cosine

Pearson's Correlation Coefficient (\( r \)) is simply the Cosine of the Angle between two Centered vectors (mean subtracted).

  • \( r = 1 \): Vectors point same way (Angle 0°).
  • \( r = 0 \): Vectors are Orthogonal (Angle 90°). Uncorrelated.
  • \( r = -1 \): Vectors are Opposite (Angle 180°). Negatively correlated.

3. Financial Correlation

Analyzing stock price movements as vector angles.

import numpy as np

# Daily returns of two stocks
stock_A = np.array([0.1, 0.2, -0.1, 0.1, 0.3])
stock_B = np.array([0.05, 0.1, -0.05, 0.05, 0.15]) # Correlated
stock_C = np.array([-0.1, -0.2, 0.1, -0.1, -0.3]) # Inverse

# 1. Center the data (Subtract mean)
A_centered = stock_A - np.mean(stock_A)
B_centered = stock_B - np.mean(stock_B)

# 2. Compute Cosine Similarity
def cosine_sim(u, v):
    return np.dot(u, v) / (np.linalg.norm(u) * np.linalg.norm(v))

corr_AB = cosine_sim(A_centered, B_centered)
print(f"Correlation A-B: {corr_AB:.2f}") 
# ~1.0 (Strong Positive)

# Verify with NumPy corrcoef
np_corr = np.corrcoef(stock_A, stock_B)[0, 1]
print(f"NumPy Corr A-B:  {np_corr:.2f}")
