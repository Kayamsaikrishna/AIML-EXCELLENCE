Calculus 1.4 Limits

Improper Integrals

Measuring the infinite.

Infinity as a Limit

You can't plug in infinity. An improper integral is simply a definite integral wrapped inside a limit. \(\int_1^\infty = \lim_{t \to \infty} \int_1^t\).

1. Types of Improperness

  • Type 1: Infinite Interval
    Example: \(\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2} dx\).
    Area extends forever to the right. Does it pinch off fast enough to be finite?
  • Type 2: Infinite Discontinuity
    Example: \(\int_0^1 \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} dx\).
    Function shoots to infinity at \(x=0\). Does it hug the y-axis tight enough?

Convergent: Limit exists (finite area).
Divergent: Limit is infinite (infinite area).

2. AI/ML Connection: Probability Normalization

The Gaussian Integral

The most famous improper integral in AI is the area under the Bell Curve (Gaussian). \[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2} dx = \sqrt{\pi} \]

Since this integral converges to a finite number, we can divide by that number to make the total area equal to 1. This creates a valid Probability Distribution.

If this integral diverged, the Normal Distribution wouldn't exist, and 99% of Machine Learning math (CLT, Diffusion Models, VAEs) would collapse.

3. Python: Testing Convergence

check_convergence.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')

# 1. Convergent: 1/x^2 (decays fast)
f1 = 1 / x**2
# oo is the SymPy symbol for infinity
res1 = sp.integrate(f1, (x, 1, sp.oo))
print(f"Area of 1/x^2 (1 to Inf): {res1}")
# Output: 1

# 2. Divergent: 1/x (decays slow - Harmonic Series analog)
f2 = 1 / x
res2 = sp.integrate(f2, (x, 1, sp.oo))
print(f"Area of 1/x (1 to Inf): {res2}")
# Output: oo

# 3. Gaussian
f3 = sp.exp(-x**2)
res3 = sp.integrate(f3, (x, -sp.oo, sp.oo))
print(f"Gaussian Area: {res3}")
# Output: sqrt(pi)
