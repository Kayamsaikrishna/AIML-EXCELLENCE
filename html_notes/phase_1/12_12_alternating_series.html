Calculus 1.5 Oscillations

Alternating Series & Leibniz Test

Convergence through cancellation.

The Tug of War

An alternating series flips signs every time: positive, negative, positive... \[ \sum (-1)^{n+1} a_n = a_1 - a_2 + a_3 - a_4 \dots \]

Leibniz Test (AST): The series converges simply if:

  1. The terms are decreasing: \(a_{n+1} \leq a_n\).
  2. The limit is zero: \(\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0\).

This is easier to satisfy than positive convergence because the terms "cancel" each other out.

2. AI/ML Connection: Momentum & Oscillations

Damping the Swing

In Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), the optimizer often overshoots the minimum. We step too far left, then correct and step right, creating an alternating path.

If the Learning Rate is too high, the oscillations grow (Divergence). If we use a **Learning Rate Scheduler** (decaying the learning rate), we satisfy the Leibniz condition: the steps get smaller and approach zero. This ensures that the "Series of Updates" converges to a specific set of weights, stabilizing the model.

3. Python: Alternating Harmonic Sum

alternating.py
import numpy as np

# Alternating Harmonic Series: 1 - 1/2 + 1/3 - 1/4 ...
# Unlike the regular Harmonic Series (which diverges), this converges to ln(2).

n_terms = 1000
alt_sum = 0
for n in range(1, n_terms + 1):
    term = 1/n
    if n % 2 == 0:
        alt_sum -= term
    else:
        alt_sum += term

print(f"Sum (n={n_terms}): {alt_sum:.6f}")
print(f"Target (ln 2): {np.log(2):.6f}")
# Convergence is slow but guaranteed.
