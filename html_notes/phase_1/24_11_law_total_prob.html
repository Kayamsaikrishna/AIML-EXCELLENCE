11. Law of Total Probability

Looking at all possible paths to an outcome.

1. Partitioning the Space

The Law of Total Probability allows us to find the probability of an event \(B\) by summing its probability across a set of mutually exclusive, exhaustive "Partitions" of the sample space (\(A_1, A_2, \dots\)).

2. The Formula

$$ P(B) = \sum_{i} P(B | A_i) \cdot P(A_i) $$

Imagine you have three machines (A, B, C) producing parts. The Law of Total Probability lets you calculate the total probability of a "Defective Part" by combining the defect rate of each machine weighted by its production volume.

3. AI/ML Connection: Mixture Models

Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM)

In Unsupervised Learning, we often assume that data comes from multiple different "Sub-Populations". For example, a dataset of customer heights might be a mixture of two groups: Men and Women.

A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) uses the Law of Total Probability to calculate the likelihood of a data point:

$$ P(x) = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \cdot \mathcal{N}(x; \mu_k, \sigma_k) $$

Where $\pi_k$ is the prior probability of being in group $k$, and the Normal distribution is the conditional probability of the value given that group. This law is what allows AI to model Multimodal Data (data with multiple "humps" or clusters). Without it, models would try to find a single average that represents nobody well; with it, AI can understand complex, diverse populations.

4. Python: Weighted Probability Sum

# 3 Clusters: A (30%), B (50%), C (20%)
# Conditional probability of event 'X' in each cluster
p_clusters = [0.3, 0.5, 0.2]
p_x_given_cluster = [0.1, 0.4, 0.8]

# Law of Total Probability
p_x_total = sum(p * q for p, q in zip(p_clusters, p_x_given_cluster))

print(f"Total Probability of X: {p_x_total:.2f}")
