Number Systems

A rigorous, foundational exploration of mathematical sets.
From the counting numbers of antiquity to the hypercomplex dimensions of modern robotics.

1. The Hierarchy of Numbers

Mathematics describes the universe through language, and "Number Systems" are the alphabet of this language. In Artificial Intelligence, the choice of number system is never arbitrary—it is a distinct engineering decision involving trade-offs between Precision (Granularity), Range (Magnitude), and Cost (Compute/Memory).

We visualize these systems as nested sets. Every Natural number is an Integer; every Integer is a Rational number; and so forth. Understanding this hierarchy allows us to "cast" data safely upwards without data loss, while warning us of the dangers of casting downwards.

$$ \mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C} $$

2. Natural Numbers (\(\mathbb{N}\))

The set of Natural numbers is the most primitive mathematical object, arising from the need to count discrete items. In rigorous set theory (Peano axioms), they are constructed starting from 0 (or 1) and defining a "Successor" function \(S(n) = n + 1\).

Properties

  • Countably Infinite: You can list them one by one forever.
  • Closed under Addition & Multiplication: Adding two natural numbers always yields a natural number.
  • Not Closed under Subtraction: \(5 - 7\) is not Natural. This limitation led to Integers.
Dimensionality & Tensor Shapes

Natural numbers are the language of Structure in Deep Learning.

  • Tensor Rank: A scalar has rank 0. A vector has rank 1. A matrix has rank 2. These ranks are always \( \in \mathbb{N}_0 \).
  • Shape Definition: When we define a layer `nn.Linear(128, 64)`, the input/output features must be natural numbers. You cannot have "3.5 neurons" or "negative 2 layers".
  • Epochs & Steps: The concept of discrete time steps in RNNs or Training Epochs relies entirely on \(\mathbb{N}\).

The Dictionary of AI: Tokenization

Large Language Models (LLMs) do not read text; they read Natural Numbers.

The entire vocabulary of GPT-4 is mapped to a set of integers \(\{0, 1, ..., 100255\}\).
The word "Apple" might be ID 452. The word " Apple" (with space) might be 1209.

Why Natural? Because these are indices in a Lookup Table (Embedding Matrix). You cannot look up the "2.5-th" row of a matrix.

3. Integers (\(\mathbb{Z}\))

Originating from the need to denote debt or deficit, Integers introduce the concept of "Direction" on the number line. Specifically, for every natural number \(n\), there exists an additive inverse \(-n\) such that \(n + (-n) = 0\).

Deep Theoretical Context

Integers form a Ring structure in abstract algebra. They allow for commutative addition and multiplication but lack multiplicative inverses (division usually fails to stay in the set).

Discrete State Spaces

In AI, Integers govern the world of Decisions and Categorization.

1. Classification Labels: In a Softmax classifier, the target \(y\) is an integer index. Even if the probability output is continuous (\(\mathbb{R}\)), the "Truth" is discrete (Class 5 or Class 6).

2. Reinforcement Learning (Grid Worlds): An agent's position in a grid world is defined by coordinates \((x, y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2\). You cannot be at \(x=2.5\).

3. VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized VAE): This advanced generative model maps continuous embeddings into a discrete codebook of integers, compressing data massively.

The God Particle of Chaos: Random Seed

How do we reproduce scientific results if AI is probabilistic?

We use a Random Seed—a single Integer (e.g., `42`). Functional mapping: \[ f(\text{Seed} \in \mathbb{Z}) \rightarrow \text{Deterministic Sequence of random Reals} \] In distributed training, each GPU gets a unique integer rank (0, 1, 2...) to ensure they work on different data slices.

4. Rational Numbers (\(\mathbb{Q}\))

Rational numbers represent Ratios. They are the first field in our hierarchy, meaning we can add, subtract, multiply, and divide (except by zero). Any repeating decimal (like 0.333...) is Rational.

Why Aren't Rationals Enough?

The ancient Pythagoreans believed everything was a rational ratio. They were terrified to discover that the hypotenuse of a unit triangle (\(\sqrt{2}\)) could not be expressed as \(p/q\). This proved that the number line had "holes" in it—infinitely many of them.

Quantization & Model Compression

Rational numbers are making a huge comeback in Edge AI.

To run a massive model (like LLaMA-7B) on a phone, we cannot store weights as high-precision Reals. We Quantize them. We might map the continuous range of weights to discrete rational steps (e.g., \(k/256\) for 8-bit quantization).

This effectively projects the model from \(\mathbb{R}\) down to \(\mathbb{Q}\), sacrificing a tiny bit of accuracy for massive speed and memory gains.

5. Irrational Numbers (\(\mathbb{P}\))

Real numbers that cannot be written as a simple fraction. Their decimal expansion is non-terminating and non-repeating. \(\sqrt{2}\), \(\pi\), and \(e\) are famous examples.

The Constants of AI

Specific irrational constants define entire architectures:

  • \(e\) (Euler's Number): The base of the Natural Logarithm. It appears in Softmax, Sigmoid, and Tanh activation functions. Without \(e\), we wouldn't have smooth probability distributions.
  • \(\pi\) (Pi): Essential for the Normal Distribution (Gaussian). It appears in the PDF used for initializing weights and in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs).

6. Real Numbers (\(\mathbb{R}\))

Real numbers fill the gaps. They include transcendentals like \(\pi\) and \(e\). Mathematically, \(\mathbb{R}\) is a "Complete Ordered Field".

The Completeness Axiom: This is the key property. It ensures that if you zoom in infinitely on the number line, you never hit a gap. This "smoothness" is non-negotiable for Calculus.

The Engine of Gradient Descent

Why do we use Float32 (Reals) for almost all training?

  • Differentiability: For Backpropagation to work, our loss function surface must be smooth (continuous and differentiable). You cannot compute a gradient (slope) on a staircase (Integers). You need a smooth hill (Reals).
  • Weights: A neuron's weight might need to be \(0.0000341\). Discrete steps are too coarse to capture this nuance.

The Manifold Hypothesis

Real numbers in high dimensions (e.g., \(\mathbb{R}^{768}\)) behave strangely.

The Manifold Hypothesis states that real-world data (like images of faces) lies on a lower-dimensional continuous surface (manifold) embedded within the high-dimensional real space.

Generative AI (like Stable Diffusion) works by learning to walk along this continuous manifold of Real numbers from noise to image.

7. Complex Numbers (\(\mathbb{C}\))

Complex numbers solve equations that Reals cannot (like \(x^2 + 1 = 0\)). They introduce a secondary axis (Imaginary), turning the number line into a number plane.

Form: \(z = a + bi\) or Polar Form \(z = re^{i\theta}\).

Fourier Transforms & Signal AI

Complex numbers are natively suited for Rotation and Oscillation.

In Speech Recognition, raw audio waves are fed into an FFT (Fast Fourier Transform). The FFT decomposes the sound into frequencies using complex arithmetic. The magnitude \(|z|\) gives the volume, and the phase \(\theta\) gives timing information. Without \(\mathbb{C}\), Siri and Alexa would not exist.

Modern SOTA: RoPE (Rotary Positional Embeddings)

Why is LLaMA better than older Transformers? Partly due to how it handles position.

Instead of adding a number to a vector, RoPE rotates the token embedding across the 2D plane using Complex multiplication: \[ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} e^{i m \theta} \] where \(m\) is the position index. This allows the model to understand "relative distance" between words naturally, through the angle of rotation in \(\mathbb{C}\).

8. Computer Number Systems: Binary & Hexadecimal

Humans use Decimal (Base-10) because we have 10 fingers. Computers use Binary (Base-2) because they have switches (On/Off). AI engineers often dwell in Hexadecimal (Base-16).

The Basics

  • Binary (\(\mathbb{Z}_2\)): Digits \( \{0, 1\} \). The fundamental language of logic gates.
  • Hexadecimal (\(\mathbb{Z}_{16}\)): Digits \( \{0...9, A...F\} \). A compact way to read binary (1 hex digits = 4 bits).
$$ 1011_2 = 11_{10} = B_{16} $$
Masking & Memory

LoRA (Low Rank Adaptation): We define masks using Hex (e.g., `0xFF`) to freeze/unfreeze weights.

Memory Dumps: Debugging C++ TensorFlow ops often requires reading Hex addresses.

9. The Hardware Reality: IEEE 754 & Floating Point

Here is the dark secret: Computers cannot actually store Real numbers.

\(\mathbb{R}\) has infinite precision. Computer RAM is finite. Therefore, computers use a scientific notation approximation called Floating Point.

Type Bits Precision Use Case in AI
Float32 (Single) 32 ~7 decimal digits Standard training (TensorFlow/PyTorch default).
Float16 (Half) 16 ~3 decimal digits Inference acceleration (NVIDIA Tensor Cores).
BFloat16 (Brain Float) 16 ~3 digits, wider range Training Large Language Models (prevents overflow).
Float64 (Double) 64 ~15 decimal digits Scientific computing (Simulations), rarely Deep Learning.

Numerical Instability: The Silent Bug

Because Floats are just approximations, they have gaps. \( (a + b) + c \) is not always equal to \( a + (b + c) \) in computer float math!

Catastrophic Cancellation: Subtracting two nearly identical numbers (e.g., \(1.000002 - 1.000001\)) can result in mostly garbage noise digits. This destroys gradients in deep networks, leading to NaNs (Not a Number) during training.

10. Entering the 4th Dimension: Quaternions (\(\mathbb{H}\))

Mathematics doesn't stop at Complex numbers. In 1843, William Rowan Hamilton carved the formula for Quaternions into a bridge in Dublin. They extend the number system to 4 dimensions: \( q = w + xi + yj + zk \).

Robotics & 3D Orientation

Why do we need 4D numbers for a 3D world?

The Problem (Gimbal Lock): Using standard 3D angles (Pitch, Yaw, Roll) to rotate a robot arm or drone camera has a fatal flaw. If two axes align, you lose a degree of freedom, and the system "locks".

The Solution: Quaternions provide smooth, interpolation-friendly (SLERP) rotation without Gimbal lock. They are the standard for SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) in autonomous vehicles.

11. The Story of Evolution: Why Do We Need Them All?

To truly grasp when to opt for a specific number system, we must understand the historical "bugs" they were patched to fix.

Act 1: The Bug of Debt

Early humans could count (\(\mathbb{N}\)), but couldn't do commerce. "I have 2 goats and owe you 5."
Patch: Integers (\(\mathbb{Z}\)) enabled direction (Debt/Credit).

Act 2: The Bug of Sharing

You can't split 3 tasks between 4 threads evenly with Integers.
Patch: Rationals (\(\mathbb{Q}\)) enabled fine-grained division.

Act 3: The Bug of Geometry

The world is continuous. A robot moving from point A to B passes through infinite points. Rationals have gaps.
Patch: Reals (\(\mathbb{R}\)) filled the gaps, allowing Calculus (and Gradient Descent) to work.

12. Engineering Decision Framework

When designing an AI system, you are the architect. Choosing the wrong "brick" (number system) causes the building to collapse.

Scenario Recommended Type Rational Architecture
Counting People/Objects Integer (int64) Discrete truth. You cannot have 5.0001 people.
Training LLMs BFloat16 Trade-off: Sacrifice precision for massive Range (prevent overflow).
Financial Forecasting Decimal (Fixed) Floating point errors ("penny drifting") are illegal in banking.
Audio Signal Processing Complex (complex64) Necessary to capture Phase and Amplitude simultaneously.

13. Practical Lab: The "Silent Killer"

In this lab, we witness Integer Overflow, a bug that has crashed rockets and self-driving cars.

🐍 Python Simulation

import numpy as np

# A pixel in an image is usually 8-bit (0 to 255)
pixel_value = np.array([250], dtype=np.uint8)

print(f"Original Pixel: {pixel_value[0]}")  # 250 (Bright White)

# We try to brighten the image by adding 10
new_pixel = pixel_value + 10 

print(f"Brightened Pixel: {new_pixel[0]}") 
# RESULT: 4 (Pitch Black) -> CATASTROPHIC FAILURE

The Lesson: The math world (\(\mathbb{N})\) is infinite. The computer world (uint8) wraps around like a clock. 250 + 10 = 260. 260 mod 256 = 4.

Analysis: Always cast your data to a higher set (e.g., float32) before performing arithmetic operations.

🧪 Lab 2: The Parallel Summation Bug (Order Matters!)

Mathematicians say \( (A+B)+C = A+(B+C) \).
Computers say False.

small_num = 1e-18  # Extremely small number
large_num = 1.0    # 1.0

# Case 1: Add small numbers first, then the large one
sum_1 = (small_num + small_num) + large_num

# Case 2: Add small number to large, then small again (Swallowing)
sum_2 = (large_num + small_num) + small_num 

print(f"Sum 1 == Sum 2? {sum_1 == sum_2}") 
# RESULT: False! 
# In Case 2, 1.0 + 1e-18 is just 1.0 (Precision lost instantly).

Impact: In distributed training (AllReduce), summing gradients from 1000 GPUs in the wrong order can result in significant accuracy loss. We use Kahan Summation or higher precision accumulators (Float32 master weights) to fix this.
