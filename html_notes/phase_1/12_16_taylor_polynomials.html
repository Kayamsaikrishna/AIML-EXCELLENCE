Calculus 1.5 Approximation

Taylor Polynomials

Reconstructing the whole from a single point.

The DNA of Functions

A Taylor Series allows us to reconstruct a complex function \(f(x)\) simply by knowing its derivatives at a single point \(a\). It is the sum of infinite derivatives:

\[ f(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!} (x - a)^n \]

The first few terms are the best "Linear", "Quadratic", and "Cubic" approximations of the function near \(a\).

2. AI/ML Connection: Gradient Descent Logic

Linearizing the Loss Landscape

Why does Gradient Descent work? It relies on the First-Order Taylor Approximation: \[ f(x + \Delta x) \approx f(x) + \nabla f(x) \cdot \Delta x \]

This equation says: "Locally, every function looks like a flat plane (slope)." By moving in the direction of the negative gradient, we slide down this local plane.

Advanced optimizers like Newton's Method use the Second-Order Taylor Approximation (Hessian Matrix), which accounts for curvature (\(x^2\)). \[ f(x + \Delta x) \approx f(x) + \nabla f(x)\Delta x + \frac{1}{2} \Delta x^T H \Delta x \]

3. Python: Symbolic Expansion

taylor_expand.py
import sympy as sp

# Expand e^x around a=0
x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = sp.exp(x)

# Get the series up to x^5
series_expansion = sp.series(f, x, 0, 6)

print(f"Taylor Series of e^x:")
print(series_expansion)
# Output: 1 + x + x**2/2 + x**3/6 + x**4/24 + x**5/120 + O(x**6)

# This confirms that e^x is just a polynomial if you have infinite terms!
