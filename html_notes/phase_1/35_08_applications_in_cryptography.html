Module 42

Applications in Cryptography

Detailed exploration of Applications in Cryptography in the context of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning.

Concept Overview

Number theory forms the backbone of modern cryptography. Algorithms like RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman) and ECC (Elliptic Curve Cryptography) rely on the computational hardness of mathematical problems involving integers, such as integer factorization and the discrete logarithm problem.

Key Cryptographic Primitives

  • RSA Encryption: Relies on the difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers.
    $$ n = p \times q $$ $$ \phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1) $$ $$ d \cdot e \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)} $$
    Where \( (n, e) \) is the public key and \( d \) is the private key.
  • Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange: Enables secure exchange of keys over a public channel using modular exponentiation.
  • Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC): Offers equivalent security to RSA with smaller key sizes, critical for mobile and IoT devices.
AI Insight: Privacy-Preserving Machine Learning

In the era of data privacy (GDPR, CCPA), number theoretic cryptography enables Privacy-Preserving AI:

  • Homomorphic Encryption (HE): Allows computations to be performed on encrypted data without decrypting it first. This means an AI model can make predictions on encrypted patient data without ever "seeing" the raw medical records.
  • Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMPC): Enables multiple parties to jointly train a model (Federated Learning) without sharing their local datasets, relying on cryptographic protocols to aggregate updates securely.
