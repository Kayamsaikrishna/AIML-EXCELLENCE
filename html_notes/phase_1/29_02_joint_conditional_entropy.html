02. Joint & Conditional Entropy

How much uncertainty is left when we know something?

1. The Coupled Uncertainty

Joint Entropy $H(X, Y)$ measures the total uncertainty in a system with two variables. Conditional Entropy $H(Y|X)$ measures how much uncertainty remains in $Y$ given that we already know $X$.

$$ H(Y|X) = H(X, Y) - H(X) $$

2. Information never hurts

One of the most important theorems in information theory is $H(Y|X) \le H(Y)$. This means that knowing more information *never* increases uncertainty—it either stays the same or goes down. This is why adding features to an AI model (usually) helps.

3. AI/ML Connection: Sequence Learning and Markov Chains

Reducing Choice via Context

In AI, Conditional Entropy is the Perplexity of a model.

When an LLM (GPT-4) predicts the next word, it is minimizing $H(\text{Next Word} | \text{Context})$. If the context is "The capital of France is...", the conditional entropy of the next word is nearly zero (it's "Paris"). If the context is "He went to the...", the entropy is much higher. In Autoencoders**, we minimize the conditional entropy of the input given the bottleneck code. By mastering this relationship, AI engineers can measure Data Quality: if $H(Y|X)$ is high, it means the features $X$ do not have enough information to predict $Y$, and no amount of "Deep Learning magic" will fix the problem. You need better data, not more layers.
