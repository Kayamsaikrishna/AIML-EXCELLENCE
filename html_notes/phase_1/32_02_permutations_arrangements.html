Combinatorics Ordering

Permutations & Ordered Arrangements

Order Matters. How many ways can we arrange a subset of items where the sequence of participation completely changes the result?

The Power of Factorials

A Permutation is an ordered arrangement of objects. If you have \(n\) distinct items and you want to arrange all of them, the number of ways is \(n! = n \times (n-1) \times ... \times 1\). This number grows faster than almost any other function in computing.

1. Permutations of \(n\) objects taken \(r\) at a time

When we only choose a part of the set to arrange.

$$ P(n, r) = {}^n P_r = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!} $$

Example: Out of 10 athletes, how many ways can we award Gold, Silver, and Bronze medals?

Order matters! This is \(P(10, 3) = \frac{10!}{7!} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 = 720\).

2. Permutations with Repetition

What if some items are the same? (e.g., the letters in "GOOGLE").

$$ N = \frac{n!}{n_1! n_2! ... n_k!} $$

Where \(n_1, n_2...\) are the frequencies of identical items.

Example: "APPLE" has 5 letters, but 'P' repeats twice.

Total arrangements = \(5! / 2! = 120 / 2 = 60\).

Deep Learning NLP

Why Permutations matter in AI

  • Sequential Modeling: In NLP, a "Sentence" is a unique permutation of tokens. Changing the order (permutation) changes the meaning (e.g., "The cat ate the rat" vs. "The rat ate the cat"). Transformers use Positional Encodings specifically to capture this order.
  • Ranking & Sorting: LTR (Learning to Rank) models, used in Search Engines, predict the optimal permutation of documents for a given query. Evaluating them requires metrics like NDCG which care about the exact order.
  • Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP): In combinatorial optimization, finding the shortest route through \(n\) cities is about finding the "best" permutation of those cities. With \(n=20\), you have \(20! \approx 2.4 \times 10^{18}\) paths to check.
  • Ensemble Diversity: Sometimes we train models on different permutations of the data to ensure they haven't just memorized the sequence.
