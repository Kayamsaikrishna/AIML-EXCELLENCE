13. Renyi Entropy

Generalizing uncertainty for sensitive systems.

1. The Alpha Parameter

Renyi Entropy ($H_\alpha$)** generalizes Shannon entropy by adding a parameter $\alpha$. As $\alpha \to 1$, Renyi entropy becomes Shannon entropy.

$$ H_\alpha(X) = \frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \log \left( \sum_{i=1}^n p_i^\alpha \right) $$
  • $\alpha = 0$: Hartley entropy (log of the number of possible outcomes).
  • $\alpha = 2$: Collision entropy (likelyhood of two random samples being the same).
  • $\alpha = \infty$: Min-entropy (focuses only on the most probable outcome).

2. Why generalize?

Shannon entropy measures the *average* surprise. But in security or privacy, we often care about the *worst-case* surprise ($\alpha = \infty$). Renyi entropy allows us to tune our "Surprise threshold."

3. AI/ML Connection: Differential Privacy and Diversity

Measuring Information Leakage

In AI, Renyi Differential Privacy (RDP)** is the state-of-the-art for training private models.

When training an AI on sensitive medical data, we use Renyi divergence to bound how much a single patient's data can influence the weights. Higher $\alpha$ values provide tighter bounds on the "Privacy Budget." In Reinforcement Learning**, Renyi entropy is used to measure **Strategy Diversity**: we want the AI to not just find one way to win, but to maintain a distribution of strategies with specific entropy properties. It allows for a more "Aggressive" exploration than standard Shannon regularizers. It is the math of **Robustness in Unpredictable Environments**.

4. Mathematical Fact

$H_\alpha$ is a non-increasing function of $\alpha$. Min-entropy ($H_\infty$) is always less than or equal to Shannon entropy. This proves that the "average" case is always more optimistic than the "worst" case.
