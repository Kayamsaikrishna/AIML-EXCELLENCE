Calculus 1.3 Inversion

Inverse Trig Derivatives

From angles to ratios, and back again. The derivatives turn trigonometry into algebra.

The Algebraic Surprise

The derivative of \(\arcsin(x)\) has no trig functions in it. It's purely algebraic: \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\). This happens because of the Pythagorean identity \(sin^2 + cos^2 = 1\).

1. The Big Three

Arcsine

\( \frac{d}{dx}[\arcsin x] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \)

Defined only for \(x \in (-1, 1)\)

Arccosine

\( \frac{d}{dx}[\arccos x] = \frac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \)

Negative of Arcsine

Arctangent

\( \frac{d}{dx}[\arctan x] = \frac{1}{1+x^2} \)

Defined for ALL x. Super smooth.

Arc-Co-Functions

Just add a negative sign to their partners.

2. AI/ML Connection: Regression of Angles

Why we love Arctan

In Computer Vision (e.g., predicting the rotation of a car in an image), we need to predict an angle.

  • Arcsine/Arccosine: Dangerous. If the model outputs \(1.0001\) (just slightly out of bounds), the derivative becomes imaginary or undefined (division by zero at edges). This causes "NaN" (Not a Number) errors to kill your training run.
  • Arctangent: Safe. It accepts any real number input \((-\infty, \infty)\) and maps it to \((-\pi/2, \pi/2)\). Its derivative \(\frac{1}{1+x^2}\) is a beautiful "bell curve" (Cauchy distribution) that is always finite and positive.

Therefore, we generally use \(\arctan(y/x)\) (or the `atan2` variant) when building loss functions for angles.

3. Python: The Safe Derivative

arctan_stability.py
import numpy as np

def arcsin_deriv(x):
    # DANGEROUS: Division by zero at x=1 or x=-1
    if abs(x) >= 1:
        return float('nan')
    return 1 / np.sqrt(1 - x**2)

def arctan_deriv(x):
    # SAFE: Defined everywhere
    return 1 / (1 + x**2)

# Test at the "Edge of Sanity"
x_edge = 0.9999
print(f"Arcsin Deriv at {x_edge}: {arcsin_deriv(x_edge):.4f} (Huge!)")
print(f"Arctan Deriv at {x_edge}: {arctan_deriv(x_edge):.4f} (Reasonable)")

x_out = 5.0
print(f"Arcsin Deriv at {x_out}: {arcsin_deriv(x_out)}")  # NaN
print(f"Arctan Deriv at {x_out}: {arctan_deriv(x_out):.4f}") # 0.0385 (Stable)

print("\nConclusion: Use Arctan for robust angle regression.")
