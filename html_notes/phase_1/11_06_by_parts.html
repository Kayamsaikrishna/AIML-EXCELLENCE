Calculus 1.4 Techniques

Integration by Parts

Trading a hard integral for an easier one.

Reverse Product Rule

We know \( (uv)' = u v' + v u' \). Integrating both sides gives: \( uv = \int u dv + \int v du \). Rearranging gives the formula for "Parts".

1. The Formula

$$ \int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du $$

Selection Strategy (LIATE): Use this order to pick \(u\):

  1. Logs (\(\ln x\))
  2. Inverse Trig (\(\arctan x\))
  3. Algebraic (\(x^2, 3x\))
  4. Trig (\(\sin x\))
  5. Exponentials (\(e^x\))

2. AI/ML Connection: Entropy & Variational Inference

Expectation of Log-Probabilities

In Machine Learning, we constantly evaluate Entropy \(H(x) = - \int p(x) \ln p(x) dx\) and KL Divergence.

Often, these integrals involve terms like \( \int x^2 e^{-(x-\mu)^2} dx \). To derive the closed-form solutions (which allows us to train things like VAEs efficiently), we perform Integration by Parts repeatedly (often called "computing moments of the distribution").

Without "Parts", Bayesian Inference would be entirely numerical (and slow). With "Parts", we get fast algebraic updates for Gaussian processes.

3. Python: Symbolic 'Parts'

integration_by_parts.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
# Problem: Integral of x * cos(x)
# u = x (Algebraic), dv = cos(x) (Trig)
# du = dx, v = sin(x)
# Result: x*sin(x) - Integral(sin(x)) = x*sin(x) + cos(x)

f = x * sp.cos(x)
result = sp.integrate(f, x)

print(f"Function: {f}")
print(f"Integral: {result}")
# Output: x*sin(x) + cos(x)

# Example 2: Logarithm
# Integral of ln(x) -> Use u=ln(x), dv=1
f2 = sp.log(x)
result2 = sp.integrate(f2, x)
print(f"Integral of ln(x): {result2}")
# Output: x*log(x) - x
