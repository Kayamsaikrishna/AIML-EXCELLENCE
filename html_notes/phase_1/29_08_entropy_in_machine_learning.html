Entropy in Machine Learning

From Theory to Tensor. How entropy drives the training, evaluation, and decision-making of modern AI models.

The Driving Force of Learning

In Machine Learning, training a model is essentially an act of Entropy Transfer. We take the high-entropy (noisy) weights of a fresh model and use data to "force" them into a low-entropy (structured) state that can make predictable decisions.

1. Decision Trees: Information Gain

One of the earliest applications of entropy in AI is in Decision Tree algorithms like ID3 and C4.5. The computer asks: "Which question will reduce my uncertainty the most?"

$$ IG(S, A) = H(S) - \sum_{v \in Values(A)} \frac{|S_v|}{|S|} H(S_v) $$
  • H(S): Entropy of the dataset before a split.
  • Weighted Sum: Expected entropy after the split on feature A.
  • The algorithm picks the feature with the highest Information Gain.

2. Neural Networks: Cross-Entropy Loss

When training a classifier, we want the predicted distribution \(Q\) (Softmax output) to match the true distribution \(P\) (the One-Hot label). We minimize Cross-Entropy:

$$ \mathcal{L} = -\sum P(x) \log Q(x) $$

Minimizing Cross-Entropy is mathematically equivalent to maximizing the Log-Likelihood of the correct class. It effectively punishes the model if it is "confident but wrong" (very low probability for the true class).

3. Aleatoric vs. Epistemic Uncertainty

Entropy allows us to distinguish between two types of "I don't know" in AI:

Aleatoric (Data Noise)

Inherent randomness in the process. (e.g., a blurry photo of a cat). No matter how much data you have, this entropy never goes to zero.

Epistemic (Model Ignorance)

Uncertainty because the model has never seen this type of data before. We can measure this by the entropy across multiple model predictions (Deep Ensembles).

Active Learning LLMs

Entropy-Based Selection

  • Active Learning: To save money on labeling, we only ask humans to label the data points where the model's prediction has the highest predictive entropy. These are the "educational" points.
  • Prompt Engineering: In few-shot learning, the selection of examples that minimize the conditional entropy of the LLM's response can lead to much more objective and accurate answers.
  • Contrastive Learning: By minimizing the entropy of the similarity matrix, models like SimCLR or CLIP learn to push unrelated items far apart in the embedding space.
