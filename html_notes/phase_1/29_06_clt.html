06. Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

The universe's destiny is Gaussian.

1. Definition

The Central Limit Theorem states that the sum (or average) of a large number of independent, identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with finite variance will follow a **Normal Distribution**, regardless of the shape of the original distribution.

$$ Z = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty $$

2. The "Law of Additive Noise"

If you take 100 random variables from a Uniform distribution, a Poisson distribution, or a coin flip, and you add them all together, the result is always a **Bell Curve**. This "Universal" behavior is why the Normal distribution is so frequent in nature and AI.

3. AI/ML Connection: Noise, Weights, and Residuals

The Gaussian Baseline

The CLT is why we assume **Gaussian Noise** in almost every AI model.

In **Linear Regression**, we assume the error term $\epsilon$ is normally distributed. Why? Because the "Total Error" is usually a sum of millions of tiny, independent random factors—by the CLT, that sum must be Gaussian. In **Neural Network Initialization**, we often initialize weights using a Normal distribution (like Xavier or Kaiming initialization). This ensures that the sums calculated by neurons in the first layer follow a predictable Gaussian range, preventing "Vanishing" or "Exploding" gradients. Finally, the CLT justifies using **Z-Scores** to normalize data; we can trust that standardized data follows a known distribution once our sample size is large enough.

4. Python: The CLT Demo

# Summing Uniform random variables
n_samples = 10000
sum_data = np.zeros(n_samples)

for _ in range(50): # Summing 50 variables
    sum_data += np.random.uniform(0, 1, n_samples)

plt.hist(sum_data, bins=50, density=True)
plt.title("CLT: Sum of Uniforms is Gaussian!")
plt.show()
