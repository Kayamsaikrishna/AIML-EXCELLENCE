Calculus 1.6 Flux

Surface Integrals

Flowing through a 3D boundary.

From Lines to Sheets

A Line Integral sums a field along a path. A Surface Integral sums a field across a surface \( S \) (like a balloon skin). The most common type is the Flux Integral: \[ \Phi = \iint_S \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{S} = \iint_S \vec{F} \cdot \vec{n} \, dA \] This measures the total "amount" of field \( \vec{F} \) passing *through* the surface. \( \vec{n} \) is the normal vector pointing outward.

2. AI/ML Connection: Diffusion Models

Probability Flux

In Diffusion Models (like Stable Diffusion), we model the "flow" of probability density from a clean image to pure noise (forward process) and back (reverse diffusion). Imagine the probability distribution of all possible images as a cloud in high-dimensional space.

The Probability Flux describes how this cloud moves and expands over time. To ensure the model is valid, we must ensure that the total probability "mass" is conserved. Calculating the Surface Integral of the probability flux across boundaries allows researchers to derive the Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) used for fast sampling (e.g., DDIM samplers).

3. Python: Flux Calculation

surface_flux.py
import sympy as sp

# Field F = < x, y, z > (Radial Outflow)
# Surface: Sphere of radius 2
# Parametrization: r(u, v) = < 2sin(u)cos(v), 2sin(u)sin(v), 2cos(u) >
# u in [0, pi], v in [0, 2pi]

u, v = sp.symbols('u v')
r = 2
x = r * sp.sin(u) * sp.cos(v)
y = r * sp.sin(u) * sp.sin(v)
z = r * sp.cos(u)

R = sp.Matrix([x, y, z])
F = sp.Matrix([x, y, z]) # The field at the surface

# Calculate Normal Vector n = dR/du x dR/dv
Ru = sp.diff(R, u)
Rv = sp.diff(R, v)
n = Ru.cross(Rv)

# Flux = Integral(F . n) du dv
integrand = F.dot(n)
# Simplify integrand before integrating (it will be huge)
integrand = sp.simplify(integrand) 
# Should be 4*sin(u) * r * r -> something like that. Actually 4*sin(u)*4 = 16sin(u)

flux = sp.integrate(sp.integrate(integrand, (v, 0, 2*sp.pi)), (u, 0, sp.pi))

print(f"Calculated Flux: {flux}")
# Expected: 4 * pi * r^3 = 32pi for vector field 
# Wait, Divergence Theorem: Div(F)=3. Vol=4/3 pi r^3. Flux=3 * Vol = 4 pi r^3.
# Correct result: 32 * pi.
