06. Limit Laws & Properties

The tools for calculating limits analytically.

1. The Algebraic Laws

If we know the limits \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L\) and \(\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = M\), we can use these laws:

  • Sum Law: \(\lim [f(x) + g(x)] = L + M\)
  • Difference Law: \(\lim [f(x) - g(x)] = L - M\)
  • Constant Multiple Law: \(\lim [c \cdot f(x)] = c \cdot L\)
  • Product Law: \(\lim [f(x) \cdot g(x)] = L \cdot M\)
  • Quotient Law: \(\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}\) (if \(M \neq 0\))
  • Power Law: \(\lim [f(x)]^n = L^n\)

2. Direct Substitution

For polynomials and most "well-behaved" functions (rational, trig, log), we can calculate the limit by simply plugging in the value, provided there is no division by zero or negative square root.

$$ \lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 5x - 1) = 3(2)^2 + 5(2) - 1 = 12 + 10 - 1 = 21 $$

3. AI/ML Connection: Gradient Composition

Composing Complex Operations

In Neural Networks, the final output is a composition of many simple limits (functions). When we apply the Chain Rule during backpropagation, we are relying on these limit laws to decompose the complex gradient of the entire network into a product of local gradients. If the limit laws didn't hold for combined operations, calculating the derivative of a 100-layer network would be impossible.

4. Python: Symbolic Computation

We can use the SymPy library to calculate limits analytically.

from sympy import Symbol, limit, sin

x = Symbol('x')
f = (x**2 + 3*x + 2) / (x + 1)

# Analytically computing the limit as x -> -1
# Factorized: (x+2)(x+1)/(x+1) -> x+2. As x -> -1, L = 1.
result = limit(f, x, -1)
print(f"Analytical Limit: {result}")
