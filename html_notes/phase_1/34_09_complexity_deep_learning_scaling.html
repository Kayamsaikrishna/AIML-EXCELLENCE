Complexity Theory Scaling Laws

Complexity in Deep Learning Scaling

Predicting the Future. Understanding the mathematical relationship between model size, data volume, and compute budget—the drivers of the LLM revolution.

The Chinchilla Moment

In 2022, DeepMind discovered that most models were "over-parameterized" but "under-trained." Complexity in Scaling is the study of how to distribute a fixed "compute budget" (FLOPs) between model parameters and training data to achieve minimal loss.

1. The Chinchilla Scaling Law

The optimal relationship between parameters ($N$) and data size ($D$) given a fixed compute budget ($C$):

$$ C \approx 6ND $$ $$ L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta} $$

Where $L$ is the loss, and $\alpha, \beta$ are scaling exponents. For Transformer models, research suggests that $N$ and $D$ should be scaled equally (e.g., doubling compute means increasing both parameters and data by $\approx 41\%$).

2. Computational Complexity of Scaling

FLOPs (Floating Point Operations)

A measure of total "work." Training a GPT-3 class model (175B) takes roughly $3.14 \times 10^{23}$ FLOPs. Scaling to a 10T model would require an orders-of-magnitude leap in energy and hardware.

Training Throughput

Scaling complexity isn't just about total work, but how much work can be done *in parallel*. Amdahl's Law and Communication Complexity define the limits of parallel scaling.

Deep Learning Future Tech

The Limits of Growth

  • Data Exhaustion: We are approaching the limit of high-quality human text data on the web. Scaling complexity is shifting from $O(D)$ (more data) to $O(I)$ (more iterations/synthetic data).
  • Inference Cost: While scaling parameters improves accuracy, it increases inference latency ($O(P)$). This is why techniques like MoE (Mixture of Experts) are popular—they scale parameters while keeping "active" compute constant.
  • Energy Complexity: Training giant models consumes MegaWatts of power. The complexity of AI is increasingly being measured in Joules per Token.
  • Emergent Abilities: Research suggests that certain capabilities (like logic or coding) only "emerge" once the complexity (compute/parameters) crosses a specific threshold.
