Properties of Correlation

Understanding the boundaries and behavior of the linear coefficient.

1. The Bounds: \([-1, 1]\)

For any Random Variables \(X\) and \(Y\): $$ -1 \le \text{Corr}(X, Y) \le 1 $$

Why? This is a direct result of the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality. It ensures that the covariance cannot exceed the spread of the individual variables.

Deep Intuition

In Linear Algebra, the correlation is actually the Cosine of the angle between two centered vectors.
- If \(\rho = 1\), the vectors point in the same direction (angle = 0°).
- If \(\rho = 0\), the vectors are orthogonal (angle = 90°).
- If \(\rho = -1\), the vectors point in opposite directions (angle = 180°).

2. Invariance to Linear Scaling

$$ \text{Corr}(aX + b, cY + d) = \text{Corr}(X, Y) $$ (Assuming \(a, c > 0\))

ML Impact: This is a huge property. It means that whether you measure temperature in Celsius or Fahrenheit, or height in inches or meters, the correlation coefficient stays identical.

Unlike covariance, correlation doesn't care about your units. It only cares about the pattern.

3. Symmetry

$$ \rho_{X,Y} = \rho_{Y,X} $$ Correlation is a shared property. It doesn't imply that \(X\) causes \(Y\) or \(Y\) causes \(X\). It only measures their relationship in a symmetric way.

4. Zero Correlation vs. Independence

Statistics Caveats

The Trap

Independence implies zero correlation.
Zero correlation does NOT imply independence.

If \(X \sim \text{Uniform}(-1, 1)\) and \(Y = X^2\), they are purely dependent (if you know \(X\), you know \(Y\) exactly). But their Pearson correlation is 0 because the relationship is non-linear (it wraps back on itself).
