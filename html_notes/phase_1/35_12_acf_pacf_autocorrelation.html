ACF & PACF (Autocorrelation)

Measuring how much the past echoes into the future.

1. The Echo Loop

ACF (AutoCorrelation Function) measures the correlation of a time series with itself at different lags. If ACF is high at lag 1, yesterday's value strongly predicts today's.

2. The "Direct" Effect: PACF

PACF (Partial AutoCorrelation Function)** measures the correlation between $Y_t$ and $Y_{t-k}$ *after* removing the effects of all intermediate lags. It tells you the "Direct" influence of a past step.

  • ACF helps identify 'q' (Moving Average order).
  • PACF helps identify 'p' (AutoRegressive order).

3. AI/ML Connection: Lag Selection and Context Windows

Determining the History Requirement

In AI, ACF and PACF are used to decide the Input Dimension of a Model**.

If you are building an **LSTM** for stock prices, how many days should the AI look back? 30? 60? 365? By looking at the PACF, an AI engineer identifies where the "Information" dies out. If the PACF becomes insignificant at lag 12, then a **Context Window** of 12 steps is sufficient. This is critical for Transformer Optimization**: instead of an infinite attention window, we can use "Local Attention" based on the autocorrelation profile of the data. It saves billions of FLOPs during inference by ensuring the model isn't "Thinking" about irrelevant history. Understanding the echo loop is what allows AI to separate **True Dynamics** from **Random Noise**.

4. Python: Plotting Correlograms

from statsmodels.graphics.tsaplots import plot_acf, plot_pacf

# 40 lags of autocorrelation
plot_acf(data, lags=40)
plot_pacf(data, lags=40)
# Points outside the blue shaded area are 'Statistically Significant'
