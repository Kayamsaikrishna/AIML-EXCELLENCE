14. Logarithms: Taming Giants

Turning Multiplication into Addition. The numerical savior of AI.

1. The Definition

$$ y = \log_b(x) \iff b^y = x $$

Logarithms ask: "How many times must I multiply the base to get x?"

Key Property: Product Rule

$$ \log(a \cdot b) = \log(a) + \log(b) $$

This property is the foundation of Information Theory and Maximum Likelihood Estimation.

2. The Underflow Problem

Probabilities are small numbers ($0.0 \le p \le 1.0$). If you multiply many probabilities (e.g., in a language model predicting the next 100 words), the number becomes incredibly tiny.

$$ 0.1^{100} \approx 0.000...001 $$

Computers cannot store this. They round it to exactly **zero**. This is called Underflow.

The Solution: Log-Likelihood

Instead of maximizing likelihood \( L = \prod p_i \), we maximize log-likelihood \( \log L = \sum \log p_i \).

  • Multiplication becomes Addition (Stable).
  • Tiny numbers become identifiable negative numbers (e.g., \(\log(10^{-9}) = -20.7\)).
  • The peak (maximum) stays at the exact same location because log is monotonic.

3. Coding Stability

Demonstrating how Logs save us from numerical death.

// Python: Why we use Logs
import math

# A sequence of probabilities (e.g., confidence in a sentence)
probs = [0.1] * 400  # 400 events with 10% chance each

# 1. Direct Multiplication
product = 1.0
for p in probs:
    product *= p

print(f"Product: {product}") 
# Output: 0.0 (Underflow! Information lost.)

# 2. Sum of Logs
log_sum = 0.0
for p in probs:
    log_sum += math.log(p)

print(f"Log Sum: {log_sum}") 
# Output: -921.03
# We still have the precise value. We can compare it to other models.
