1. The Log-Product Rule

The single most useful property in Applied Math:

$$ \log(a \cdot b) = \log(a) + \log(b) $$

Why AI Needs This?

Probabilities are small numbers ($p < 1$). If you multiply 1,000 probabilities:

$$ 0.9^{1000} \approx 1.7 \times 10^{-46} $$

Computers cannot store this (Underflow). But in Log-space:

$$ 1000 \cdot \log(0.9) \approx -105.3 $$

This is a perfectly manageable number.

2. Logs as Information (Bits)

Claude Shannon defined information using base-2 logs:

$$ I(x) = -\log_2 p(x) $$

The lower the probability of an event, the higher its "Surprise" (Information content). This is the basis of Cross-Entropy Loss.

3. Numerical Stability in Python

Demonstrating Underflow vs Log-Sum-Exp.

import numpy as np

# 1. Product Underflow
probs = np.full(1000, 0.9)
product = np.prod(probs)
print(f"Direct Product: {product}") 
# Likely prints 0.0 or a very tiny float

# 2. Log-Sum Trick
log_probs = np.sum(np.log(probs))
print(f"Sum of Logs: {log_probs}") 
# Prints -105.36... Safe!

# 3. NLL Loss (Negative Log Likelihood)
# Minimizing NLL is equivalent to Maximizing Probability
nll = -log_probs
print(f"Loss to Minimize: {nll}")
