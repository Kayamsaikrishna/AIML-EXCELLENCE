02. Matrix Representation of Transformations

Finding the matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) for any linear map \(T\).

1. The Basis Connection

Every linear transformation \(T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m\) is uniquely determined by what it does to the Basis Vectors \(\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \dots, \mathbf{e}_n\).

$$ \mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} | & | & & | \\ T(\mathbf{e}_1) & T(\mathbf{e}_2) & \dots & T(\mathbf{e}_n) \\ | & | & & | \end{bmatrix} $$

This "Standard Matrix" allows us to compute \(T(\mathbf{x})\) simply as \(\mathbf{Ax}\).

2. Standard Transformations

  • Scaling: \(\begin{bmatrix} k & 0 \\ 0 & k \end{bmatrix}\)
  • Rotation (\(\theta\)): \(\begin{bmatrix} \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{bmatrix}\)
  • Reflection (x-axis): \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\)

3. AI Context: Convolutional Kernels

Matrix form of Filters

In Computer Vision, a blurring or sharpening filter is a localized operation. However, it can be written as a massive Toeplitz Matrix acting on the flattened image vector. This proves that Convolution is a specialized Linear Transformation.

4. Python: Constructing the Matrix

import numpy as np

# A transformation that flips x and doubles y
def T(v):
    return np.array([-v[0], 2*v[1]])

# Find the standard matrix A
e1 = np.array([1, 0])
e2 = np.array([0, 1])

A = np.column_stack((T(e1), T(e2)))

print(f"Standard Matrix A:\n{A}")

# Verification
x = np.array([3, 4])
print(f"T(x):  {T(x)}")
print(f"A @ x: {A @ x}")
