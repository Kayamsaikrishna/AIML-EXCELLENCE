Stats 1.4 Direct Matching

Method of Moments (MoM)

A simple, intuitive way to estimate parameters by forcing the data's "moments" to match the model's "moments".

The Intuition

"If the average height in my sample is 5.8 feet, I will assume the average height in the population is also 5.8 feet."

MoM generalizes this: To find \(k\) unknown parameters, you set up \(k\) equations matching the sample mean, sample variance, etc., to their theoretical formulas.

1. The Process

Set the \(k\)-th sample moment equal to the \(k\)-th theoretical moment: $$ \frac{1}{n} \sum X_i^k = E[X^k] $$

Example: Gamma Distribution - Mean: \(E[X] = \alpha \beta\) - Variance: \(E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 = \alpha \beta^2\)
You calculate the sample mean and variance, then solve for \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

2. Pros and Cons

Advantages

  • Extremely simple to calculate.
  • Does not require calculating complex likelihoods.
  • Good for "Rough Estimates".

Disadvantages

  • Usually not efficient (wider variance than MLE).
  • Estimates can sometimes be impossible (e.g., negative variance).

3. ML Connection: Initialization

Deep Learning GMMs

The Speed-accuracy Tradeoff

In complex models like Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs), finding the perfect parameters via MLE takes many iterations of Expectation-Maximization.

Data scientists often use the Method of Moments to get a "good enough" starting point for the parameters. These rough estimates are then used to initialize the more intensive MLE or gradient descent processes, ensuring faster convergence.
