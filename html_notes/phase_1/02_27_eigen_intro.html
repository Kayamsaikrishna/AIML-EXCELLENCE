1. The Definition

When a matrix \( A \) acts on a vector, it usually rotates and stretches it. However, some special vectors do not turn; they only get stretched.

$$ A\mathbf{v} = \lambda \mathbf{v} $$
  • \( \mathbf{v} \): Eigenvector. The "Axis" of rotation/stretch.
  • \( \lambda \): Eigenvalue. The amount of stretch.

2. Stability Analysis

In Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), we multiply the state vector \( h \) by the weight matrix \( W \) hundreds of times.

$$ h_{t} = W h_{t-1} \implies h_{T} = W^T h_0 $$

If the Spectral Radius (largest eigenvalue \( |\lambda_{max}| \)) > 1, the signal Explodes. If \( < 1 \), it Vanishes. This is why we use Orthogonal Initialization (where \( |\lambda| = 1 \)).

3. Calculating the Spectrum

Finding the eigenvalues using NumPy.

import numpy as np

# A Shear Matrix
A = np.array([
    [1, 1],
    [0, 1]
])

# Compute Eigenvalues/Vectors
vals, vecs = np.linalg.eig(A)

print(f"Eigenvalues: {vals}")
# [1. 1.] (Repeated eigenvalue)

print("\nEigenvectors (Columns):")
print(vecs)
# Note: Shear matrices only have 1 eigenvector [1, 0]
# The second one printed might be due to numerical noise or generalized implementation.

# Validating
v = vecs[:, 0]
lambda_val = vals[0]

lhs = np.dot(A, v)
rhs = lambda_val * v

print(f"\nCheck Av = lambda*v: {np.allclose(lhs, rhs)}")
