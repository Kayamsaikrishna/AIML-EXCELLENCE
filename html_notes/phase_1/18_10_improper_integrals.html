10. Improper Integrals

Integrating to infinity and beyond.

1. Types of Improper Integrals

An integral is improper if it has an infinite limit or an infinite discontinuity within the interval.

  • Infinite Limits: \(\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2} dx\). We solve this by taking the limit: \(\lim_{t \to \infty} \int_{1}^{t}\).
  • Infinite Discontinuities: \(\int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} dx\). Here, the function blows up at \(x=0\). We use a limit approaching 0 from the right.

2. Convergence vs. Divergence

If the limit exists and is a finite number, the integral is said to converge. If the limit goes to infinity or does not exist, the integral diverges and the "area" is infinitely large.

3. AI/ML Connection: Probability Total Area

Normalizing the World

One of the most important rules in AI is that the area under a Probability Density Function (PDF) must sum to exactly 1. For distributions with infinite ranges (like the Gaussian/Normal Distribution), this is an improper integral:

$$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2} dx = \sqrt{\pi} $$

If this improper integral diverged, probabilities wouldn't make sense. AI engineers use these integrals to calculate Z-scores and p-values, which are the foundations of determining if a model's improvement is statistically significant or just random noise.

4. Python: Improper Integration

import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = 1 / x**2

# Integrate from 1 to infinity
area = sp.integrate(f, (x, 1, sp.oo))

print(f"Integral of 1/x^2 from 1 to oo: {area}")
# Result: 1 (It converges!)

# Try 1/x
area_divergent = sp.integrate(1/x, (x, 1, sp.oo))
print(f"Integral of 1/x from 1 to oo: {area_divergent}")
# Result: oo (It diverges!)
