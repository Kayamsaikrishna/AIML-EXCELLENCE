Calculus 1.4 Approximation

Numerical Integration

When exact math fails, estimation takes over.

The Unsolvable Majority

Most functions (like \(e^{-x^2}\), \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\)) simply do not have an antiderivative that can be written with elementary functions. For these, we stop looking for \(F(x)\) and start approximating the area directly.

1. Algorithms

  • Trapezoidal Rule: Connects points with straight lines (linear interpolation). Good accuracy \(O(h^2)\).
  • Simpson's Rule: Connects points with parabolas (quadratic interpolation). Very high accuracy \(O(h^4)\).

$$ T_n = \frac{\Delta x}{2} [f(x_0) + 2f(x_1) + ... + f(x_n)] $$

2. AI/ML Connection: Monte Carlo Integration

Sampling the Latent Space

In Bayesian Neural Networks and Reinforcement Learning, we often need to integrate over 1,000+ dimensions.

Grid-based methods (like Simpson’s Rule) fail because the number of points grows exponentially (The Curse of Dimensionality). The solution is Monte Carlo Integration: \[ \int f(x) p(x) dx \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N f(x_i) \] We approximate the integral by just taking the average of random samples. This method converges regardless of dimension, making modern AI possible.

3. Python: Simpson vs. Monte Carlo

monte_carlo_demo.py
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import simpson

def f(x):
    return np.exp(-x**2)

# 1. Deterministic (Simpson's Rule)
# Integrals in 1D are fast with Simpson
x = np.linspace(-3, 3, 1000)
y = f(x)
area_simp = simpson(y, x=x)
print(f"Simpson's Area: {area_simp:.5f}")

# 2. Stochastic (Monte Carlo)
# Better for high dimensions
N = 10000
# Sample uniformly from [-3, 3]
samples = np.random.uniform(-3, 3, N)
# Average height * width of interval
area_mc = np.mean(f(samples)) * (3 - (-3))

print(f"Monte Carlo Area: {area_mc:.5f}")
# MC will be close, but noisy.
