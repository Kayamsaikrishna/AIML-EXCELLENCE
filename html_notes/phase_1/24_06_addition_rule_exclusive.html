06. Addition Rule for Mutually Exclusive Events

The simplest way to combine possibilities.

1. Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events $A$ and $B$ are Mutually Exclusive (or Disjoint) if they cannot occur at the same time. Their intersection is empty: $A \cap B = \emptyset$.

2. The Formula

If $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, the probability of either occurring is simply the sum of their individual probabilities:

$$ P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) $$

This is the third Kolmogorov Axiom in its simplest form.

3. AI/ML Connection: One-Hot Encoding

Unique Identities

In Categorical Classification (like MNIST digit recognition), the classes are mutually exclusive. An image of a "7" cannot also be an image of a "3".

Because these categories are disjoint, the output of our Softmax Layer treats them as such. The probabilities for all classes sum to 1. If we want to know the probability that a digit is "either a 1 or a 7", we just add their individual probabilities. One-Hot Encoding is the architectural representation of mutually exclusive events: only one "event" (bit) can be active at a time. This rule allows us to treat multiclass classification as a simple additive probability space.

4. Python: Mutually Exclusive Check

# Model predicts: [Cat: 0.7, Dog: 0.2, Bird: 0.1]
probs = {"Cat": 0.7, "Dog": 0.2, "Bird": 0.1}

# P(Mammal) = P(Cat) + P(Dog)
p_mammal = probs["Cat"] + probs["Dog"]

print(f"P(Mammal): {p_mammal:.2f}")
# Simple addition works because a Cat cannot be a Dog.
