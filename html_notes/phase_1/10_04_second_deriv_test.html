Calculus 1.3 The Shortcut

The Second Derivative Test

Why check neighbors when you can just check the curvature at the point?

Local Geometry

If you are standing on a flat spot (slope=0) and the ground curves UP around you, you must be in a hole (Minimum). If it curves DOWN, you are on a hill (Maximum).

1. The Test

Given \(f'(c) = 0\):

  • \(f''(c) > 0\): Concave Up \( \to \) Local Minimum.
  • \(f''(c) < 0\): Concave Down \( \to \) Local Maximum.
  • \(f''(c) = 0\): Inconclusive. (Could be min, max, or inflection). Use 1st Deriv test.

2. AI/ML Connection: The Hessian

Multivariable Curvature

In deep learning, we have thousands of weights. The "Second Derivative" becomes a matrix of partial derivatives called the Hessian Matrix \(H\).

The "Second Derivative Test" for ML involves checking the Eigenvalues of \(H\):

  • All Eigenvalues > 0: Local Minimum (Stable, Good).
  • All Eigenvalues < 0: Local Maximum (Unstable, we roll away).
  • Mixed (+ and -): Saddle Point.

Since calculating \(H\) is expensive, optimizing without it (using just gradients) but avoiding Saddle Points is a major research area.

3. Python: Automated Classifier

classify_extrema.py
import sympy as sp

x = sp.Symbol('x')
f = x**3 - 3*x + 5

# 1. Derivatives
f1 = sp.diff(f, x)
f2 = sp.diff(f1, x)

# 2. Find Critical Points
crit_points = sp.solve(f1, x)

print(f"Function: {f}")
print(f"Critical Points: {crit_points}")
print("-" * 30)

for c in crit_points:
    concavity = f2.subs(x, c)
    if concavity > 0:
        result = "Local MIN (Box)"
    elif concavity < 0:
        result = "Local MAX (Hill)"
    else:
        result = "Inconclusive"
        
    print(f"Point x={c}: f''={concavity} -> {result}")
