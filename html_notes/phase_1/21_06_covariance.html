Prob 1.4 The Direction

Covariance

Measuring the joint variation of two random variables.

The Trend Detector

Variance tells you how much a single variable "spreads out". Covariance tells you if two variables spread out together.

- If high values of \(X\) tend to occur with high values of \(Y\), covariance is positive.
- If high values of \(X\) tend to occur with low values of \(Y\), covariance is negative.
- If there is no linear relationship, covariance is zero.

1. The Formula

Definition: $$ \text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[(X - E[X])(Y - E[Y])] $$ Computational Shortcut: $$ \text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] $$

Relation to Variance: The covariance of a variable with itself is just the variance: $$ \text{Cov}(X, X) = \text{Var}(X) $$

2. Interpretation and Units

The magnitude of covariance is hard to interpret because it depends on the units of \(X\) and \(Y\).

  • If \(X\) is Height (cm) and \(Y\) is Weight (kg), covariance is in units of cm·kg.
  • If you change Height to meters, the covariance magnitude changes, even though the relationship is the same.

This limitation is why we use Correlation (scaled covariance) for easier interpretation.

3. ML Connection: PCA and Data Redundancy

Dimensionality Reduction Unsupervised

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

PCA is a technique that reduces the number of features while keeping as much information as possible. It works by looking at the Covariance between features.

If two features (like "Height in inches" and "Height in cm") have high positive covariance, they are redundant. PCA finds new "Principal Components" (directions) that have zero covariance (orthogonal), effectively compressing the data by removing correlated noise.

4. Properties

  • \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = \text{Cov}(Y, X)\) (Symmetric).
  • \(\text{Cov}(aX, bY) = ab \, \text{Cov}(X, Y)\).
  • \(\text{Cov}(X, c) = 0\) (A constant doesn't vary).
