Prob 1.4 Tail Bounds

Markov's Inequality

Using the mean to put a ceiling on the "worst-case" probability.

The Power of Averages

Markov's Inequality is the most basic "tail bound". It tells you something profound: If you know the average of a non-negative variable, you can limit how often that variable can be very large.

"If the average height is 6 feet, it's impossible for 50% of people to be 20 feet tall."

1. The Formula

For any non-negative random variable \(X\) and any constant \(a > 0\): $$ P(X \ge a) \le \frac{E[X]}{a} $$

Key Restriction: \(X\) must be non-negative (\(X \ge 0\)). If \(X\) can be negative, the mean tells us nothing about the tails (because large negative values could cancel out large positive ones).

2. Intuition: The Weight of the Tail

Imagine a distribution. If 10% of the population has 10 times the average income, that 10% accounts for 100% of the total income. There's no room left for anyone else.

3. ML Connection: Data Quality Control

Data Engineering Robustness

Monitoring Loss Spikes

In production monitoring, we often track the average latency (\(E[X]\)). If a system's average latency is 10ms, Markov tells us that no more than 1 in 10 requests (10%) can take longer than 100ms.

While Markov is "loose" (it's often a very pessimistic upper bound), it is universal. It requires zero knowledge about the shape of the distribution (whether it's normal, exponential, or weird noise), as long as it's non-negative.

4. Limitations

Pessimistic Bound: Markov is usually not "tight". The actual probability of exceeding a value \(a\) is often much lower than \(E[X]/a\). However, its beauty lies in its simplicity and generality. It serves as the foundation for the much tighter **Chebyshev Inequality**.
