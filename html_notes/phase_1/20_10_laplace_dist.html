Prob 1.4 The Sharp

The Laplace Distribution

The "Double Exponential": A sharper, peakier alternative to Gaussian.

Peakiness and Robustness

The Laplace distribution looks like two exponential distributions back-to-back. Compared to the Normal distribution, it has a sharper peak at the mean and heavier tails.

While the Gaussian is associated with L2 distance (squared), the Laplace is associated with L1 distance (absolute value).

1. The Formula

A Random Variable \(X\) follows a Laplace distribution with mean \(\mu\) and scale \(b > 0\): $$ f(x) = \frac{1}{2b} \exp\left( -\frac{|x-\mu|}{b} \right) $$
  • The term \(|x-\mu|\) creates the "V" shape in the exponent.
  • Unlike the Gaussian which drops off as \(x^2\), the Laplace drops off as \(|x|\).

2. Moments

  • Mean: \(E[X] = \mu\).
  • Variance: \(\text{Var}(X) = 2b^2\).

3. ML Connection: L1 Regularization (Lasso)

Deep Learning Regularization

Why L1 leads to Sparsity?

In Linear Regression, L1 Regularization (Lasso) adds a penalty term proportional to the absolute values of the weights: \(\lambda \sum |w_i|\).

From a Bayesian perspective, this is equivalent to assuming a Laplace Prior on the weights.
Because the Laplace distribution is extremely "pointy" at zero, the optimization process is strongly encouraged to set many weights exactly to zero.

Gaussian Prior (L2) \(\to\) Small weights but rarely zero.
Laplace Prior (L1) \(\to\) Sparse weights (mostly zero).

MAE vs. MSE

Minimizing the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) is equivalent to Maximum Likelihood Estimation where the errors follow a Laplace distribution. This makes MAE more robust to outliers than MSE.

4. Differential Privacy

The Laplace distribution is the crown jewel of Differential Privacy. To protect personal data, we add "Laplace Noise" to query results. The scale of the noise is proportional to the "sensitivity" of the query (how much one person can change the result).
