38. Law of Sines & Law of Cosines

Solving the Oblique (Non-Right) Triangle.

1. The Formulas

$$ \frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} \quad \text{(Law of Sines)} $$ $$ c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos C \quad \text{(Law of Cosines)} $$

Note: Law of Cosines is the general version of Pythagoras. If \(C = 90^\circ\), then \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\), and we get \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\).

2. SLAM & Spatial AI

Triangulation in the Wild

In **Robotic Navigation (SLAM)**, a robot sees a landmark from two different positions. Knowing the distance it traveled (base are) and the angles it measured, it uses the **Law of Sines** to triangulate the exact GPS coordinate of that landmark.

  • Satellite Geolocation: Calculating the distance to an object using signal delay from three satellites is a 3D extension of the Law of Cosines.
  • Mesh Generation: In 3D graphics AI, we simplify complex shapes into triangles. These laws ensure the mesh is geometrically consistent.

3. Python: Landmarked Triangulation

import numpy as np

# A triangle with sides a=5, b=7 and included angle C=60°
a = 5
b = 7
angle_C_deg = 60
angle_C_rad = np.deg2rad(angle_C_deg)

# Law of Cosines to find side c
c_sq = a**2 + b**2 - 2*a*b*np.cos(angle_C_rad)
c = np.sqrt(c_sq)

print(f"Third side length c: {c:.2f}") 
# Output: 6.24
