Bipartite Graphs & Matching Theory

Dividing and Pairing. Modeling scenarios where two distinct sets of entities interact, and finding the optimal way to match them.

The Bipartite Property

A graph is Bipartite if its vertices can be divided into two disjoint sets \(U\) and \(V\) such that every edge connects a vertex in \(U\) to one in \(V\). No edge exists within \(U\) or within \(V\). This is the mathematical model for Recommendation Systems (Users and Movies).

1. Matching Theory

Matching (\(M\))

A subset of edges where no two edges share a vertex. A Perfect Matching covers every vertex in the graph.

Hall's Marriage Theorem

A Bipartite graph has a matching that covers \(U\) if and only if for every subset \(S \subseteq U\), the number of neighbors is at least the size of \(S\): \(|N(S)| \geq |S|\).

2. Maximum Bipartite Matching

Finding the largest possible matching in a bipartite graph. This is often solved using Max-Flow logic or the Hopcroft-Karp algorithm.

Assignment Problem: Assigning \(n\) tasks to \(m\) workers such that efficiency is maximized and each worker gets at most one task. This is a Weighted Bipartite Matching problem.

Recommendation Deep Learning

Matching in AI

  • Collaborative Filtering: The User-Item interaction matrix is literally a Bipartite Graph. Recommending a movie is finding "strong" potential edges in this graph.
  • Object Tracking: In Computer Vision, after detecting objects in frame \(t\) and frame \(t+1\), we use Bipartite Matching (the Hungarian Algorithm) to determine which detection in the new frame corresponds to which object from the old frame.
  • Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): The training process can be viewed as a Bipartite competition/matching between the Generator and the Discriminator.
  • Word Alignments: In Machine Translation, matching words from Source language to Target language is a bipartite alignment task.
